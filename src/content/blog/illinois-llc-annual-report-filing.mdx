## What Is the Illinois LLC Annual Report?

The Illinois LLC annual report is a mandatory yearly filing required under Section 50-1 of the Illinois Limited Liability Company Act. Every Illinois LLC — whether domestic (formed in Illinois) or foreign (formed elsewhere but registered to do business in Illinois) — must file this report with the Illinois Secretary of State once per year to maintain good standing.

The annual report is not a tax return, and it doesn't replace any federal or state tax obligations. It's a standalone administrative filing that confirms your LLC's basic information is still current. Specifically, the Illinois LLC annual report updates the following:

- **Registered agent and registered office address** — the person or entity in Illinois designated to receive service of process
- **Principal place of business** — your LLC's main operating address
- **Names and business addresses of managers** (for manager-managed LLCs) or **members** (for member-managed LLCs)
- **The federal employer identification number (FEIN)** of the LLC
- **A description of the business** the LLC conducts in Illinois

If none of this information has changed since your last filing, you can simply confirm it and pay the fee — the report becomes a quick "all clear" check-in. If anything has changed, this is the moment to update it. Failing to keep your registered agent and registered office address current is one of the leading reasons Illinois LLCs lose lawsuits by default judgment in 2026: legal documents get mailed to a stale address, the owner never sees them, and the deadline to respond passes.

## When Is Your Illinois LLC Annual Report Due?

Here's where Illinois trips up many business owners: **there is no single universal due date**. Instead, your Illinois LLC annual report is due on the **first day of the anniversary month of your LLC's formation**, every single year.

A few concrete examples:
- LLC formed on **March 14, 2024** → annual report due every year on **March 1**
- LLC formed on **November 30, 2022** → annual report due every year on **November 1**
- LLC formed on **June 5, 2025** → first annual report due **June 1, 2026**

You actually have a small filing window: the Illinois Secretary of State accepts the annual report up to **60 days before** the first day of your anniversary month. So if your due date is March 1, you can file as early as January 1. Most experienced business owners file as soon as the window opens to avoid forgetting later.

To find your exact due date, search the [Illinois Secretary of State Business Entity Database](https://apps.ilsos.gov/businessentitysearch/) using your LLC name. The free public lookup will show your formation date, current status, and next annual report due date. Bookmark that page — you'll come back to it every year.

> **A note from my own experience:** I've helped a number of Illinois business owners untangle administrative dissolutions, and in nearly every case the root cause was the same: they assumed Illinois used a calendar-year deadline like Florida (May 1) or Texas (May 15), so they filed in April or May only to learn their actual deadline had been six months earlier. Don't assume — check your formation month on the SOS website right now if you haven't filed yet this year.

## How Much Does the Illinois LLC Annual Report Cost?

The standard Illinois LLC annual report filing fee in 2026 is **$75**, paid directly to the Illinois Secretary of State at the time of filing. This is the same flat fee regardless of how many members your LLC has, your gross revenue, or how long the LLC has been in existence.

Here's the full fee schedule you should know:

- **Annual report (filed on time):** $75
- **Annual report (filed late):** $75 + $100 late penalty = **$175 total**
- **Reinstatement after administrative dissolution:** $200 reinstatement fee + all unpaid annual reports + accrued penalties (often $400–$700+ depending on how long the LLC has been dissolved)
- **Series LLC annual report:** $75 base fee + $50 per active series being maintained

Series LLCs are popular among Illinois real estate investors because each protected series has its own liability shield. But each active series adds $50 to your annual report cost, so a Series LLC with 5 active series would owe $325 per year ($75 + 5 × $50).

Compared to other states, Illinois sits near the middle of the pack. [Wyoming charges $60](/blog/wyoming-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/), Delaware charges a flat $300 franchise tax for LLCs, and California hits LLCs with an $800 minimum annual franchise tax plus an $20 statement fee. Illinois at $75 is reasonable. For a side-by-side state comparison, see our [Best State to Form an LLC](/blog/best-state-to-form-an-llc/) guide.

Payment options through the Illinois Secretary of State online portal include credit card (Visa, MasterCard, Discover, American Express), debit card, and ACH/electronic check. There's a small convenience fee for credit card payments — typically around $3–$5 — and no fee for ACH.

## How to File Your Illinois LLC Annual Report Online (Step-by-Step)

Filing online is by far the easiest method, and the Illinois Secretary of State actively encourages it. Paper filings exist but are slower, more error-prone, and require mailing a check. Here's the exact process for the online Illinois LLC annual report filing in 2026:

### Step 1: Gather Your Information

Before you start, have the following ready:
- Your LLC's **file number** (assigned by the Illinois SOS at formation — found on your original certificate or in the Business Entity Database)
- Your LLC's current **registered agent name and Illinois address**
- Your LLC's **principal office address**
- **Names and business addresses** of all managers (manager-managed) or members (member-managed)
- Your LLC's **FEIN** (federal employer ID number)
- A **credit card or bank account** for payment

### Step 2: Go to the Illinois Secretary of State LLC Portal

Navigate to the [Illinois SOS LLC services portal](https://apps.ilsos.gov/businessservices/) and select "File Annual Report" from the LLC services menu. You don't need to create an account for a one-time filing — you can log in with just your LLC file number and an additional verification field.

### Step 3: Verify Your LLC's Information

The system will pre-populate your LLC's current information on file. Review every field carefully:
- Registered agent name and address
- Registered office address (must be the same as the registered agent's address)
- Principal place of business
- Manager or member names and addresses
- Business purpose description

If any field has changed, update it now. Pay particular attention to the registered agent — if your registered agent has resigned, moved, or you've switched services, this is the moment to fix it. (For more on this, see our guide on [how to change your registered agent](/blog/how-to-change-registered-agent/).)

### Step 4: Confirm and Pay

Once you've verified all information, confirm the filing and pay the $75 fee. You'll receive an instant electronic receipt — save the PDF and keep it with your business records. The Illinois Secretary of State typically processes online annual reports the same business day, sometimes within minutes.

### Step 5: Update Your Records

Mark the next year's deadline on your calendar (anniversary month, day 1) immediately. I recommend setting a reminder for **30 days before** the next due date, plus a backup reminder **7 days before**. Better yet, use a registered agent service that proactively reminds you. ## What Happens If You Miss the Illinois Annual Report Deadline?

Illinois has a clear escalation path for delinquent annual reports. Here's exactly what happens at each stage in 2026:

**Day 1 past due (the day after your anniversary month begins):**
A $100 late penalty is automatically added to your $75 base fee. Your filing total becomes $175.

**60+ days past due:**
Your LLC's status in the Business Entity Database changes from "Active" to "Not in Good Standing." You can still operate, but you can't get a Certificate of Good Standing — which is required for things like business loans, opening certain bank accounts, applying for some commercial leases, and registering to do business in another state.

**180 days past due:**
The Illinois Secretary of State begins the **administrative dissolution** process. Your LLC will be administratively dissolved by operation of law. Once dissolved, your LLC technically loses its limited liability protection — meaning if you continue operating as a "dissolved" LLC, you could be treated as a sole proprietor or general partnership for liability purposes, exposing your personal assets.

**Beyond dissolution:**
If your LLC is administratively dissolved, you have up to **5 years** to reinstate it by paying the $200 reinstatement fee plus all back annual report fees and late penalties. After 5 years, reinstatement is no longer possible — you'd need to form a brand new LLC with a different name (since the old name may be reserved or simply unavailable).

The financial math is brutal once you fall behind. An LLC that's been dissolved for two missed annual reports could owe: $200 reinstatement + 2 × ($75 + $100) = **$550 minimum** to come back into good standing. This is why the [consequences of letting your LLC lapse](/blog/what-happens-if-you-don-t-renew-your-llc/) are far more expensive than the original $75 filing.

## How to Reinstate a Dissolved Illinois LLC

If your Illinois LLC has already been administratively dissolved due to a missed annual report, don't panic — reinstatement is straightforward as long as you act within 5 years of the dissolution date.

The reinstatement process requires:
1. **Filing all delinquent annual reports** (one for each year you missed)
2. **Paying all accumulated fees and penalties** — base fees, late penalties, and the $200 reinstatement fee
3. **Submitting an Application for Reinstatement (Form LLC-35.40)** through the Illinois SOS online portal or by mail
4. **Confirming your registered agent is still valid** — if your old registered agent has resigned or moved, you'll need to appoint a new one before reinstatement is approved

Reinstatement typically takes 7–10 business days online. Once approved, your LLC is restored as if it had never been dissolved — meaning the limited liability shield retroactively covers any business activity during the dissolution gap. (However, this retroactive protection does *not* apply if you were dissolved for more than 5 years, which is why the timing matters.)

In my experience, the most common reinstatement headache isn't the paperwork — it's discovering mid-process that your original registered agent has resigned, your business name has been claimed by someone else, or your federal EIN records are inconsistent with state records. ## Common Illinois Annual Report Mistakes to Avoid

Even though the Illinois LLC annual report filing is simple, owners make the same mistakes year after year. Here are the ones I see most often:

**1. Confusing the annual report with the LLC's tax return.**
Illinois has a separate Pass-Through Entity tax return (Form IL-1065 or IL-1120-ST) and a personal property replacement tax. The annual report is purely an SOS filing — it does not satisfy any tax obligation.

**2. Using the registered agent's address as the principal office.**
These can be the same, but they don't have to be. If you use a registered agent service like [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/), your registered agent address will be their office — but your principal office should be wherever you actually operate from. Mixing these up is a common error that can cause notices to go to the wrong place.

**3. Filing late by one day.**
The $100 late penalty kicks in on day one — there's no grace period. If your due date is March 1 and you file on March 2, you owe $175 instead of $75. The Illinois SOS does not waive late fees.

**4. Not updating member/manager information after changes.**
If you've added or removed a member during the year (see our guide on [how to add a member to an LLC](/blog/how-to-add-a-member-to-an-llc/)), your annual report must reflect the current roster. Filing with outdated information could create disputes during litigation or financing.

**5. Forgetting the BOI report is separate.**
Don't confuse the Illinois LLC annual report (state-level) with the federal Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report (federal, filed with FinCEN). They're entirely different filings with different deadlines and different penalties. See our complete [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for the federal requirements.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does the Illinois LLC annual report cost in 2026?

The Illinois LLC annual report filing fee is **$75** in 2026, paid to the Illinois Secretary of State. If you file late, an additional $100 penalty applies, bringing the total to $175. Series LLCs pay $75 base plus $50 for each active series being maintained.

### When is my Illinois LLC annual report due?

Your Illinois LLC annual report is due on the **first day of your LLC's anniversary month**, every year. So if your LLC was formed on July 22, your annual report is due every July 1. The Illinois SOS opens the filing window 60 days before this date, and a $100 late penalty applies the moment you miss it.

### Can I file my Illinois LLC annual report online?

Yes — and it's the easiest method. Visit the [Illinois Secretary of State LLC portal](https://apps.ilsos.gov/businessservices/), enter your LLC file number, verify your information, and pay the $75 fee by credit card or ACH. Online filings typically process within hours.

### What happens if I don't file my Illinois LLC annual report?

If you miss your due date, a $100 late penalty is added immediately. After 60 days you lose good-standing status. After **180 days** the Illinois Secretary of State will administratively dissolve your LLC, costing your business its limited liability protection. You can reinstate within 5 years for $200 plus all back fees and penalties, but after 5 years the LLC cannot be revived.

### Does Illinois require an annual report for foreign LLCs?

Yes. Foreign LLCs (those formed in another state but registered to do business in Illinois) must file the same annual report on the same anniversary-month schedule for the same $75 fee. The information required is identical, including a registered agent with an Illinois street address.

### How do I find my Illinois LLC's annual report due date?

Search the [Illinois Secretary of State Business Entity Database](https://apps.ilsos.gov/businessentitysearch/) for free using your LLC's name. The lookup shows your formation date, current status, and exact next annual report due date. Bookmark this page for annual reference.

### Can a registered agent service file my Illinois annual report for me?

Absolutely. Services like [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) ($199/year), [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) ($299/year), [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) ($125/year), and [Bizee](/go/bizee/) ($99/year) all offer Illinois annual report filing as part of compliance plans. ### What's the difference between the Illinois annual report and a BOI report?

The Illinois annual report is a **state-level** filing with the Illinois Secretary of State, due yearly for $75. The BOI report is a **federal** filing with FinCEN under the Corporate Transparency Act, separate from any state filing. You typically need to file both, on different schedules and at different costs. See our [complete BOI report guide](/blog/what-is-a-boi-report-and-who-needs-to-file/) for federal requirements.

### Is the Illinois LLC annual report the same as my Illinois tax return?

No. They're entirely separate filings. The annual report is an administrative SOS filing that confirms your LLC's basic information. Your tax obligations are filed separately with the Illinois Department of Revenue (Form IL-1065 for partnerships and most multi-member LLCs, or pass-through to your personal return for single-member LLCs). Filing one does not satisfy the other.
