## Why Form an LLC in Michigan?

Before getting into the mechanics, it's worth understanding what makes Michigan a compelling state for LLC formation in 2026.

**One of the lowest filing fees in the nation.** At $50, Michigan's Articles of Organization fee undercuts Texas ($300), New York ($200 plus a mandatory publication requirement that can run $1,000–$2,000), and even most of its Midwest neighbors. For a sole proprietor weighing the jump to a formal entity, the cost barrier is about as low as it gets.

**Strong liability protection.** Like all LLCs, a Michigan LLC creates a legal separation between your personal finances and your business debts and lawsuits. If a client sues your business or a creditor comes after your company, your home, car, and personal savings are generally shielded. **A modern, flexible LLC statute.** Michigan LLCs are governed by the Michigan Limited Liability Company Act (Act 23 of 1993, as amended). It gives owners broad freedom to structure management and profit-sharing however they like through an operating agreement, while providing sensible defaults for those who don't.

**A diversified, recovering economy.** Detroit's continued comeback, the EV and battery investment wave rippling out from the auto sector, and growing startup activity in Ann Arbor and Grand Rapids have made 2026 a genuinely interesting year to launch in Michigan. The one trade-off to know up front: unlike states such as Ohio, Michigan does require an annual filing (the annual statement) to stay active. It's inexpensive at $25, but it's a deadline you can't ignore. I'll cover it in detail below.

## How to Start an LLC in Michigan: Step-by-Step

Here is the complete process for how to start an LLC in Michigan, laid out in the exact order you should follow.

### Step 1: Choose Your LLC Name

Your Michigan LLC name must be distinguishable from every other business name on file with LARA's Corporations Division. It must also include one of these designators:

- "Limited Liability Company"
- "LLC" or "L.L.C."
- "Limited Company," "LC," or "L.C."

**Name availability check:** Run your proposed name through the [LARA Business Entity Search](https://cofs.lara.state.mi.us/SearchApi/Search/Search) before filing. A conflict with an existing or even recently dissolved entity will get your Articles rejected.

A few additional rules:
- Words implying government affiliation are prohibited without approval
- Restricted words like "Bank," "Insurance," "Attorney," or "University" require additional licensing or agency sign-off
- If you plan to operate under a name different from your LLC's legal name, you'll register an Assumed Name (DBA) with LARA for $25, valid for five years

**Optional name reservation:** Michigan lets you reserve a name for six months for a $25 fee. Worth doing if you've landed on a name but aren't ready to file yet.

### Step 2: Appoint a Resident Agent

Michigan uses the term "resident agent" rather than registered agent — same role, different label. Your resident agent must:

- Have a physical Michigan street address (a P.O. box alone is not acceptable as the registered office)
- Be available during normal business hours to accept legal documents and state correspondence
- Be either an individual Michigan resident or a business entity authorized to do business in Michigan

You can serve as your own resident agent. But in my experience, this is a decision a lot of business owners come to regret — your name and address become part of the public record, you'll likely see a spike in junk mail and solicitations, and there's a real risk of missing a legal notice if you're traveling or simply away from your desk when a process server shows up.

Professional registered agent services run $49–$299 per year and handle all of this cleanly. For a full explanation of the role and how to choose a provider, see our [registered agent guide](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/).

### Step 3: File Your Articles of Organization

This is the filing that legally brings your Michigan LLC into existence. You'll submit Form CSCL/CD-700 — the Articles of Organization — to LARA's Corporations, Securities & Commercial Licensing Bureau.

**What the Articles require:**
1. LLC name
2. The purpose of the company (a general-purpose statement is fine for most businesses)
3. Resident agent name and Michigan registered office street address
4. Duration of the LLC (perpetual, unless you specify otherwise)
5. Organizer signature

**Filing options and fees:**

| Filing Method | Processing Time | Fee |
|---------------|----------------|-----|
| Online (recommended) | A few business days | $50 |
| Mail | 2–4 weeks | $50 |
| Expedited (24-hour) | 1 business day | +$50 |
| Expedited (same-day) | Same business day | +$100 |
| Expedited (2-hour / 1-hour) | 2 hours / 1 hour | +$500 / +$1,000 |

File online through the [LARA Corporations Online Filing System (COFS)](https://cofs.lara.state.mi.us/). Online filing is faster, generates an immediate confirmation, and substantially reduces the risk of a rejected filing due to incomplete paperwork.

### Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Michigan does not legally require an operating agreement, but I strongly urge every LLC owner — especially multi-member LLCs — to put one in writing before they begin operating.

Without a written operating agreement, the default provisions of the Michigan Limited Liability Company Act govern how your business is managed, how profits are split, how disputes get resolved, and what happens when a member wants out. Those defaults frequently don't match what the founders actually intended.

A solid operating agreement should cover:
- Ownership percentages and initial capital contributions
- Voting rights and decision-making procedures
- How profits and losses are distributed
- Buy-sell provisions (what happens if a member exits, dies, or wants to sell)
- Dissolution procedures

Our [LLC Operating Agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) covers all the key provisions in detail. ZenBusiness includes an operating agreement template in its higher tiers; [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) offers attorney-drafted versions at additional cost, which is worth considering if your ownership structure is complex.

### Step 5: Obtain an EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your LLC's federal tax ID — the equivalent of a Social Security number for your business. You'll need it to:

- Open a business bank account
- Hire employees
- File Michigan and federal tax returns
- Apply for most business licenses and a Michigan sales tax license

**Cost:** Free, applied for directly through the [IRS website](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). The online application takes roughly 10 minutes and delivers your EIN immediately.

Single-member LLCs with no employees can technically use the owner's Social Security number, but getting a separate EIN is strongly recommended for clean financial separation and professional credibility — and beware that some formation services upsell EIN retrieval for $50–$99 when you can get it free in ten minutes yourself.

### Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

This step is critical, not optional. Commingling personal and business funds is one of the fastest ways to lose your LLC's liability shield through a legal doctrine called "piercing the corporate veil." If a court decides you're not treating the LLC as a genuinely separate entity, a plaintiff can reach your personal assets.

To open a Michigan business bank account, you'll typically need:
- Your EIN
- A copy of your stamped Articles of Organization
- Your operating agreement
- Government-issued ID

Most Michigan banks and credit unions offer business checking, and several neobanks now offer free LLC accounts with no monthly fees.

### Step 7: Register for State Taxes and Get Required Licenses

Michigan has no general statewide business license, but your industry and location may require specific registrations:

- **Michigan sales tax license** — required if you sell taxable goods; register through [Michigan Treasury Online](https://mto.treasury.michigan.gov/). Michigan's sales tax rate is 6%.
- **Employer registration** — if you hire employees, register for Michigan withholding tax and unemployment insurance
- **Professional licenses** — required for contractors, cosmetologists, healthcare providers, real estate agents, and many other regulated professions through LARA's licensing bureaus
- **Local permits and zoning** — Detroit, Grand Rapids, Ann Arbor, Lansing, and other municipalities have their own requirements, particularly for home-based and retail businesses

## Michigan LLC Ongoing Compliance Requirements

One of the most common questions from new Michigan LLC owners: "What do I have to do every year to stay in compliance?"

**File your annual statement.** This is the big one Michigan owners can't skip. Every Michigan LLC must file an annual statement with LARA by **February 15** each year. The fee is $25. (Your first statement is due the year after the year of formation — LLCs formed after September 30 get a slight break and aren't required to file the following February 15.) Miss it repeatedly and your LLC can fall out of good standing and eventually be dissolved by the state. A compliance service or calendar reminder is well worth setting up.

**Keep your resident agent current.** If your resident agent or registered office address changes, file a change with LARA promptly. Failing to maintain a valid resident agent can put your LLC out of good standing.

**Stay current on state taxes.** If you hold a sales tax license or have employees, file your Michigan sales, use, and withholding returns on schedule through Michigan Treasury Online.

**Renew professional licenses.** If your business requires state-issued professional licensing, keep those current through the relevant LARA bureau.

**File your federal BOI Report.** As of 2026, most LLCs must file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN. This is a federal requirement with real penalties for non-compliance, and the rules have been actively revised over the past year. See our [BOI Report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for current deadlines and who is — and isn't — required to file.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Michigan in 2026?**

The minimum cost is $50 — the state filing fee for the Articles of Organization. If you use a formation service, add $0–$199 for the service plan. Resident agent service runs $49–$299 per year if you use a professional provider. Michigan also charges a $25 annual statement fee each year to keep your LLC active.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in Michigan?**

Online filing through LARA's COFS portal typically processes within a few business days. Mail filings take 2–4 weeks. Expedited service is available for an extra $50 (24-hour), $100 (same-day), $500 (2-hour), or $1,000 (1-hour).

**Does Michigan require an annual report for LLCs?**

Yes — Michigan calls it the annual statement. Every Michigan LLC must file it with LARA by February 15 each year and pay a $25 fee. This differs from states like Ohio, which require no annual report at all. Set a calendar reminder or use a compliance service so you don't miss it.

**Do I need a resident agent for my Michigan LLC?**

Yes. Every Michigan LLC must maintain a resident agent with a physical Michigan street address who is available during business hours. You can serve as your own resident agent, but most owners use a professional service to protect their privacy and ensure they never miss a legal notice.

**Do I need an operating agreement for a Michigan LLC?**

Michigan law does not require one, but it is strongly recommended — especially for LLCs with two or more members. Without a written agreement, the default provisions of the Michigan Limited Liability Company Act control how your LLC is managed and how disputes are resolved, which may not reflect your intentions.

**What taxes does a Michigan LLC pay?**

By default, single-member Michigan LLCs are disregarded entities for federal tax purposes (income reported on the owner's personal return), and multi-member LLCs are taxed as partnerships. Michigan income then flows to your personal return, taxed at the state's flat 4.25% rate in 2026. Michigan's 6% Corporate Income Tax generally applies only to entities taxed as C-corporations. If you sell taxable goods, you'll also collect and remit 6% sales tax. Consult a CPA for guidance specific to your business.

**Can I form a Michigan LLC if I live in another state?**

Yes. Non-residents can form and own a Michigan LLC without restriction. You'll need to appoint a resident agent with a physical Michigan address, but there is no residency requirement for the LLC's members or managers.

**Can my Michigan LLC be taxed as an S-Corp?**

Yes. An S-Corp is a federal tax election, not a separate entity type. Once your net profits grow large enough (often around $40,000–$60,000), electing S-Corp taxation can reduce self-employment taxes. Our [LLC vs. S-Corp guide](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) covers when and how to make the election.
