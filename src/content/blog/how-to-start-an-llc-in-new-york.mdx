We'll walk through exactly what that means and what it costs, along with every other step, fee, and deadline you need to hit in 2026.

The Articles of Organization filing fee is $200, but that's rarely the number that determines your total cost. Between the mandatory newspaper publication, a registered agent, and optional expedited processing, a New York LLC can run anywhere from about $250 to well over $2,000 depending on your county. Let's get into the details so that doesn't happen to you.

## Why Form an LLC in New York?

An LLC (limited liability company) separates your personal assets — your house, your savings, your car — from your business's debts and liabilities. If you're operating as a sole proprietor in New York right now, a single lawsuit or unpaid business debt could expose your personal finances. Forming an LLC creates a legal wall between the two. If you want the fundamentals first, our guide on [what is an LLC](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) covers the entity type in plain language.

New York also gives LLCs flexible tax treatment (pass-through by default, with the option to elect S-corp or C-corp taxation), simpler recordkeeping than a corporation, and credibility with banks, vendors, and clients who prefer working with a registered entity over an individual.

## Step 1: Choose a Name for Your New York LLC

Your name must include "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." and must be distinguishable from every other entity on file with the New York Department of State. You can check name availability through the [Department of State's Division of Corporations](https://dos.ny.gov/corporations) database before you file anything.

A few New York-specific naming rules to know:

- Certain words ("Bank," "Attorney," "University," "Insurance") require additional approvals or licensed professionals on file.
- New York does not require you to include a business purpose statement in your name, but professional service LLCs (law, medicine, accounting) generally must form as a PLLC instead of a standard LLC.
- You can reserve a name for 60 days for a $20 fee if you're not ready to file yet.

## Step 2: Designate a Registered Agent

Every New York LLC needs a way to receive legal and state correspondence. New York is unusual here: the Secretary of State is automatically your agent for service of process, whether you like it or not. However, that only means lawsuits get forwarded to whatever address you list on file — it doesn't handle the mail forwarding, compliance reminders, or privacy protection a dedicated [registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) service provides.

Most owners still designate a commercial registered agent for two reasons: privacy (your home address doesn't become a public record) and reliability (a missed forwarded notice from the state can mean a missed lawsuit deadline). If privacy is your main concern, [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) has built its reputation specifically around not listing client addresses in marketing databases and using its own address on public filings — it's the one service in this space I'd point to first when a client's biggest worry is keeping their home address off Google.

Registered agent service typically runs $99–$300/year if purchased standalone, though most formation packages from [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) and [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) bundle the first year in with formation.

## Step 3: File Your Articles of Organization

This is the actual formation document, filed with the New York Department of State, Division of Corporations. It requires:

- LLC name
- County where the office is located
- Registered agent designation (the Secretary of State by default, or your own agent)
- Effective date (immediate or a delayed date up to 60 days out)

**Filing fee:** $200, payable to the Department of State.

**Processing time:** Standard processing runs about 7 business days by mail, but New York offers expedited tiers: 24-hour ($25), same-day ($75), and 2-hour ($150) processing. If you're trying to open a business bank account or sign a lease quickly, the expedited fee is usually worth it.

You can file directly on the DOS website, by mail, or through a formation service. This is the step where using [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) or [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) saves the most time — both file electronically on your behalf and track the confirmation, rather than you refreshing a state portal.

## Step 4: Satisfy New York's Publication Requirement

This is the step that makes starting an LLC in New York meaningfully different from almost every other state, and it's the one people skip at their peril.

Within 120 days of formation, New York law (LLC Law §206) requires you to publish a notice of your LLC's formation in **two newspapers** — one daily, one weekly — designated by the county clerk in the county where your LLC's office is located. The notice must run once a week for **six consecutive weeks**. After publication, each newspaper provides an affidavit of publication, which you then file with the Department of State along with a Certificate of Publication and a $50 filing fee.

Here's the part that catches people off guard: **cost varies enormously by county.** In sparsely populated counties, six weeks of publication in two papers might cost $300–$600 total. In New York County (Manhattan), where newspaper ad rates are astronomical, the same requirement can cost **$1,500–$2,000 or more**. This is a well-known reason many small business owners intentionally list a registered agent address in a lower-cost county, such as Albany, rather than their actual Manhattan office — a completely legal workaround, but one you should discuss with a formation service or attorney before assuming it's right for your situation.

Miss the 120-day window, and your LLC's authority to conduct business in New York can be suspended until you comply — you don't lose the entity, but you lose your ability to maintain lawsuits in New York courts until you fix it. In my experience, this is the single most common compliance failure I see among first-time New York filers, and it's almost always a case of nobody telling them the requirement existed in the first place. A good formation service will flag this automatically; [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) and [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) both include publication guidance in their New York-specific filing flow, which is one reason I recommend using a service rather than filing solo in this particular state.

## Step 5: Create an Operating Agreement

New York is one of the few states that legally requires LLCs to adopt a written operating agreement, either before, at the time of, or within 90 days after filing the Articles of Organization. There's no requirement to file it with the state — it stays internal — but not having one is a compliance violation, and courts have used the absence of one against LLC owners in liability disputes when the "corporate veil" was challenged.

At minimum, your [operating agreement](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) should cover:

- Ownership percentages and capital contributions
- Profit and loss allocation
- Management structure (member-managed vs. manager-managed)
- Voting rights and what requires unanimous consent
- Buyout and dissolution procedures

Single-member LLCs need this just as much as multi-member ones — it's often the document that convinces a court (or the IRS) that your LLC is a genuine separate entity rather than an extension of your personal finances. Most formation packages from ZenBusiness, LegalZoom, and Tailor Brands include an operating agreement template in their mid-tier plans; attorneys will charge $200–$500 to draft a custom one if your ownership structure is complex.

## Step 6: Get an EIN and Set Up Your Taxes

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS is required to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal taxes — even single-member LLCs need one if they want to keep business banking separate from personal (which you should, for liability purposes).

You can [apply directly through the IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) for free in about 15 minutes if you have a Social Security Number or ITIN. If you're a non-U.S. resident without an SSN, the process takes longer and typically requires fax or mail submission — several formation services, including [Inc Authority](/go/inc-authority/) and [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/), offer EIN acquisition as an add-on for non-resident filers specifically because of this friction.

On the tax side, New York multi-member LLCs (and single-member LLCs that elect corporate tax treatment) are subject to an annual **LLC filing fee** based on New York-source gross income — ranging from $25 for income under $100,000 up to $4,500 for LLCs with $25 million or more in New York-source income. This is separate from the $200 formation fee and separate from the biennial statement fee below, and it's one of the most overlooked recurring costs new owners budget for incorrectly. Talk to a CPA about your projected first-year revenue before you assume your only ongoing state cost is the biennial filing — the U.S. [Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure) has a solid overview of how entity choice affects tax obligations if you want a federal-level primer before that conversation.

## Step 7: Stay Compliant After Formation

Two ongoing obligations matter most after your New York LLC is formed:

**Biennial Statement.** Every New York LLC must file a Biennial Statement with the Department of State every two years, in the calendar month your LLC was originally formed. The fee is a modest $9, but missing it repeatedly can lead to administrative dissolution. Our full breakdown of the [New York LLC biennial statement](/blog/new-york-llc-biennial-statement/) covers exact deadlines and how to check your filing month.

**Beneficial ownership disclosure.** Following the federal Corporate Transparency Act rollercoaster of the past few years, New York enacted its own state-level LLC Transparency Act, with beneficial ownership disclosure obligations phasing in starting January 1, 2026. This is a genuinely new wrinkle for 2026 filers specifically — if you formed your LLC in prior years, you'll want to confirm your reporting status now rather than waiting for a penalty notice. Our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) explains the federal side of beneficial ownership reporting, which still applies to many entities depending on where things land through 2026.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in New York in 2026?**
The state filing fee is $200. With the mandatory publication requirement, total first-year costs typically range from $550 to $2,750 depending on your county — Manhattan and other high-ad-rate counties push toward the top of that range.

**Do I need to publish a notice to form an LLC in New York?**
Yes. New York LLC Law §206 requires publication in two newspapers (one daily, one weekly) for six consecutive weeks within 120 days of formation, followed by filing a Certificate of Publication with a $50 fee. This is unique to New York among most states and is frequently missed by first-time filers.

**How long does it take to start an LLC in New York?**
Standard Articles of Organization processing takes about 7 business days by mail. Expedited options include 24-hour ($25), same-day ($75), and 2-hour ($150) processing. The publication requirement itself takes a minimum of six weeks to complete regardless of how fast your initial filing is approved.

**Do I need a registered agent in New York?**
The Secretary of State is automatically designated as your agent for service of process, so it's not strictly mandatory to hire one. Most owners still use a commercial registered agent for privacy and reliable mail handling — see our [registered agent guide](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) for the tradeoffs.

**What happens if I miss the New York publication deadline?**
Your LLC's authority to conduct business and maintain lawsuits in New York can be suspended until you complete publication and file the Certificate of Publication. The entity itself isn't dissolved, but you lose certain legal standing until you cure it.

**Can I be my own registered agent in New York?**
Yes, as long as you have a physical New York address (not a P.O. box) and are available during business hours. Many owners still choose a commercial agent to avoid having a home address listed on public state records.

**Do I need an operating agreement for a single-member LLC in New York?**
Yes. New York requires all LLCs, including single-member ones, to adopt a written operating agreement within 90 days of formation, even though it isn't filed with the state.

**How much is the New York LLC biennial statement fee?**
$9, due every two years in the anniversary month of your LLC's formation. Late or missed filings can eventually lead to administrative dissolution — see our [biennial statement guide](/blog/new-york-llc-biennial-statement/) for exact timing.
