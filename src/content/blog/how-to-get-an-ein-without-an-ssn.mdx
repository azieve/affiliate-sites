If you're launching a U.S. company but don't have a Social Security number, the first thing you'll hit is the IRS online EIN form, which asks for one. That isn't a dead end. Knowing **how to get an EIN without an SSN** comes down to choosing the right application channel for your situation, and in 2026 there are three well-established routes: phone, fax, and mail, plus an online route if you hold an ITIN.

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is the nine-digit federal tax ID your LLC needs to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal tax returns. The IRS issues it for free. Many founders who are new to the U.S. system assume they must pay a service to get one. You don't, although a formation company such as [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) can handle the state filing and paperwork around it while you sort out your tax ID. If you haven't formed your company yet, our guide to [what an LLC is](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) is a good starting point.

This guide walks through each method, the exact wording to use on the form, realistic timelines, and the mistakes that cause delays. Nothing here replaces advice from a tax professional, but it will get you to the right door.

## Can You Really Get an EIN Without an SSN?

Yes. The IRS does not require an SSN to issue an EIN. What it requires is a "responsible party," the individual who ultimately owns or controls the entity. The IRS online application asks that person for an SSN or an ITIN. If they have neither, the online tool is closed to them, but the IRS keeps separate channels open for applicants in exactly that position.

Here is the practical breakdown:

| Your situation | Best route | Typical timing |
|---|---|---|
| Non-U.S. resident, no SSN or ITIN | Phone (international line) | Same call |
| No SSN or ITIN, phone isn't practical | Fax Form SS-4 | About 4 business days |
| No SSN or ITIN, no rush | Mail Form SS-4 | Roughly 4 to 6 weeks |
| U.S. resident or foreign owner holding an ITIN | Online application | Immediate |
| Any of the above, with professional help | Third-party designee | Varies |

The timelines above reflect IRS guidance on its EIN pages at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number). Processing times can shift, so check the current figures before you commit to a launch date.

## Method 1: Apply by Phone (Fastest for Non-U.S. Applicants)

For an **EIN for a foreign LLC owner**, the phone is usually the quickest option. The IRS operates an international number, 267-941-1099, which is not toll-free. It is staffed Monday through Friday, generally from 6:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m. Eastern time. Confirm the current number and hours on IRS.gov before dialing.

Before you call, complete a Form SS-4 so you can read your answers to the agent. The agent will go through the form with you and, if everything checks out, give you the EIN verbally. The IRS then mails a confirmation letter (Notice CP 575) to the address you provided.

Tips from people who have done this:

- **Call early in the Eastern-time day.** Hold times are shorter and you avoid time-zone confusion.
- **Have your LLC's formation date and state ready.** The agent will ask when the LLC was formed and where. Form the LLC first. Applying for an EIN before the entity legally exists is one of the most common reasons applications get rejected.
- **Use a mailing address you can actually receive mail at.** International addresses are accepted, but a delayed confirmation letter can stall bank onboarding.

In my experience, the phone route is where first-time applicants feel most nervous and least need to be. The agents handle foreign applicants every day, and a completed SS-4 in front of you makes the call take 10 to 20 minutes.

## Method 2: Fax or Mail Form SS-4

If a phone call is impractical because of time zones, language, or connection quality, you can send Form SS-4 by fax or by mail.

### Filling Out Form SS-4 Without an SSN

[Form SS-4](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-ss-4) is a one-page form, but a few lines trip up non-SSN applicants:

- **Line 7b (SSN, ITIN, or EIN of the responsible party):** If you have none, write "Foreign" if you're a non-U.S. person without a U.S. taxpayer ID. Some applicants use "N/A." The IRS instructions for Form SS-4 explain the accepted entries, so check the current version.
- **Line 1 (Legal name):** This must match your Articles of Organization exactly, including "LLC" or "L.L.C."
- **Line 8a to 8c (Is this an LLC? Number of members):** Check "Yes" and enter the number of members. For a single-member LLC, enter "1."
- **Line 9a (Type of entity):** A single-member LLC is typically a disregarded entity and is commonly reported as "Other" with a note, or as sole proprietor, depending on the IRS instructions. A multi-member LLC taxed as a partnership checks "Partnership." Review the form instructions, because the wrong box changes how the IRS sets up your account.
- **Line 18 (Has the applicant previously applied for an EIN?):** Answer honestly. Duplicate applications create real headaches.
- **Signature:** The responsible party signs. If a third-party designee files for you, you also complete the designee section.

### Where to Send It

Per IRS guidance, international applicants can fax the completed form to the IRS's international EIN fax line, with responses typically returned in about four business days if you include a return fax number. By mail, you send the form to the IRS EIN International Operation in Cincinnati, Ohio, and the wait is considerably longer, often four to six weeks. Verify the current fax number and mailing address on the IRS EIN page before sending anything, as I've seen outdated numbers copied across blogs.

## Method 3: Use an ITIN for the Online Application

If you hold an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number, you can use it in place of an SSN. The IRS online EIN application accepts an ITIN for the responsible party, and the EIN is issued immediately at the end of the session. For many owners, an **EIN with ITIN** is the smoothest path by far.

The catch is that getting the ITIN is its own process. It requires Form W-7 and identity documents, usually a passport, and the IRS generally expects an ITIN applicant to have a federal tax purpose, such as filing a return. The exact documentation rules have specific exceptions, so read our walkthrough on [how to get an ITIN as a foreign LLC owner](/blog/how-to-get-an-itin-as-a-foreign-llc-owner/) before assuming it saves you time. If you only need an EIN to open an account, the phone or fax route is typically faster than obtaining an ITIN first.

## What Happens After You Have the EIN

Getting the number is the start of your tax obligations, not the end. Two areas catch foreign-owned LLCs off guard.

**Form 5472.** A foreign-owned single-member LLC is generally treated as a "reporting corporation" for certain IRS reporting purposes and must file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when it has reportable transactions with its owner. The penalty for failing to file is $25,000 per form, per the IRS. Our [Form 5472 filing guide](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/) explains the details.

**Beneficial ownership reporting.** FinCEN's rules changed significantly in 2025, when an interim final rule removed the requirement for U.S.-formed companies and their owners, while foreign companies registered to do business in the U.S. remained covered. Because rules like this continue to evolve, check [FinCEN's BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) and our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for the current status in 2026.

Also plan for state-level costs and a registered agent. Every LLC needs a U.S. address for service of process, which matters even more if you live abroad. See [what a registered agent is](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) for how that works.

## Common Mistakes That Delay Your EIN

After watching enough founders go through this, the same problems keep showing up:

- **Applying before the LLC exists.** The IRS wants a legally formed entity.
- **Name mismatches.** "Acme Consulting LLC" on the SS-4 and "Acme Consulting, LLC" on the state filing can raise flags.
- **Wrong entity type.** Choosing the wrong box affects how your LLC is set up for tax. If you're unsure how your LLC will be taxed, review [LLC vs S-Corp](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) and [how LLCs are taxed](/blog/llc-taxes-how-are-llcs-taxed/).
- **Duplicate applications.** Applying twice after a slow response can create two EINs for one entity.
- **Skipping the follow-up.** If you don't receive the CP 575 letter, request a copy. Banks often ask for it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I get an EIN without an SSN or ITIN?

Yes. Non-U.S. applicants without either number can apply by phone, fax, or mail using Form SS-4. The online application is not available without an SSN or ITIN for the responsible party.

### How long does it take to get an EIN without an SSN?

By phone, you can receive the EIN during the call. By fax, expect roughly four business days. By mail, expect around four to six weeks. These are IRS-published estimates and may change, so verify before relying on them.

### Do I need an ITIN to get an EIN?

No. An ITIN makes the online application available to you, but it isn't required. Many foreign LLC owners get an EIN through the phone or fax process without ever obtaining an ITIN.

### How much does it cost to get an EIN without an SSN?

Nothing when you apply directly with the IRS. You only pay if you choose to use a third-party service or professional to assist you.

### What do I write on Form SS-4 if I don't have an SSN?

On line 7b, the instructions direct non-U.S. applicants without a U.S. taxpayer ID to enter "Foreign." Always check the latest Form SS-4 instructions on IRS.gov for the accepted wording.

### Can a non-U.S. resident own a U.S. LLC and get an EIN?

Generally yes. Non-residents can own U.S. LLCs, and our guide on [whether a foreigner can own an LLC in the US](/blog/can-a-foreigner-own-an-llc-in-the-us/) covers the rules. You'll still need to follow the tax filing requirements that apply to foreign-owned entities.

### Will I need an EIN for my bank account?

Most U.S. banks require one to open an LLC business account, along with your Articles of Organization and operating agreement. Some banks have extra requirements for non-resident owners, so ask before you apply.

### What if I lose my EIN confirmation letter?

You can request a replacement of your EIN confirmation by contacting the IRS. Keep a secure copy of the original to avoid delays opening accounts.
