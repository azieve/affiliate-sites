If you're weighing BetterLegal vs ZenBusiness, you're really deciding how much hand-holding you want after the LLC is filed. Both companies will file your Articles of Organization with the state. The difference shows up in the first-year price, the ongoing compliance support, and how much you'll be pushed toward add-ons.

Short version: [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) is the better fit for most first-time owners. It has a $0 service-fee entry tier (plus state fees), clearly tiered plans, and built-in compliance reminders. BetterLegal can appeal if you want a more legal-flavored, modern approach and are comfortable verifying details yourself. This guide breaks down both, and adds [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) as a third reference point, since many people searching this comparison also look at "BetterLegal vs ZenBusiness vs LegalZoom."

## How ZenBusiness Works and What It Costs

ZenBusiness is built around three tiers. The Starter plan carries no service fee beyond your state filing fee, which is attractive if you only need the filing done. The Pro plan (roughly $199 per year) adds an operating agreement, a registered agent for the first year, EIN assistance, and a website and domain offer. The Premium plan (roughly $349 per year) layers on extras such as rush processing and additional business tools.

The gotcha with any of these services is renewal. Registered agent service in the ZenBusiness line-up typically renews at around $199 per year after the included period, so budget for it. If you're cost-sensitive, our [cheapest way to form an LLC](/blog/cheapest-way-to-form-an-llc/) guide covers how to avoid paying for services you don't need.

What I like about ZenBusiness is the guardrails. Its compliance reminders flag annual report deadlines, which matters more than people think. In my experience, the most expensive mistake new owners make isn't picking the wrong formation service. It's missing the first annual report and watching the state move the LLC to delinquent status. I've seen too many business owners treat formation as the finish line when it's really the starting gun.

## How BetterLegal Compares

BetterLegal positions itself as a more modern, legal-oriented formation provider. It appeals to people who want a cleaner onboarding flow and a company that leans into the legal side of things rather than the "business bundle" side.

Where I'd be careful:

- **Plan clarity.** Before you pay, confirm exactly what's included: registered agent term, operating agreement, EIN filing, and any compliance monitoring.
- **Renewal pricing.** Ask what the registered agent and any subscription cost in year two.
- **Support depth.** Check whether you get phone or live support, or only email and chat.
- **Track record.** ZenBusiness and LegalZoom have far larger user bases, which means more public reviews to sanity-check against.

None of this makes BetterLegal a bad choice. It means the burden of verification is on you. We've also written a direct [LegalZoom vs BetterLegal](/blog/legalzoom-vs-betterlegal/) comparison if you want that angle.

## BetterLegal vs ZenBusiness: Feature-by-Feature

**Pricing transparency.** ZenBusiness publishes a simple three-tier structure and you can see what's included before checkout. That's an advantage for anyone comparing quickly. With BetterLegal, compare the checkout total, not the headline price.

**Formation speed.** Standard processing time is mostly set by the state. A state with fast online filing (many process within days) will be fast with either service. Rush options exist at higher tiers, but they only speed up what the service controls, not the state's queue. If speed is your top concern, our guide on the [best LLC service for small business](/blog/best-llc-service-for-small-business/) covers how each provider handles it.

**Compliance after formation.** This is where ZenBusiness tends to pull ahead for beginners. Annual report reminders, registered agent forwarding, and a document vault reduce the odds you miss a filing. Missing one can lead to late fees, loss of good standing, or administrative dissolution depending on the state.

**Operating agreement.** Even single-member LLCs should have one. It documents ownership and how decisions are made, and it helps support the separation between you and the business if a creditor ever challenges it. Read our [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) to see what it should contain.

**Upsells.** Every formation service upsells. ZenBusiness will offer a website, domain, and bookkeeping-type extras. Skip what you don't need. You can obtain an EIN directly from the IRS at no cost through [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online), so don't pay a premium just for that.

## Hidden Costs No One Mentions

1. **State filing fees.** These range widely, from roughly $35 in Kentucky to $500 in Massachusetts. The service fee is on top of that.
2. **State-specific publication.** New York requires a publication process that can cost well over a thousand dollars in some counties. No formation service absorbs that.
3. **Annual and franchise taxes.** California charges an $800 minimum franchise tax; see our [California LLC $800 franchise tax guide](/blog/california-llc-800-franchise-tax/).
4. **Registered agent renewal.** Often the largest year-two surprise.
5. **Rush fees.** Optional, but easy to click through without noticing.

## Timely Compliance Notes for 2026

One piece of news that changed the formation conversation: under FinCEN's 2025 interim final rule, U.S.-formed companies were exempted from beneficial ownership information (BOI) reporting under the Corporate Transparency Act, narrowing it largely to foreign-formed entities registered in the U.S. Rules like this can change again, so check [FinCEN's BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) before assuming anything, and see our [BOI report changes and updates for 2026](/blog/boi-report-changes-and-updates-2026/).

Also remember that LLC taxation is a choice you make with a tax professional. A single-member LLC is taxed as a disregarded entity by default, and electing S-corp status may or may not reduce self-employment tax depending on your profit level. Our [LLC vs S-Corp](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) guide explains the mechanics, but your CPA should make the call.

## FAQ: BetterLegal vs ZenBusiness

### Is BetterLegal better than ZenBusiness?
For most first-time owners, ZenBusiness is the safer pick thanks to its clear tiers, compliance reminders, and larger track record. BetterLegal may suit you if its current plan matches your needs at a lower total cost.

### How much does it cost to form an LLC with ZenBusiness?
The Starter plan has no service fee beyond the state filing fee. The Pro plan is around $199 per year and Premium around $349 per year. Always confirm at checkout.

### Do I need a formation service to start an LLC?
No. You can file directly with your state, usually for just the state fee. A service saves time and adds registered agent and compliance tools. Our [what is an LLC](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) guide covers the basics.

### What is the difference between ZenBusiness and LegalZoom?
ZenBusiness tends to be cheaper for formation with a simpler plan structure. LegalZoom offers a broader legal product catalog and brand recognition at a generally higher price.

### Do BetterLegal and ZenBusiness include a registered agent?
Paid tiers of ZenBusiness include one for the first year, then it renews at a fee. Check BetterLegal's current plan details for its terms. Learn more in [what is a registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/).

### How long does it take to form an LLC?
Timing depends mostly on the state. Many approve online filings within a few business days, while others take weeks. Rush options only help so far.

### What happens if I miss my LLC annual report?
You may owe late fees and risk losing good standing or administrative dissolution. See [what happens if you don't renew your LLC](/blog/what-happens-if-you-don-t-renew-your-llc/).
