On paper, both file your Articles of Organization and get your business entity off the ground. In practice, the experience — and the cost — differs more than most comparison articles admit.

Before diving in: for most founders forming a standard LLC without complex ownership structures or regulated industry requirements, neither LLC Attorney nor LegalZoom offers the best value in 2026. [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) starts at $0 + state fees, includes a worry-free compliance guarantee, and consistently outperforms LegalZoom on price, support quality, and ease of use. Here's the full LLC Attorney vs LegalZoom breakdown, including pricing, hidden costs, feature gaps, and a honest verdict on who should use what.

## What Is LLC Attorney?

[LLC Attorney](/go/llc-attorney/) is an attorney-backed LLC formation service that distinguishes itself from template-driven platforms by having licensed attorneys supervise each filing. Rather than running your paperwork through an automated system, LLC Attorney routes documents through legal review before submission — attorneys check the Articles of Organization, flag potential naming conflicts, and validate the operating agreement before anything goes to the state.

**LLC Attorney pricing in 2026:**
- Starter plan: $49 + state fees
- Operating agreement: Included and attorney-reviewed
- EIN filing: Available as add-on (~$70)
- Registered agent service: $119/year
- Attorney consultation: Included in scope of formation

The pitch is genuine: for roughly $49 in service fees, you get a set of human eyes with legal credentials reviewing your formation documents. That's a real differentiator from the fully automated pipelines that dominate the rest of the industry.

## What Is LegalZoom?

[LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) is the original online legal services company — founded in 2001 and now publicly traded on the NASDAQ (ticker: LZ). Over 4 million businesses have been formed through its platform. Name recognition counts for something, but it isn't a proxy for quality or value, and LegalZoom's price structure has gotten significantly more expensive over the past few years.

**LegalZoom LLC formation pricing in 2026:**
- Basic: $0 + state fees (Articles of Organization filing only — no operating agreement, no EIN)
- Pro: $249 + state fees (adds operating agreement, EIN filing, one year registered agent)
- Premium: $299 + state fees (adds attorney guidance, business document templates)
- Registered agent renewal: $249/year starting in year two

LegalZoom's business model has evolved toward subscriptions. Its LZ Legal Advantage plan ($249/year) layers on ongoing attorney access, document storage, and compliance alerts — features that are genuinely useful but represent a meaningful ongoing cost commitment.

The catch with LegalZoom: its upsell architecture is aggressive. Users regularly report that a "$0 Basic" formation quietly becomes a $400–$600 experience as add-ons accumulate at checkout. The registered agent renewal fee at $249/year is the highest in the industry — nearly double what [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) charges at $125/year, and $130/year more than LLC Attorney's $119/year rate.

## Speed and Turnaround Time

Turnaround matters more than most comparison articles acknowledge. Every day your LLC isn't formed is a day you can't open a business bank account, can't sign contracts in the entity's name, can't invoice clients as the LLC.

**Standard processing times in 2026:**
- LLC Attorney: 3–5 business days
- LegalZoom: 7–10 business days (standard); $100+ for rush service
- ZenBusiness: Typically 2–3 weeks standard; expedited available for $50–$100

The important caveat: state processing times are the real variable and are outside any service's control. Wyoming and Delaware process same-day or next-day. California runs 3–6 weeks on standard filings. Texas is 7–10 business days.

Where LLC Attorney's attorney review pays off on speed: a rejected filing adds 2–4 weeks to your timeline if the state sends back a deficiency notice for a name conflict, address problem, or missing information. In my experience working with small business owners, I've seen $0 "free" formations turn into $600+ problems in time lost and amendment fees when automated systems filed documents with errors that a quick human review would have caught.

Attorney review doesn't just add quality — done right, it reduces the risk of rejection that can make a "fast" service actually the slowest option.

## When to Choose LLC Attorney

LLC Attorney earns its premium in a specific set of circumstances:

**1. Regulated industry formation.** Healthcare practices, financial advisory firms, real estate brokers, insurance agencies — industries where formation document errors can create downstream licensing complications. A mistake in how the entity's purpose is described, or a name that conflicts with a professional licensing requirement, can derail licensure applications. Attorney review catches those issues.

**2. Multi-member LLCs with unequal economics.** If you're forming with two or three partners who have different ownership percentages, profit-sharing arrangements, or different capital contribution structures, an attorney-reviewed operating agreement is worth the price. The boilerplate operating agreements on automated platforms aren't designed for complex structures.

**3. States with unusual formation requirements.** California's Statement of Information, New York's publication requirement (which can cost $1,200–$2,000+ in publication fees), and a few other states have requirements that trip up automated systems. An attorney familiar with the state's requirements reduces error risk.

**4. Prior legal complexity.** If you've had a previous LLC dissolved for compliance failures, have existing contracts or IP being assigned to the new entity, or are forming alongside a trust structure, starting with attorney supervision is cheaper than cleaning up a filing error six months later.

**5. You want peace of mind, not just a document.** For some founders, knowing a licensed attorney looked at their formation documents is worth the modest premium. That's a legitimate reason.

## When to Choose LegalZoom

LegalZoom's case for itself is strongest in a narrower set of use cases than its marketing suggests:

**1. You need a one-stop legal platform.** Trademark registration, demand letters, partnership agreements, estate planning — LegalZoom's platform covers more legal territory than any other online service. If you want to centralize multiple legal tasks, the platform breadth justifies the cost.

**2. You need trademark registration alongside formation.** LegalZoom's trademark practice is legitimately strong and competitively priced relative to attorney fees. If trademark protection is an immediate priority, bundling formation and trademark with one provider has operational convenience.

**3. You're already subscribed to LZ Legal.** If you're paying $249/year for LegalZoom's attorney access subscription, bundling your formation into the same platform avoids onboarding friction.

**4. Your lender or partner expects LegalZoom documents.** Some traditional lenders and SBA programs have familiarity with LegalZoom's document formats. In rare cases, brand recognition smooths a banking or financing process.

That's a real but limited case. For most founders comparing LLC Attorney vs LegalZoom, LegalZoom's formation-specific value proposition doesn't justify its registered agent fees and upsell model.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is LLC Attorney a real law firm?**

LLC Attorney is a legal technology company staffed by licensed attorneys who review formation documents — it is not a traditional law firm, and the attorney relationship is scoped to the formation filing. For ongoing legal representation, complex business transactions, litigation, or advice beyond entity formation, you would need to retain separate counsel. Think of LLC Attorney as quality-controlled filing with legal credentials, not a substitute for retained legal counsel.

**How much does LegalZoom charge for LLC formation in 2026?**

LegalZoom's Basic plan is $0 + state fees but includes only the Articles of Organization filing — no operating agreement, no EIN, and minimal support. The Pro plan is $249 + state fees and adds an operating agreement, EIN filing, and one year of registered agent service. The Premium plan is $299 + state fees and includes attorney guidance. Registered agent renewal starts at $249/year in year two — the highest renewal rate in the industry.

**Does LLC Attorney include a registered agent?**

Yes, LLC Attorney offers registered agent service at $119/year. That's significantly more affordable than LegalZoom's $249/year renewal rate and competitive with most mid-tier services. However, if registered agent pricing is your primary concern, Northwest Registered Agent at $125/year and ZenBusiness at $199/year (with a full free year on the Pro plan) are also worth comparing.

**Do I really need an attorney to form an LLC?**

For most standard LLC formations — single-member LLC in a business-friendly state with straightforward ownership — no. Formation services file Articles of Organization correctly every day through automated systems. Attorney oversight adds real value in regulated industries, complex multi-member structures, states with unusual requirements, or situations involving existing legal complexity. The [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed) recommends consulting a tax professional about entity selection, but the filing itself rarely requires attorney involvement for simple structures.

**What is the fastest LLC formation service in 2026?**

Processing speed depends primarily on the state, not the formation service. Wyoming and Delaware can process same-day or next-day with expedited state fees. California runs 3–6 weeks on standard filings regardless of which service you use. Among the services themselves, LLC Attorney (3–5 days standard) and ZenBusiness (expedited options available) tend to outpace LegalZoom (7–10 days standard). Attorney review on LLC Attorney's end can also reduce the risk of rejection-related delays.

**Can I switch registered agents after my LLC is formed?**

Yes. Changing your registered agent after formation is a standard process in every state, typically requiring a state filing fee of $10–$50 and a signed consent form from the new agent. See our step-by-step [guide to changing your registered agent](/blog/how-to-change-registered-agent/) for the process by state.

**What does "attorney-backed" actually mean at LLC Attorney?**

It means a licensed attorney reviews your formation documents before filing. It does not mean you have an ongoing attorney-client relationship, that an attorney represents your business in other matters, or that you have access to legal advice beyond the formation scope. The review is scoped to catching errors in your Articles of Organization and operating agreement before they go to the state — genuinely useful quality control, not comprehensive legal representation.

**Is LegalZoom worth it in 2026?**

LegalZoom is worth it if you need a comprehensive legal services platform — trademarks, contracts, estate planning, and business formation all in one place — and you're prepared for its pricing structure. For LLC formation specifically, its registered agent renewal costs and upsell model make it less competitive than ZenBusiness on value. LegalZoom's strongest use case is users who need multiple legal products and want to centralize them with a single provider they trust.
