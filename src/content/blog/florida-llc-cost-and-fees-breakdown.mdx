## The $125 Florida LLC Filing Fee — What It Covers

The Florida LLC filing fee is $125, paid to the Florida Division of Corporations when you submit your Articles of Organization through [Sunbiz.org](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/). This is the one cost that is entirely unavoidable — every LLC formed in Florida pays it, no exceptions.

What that $125 gets you:
- Your LLC is legally registered with the State of Florida
- A filing receipt confirming your entity's active status
- A public record entry in the Sunbiz database

**How to file:** Online submissions at Sunbiz.org are typically processed within 1–3 business days. Paper filings sent by mail to the Division of Corporations in Tallahassee take 2–4 weeks and are best avoided unless you have a specific reason to file by mail.

**Name reservation (optional — $25):** If you want to lock in your LLC name before you're ready to file the full Articles of Organization, you can reserve it for 120 days for $25. This is useful if you're still finalizing your business structure or waiting on co-founders to sign off.

One detail that trips up first-time filers: your LLC name must include "LLC," "L.L.C.," "Limited Liability Company," or an accepted abbreviation. Florida's Division of Corporations will reject filings with non-compliant names — and the $125 fee is non-refundable. Double-check your name before submitting.

## Florida LLC Annual Report Fee — The Cost Most People Miss

Here's where the ongoing Florida LLC cost and fees breakdown becomes more significant: the annual report requirement.

Every Florida LLC must file an annual report with the Division of Corporations each year. The fee is **$138.75**, and it's due by **May 1st** — every year, without exception. Skipping it doesn't just result in a fine; it will eventually lead to your LLC being administratively dissolved by the state, stripping it of its legal status and liability protection.

**Miss the May 1st deadline by even one day, and a $400 late fee kicks in immediately**, bringing your total for that year to $538.75. In my experience, the annual report late fee is one of the most common and most avoidable financial mistakes small business owners make. I've seen founders who spent months carefully planning their business lose hundreds of dollars simply because they didn't set a calendar reminder. Put May 1st in your calendar now — recurring, every year.

**How to file:** Online at Sunbiz.org. If your information hasn't changed, the process takes about five minutes. You'll need your LLC's document number, which is printed on your original filing receipt and visible on the Sunbiz public search.

**Florida vs. other states:** At $138.75, Florida's annual report fee is higher than Wyoming ($60 minimum) but far lower than California's $800 annual franchise tax minimum. For a broader comparison of [how much an LLC costs](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/) across different states, the picture generally favors Florida for cost-conscious founders.

## Florida Registered Agent Costs

Florida law requires every LLC to designate a registered agent — a person or entity with a physical Florida street address who can receive legal documents, government notices, and official state correspondence on behalf of your business during regular business hours. You have three realistic options.

**Option 1: Serve as your own registered agent ($0/year)**
Any Florida resident can serve as the LLC's registered agent using their home or office address. The cost is zero, but your address becomes part of the public record on Sunbiz, you must be physically present and available at that address during business hours, and any address change requires immediate state filing. For founders who work from home or travel frequently, this creates real practical problems.

**Option 2: Hire a professional registered agent service ($49–$299/year)**
Professional services provide a Florida street address, accept all legal and government mail on your behalf, and typically offer a digital portal where you can access documents. This protects your privacy and ensures you never miss a critical legal notice regardless of where you are.

## Optional Costs That Add Up Quickly

Beyond the mandatory fees, several optional (but practically necessary) costs come up for most Florida LLCs. None of these are legally required by the state of Florida, but skipping some of them creates real risk.

**EIN — Free**
Your Employer Identification Number is your LLC's tax ID, issued by the IRS. Apply directly at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) — the online process is free, instant, and takes about 10 minutes. Do not pay a third-party service to obtain your EIN; there is no reason to.

**Operating Agreement — $0 to $150+**
Florida does not legally require an operating agreement for LLCs, but every attorney will tell you to have one. Without a written agreement, Florida's default LLC statutes govern your business — which may not reflect your actual intentions around ownership percentages, profit distribution, or what happens when a member wants to exit. Basic templates are included free with most formation services. For custom or attorney-reviewed documents, budget $100–$500 depending on complexity. See our [LLC Operating Agreement Guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) for specifics.

**Local Business Licenses — $25 to $100+**
Florida has no statewide general business license, but most cities and counties require a local business tax receipt (formerly called an occupational license). Miami-Dade, Broward, and Orange County all have their own requirements. Standard businesses typically pay $25–$100 annually. Regulated industries — contractors, food service, healthcare, childcare — face additional licensing requirements and higher fees at both the state and county level.

**Fictitious Name / DBA Registration — $50**
If your LLC will operate under a trade name different from its registered legal name, you'll need to register a fictitious name with the Division of Corporations for $50. Good news: as of recent Florida regulatory changes, the old requirement to publish fictitious name registrations in a local newspaper has been eliminated, removing a cost that previously ran $50–$150 depending on the county.

**BOI Report — Free (federal requirement)**
As of 2026, most new LLCs must file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN under the Corporate Transparency Act. Filing directly with FinCEN is free. Formation services may charge $25–$100 to handle this on your behalf. This isn't a Florida-specific cost, but it's a compliance requirement every Florida LLC owner needs to know about.

**Certified Copies and Certificates — $5 to $30**
Banks sometimes request a certified copy of your Articles of Organization ($30) or a Certificate of Status ($5) when opening a business bank account or applying for financing. These are optional until someone asks for them.

## Florida vs. Other States — Is Forming Here Worth It?

If your business operates in Florida, forming your LLC here is almost always the right call. Forming in another state — Delaware, Wyoming, Nevada — and then registering as a foreign LLC in Florida means paying formation fees in both states, maintaining two registered agents, and managing compliance in two jurisdictions. That complexity and cost rarely makes sense for a business whose actual operations are Florida-based.

Florida's biggest financial advantage for LLC owners is its **absence of state personal income tax**. Most single-member LLCs are taxed as disregarded entities (pass-through to the owner's personal return), which means LLC profits flow straight to your federal return with no state income tax bite. That's a meaningful long-term savings compared to California (up to 13.3% state income tax) or New York (up to 10.9%).

Florida does impose a **5.5% corporate income tax** on LLCs that elect to be taxed as C-corporations, and certain minimum tax thresholds can apply. For most small and mid-size LLCs taxed as pass-throughs, this doesn't apply — but if you're planning a significant scale-up or considering a corporate tax election, consult a CPA before formation. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Florida?**
The minimum cost to start a Florida LLC is $125 — the mandatory Articles of Organization filing fee paid to the Florida Division of Corporations. If you serve as your own registered agent and file directly on Sunbiz.org, that's your only upfront mandatory expense. In practice, most business owners spend $300–$600 in year one when you include the annual report fee, a registered agent, and a basic local business license.

**What is the Florida LLC annual report fee in 2026?**
The Florida LLC annual report fee is $138.75 and must be filed by May 1st each year. If you miss the deadline, a $400 late fee is added immediately on May 2nd, bringing the total to $538.75 for that filing cycle. The annual report is submitted online at Sunbiz.org and takes about five minutes if your LLC information hasn't changed.

**Do I need a registered agent for a Florida LLC?**
Yes — Florida law requires every LLC to designate a registered agent with a physical Florida address. You can serve as your own registered agent if you're a Florida resident, but your address becomes public record and you must be available at that address during business hours. Professional registered agent services typically cost $49–$299/year and keep your personal address off the public record.

**Can I form a Florida LLC for free?**
Not entirely. The state's $125 filing fee is non-waivable, but you can eliminate service fees by filing directly at Sunbiz.org and using a formation service like [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) or [Inc Authority](/go/inc-authority/) that charges $0 for their baseline formation package. The absolute minimum is $125 if you handle everything yourself and exclude year-one annual report costs.

**What happens if I don't file the Florida LLC annual report?**
Missing the May 1st deadline adds a $400 late fee immediately. If you fail to file at all, Florida will administratively dissolve your LLC — ending its legal status and liability protection. Reinstatement is possible but requires filing all overdue reports plus a reinstatement fee. This is one of the most preventable compliance failures in LLC ownership.

**Is there a state income tax on Florida LLC profits in 2026?**
Florida imposes no personal state income tax, which is a significant advantage for LLC owners taxed as pass-through entities. LLC profits flow to your personal federal return, and you'll owe no state income tax to Florida. If your LLC is taxed as a C-corporation, Florida's 5.5% corporate income tax applies. S-corp elections also pass through at the personal level with no Florida state income tax.

**Do I need an operating agreement for a Florida LLC?**
Florida does not legally require an operating agreement, but every experienced business attorney will tell you to have one — especially for multi-member LLCs. Without it, Florida's default LLC statutes govern profit distribution, voting rights, and member exits, which may not align with what you actually want. Most formation services include a basic template at no cost; custom attorney-drafted agreements run $100–$500.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in Florida?**
Online filings at Sunbiz.org are typically processed in 1–3 business days. Paper mail filings take 2–4 weeks. Formation services file on your behalf with similar processing times, though some offer paid expedited processing if you need approval faster.
