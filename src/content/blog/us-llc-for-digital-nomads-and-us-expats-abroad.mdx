If you are an American working from Lisbon, Chiang Mai or Mexico City, you can still form and run a US company. There is no rule that an LLC owner has to live in the United States, and a US LLC for digital nomads and US expats abroad solves several practical problems at once: clients get a familiar entity to pay, you get a US business bank account, and your personal assets sit behind a liability shield.

The formation itself is the easy part. You can file online from anywhere, and a service such as [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) will prepare the articles for $0 plus the state filing fee on its entry plan. What deserves your attention is everything around the filing: which state to use when you no longer live in one, how the IRS taxes a citizen who earns money overseas, and what the country you are sitting in thinks of your American company.

This guide covers those questions for 2026. It is written for US citizens and green card holders. If you are not a US person, the rules are very different, so start with our guide on whether [a foreigner can own an LLC in the US](/blog/can-a-foreigner-own-an-llc-in-the-us/) instead.

## Why Americans Abroad Still Use a US LLC

**Liability protection.** A freelancer without an entity is a sole proprietor, and a contract dispute reaches personal savings. An LLC, properly maintained, separates business liabilities from personal ones. If the concept is new to you, our primer on [what an LLC is](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) explains the basics.

**Banking and payments.** US business bank accounts, Stripe, and most US client procurement systems expect a US entity with an EIN. Getting paid as "Jane Smith, somewhere in Portugal" creates friction that "Smith Consulting LLC" does not.

**Simplicity on the US side.** A single-member LLC is a disregarded entity by default. It does not file its own federal income tax return; the income goes on Schedule C of your personal Form 1040, which you must file anyway as a US citizen.

**No foreign-entity reporting.** Americans who incorporate a company in their host country often trigger Form 5471 and the GILTI regime, which are expensive to comply with. A US LLC avoids those forms. It also avoids [Form 5472](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/), which applies to foreign-owned LLCs, not to ones owned by US persons.

An LLC does not, by itself, reduce your tax bill. It is a legal wrapper. The tax results below come from where you live and work, not from the entity.

### The domicile trap

Here is the point I see people miss most often. Leaving the country does not automatically end your tax residency in your last state. California, Virginia, New Mexico and South Carolina are known for treating former residents as still domiciled there unless they have clearly established a new home elsewhere. Keeping a driver's license, voter registration, a home, or a business registered in that state all count as evidence that you never left.

If you moved abroad from California and form your LLC there, you owe the $800 annual franchise tax and you hand the Franchise Tax Board an argument that you are still a resident. In my experience, expats from high-tax states are better served by forming in a state with no income tax and cutting other ties cleanly. If you are still considered a resident of your old state, forming elsewhere will not fix that on its own, and a state may also say the LLC is "doing business" there if you manage it during long visits home. This is worth an hour with a tax professional who handles expat returns.

## How the IRS Taxes Your LLC Income Abroad

The United States taxes citizens on worldwide income regardless of where they live. Three rules shape the result for an LLC for US expats.

### The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion

The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE), claimed on Form 2555, lets a qualifying taxpayer exclude foreign earned income from federal income tax, up to $130,000 for tax year 2025 and $132,900 for 2026. Net profit from services you personally perform through a single-member LLC counts as earned income. The foreign earned income exclusion LLC combination works because the LLC is disregarded: the IRS looks through it to you.

To qualify you need a tax home in a foreign country and must pass one of two tests:

- **Physical presence test:** 330 full days in foreign countries during any 12 consecutive months. Days in the US, and days in transit over international waters, do not count.
- **Bona fide residence test:** residence in a foreign country for an uninterrupted period that includes a full tax year.

Nomads usually rely on the physical presence test because they rarely establish residence anywhere. Count your days carefully. Income for work performed while you are physically in the United States is US-source and cannot be excluded, even if the client is foreign. The [IRS explains the requirements here](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion).

If you live in a country with income tax rates higher than US rates, the Foreign Tax Credit (Form 1116) is often a better choice than the FEIE. You generally cannot use both on the same income.

### Self-employment tax still applies

This is the surprise. The FEIE reduces income tax only. Expat self-employment tax of 15.3% (12.4% Social Security up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus 2.9% Medicare on all net earnings) is still due on your LLC profit. An expat who nets $100,000 and excludes all of it from income tax still owes roughly $14,100 in self-employment tax.

The main exception is a totalization agreement. The US has these with about 30 countries, including the UK, Germany, France, Spain, Portugal, Japan and Australia. If you are covered by and paying into the social security system of one of those countries, you can obtain a certificate of coverage and be exempt from the US tax. Popular nomad bases such as Thailand, Mexico, Colombia and Indonesia have no agreement in force. The [Social Security Administration lists the agreements](https://www.ssa.gov/international/agreements_overview.html). Our guide to [self-employment tax for LLC owners](/blog/self-employment-tax-for-llc-owners/) covers the calculation.

### Does an S-corp election help?

Sometimes. An LLC taxed as an S-corp pays you a reasonable salary, which is subject to payroll tax, and the remaining profit passes through without the 15.3% charge. Your salary for work done abroad can still qualify for the FEIE. The distributions, however, are not earned income and cannot be excluded, so they are taxable unless offset by foreign tax credits. Add payroll software and a separate Form 1120-S, and the election tends to pay off only at higher profit levels. See [LLC vs S-corp](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) for the general math, and have an expat-focused CPA run your numbers before electing.

### Other filings to know

- **Deadlines.** Americans abroad get an automatic extension to June 15 to file, but interest runs on unpaid tax from April 15.
- **FBAR and Form 8938.** If your foreign financial accounts, including any the LLC holds, total more than $10,000 at any point in the year, you file FinCEN Form 114. Higher balances trigger Form 8938.
- **BOI reports.** Under FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule, LLCs formed in the United States are exempt from beneficial ownership reporting. Rules have shifted several times, so check our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) and [FinCEN's page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) for the current position.
- **QBI deduction.** The 20% qualified business income deduction generally requires income connected with a US trade or business. Income from services performed entirely abroad typically does not qualify.

## The Other Half: Your Host Country

Forming a US LLC for digital nomads and US expats abroad settles your position with the IRS. It does not settle your position with the country where you sit with your laptop.

If you stay long enough to become tax resident, commonly 183 days though the tests vary, that country will usually want to tax your worldwide income, LLC profit included. Some also treat a company managed from their territory as a local tax resident or as having a permanent establishment there.

There is a further wrinkle. Several countries do not recognize the US "disregarded entity" concept and treat the LLC as a corporation. The UK is the best-known example. That mismatch can make it harder to claim credit for foreign tax against your US bill, and it has produced double taxation for unprepared expats.

Digital nomad visas, which have spread widely since 2020 and are now offered by dozens of countries, sometimes come with special tax treatment and sometimes do not. Read the tax terms of the visa, not just the immigration terms. Tax treaties help less than people expect because of the "saving clause," which lets the US tax its citizens as if most of the treaty did not exist.

## Setting Up From Overseas: Step by Step

1. **Pick the state** using the criteria above.
2. **Appoint a registered agent.** Every state requires an agent with a physical address in the state. Living abroad, you cannot serve as your own, so this is a required cost of roughly $100 to $250 a year. Our explainer on [what a registered agent does](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) has the details.
3. **File the articles of organization** online. Wyoming and New Mexico typically process within a few business days.
4. **Get an EIN.** With a Social Security number you can apply free on IRS.gov in about ten minutes. Follow our [free EIN walkthrough](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-for-your-llc-free-irs-method/).
5. **Sign an operating agreement.** Banks ask for it, and it supports the separation between you and the business. See the [operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/).
6. **Arrange a US mailing address.** A virtual mailbox that scans mail costs about $10 to $30 a month. Do not use a relative's home in a high-tax state if you are trying to break residency there.
7. **Open a bank account.** Online-first business banking platforms such as Mercury and Relay open accounts remotely for US-formed LLCs. Traditional banks often want you in a branch. A multi-currency account such as Wise Business is useful if clients pay in euros or pounds. More in our [LLC bank account guide](/blog/llc-bank-account-how-to-open-one/).

Expect year-one costs of about $250 to $500 in a low-fee state: filing fee, registered agent, and mailbox.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I form a US LLC while living abroad?

Yes. No state requires LLC owners to live in the US. You need a registered agent with a physical address in the formation state, and you can file everything else online.

### What is the best state for a digital nomad LLC?

Wyoming and New Mexico are the usual picks because of low fees, no state income tax on the LLC, and minimal reporting. Delaware suits companies planning to raise investment. If you are still a legal resident of a state, that state's rules may apply regardless of where you form.

### Do I pay US taxes on LLC income if I live overseas?

Yes, you still file. US citizens report worldwide income. The FEIE can exclude up to $132,900 of foreign earned income for 2026 from income tax, or you can claim foreign tax credits, but self-employment tax generally still applies.

### Does the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion eliminate self-employment tax?

No. The exclusion applies to income tax only. You owe the 15.3% self-employment tax on net LLC profit unless a totalization agreement and a certificate of coverage exempt you.

### Do I need a US address for my LLC?

You need a registered agent address in the state, and in practice a US mailing address for the IRS and your bank. A registered agent plus a virtual mailbox covers both.

### Will my host country tax my US LLC?

It may. If you become tax resident there, most countries tax your worldwide income, and some treat the LLC as a local corporate taxpayer. Check local rules before staying long term.

### Do I have to file a BOI report or Form 5472?

Under current FinCEN rules, US-formed LLCs are exempt from BOI reporting. Form 5472 applies to foreign-owned LLCs, not to an LLC owned by a US citizen.

## The Bottom Line

A US LLC for digital nomads and US expats abroad is inexpensive and can be set up in a week without setting foot in the country. Form in a low-fee state, pay for a registered agent, get an EIN and a bank account, and keep business money separate.

Then spend your real effort on tax planning: count your days for the FEIE, budget for self-employment tax, cut ties with a sticky former state, and find out how your host country sees the entity. The LLC is the simple piece. In 2026, with more countries courting remote workers and more tax authorities sharing data, getting the surrounding pieces right matters more than which service files your paperwork.
