## What Is the Delaware LLC Annual Franchise Tax?

Despite the intimidating name, the Delaware LLC annual franchise tax is not a tax on your income or revenue. It's a flat annual fee that Delaware charges every LLC registered in the state — a kind of "license to exist" that keeps your entity in good standing with the Delaware Division of Corporations.

The key distinction to understand: **Delaware LLCs pay a simple flat fee**, while Delaware corporations face a much more complex calculation involving authorized shares or assumed par value capital methods (which can run into tens of thousands of dollars for large companies). If you've ever read alarming headlines about Delaware franchise tax bills, those almost always refer to corporations, not LLCs.

For LLCs — including most small businesses, holding companies, and startups — the annual franchise tax is refreshingly straightforward.

## When Is the Delaware LLC Franchise Tax Due?

The Delaware LLC annual franchise tax is due **June 1 of each year**. This is a hard deadline — there are no extensions available for LLCs.

The tax covers the current calendar year. So the payment you make on or before June 1, 2026 covers your 2026 franchise tax obligation. Newly formed LLCs are required to pay their first franchise tax by June 1 of the year following formation — so if you formed your LLC in October 2025, your first payment is due June 1, 2026.

This annual cadence is easy to miss, especially for single-member LLCs where there's no accounting staff watching the calendar. I've seen too many business owners — particularly those who formed a Delaware LLC for an online business or side project — get blindsided by a penalty notice because the June 1 deadline slipped through the cracks.

## What Happens If You Miss the Deadline?

Late payment of the Delaware LLC annual franchise tax carries real consequences:

- **$200 late penalty** — assessed immediately after June 1 if the tax has not been paid
- **1.5% monthly interest** on the unpaid balance (the $300 tax plus the $200 penalty)
- **Loss of good standing** — your LLC's status becomes "delinquent" in Delaware's records
- **Risk of voiding your LLC** — sustained non-payment can eventually result in Delaware canceling your LLC

Loss of good standing is more than a clerical issue. If your LLC is delinquent, you can't legally obtain a Certificate of Good Standing, which many banks, investors, vendors, and government contracts require. You also can't qualify your LLC to do business in another state (called "foreign qualification") while delinquent. According to the [Delaware Division of Corporations](https://corp.delaware.gov/), restoring a cancelled LLC requires filing a Certificate of Revival plus paying all back taxes, penalties, and interest — which adds up quickly.

If your LLC has been delinquent for multiple years, the reinstatement process can easily cost more than $1,000 in accumulated fees, even for a simple LLC.

## How to Pay the Delaware LLC Annual Franchise Tax

Payment is made online through the Delaware Division of Corporations website. Here's the step-by-step process:

1. **Go to the Delaware Division of Corporations** at [corp.delaware.gov](https://corp.delaware.gov/)
2. Click on **"File Annual Report / Pay Taxes"** (note: LLCs don't file an annual report — the system will route you to the tax payment page)
3. Enter your **LLC's file number** (found on your original Certificate of Formation or any official Delaware correspondence)
4. Confirm your entity information
5. Pay via **credit card or ACH**

The entire process takes about five minutes once you have your file number handy. Delaware does not send paper invoices or reminders — it's entirely your responsibility to remember and initiate payment. This is one of the strongest arguments for maintaining a professional registered agent service: a good registered agent will send you compliance reminders well before June 1.

**Important note:** LLCs do NOT file an annual report in Delaware. This is a common point of confusion because Delaware corporations must file both a franchise tax return and an annual report. For LLCs, it's purely a tax payment — no paperwork required.

## Delaware LLC vs. Delaware Corporation: Understanding the Tax Difference

This is where many business owners get confused — and sometimes panicked. If you search "Delaware franchise tax," you'll find horror stories about $50,000 or $80,000 franchise tax bills. Those figures apply exclusively to **Delaware corporations** using the Authorized Shares Method of calculation.

Under the Authorized Shares Method, a corporation with 10 million authorized shares can owe over $90,000 in franchise tax annually. The alternative Assumed Par Value Capital Method typically produces a lower bill for corporations with large share authorizations — but it's still complex and potentially significant.

**Delaware LLCs are entirely exempt from this complexity.** The $300 flat fee applies regardless of your capitalization, member count, or revenue. This is one reason many venture-backed startups that need equity structures choose to incorporate as corporations in Delaware (accepting the complexity) while investors and holding entities often prefer the LLC structure.

## Is a Delaware LLC Worth It? Weighing the Annual Cost

For business owners who don't live or operate in Delaware, the honest calculus involves comparing the benefits of Delaware's legal environment against the ongoing costs:

**Annual Delaware LLC cost (non-resident):**
- $300 franchise tax
- ~$125–$300 registered agent fee
- Foreign qualification fee in your home state (varies: $50–$750)
- Potentially, annual report fees in your home state for the foreign-qualified entity

**Annual home-state LLC cost (e.g., Texas):**
- $0 franchise tax for most small LLCs (Texas has a no-tax-due threshold of $2.47 million in revenue)
- $0 registered agent fee if you serve as your own
- No foreign qualification required

For many small businesses with no particular need for Delaware's legal flexibility or investor requirements, forming in your home state is simpler and cheaper. Delaware makes the most sense when you're planning to raise outside capital (investors often require Delaware C-corps or Delaware LLCs), need access to the Court of Chancery for complex disputes, or have multi-state operations where Delaware's legal clarity is an advantage.

## Maintaining Delaware LLC Good Standing Beyond the Franchise Tax

The franchise tax is the primary ongoing obligation, but Delaware LLC compliance in 2026 also includes:

- **Maintaining a registered agent** with a Delaware address at all times — failure to do so is a separate compliance violation. See our guide on [what is a registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) for a full breakdown of what this role involves.
- **Keeping internal records current** — while Delaware doesn't require you to file an annual report, you should maintain an up-to-date operating agreement, member records, and resolutions for any major decisions.
- **BOI reporting** — as of 2024, most LLCs are required to file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN under the Corporate Transparency Act. This is a federal requirement separate from Delaware's state obligations.
- **Federal and state income taxes** — the franchise tax does not cover any income tax obligations, which are handled separately based on your LLC's tax classification.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much is the Delaware LLC annual franchise tax in 2026?**

The Delaware LLC annual franchise tax is a flat $300 per year in 2026. This rate has been consistent for several years and applies equally to all LLCs regardless of size, revenue, or number of members.

**When is the Delaware LLC franchise tax due?**

The Delaware LLC annual franchise tax is due on **June 1 of each year**. There are no extensions. Payments made after June 1 are subject to a $200 late penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest on the total unpaid amount.

**Do Delaware LLCs have to file an annual report?**

No. Unlike Delaware corporations, Delaware LLCs are **not required to file an annual report**. The only ongoing state obligation is paying the $300 annual franchise tax by June 1.

**What happens if I don't pay the Delaware LLC franchise tax?**

Your LLC becomes delinquent in Delaware's records. You'll owe a $200 late penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest. Sustained non-payment can result in Delaware voiding or canceling your LLC — and reinstatement requires paying all back taxes, penalties, and interest, plus a revival filing fee.

**Is the Delaware LLC franchise tax based on revenue or income?**

No. The $300 fee is a **flat annual tax** unrelated to your LLC's revenue, profit, or number of employees. This distinguishes it sharply from Delaware's corporation franchise tax, which can run into the tens of thousands of dollars based on authorized shares.

**Do I need a registered agent to pay the Delaware LLC franchise tax?**

You need a registered agent to maintain a valid Delaware LLC — it's required by law. However, paying the franchise tax itself is done directly through the Delaware Division of Corporations website, not through your registered agent. That said, a good registered agent service will remind you about the June 1 deadline.

**Can I form a Delaware LLC and operate in another state?**

Yes, but you'll need to **foreign qualify** in any state where you're doing business. That typically involves an additional filing fee, annual report, and state-level fees in your operating state — on top of Delaware's $300 franchise tax. For many small businesses, this makes forming in your home state the more cost-effective option.

**How do I know if my Delaware LLC is in good standing?**

You can check your LLC's standing status for free through the [Delaware Division of Corporations entity search](https://icis.corp.delaware.gov/Ecorp/EntitySearch/NameSearch.aspx). Good standing requires an active registered agent on file and no outstanding franchise tax obligations.
