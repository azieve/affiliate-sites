## Why Form an LLC in Delaware in 2026?

Delaware's appeal for LLCs rests on a few concrete advantages:

- **No state corporate income tax on out-of-state income.** If your Delaware LLC doesn't do business within Delaware, you generally owe no state income tax to Delaware — though you'll still owe tax in your home state if you operate there.
- **The Court of Chancery.** Delaware has a dedicated business court with judges (not juries) who specialize in corporate law, which produces faster, more predictable rulings on LLC disputes.
- **Strong operating agreement flexibility.** The Delaware LLC Act gives members broad latitude to customize governance, profit distribution, and management structure in the operating agreement — more so than many other states.
- **Privacy.** Delaware's Certificate of Formation does not require you to list LLC members or managers publicly, which is a meaningful draw for owners who want to keep ownership off public record.
- **Investor familiarity.** Venture capital firms and institutional investors are simply more comfortable with Delaware entities, which matters if you're planning to raise a funding round in 2026 or beyond.

That said, Delaware isn't automatically the right call for every business. If you're a single-member LLC running a local service business in, say, Ohio, forming in Delaware means you'll also need to register as a foreign LLC in Ohio — paying two sets of fees and maintaining two registered agents. For most small, single-state businesses, forming in your home state is simpler. Delaware tends to make the most sense for holding companies, businesses planning to raise outside capital, real estate investors using multiple LLCs, or owners who specifically want the privacy and legal-system benefits. For a broader comparison of where forming makes sense, see our guide on the [best state to form an LLC](/blog/best-state-to-form-an-llc/).

## How to Start an LLC in Delaware: Step-by-Step

Here's the exact sequence for how to start an LLC in Delaware, from name selection to your first compliance filing.

### Step 1: Choose and Reserve Your LLC Name

Your name must include "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." and must be distinguishable from other entities already on file with the Delaware Division of Corporations. You can check name availability through the state's entity search tool. If you're not ready to file immediately, Delaware lets you reserve a name for 120 days for a $75 fee — useful if you're still finalizing your operating agreement or waiting on a trademark clearance.

### Step 2: Appoint a Delaware Registered Agent

Every Delaware LLC is legally required to maintain a registered agent with a physical Delaware street address (no P.O. boxes) who is available during business hours to accept service of process. If you don't live in Delaware — and most people forming here don't — you'll need to hire a commercial registered agent service. This is a required, non-negotiable line item, so it's worth reading our full breakdown of [what a registered agent does](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) before you pick one.

Pricing varies more than you'd think: [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) charges $125/year and is widely regarded as the strongest option specifically for owners who prioritize privacy, since Northwest doesn't sell customer data and uses its own address rather than yours on public filings. [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) and [Bizee](/go/bizee/) both include a free first year of registered agent service when bundled with formation, which is a meaningful discount if you're forming and hiring an agent at the same time.

### Step 3: File the Certificate of Formation

This is the core document that legally creates your LLC. It requires:
- The LLC's name
- The registered agent's name and Delaware address
- The signature of an authorized person (doesn't have to be a member)

The state filing fee is $110, and standard processing typically takes 2-3 weeks. If you need it faster, Delaware offers expedited tiers: 24-hour ($50 extra), same-day ($100 extra), 2-hour ($500 extra), and 1-hour ($1,000 extra) — the premium expedite fees exist mainly for time-sensitive closings or financing deadlines, not typical formations.

You can file this yourself directly through the Division of Corporations, or have a formation service handle it. This is where the "should I DIY or use a service" question actually gets interesting: filing yourself saves you a service fee, but you're on your own for registered agent selection, EIN application, and knowing which follow-up filings you need. A service like [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) files the Certificate of Formation for you starting at $0 plus the $110 state fee on its Starter plan, which includes a year of registered agent service — undercutting [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/), which charges $0 plus state fees for its Basic plan but sells registered agent service separately at $249/year after the first year.

### Step 4: Draft Your LLC Operating Agreement

Delaware doesn't require you to file an operating agreement with the state, but it's the single most important document you'll create, because it's what actually governs how your LLC operates — profit splits, voting rights, what happens if a member leaves, and how disputes get resolved. Delaware's flexible LLC Act means the operating agreement can override many default statutory rules, so a generic template can leave real gaps. Our [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) walks through what to include, and most formation services include a basic template with mid-tier and premium plans.

### Step 5: Get an EIN from the IRS

Your Employer Identification Number (EIN) is required to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal taxes. You can apply directly and for free through the [IRS's online EIN application](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online), which takes about 10 minutes if you have a U.S. Social Security Number or ITIN. If you're a non-U.S. resident without an SSN, the process requires a mailed or faxed Form SS-4 and can take several weeks — something formation services often handle for an added fee, which can be worth it if you're outside the U.S.

### Step 6: File Your BOI Report (If Required)

Under the Corporate Transparency Act, most LLCs must file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN disclosing who owns or controls the company. Requirements and enforcement have shifted since the rule was introduced, so check current applicability before assuming you're exempt — our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) covers current deadlines and who qualifies for exemptions.

### Step 7: Register as a Foreign LLC in Your Home State (If Applicable)

If you formed in Delaware but actually operate your business in another state — meaning you have an office, employees, or regularly conduct business there — you'll generally need to register as a "foreign LLC" in that state too. This means paying formation fees in both states, maintaining two registered agents, and filing two annual reports. This is the single most common surprise cost people run into after forming a Delaware LLC without realizing they needed dual registration, so factor it in before you decide Delaware is the right home base.

## Delaware Franchise Tax and Annual Requirements

This is where a lot of new LLC owners get caught off guard. Every Delaware LLC — regardless of income, revenue, or activity — owes a flat **$300 annual franchise tax**, due **every June 1st**. Unlike Delaware corporations, LLCs don't file an annual report; it's just the flat tax payment. Miss the deadline and Delaware tacks on a $200 penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest, and your LLC can eventually lose good standing. We cover the exact filing mechanics in our [Delaware franchise tax guide](/blog/delaware-llc-annual-franchise-tax/).

In my experience, this is the single most common compliance failure I see with Delaware LLCs — not because owners are careless, but because the $300 flat tax doesn't scale with a fee schedule the way other states' annual reports do, so it's easy to mentally file it under "already handled" and then forget it entirely by the following June. Most formation services, including ZenBusiness and LegalZoom, offer compliance reminder services or add-on filing support specifically to catch this.

Beyond the franchise tax, if your Delaware LLC elects to be taxed as an S-corp or C-corp for federal tax purposes, you'll have additional filing obligations with the IRS. This decision has real tax consequences depending on your income level and how you plan to take money out of the business — it's genuinely worth a conversation with a CPA or tax attorney before electing, rather than assuming one structure is automatically better. Nothing in this guide should be taken as tax advice for your specific situation; consult a licensed tax professional before making entity-taxation elections.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Forming a Delaware LLC

- **Forgetting the franchise tax deadline.** The $300 payment is due June 1 every year, with no reminder mailed by the state in many cases — set your own calendar reminder.
- **Not registering as a foreign LLC where you actually operate.** Forming in Delaware doesn't exempt you from registration requirements in your home state if that's where you do business.
- **Using a generic operating agreement template** that doesn't account for Delaware's flexible statutory defaults, which can leave gaps in member protection.
- **Skipping the EIN application** and trying to open a business bank account with just the Certificate of Formation — banks require the EIN.
- **Assuming Delaware always means lower taxes.** Delaware's advantage is largely for companies with no in-state operations; if you're doing business in-state, Delaware's gross receipts tax and other state-specific taxes may apply.
- **Not budgeting for the registered agent renewal** after any free first year included with a formation package expires.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Delaware?**
The state filing fee for the Certificate of Formation is $110. Add a registered agent ($100-$300/year if you're not a Delaware resident) and you're typically looking at $150-$400 for your first year, before any formation service fees.

**Do I need to live in Delaware to start an LLC there?**
No. Most Delaware LLCs are formed by non-residents. You do need a registered agent with a physical Delaware address, which any commercial registered agent service can provide.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in Delaware?**
Standard processing runs about 2-3 weeks as of 2026. Expedited options range from 24-hour ($50 extra) to 1-hour ($1,000 extra) for urgent needs like closing deadlines.

**Do I need an operating agreement for my Delaware LLC?**
Delaware doesn't require you to file one with the state, but it's strongly recommended. Without one, your LLC defaults to Delaware's statutory rules, which may not reflect how you actually want to run the business or split profits.

**What is the Delaware LLC franchise tax, and do I have to pay it every year?**
Yes — every Delaware LLC owes a flat $300 annual franchise tax due June 1, regardless of revenue or whether the business was active that year. There's no separate annual report requirement for LLCs beyond this tax payment.

**Can a foreigner (non-U.S. resident) form an LLC in Delaware?**
Yes. Non-U.S. residents can form and own Delaware LLCs. The EIN application process is slower without a Social Security Number, typically requiring a mailed or faxed Form SS-4 to the IRS rather than the instant online application.

**Do I need to register my Delaware LLC as a foreign entity in my home state?**
If you're actually conducting business — meaning you have a physical presence, employees, or regular transactions — in a state other than Delaware, most states require you to register as a foreign LLC there too, with its own fees and annual filings.

**What happens if I miss the Delaware franchise tax deadline?**
Delaware charges a $200 penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest on the unpaid balance. Extended non-payment can eventually lead to loss of good standing, which can complicate financing, contracts, or a future sale of the business.

## Final Thoughts

Delaware's reputation as the default state for serious businesses is well-earned, but it's not automatically the right choice for every LLC — the decision should hinge on whether you're planning to raise outside capital, want the specific privacy and legal-court benefits, or are building a holding structure across multiple entities. For businesses that are simply operating locally in one state, forming there directly is usually simpler and cheaper.

If Delaware is the right fit for your situation, the process itself is straightforward: choose a name, appoint a registered agent, file the $110 Certificate of Formation, draft an operating agreement, get your EIN, and mark June 1 on your calendar for the $300 franchise tax going forward. Whether you file it yourself or use a service like [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) or [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) to handle the paperwork, the core steps for how to start an LLC in Delaware in 2026 remain the same — it's the ongoing compliance calendar, not the initial filing, that trips up most owners a year or two in.
