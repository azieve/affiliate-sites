## Colorado LLC Filing Fee: The State's Mandatory Cost

The Colorado Secretary of State charges **$50** to file your Articles of Organization online. This is the single most important fee in the Colorado LLC cost breakdown — without it, your LLC doesn't legally exist.

You file directly through the [Colorado Secretary of State's website](https://www.sos.state.co.us/), and the process is almost entirely online. Processing is typically instantaneous for online filings; the state doesn't have a paper-based backlog to contend with. If you need expedited processing for some reason, Colorado doesn't offer a separate expedited fee tier — online filing is already fast.

A few costs in the same category:

|---|---|
| Articles of Organization (online) | $50 |
| Name Reservation (optional, 120 days) | $25 |
| Certified Copy of Filing | $1/page + $5 certification |
| Certificate of Good Standing | $2.25 |

One thing I've seen catch first-time filers off guard: **Colorado processes filings in real time**, but the confirmation email can sometimes lag. Don't re-submit thinking the first one didn't go through — you'll end up with duplicate filings and a headache to sort out with the Secretary of State's office.

## Colorado Annual Report: $10 Per Year (The Good News)

Colorado LLCs are required to file a **Periodic Report** every year. The fee is just **$10**, making it one of the cheapest ongoing compliance requirements in the country — significantly less than states like California ($800 minimum franchise tax) or Delaware ($300 annual franchise tax for LLCs).

Here's how the Colorado Periodic Report works:

- **Due date:** The report is due during the anniversary month of your LLC's formation. If you formed your LLC in April, you have the entire month of April each year to file.
- **Where to file:** Online via the Colorado Secretary of State's website.
- **Late fee:** If you miss your annual report deadline, you'll owe an additional **$50 late fee** — making the total $60 instead of $10. Miss it long enough and the state can administratively dissolve your LLC.
- **What it covers:** The report confirms your registered agent information, principal office address, and the identity of your registered agent. It's a short form, not a full financial disclosure.

In 2026, Colorado has kept its annual report fee unchanged — a welcome sign of stability for business owners planning their long-term budgets. Compare this to [Arizona's LLC costs](/blog/arizona-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/) ($0 annual report fee) or [Ohio's setup](/blog/ohio-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/) ($99 biennial fee), and Colorado sits in a comfortable middle ground.

## Registered Agent Costs in Colorado

## Operating Agreement: Not Required, But Essential

Colorado does not legally require LLCs to have an operating agreement — but every LLC should have one anyway. This document establishes ownership percentages, member voting rights, profit distribution rules, and what happens if a member wants to leave.

Without an operating agreement, you're governed entirely by Colorado's default LLC statutes, which may not reflect how you actually want to run your business.

**Cost options:**
- **DIY template (free–$50):** Sites like ZenBusiness include operating agreement templates with their paid plans. You can also find basic templates online for free, though quality varies.
- **Attorney-drafted ($300–$1,500+):** For multi-member LLCs or any situation with complex ownership arrangements, a business attorney should draft this document. Yes, it costs money upfront. It costs far more when a co-founder dispute lands in court because your operating agreement was a three-paragraph template from 2018.
- **Formation service templates ($0 with plan):** ZenBusiness Pro and Premium include a customizable operating agreement template, which is adequate for most single-member Colorado LLCs.

## EIN: Free from the IRS

Your Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your LLC's federal tax identification number — essentially a Social Security number for your business. You need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal taxes.

The **IRS issues EINs for free** at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). The process takes about 10 minutes online and your EIN is issued immediately.

Formation services that charge extra for EIN filing are charging you for a form you can complete in 10 minutes for $0. This is one area where DIY clearly wins.

## Hidden Costs Most Colorado LLC Owners Miss

Beyond the obvious fees, several costs tend to catch new business owners off guard in their first year:

**Business Licenses and Local Permits**
Colorado has no state-level general business license, but many municipalities require one. Denver's business license runs **$25–$75** depending on business type. Boulder has its own licensing requirements. If you're operating in multiple cities, you may need multiple local licenses.

**Colorado Sales Tax License**
If your LLC sells taxable goods or services in Colorado, you need a **Colorado Sales Tax License**, which costs **$16 one-time** through the Colorado Department of Revenue. This is often overlooked until the first sales tax return comes due.

**PLLC Licensing (Professional LLCs)**
If you're forming a professional LLC — for doctors, lawyers, accountants, or engineers — you need state professional licensing in addition to your LLC registration. These costs vary significantly by profession.

**Fictitious Business Name (Trade Name)**
Want to operate your Colorado LLC under a name different from your registered LLC name? You'll need to register a trade name (DBA) with the Secretary of State. This costs **$20** and must be renewed annually for the same $20.

**Business Banking**
Most banks charge monthly fees for business checking accounts — typically **$10–$25/month** or waived if you maintain a minimum balance. Some online business banks like Mercury and Relay offer free business checking, which I generally recommend for new LLCs keeping costs low.

**Accountant or CPA**
In my experience, the founders who skip professional accounting in year one end up paying more in year two to fix the mess. A basic small business accountant in Colorado runs **$500–$2,000/year** depending on complexity. If you're structured as a single-member LLC with pass-through taxation and simple finances, you might manage with tax software alone — but get at least one consultation with a CPA to understand your self-employment tax obligations.

## Colorado Taxes for LLCs

Colorado taxes are worth understanding before you form your LLC, because they affect your actual cost of doing business — not just your formation costs.

**State Income Tax**
Colorado has a **flat income tax rate of 4.4%** for individuals, which applies to pass-through LLC income. This is one of the lower flat rates in the country; [the Tax Foundation ranks Colorado favorably](https://taxfoundation.org/state-tax-climate/) for business tax climate.

**Pass-Through Taxation**
Single-member LLCs are taxed as sole proprietors by default. Multi-member LLCs are taxed as partnerships. Either way, LLC income "passes through" to your personal return — you pay Colorado's 4.4% on your share of LLC profits, not a separate corporate rate.

**Self-Employment Tax**
If you're an active member of your Colorado LLC, you owe federal self-employment tax (15.3% on net self-employment income up to the Social Security wage base). This isn't a Colorado-specific cost, but it's often the biggest surprise for first-year LLC owners who don't set aside quarterly estimated payments. See our [LLC quarterly tax payments guide](/blog/llc-quarterly-tax-payments-guide/) for the full breakdown.

**Sales Tax**
Colorado's state sales tax rate is **2.9%**, one of the lowest in the country. However, local jurisdictions layer additional sales tax on top, so the effective rate in Denver is around 8.81% and higher in some other cities.

**No Corporate Income Tax for LLCs**
Unlike C-corporations, your LLC doesn't pay Colorado corporate income tax. Pass-through taxation keeps the structure simple for most small businesses.

## FAQ: Colorado LLC Cost and Fees

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Colorado?**
The minimum cost to start an LLC in Colorado is $50 — the state filing fee for the Articles of Organization. If you use a registered agent service and a formation company, your first-year total typically runs $100–$300.

**What is the Colorado LLC annual fee?**
Colorado charges a $10 annual Periodic Report fee, due during your LLC's anniversary month each year. It's one of the lowest annual LLC fees in the country.

**Does Colorado require a registered agent for an LLC?**
Yes. Every Colorado LLC must maintain a registered agent with a physical Colorado address available during normal business hours. You can serve as your own registered agent or hire a service (typically $49–$299/year).

**Is there a Colorado franchise tax for LLCs?**
No. Colorado does not impose a franchise tax on LLCs. You'll pay Colorado's flat 4.4% individual income tax on pass-through LLC income, but there's no separate franchise tax the way California or Delaware charges.

**Can I form a Colorado LLC for free?**
You cannot avoid the $50 state filing fee, but some formation services advertise $0 formation fees — meaning they don't charge for their service on top of the state fee. ZenBusiness and Bizee both offer free basic formation plans.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in Colorado?**
Online filings through the Colorado Secretary of State are typically processed in real time — often within minutes during business hours. Colorado doesn't offer paid expedited processing because standard online filing is already fast.

**What happens if I miss the Colorado annual report deadline?**
If you miss your Colorado Periodic Report deadline, you'll owe a $50 late fee. If you continue to not file, the Secretary of State can administratively dissolve your LLC. Reinstatement is possible but requires additional paperwork and fees.

**Do I need a business license to operate a Colorado LLC?**
Colorado has no statewide general business license requirement, but many cities and counties require local business licenses. Denver's general business license costs $25–$75. Check with your specific city or county for requirements.

**How much does a registered agent cost in Colorado?**
Registered agent services in Colorado typically cost $49–$299 per year. Northwest Registered Agent charges $125/year. ZenBusiness charges $99/year. LegalZoom charges $249/year — on the higher end compared to competitors.

**Does a Colorado LLC need to pay self-employment tax?**
If you're an active member (working in the business), your share of LLC profits is subject to federal self-employment tax at 15.3% on the first ~$170,000 of net income (2026 figures). This isn't Colorado-specific — it's a federal requirement.
