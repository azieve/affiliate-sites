## Quick Overview: How to Start an LLC in Wisconsin in 2026

Before we go step-by-step, here's the process at a glance:

1. Choose a compliant, distinguishable LLC name
2. Appoint a Wisconsin registered agent with a physical in-state address
3. File Articles of Organization with the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions (WDFI)
4. Draft an operating agreement (not filed with the state, but essential)
5. Get a free EIN from the IRS
6. Stay compliant with Wisconsin's annual report and tax obligations

Total state filing cost: **$130 online or $170 by mail**. Processing time: typically **1 business day online**, or about a week if you file by mail. That's meaningfully faster and cheaper than states like California ($800 minimum franchise tax) or New York (publication requirements that can run into the hundreds of dollars) — one reason Wisconsin regularly shows up on lists of business-friendly states for new LLCs.

## Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Wisconsin LLC

Your LLC name must:

- Contain "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C."
- Be distinguishable from every other business entity already on file with WDFI
- Avoid restricted words (like "Bank," "Trust," or "Insurance") without additional licensing paperwork

Search the [WDFI business entity database](https://www.wdfi.org/apps/CorpSearch/Search.aspx) before you fall in love with a name — Wisconsin's "distinguishable" standard is stricter than a simple duplicate check; names that differ only by punctuation, spacing, or a generic descriptor can still be rejected.

If you're not ready to file but want to lock in a name, Wisconsin lets you reserve one for **120 days for a $15 fee**. In my experience, this is worth doing if you're still finalizing an operating agreement or waiting on a co-founder's paperwork — $15 is cheap insurance against someone else grabbing your name in the meantime.

Most full-service formation providers, including ZenBusiness and [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/), run a free preliminary name check as part of their onboarding flow, which is a nice way to catch obvious conflicts before you pay any state fee.

## Step 2: Appoint a Wisconsin Registered Agent

Every Wisconsin LLC needs a **registered agent** — a person or company designated to receive legal documents (like lawsuits and state correspondence) on the business's behalf. Wisconsin requires the agent to have a physical street address in the state (no P.O. boxes) and be available during normal business hours.

You have three real options:

1. **Be your own registered agent** — free, but your name and home or office address become part of the public record, and you must be reachable at that address during business hours, every business day.
2. **Appoint a friend, co-founder, or employee** — same trade-off as above, just shifted to someone else.
3. **Hire a Wisconsin registered agent service** — typically $99–$249/year, and it keeps your personal address off public filings.

## Step 3: File Your Wisconsin Articles of Organization

This is the step that legally creates your LLC. You'll file **Articles of Organization** (Form 502) with the Wisconsin Department of Financial Institutions, either online through WDFI's portal or by mail.

You'll need to provide:

- Your LLC's name
- The registered agent's name and Wisconsin street address
- The LLC's principal office address
- Whether the LLC will be member-managed or manager-managed
- The organizer's name and signature

**Wisconsin Articles of Organization cost $130 if filed online and $170 if filed by mail.** Online filings are typically processed within one business day — sometimes same-day — while mailed filings can take about a week once WDFI receives them. There's no expedited-processing surcharge like some states charge, because the standard online turnaround is already fast.

## Step 4: Draft an LLC Operating Agreement

Wisconsin does not legally require LLCs to have an operating agreement, and you don't file it with the state. Don't skip it anyway.

As a corporate lawyer, I've seen this document save multi-member LLCs from expensive disputes more times than I can count — and I've seen the absence of one turn a simple disagreement between business partners into a six-figure legal bill. Your operating agreement should spell out:

- Ownership percentages and capital contributions
- How profits and losses are allocated and distributed
- Voting rights and decision-making authority
- What happens if a member wants to leave, dies, or wants to sell their stake
- Management structure (member-managed vs. manager-managed)

Even single-member LLCs benefit from having one on file — banks frequently ask for it when you open a business account, and it reinforces the liability separation between you and your business if your LLC status is ever challenged in court. ## Step 5: Get an EIN From the IRS

Your Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your LLC's federal tax ID — you'll need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal taxes. You can apply directly through the [IRS website](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) for **free**, and if you have a Social Security Number, the online application typically issues your EIN instantly.

If you don't have an SSN (common for non-U.S. residents forming a Wisconsin LLC), you'll need to file Form SS-4 by fax or mail, which can take several weeks. ## Step 6: Handle Wisconsin's Ongoing Compliance

Forming the LLC is the easy part. Staying compliant is what actually protects your liability shield long-term.

**Annual Report.** Wisconsin requires domestic LLCs to file an annual report with WDFI, due by the end of the calendar quarter in which your LLC was originally formed (not a fixed date like December 31). The fee is **$25 for domestic LLCs** and $80 for LLCs formed outside Wisconsin but registered to do business there. Miss it, and WDFI can administratively dissolve your LLC — a headache to reverse that's entirely avoidable with a calendar reminder or a formation service's compliance alerts.

**State taxes.** Wisconsin LLCs are pass-through entities by default — profits flow to your personal return and are taxed at Wisconsin's individual income tax rates, which range roughly from 3.50% to 7.65% depending on your bracket. Unlike California, Wisconsin doesn't impose a separate minimum franchise tax on LLCs just for existing, which is a meaningful cost advantage. One planning tip worth discussing with your accountant: Wisconsin allows a pass-through entity (PTE) tax election that lets LLCs taxed as partnerships or S-corps pay tax at the entity level (7.9%) instead of the individual level — for owners itemizing deductions, this can effectively work around the federal $10,000 SALT deduction cap. This is a genuinely useful strategy for higher-earning owners, but it's not automatic and needs to be elected properly, so don't attempt it without a CPA.

**Sales tax.** If you sell taxable goods or services, you'll need a Seller's Permit from the Wisconsin Department of Revenue. The state sales tax rate is 5%, with some counties adding a local tax on top (bringing the effective combined rate to roughly 5–5.6% in most areas).

**BOI reporting.** Under the federal Corporate Transparency Act, many LLCs were originally required to file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN. That requirement changed significantly: in March 2025, FinCEN issued an interim final rule exempting domestic U.S. companies and their beneficial owners from BOI reporting, narrowing the requirement to foreign entities registered to do business in the U.S. Rules in this area have shifted more than once, so before assuming you're exempt, check current guidance directly at [FinCEN.gov](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) or see our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for the latest status as of 2026.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to start an LLC in Wisconsin?

The mandatory state cost is $130 if you file your Articles of Organization online, or $170 by mail. Add optional costs like a registered agent service ($99–$249/year) or name reservation ($15), and most founders land between $130 and $400 for their first year, including ongoing compliance costs.

### How long does it take to form an LLC in Wisconsin?

Online filings with WDFI are typically processed within 1 business day, and sometimes the same day. Mailed filings generally take about a week to process once received, plus mail transit time. Formation services don't speed up WDFI's internal processing, but they do eliminate the back-and-forth that causes rejected filings to sit in limbo.

### Do I need a registered agent for my Wisconsin LLC?

Yes. Every Wisconsin LLC is legally required to maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in the state. You can serve as your own agent for free, or pay a service $99–$249/year to keep your personal address off public record.

### Can I be my own registered agent in Wisconsin?

Yes, as long as you're a Wisconsin resident with a physical street address in the state and can be available during normal business hours to accept legal documents. Many home-based business owners choose a registered agent service instead specifically to avoid having their home address listed in public state records.

### Do I need a business license to operate an LLC in Wisconsin?

Wisconsin doesn't require a single, general statewide business license. However, depending on your industry and location, you may need specific licenses or permits — a seller's permit for taxable sales, professional licensing (for fields like cosmetology or contracting), or a local municipal permit. Check with your city or county clerk's office in addition to state requirements.

### What is the Wisconsin LLC annual report and when is it due?

It's a mandatory yearly filing with WDFI that confirms your LLC's current information is accurate. It costs $25 for domestic LLCs and is due by the end of the calendar quarter in which your LLC was originally formed — not a fixed calendar date. Missing it can lead to administrative dissolution of your LLC.

### Do I still need to file a BOI report for my Wisconsin LLC in 2026?

As of FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule, most domestic U.S. companies — including Wisconsin LLCs — are exempt from Beneficial Ownership Information reporting; the requirement now generally applies to foreign entities registered to do business in the U.S. Because this rule has changed more than once, confirm your current status directly at FinCEN.gov before assuming you're exempt.
