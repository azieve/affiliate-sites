Medical spas are one of the faster-growing corners of the wellness industry. Botox, fillers, laser hair removal, microneedling, IV therapy and body contouring all bring in recurring revenue, and the startup costs are lower than for a full medical practice. They also carry real liability: needles, lasers, injectables and prescription products. That makes an LLC for a med spa one of the first decisions a new owner should get right.

Med spas are also one of the few businesses where "just form an LLC online" can go wrong. Many states restrict who can own a business that delivers medical services. The wrong structure can put your license, your insurance coverage or the legality of your whole operation at risk. This guide covers what a med spa LLC protects, where it falls short, how state medical-practice rules change the setup, and how to file in 2026 without wasting money.

## Why an LLC for a Med Spa Makes Sense (and What It Can't Do)

A limited liability company separates your personal assets from the business's debts and lawsuits. If a vendor sues over an unpaid invoice, or a landlord pursues a lease default, a properly maintained LLC generally keeps your home and personal savings out of reach. For a med spa, that matters because you take on commercial leases, equipment financing and expensive devices. A single laser or body-contouring platform can run well into the tens of thousands of dollars.

What an LLC does **not** do is shield you from your own professional negligence. If you personally perform an injection and a patient is harmed, the LLC will not protect you from that claim. In my experience, this is the most common misunderstanding among first-time med spa owners. They assume the entity replaces malpractice coverage. It doesn't. The LLC protects you from the business's contractual debts and from the acts of others in many situations. Professional liability insurance protects you from your own clinical mistakes.

If you want a broader primer first, read [What Is an LLC?](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) and our comparison of an [LLC vs a sole proprietorship](/learn/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship/). Operating a med spa as a sole proprietor means every lawsuit, lease and loan can reach you personally. That is rarely a sensible risk when you are handling injectables and energy-based devices.

## The Corporate Practice of Medicine: The Issue That Changes Everything

Before you pick a filing service, understand the **corporate practice of medicine** (CPOM) doctrine. In many states, only licensed physicians, or entities owned by them, may employ doctors or control medical decisions. The rules vary a lot by state. California, New York, Texas and several others are commonly described as having strict CPOM rules. Other states are more flexible. The American Medical Association tracks the policy background on its [website](https://www.ama-assn.org/), and the American Med Spa Association ([AmSpa](https://www.americanmedspa.org/)) publishes state-by-state guidance for owners.

In practice this means that in many states a non-physician cannot simply form an LLC for a med spa, own 100% of it, and hire a doctor to sign off on treatments. Regulators in those states may treat that arrangement as the unlicensed practice of medicine. Treatments like neurotoxin injections, prescription-strength fillers and many laser procedures count as medical services in most places.

### Common structures that work around CPOM

The usual fixes fall into three buckets. Which one applies to you depends on your state and your license.

1. **Physician-owned PLLC or professional corporation.** A licensed physician owns the entity that provides the medical services. Many states require a "professional LLC" (PLLC) or similar professional entity when the owners are licensed professionals.
2. **Friendly-PC / MSO model.** A physician-owned professional entity provides the medical services. A separate management company, often a standard LLC owned by the non-physician founder, handles the business side. That includes leasing space, buying equipment, marketing, front-desk staff and billing. The two entities sign a management services agreement. This is a common med spa business structure in strict-CPOM states.
3. **Direct LLC ownership.** In more permissive states, a non-physician may own the LLC directly and contract with a medical director. Even then, supervision and delegation rules still apply.

This is not do-it-yourself territory at the final design stage. I strongly recommend paying a health-care attorney in your state for a one-time structure review before you file anything. Expect somewhere in the range of a few thousand dollars for a structure and agreement review, depending on the market. It's cheap next to a licensing board action or a denied insurance claim.

## PLLC vs LLC: Which One Does Your Med Spa Need?

The PLLC vs LLC question comes down to who owns the entity and what the entity does.

| Factor | Standard LLC | PLLC (Professional LLC) |
|---|---|---|
| Who can own it | Generally anyone | Typically only licensed professionals (e.g., physicians, sometimes NPs or PAs, depending on state) |
| Best for | Management company, or med spa in a permissive state | Entity that actually delivers medical services |
| Liability shield | Business debts and others' acts | Same, but not your own malpractice |
| Extra requirements | Standard filing | License verification, board approval or certificate in some states |
| Typical use | MSO / management entity | "Friendly" physician-owned clinical entity |

If you are a nurse practitioner, physician assistant or registered nurse founding the business, check whether your state lets you own a medical entity at all. Some states allow nurse practitioners full practice authority and ownership. Others require physician ownership or supervision. ## The Medical Director: Contract, Compensation and Risk

In many states, a med spa needs a **med spa medical director**, a licensed physician who supervises treatments, writes protocols and signs standing orders. Requirements for on-site presence, chart review and delegation differ widely. Some states require the physician to see the patient first. Others allow delegation to a nurse practitioner or PA under written protocols.

A few practical points that owners often miss:

- **The medical director agreement matters as much as the LLC.** It should state the scope of supervision, how often charts are reviewed, who carries which insurance, and what happens if the physician leaves.
- **Fee-splitting rules.** Many states restrict physicians from paying a percentage of fees to non-physicians. That's one reason MSO fees are often drafted as fixed fair-market-value payments rather than a share of medical revenue. Your attorney should confirm this for your state.
- **A single point of failure.** If your whole operation hangs on one physician who walks away, you may have to stop performing certain treatments. Consider a backup physician arrangement.
- **Insurance.** Confirm that both the physician and the entity are covered. Ask the carrier directly whether it covers every service you plan to offer, such as IV therapy, weight-loss medications or lasers.

## Taxes for a Med Spa LLC in 2026

By default, a single-member LLC is taxed as a sole proprietorship, and a multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership. In both cases, profit passes through to the owners' personal returns. According to the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc), an LLC can also elect to be taxed as a corporation. A pass-through med spa owner typically owes self-employment tax (15.3% on net earnings up to the Social Security wage base, then 2.9% Medicare above it) in addition to income tax.

### When an S corporation election can help

Once profit is consistently high, many owners explore an S corporation election. The idea is that you pay yourself a reasonable salary, which is subject to payroll taxes. Remaining profit is distributed without self-employment tax. Here is a simplified example.

- Net profit: $180,000
- Reasonable salary (illustrative): $90,000
- Distribution: $90,000

In this illustration, self-employment-type taxes would apply to the $90,000 salary rather than the full $180,000. The potential saving is real, but it's offset by payroll processing costs, a separate tax return and the "reasonable compensation" scrutiny the IRS applies. This is an illustration only, not a prediction. A med spa that earns a modest profit may not benefit. See our breakdown of [LLC vs S-Corp taxes](/blog/llc-vs-s-corp-which-is-better-for-taxes/) and talk to a CPA before electing.

Other tax items to plan for in 2026:

- **Sales tax on products.** Skincare products are commonly taxable. Medical services often are not, but rules vary by state.
- **Equipment deductions.** Section 179 and bonus depreciation rules can affect how you deduct lasers and devices. Rules change from year to year, so confirm current limits with your CPA.
- **Payroll.** If you have employees, you'll need payroll tax registration and workers' compensation coverage.
- **Quarterly estimates.** Pass-through owners generally pay estimated taxes four times a year. Our [LLC quarterly tax payments guide](/blog/llc-quarterly-tax-payments-guide/) explains the schedule.

## How to Form an LLC for a Med Spa: Step by Step

### Step 1: Confirm your state's rules first

Before spending a dollar on filing, find out whether your state restricts non-physician ownership. Check your state medical board's website and AmSpa's state summaries. Decide whether you need a PLLC, an MSO plus clinical entity, or a standard LLC.

### Step 2: Choose your state and name

Most med spas form in the state where they operate, because they must register as a foreign entity anyway if they form elsewhere. That makes forming in a "business-friendly" state pointless for a brick-and-mortar business. Our [best state to form an LLC](/blog/best-state-to-form-an-llc/) guide covers the exceptions. Your name should be available, and some states require specific words like "PLLC" or restrict the use of terms like "medical" or "clinic."

### Step 3: Appoint a registered agent

Every LLC needs a registered agent with a physical address in the state. You can serve as your own, but your address becomes public. Read [What Is a Registered Agent?](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) for the trade-offs.

### Step 4: File the formation documents

Filing fees are set by the state and vary widely. They range from roughly $35 in some states to $500 in others. Check your state's current fee schedule, since fees change.

### Step 5: Create an operating agreement

If you have partners, a physician co-owner or investors, the operating agreement is the most important document you'll sign. It should cover capital contributions, voting, what happens when a physician-owner leaves, and buy-sell terms. See our [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/).

### Step 6: Get your EIN, licenses and bank account

Get an EIN from the IRS (free if you apply directly). Then obtain local business licenses, any facility or device registrations your state requires, and open a dedicated business bank account. Keep personal and business funds separate, or you risk weakening the liability shield.

### A note on BOI reporting

Beneficial ownership reporting rules have shifted considerably since 2024. As of 2025, FinCEN's interim final rule removed the requirement for U.S.-formed companies and their owners. Rules can change again, so check our [BOI report changes and updates](/blog/boi-report-changes-and-updates-2026/) page for the latest.

## Common Mistakes Med Spa Owners Make

- **Skipping the CPOM check.** Learning that your structure is non-compliant after you've signed a lease is expensive.
- **Treating the medical director as a rubber stamp.** Regulators and plaintiffs' lawyers look closely at whether supervision was real.
- **Underinsuring.** You may need professional liability, general liability, property, cyber and workers' compensation coverage. A single policy rarely covers everything.
- **Mixing funds.** Paying personal expenses from the business account weakens the liability shield, a risk courts sometimes call "piercing the corporate veil."
- **Not budgeting for ongoing costs.** Some states charge annual fees, such as California's $800 minimum franchise tax. See our [California LLC $800 franchise tax guide](/blog/california-llc-800-franchise-tax/) if you operate there.
- **Ignoring advertising rules.** Before-and-after photos, testimonials and drug-brand claims face FDA, FTC and state-board scrutiny.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need an LLC to open a med spa?

No law requires one, but operating without any entity leaves your personal assets exposed to business debts and many claims. An LLC is the most common starting point. Depending on your state, you may need a PLLC or a management company plus a physician-owned clinical entity.

### Can a non-physician own a med spa LLC?

It depends on the state. In states with strict corporate practice of medicine rules, non-physicians usually cannot own the entity that provides medical services. They can often own a management company under a compliant arrangement. Other states permit more direct ownership. Ask a health-care attorney in your state.

### How much does it cost to start an LLC for a med spa?

State filing fees typically run from about $35 to $500. Formation services add $0 to a few hundred dollars per year. Attorney structure review, insurance and licensing come on top. See our guide on [how much an LLC costs](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/) for a general breakdown.

### Do I need a medical director for my med spa?

In many states, yes, if you perform medical procedures such as injectables or certain lasers. The exact supervision requirements differ by state and by who performs the treatment. Your state medical or nursing board is the authority.

### Should my med spa LLC elect S corporation status?

Possibly, once profits are high enough to justify payroll costs and the added compliance. It does not make sense for every owner. A CPA can model your numbers for 2026.

### Does an LLC protect me from malpractice claims?

Not from your own professional negligence. It can help limit exposure from other people's actions and business debts. Malpractice insurance remains essential.

### Can a nurse practitioner or esthetician own a med spa?

Sometimes. Nurse practitioner ownership depends on your state's practice authority and CPOM rules. Estheticians can typically own businesses limited to non-medical services. Anything involving injectables or prescription products generally requires medical supervision.

### Do I need to register in more than one state if I open multiple locations?

If you operate in another state, you generally need to register as a foreign LLC there and comply with that state's medical-practice rules. See our guide to [foreign LLC registration](/blog/foreign-llc-registration-doing-business-in-another-state/).
