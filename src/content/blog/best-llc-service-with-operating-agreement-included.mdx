## Why an Operating Agreement Matters More Than You Think

When you file articles of organization, your state confirms that your LLC exists. What it does not do is tell you how the business should be run. That's the job of the operating agreement.

An [LLC operating agreement](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) is a legally binding internal contract that defines the rules members have agreed to follow. It covers:

- **Ownership structure** — who owns what percentage of the LLC
- **Profit and loss allocation** — how earnings and losses are shared
- **Management authority** — who can sign contracts, open bank accounts, hire employees
- **Voting rights and procedures** — what decisions require a vote, and what threshold is needed
- **Member exit provisions** — what happens when a member wants to leave or sell their interest
- **Dissolution rules** — how the company is wound down if necessary

I've seen too many business owners skip this step, thinking it's a bureaucratic formality. Then a co-founder dispute emerges, a bank refuses to open a business account without documentation, or an early-stage investor backs away because the company's governance isn't in writing. The operating agreement is not optional infrastructure — it's the document that makes your LLC real in practice, not just on paper.

According to the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc), LLCs are defined and governed by state law, and while federal tax rules treat them with flexibility, the internal governance document is entirely up to the members. That means a formation service's quality — and whether they include a proper agreement — directly impacts how protected you actually are.

This is equally true for single-member LLCs. A single-member LLC operating agreement establishes the legal separation between you and your business, which is the primary protection the LLC structure provides. Without it, courts have pierced that veil and held owners personally liable. Don't let that happen because you skipped an $0 document that should have been included with your formation service.

## What to Look for in an LLC Service That Includes an Operating Agreement

Not every operating agreement is the same quality, and not every "included" claim is what it appears. Before you commit to a formation service, evaluate these five factors:

**1. Included vs. add-on pricing**
Some formation services advertise complete LLC setup but bury the operating agreement behind a higher tier or charge $75–$150 extra. Always read the plan comparison, not just the headline price.

**2. Customizable vs. static template**
A pre-filled static template is better than nothing, but if you have two or more members with non-equal ownership, special profit-sharing arrangements, or specific buyout terms, you need a document you can actually customize. The best services generate a tailored agreement based on your answers to intake questions.

**3. State-specific language**
Operating agreement requirements vary by jurisdiction. California, Delaware, Maine, Missouri, and New York have specific statutory requirements for what must be included. A generic document that ignores your state's LLC statutes can create ambiguity that undermines the protections you paid for.

**4. Attorney review availability**
For simple LLCs, a well-built template is sufficient. For businesses with outside investors, multiple equity classes, or significant intellectual property, access to attorney review is worth paying for. Some services offer this as an optional add-on.

**5. Total cost of ownership**
Look at the full two-year picture: formation fee, operating agreement (if separate), registered agent renewal, and any annual compliance fees. ## How to Use Your Operating Agreement After Formation

Getting the document is half the job. Using it correctly is the other half.

**Open a business bank account immediately.** Major banks — Chase, Bank of America, Mercury, Relay — routinely require your operating agreement when opening a business checking account. Keep a signed, finalized digital copy accessible from the moment you receive it.

**Store it with your core business records.** Your operating agreement should live alongside your articles of organization, EIN confirmation letter, and state annual report filings. Whether that's a physical binder or a cloud folder, treat it like a legal document — because it is one.

**Review it before adding a member or changing ownership.** If a business partner joins, equity is transferred, or an outside investor comes in, your operating agreement needs to be formally amended. Most formation services offer amendment assistance as an add-on, or you can work with a business attorney directly.

**Consult it before major decisions.** Operating agreement disputes are almost always the result of people forgetting what they agreed to at formation. Voting thresholds, distribution timelines, and approval requirements are spelled out in the document. Use it.

According to the [U.S. Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure), maintaining organized business records — including governance documents — is a basic compliance obligation for any formally structured entity. The operating agreement sits at the center of that.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does every LLC formation service include an operating agreement?

No — and this is one of the most common surprises founders encounter. Several services, including Inc Authority at every plan tier and Bizee at their free Silver level, do not include an operating agreement by default. The best LLC service with operating agreement included will list it explicitly in the plan features, not hide it as an add-on. Always read the feature comparison before selecting a plan.

### Is an operating agreement legally required for my LLC?

It depends on your state. California, Delaware, Maine, Missouri, and New York legally require operating agreements for LLCs formed in those states. The majority of states don't mandate one by law — but every business attorney will tell you to have one regardless. Without an operating agreement, your LLC defaults to state statutes that may distribute profits, allocate authority, or handle member exits in ways that don't reflect your actual intentions.

### Can I use a free operating agreement template I found online?

Technically yes, but the risks are meaningful. Generic templates often don't reflect your state's specific LLC statutes, may not address single-member versus multi-member distinctions correctly, and frequently omit critical provisions around buyout rights or dissolution. Using a formation service that generates a state-specific, customized operating agreement — as ZenBusiness Pro does — is significantly safer than relying on a generic Google document.

### How much does a standalone operating agreement cost?

If you hire an attorney to draft one from scratch, expect $500–$1,500 depending on complexity and market. Standalone legal document platforms charge $99–$199 for a template-based agreement. Getting one bundled into a full-service formation package — as ZenBusiness Pro at $199/year does, along with registered agent service, EIN filing, and compliance tools — is almost always the better value in 2026.

### Do single-member LLCs need an operating agreement?

Yes, unequivocally. A single-member operating agreement establishes the formal legal separation between you and your LLC, which is the fundamental protection the LLC structure provides. Without it, a creditor or court has more grounds to pierce the corporate veil and reach your personal assets. It also smooths bank account opening, professional agreements, and any future transition to a multi-member structure.

### What's the difference between a member-managed and manager-managed LLC?

In a member-managed LLC, all owners share authority to act on behalf of the company. In a manager-managed LLC, a designated manager — who may or may not be a member — handles day-to-day decisions, while other members remain passive investors. Your operating agreement specifies which structure applies and defines the scope of the manager's authority. Our [LLC member vs. manager-managed guide](/blog/llc-member-vs-manager-managed/) explains both options in detail.

### Can I amend my operating agreement after formation?

Yes. Operating agreements can be amended at any time by following whatever amendment procedure is written into the document — typically a vote of the members at or above a specified threshold. Common amendment triggers include adding a new member, transferring ownership interest, changing the management structure, or bringing in outside capital. Most formation services offer amendment assistance as a paid add-on, or you can work with a local business attorney.

### Is ZenBusiness's operating agreement equivalent to a lawyer-drafted document?

Not precisely. ZenBusiness generates a customized agreement through a structured template system — it's thorough, state-specific, and covers all standard LLC provisions. But it is not the same as retaining a business attorney to draft a bespoke agreement tailored to unusual ownership structures or complex legal scenarios. For the overwhelming majority of single-member and straightforward multi-member LLCs, ZenBusiness's agreement is more than adequate. For venture-backed entities or complex real estate structures, attorney involvement is the right call.
