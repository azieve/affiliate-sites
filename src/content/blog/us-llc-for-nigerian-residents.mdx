## Can a Nigerian Resident Own a US LLC?

Yes. No US state restricts LLC ownership by citizenship or residency. Nigerian nationals can be the sole member, one of several members, or the manager of an LLC. For a deeper look at the general rules, see our guide on [whether a foreigner can own an LLC in the US](/blog/can-a-foreigner-own-an-llc-in-the-us/).

Three things stay the same whether you live in Lagos, Abuja, or Houston:

- **The LLC needs a US registered agent.** This is a person or company with a physical address in the formation state who receives legal and government mail. Your Nigerian address can't serve this role. See our explainer on [what a registered agent is](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/).
- **The LLC needs a name, a state filing, and an operating agreement.** The agreement is optional in some states but strongly recommended for foreign owners, because banks often ask for it. Our [operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) covers what to include.
- **The LLC needs an EIN** for banking, payment processors, and tax reporting.

In my experience, the biggest misconception among first-time Nigerian founders is that the LLC is a tax shield. It is not. It is a legal container that gives you liability protection and a US business identity. Tax treatment depends on where you live and where the income is earned, which we cover below.

## Getting an EIN Without a Social Security Number

You do not need an SSN or ITIN to get an EIN. The IRS accepts applications from foreign owners through Form SS-4, and the "responsible party" can list "N/A" or "Foreign" in the SSN/ITIN field. The online EIN application generally requires a US taxpayer ID, so Nigerian owners typically use one of these routes:

1. **Fax:** Send a completed SS-4 to the IRS number listed in the form's instructions. Responses commonly arrive within about four business days.
2. **Phone:** International applicants can call the IRS line for foreign applicants (listed on [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/employer-id-numbers)) and complete the application with an agent.
3. **Mail:** This is the slowest option, often taking four to six weeks.

Some formation services, including [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) on certain plans, will prepare or file the EIN request for you. Confirm that this is included for non-resident owners before purchasing. Our step-by-step guide on [getting an EIN without an SSN](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-without-an-ssn/) covers all three methods, including the common errors that cause rejections.

## Taxes: What a Nigerian-Resident Owner Needs to Know

This is where most of the risk sits, and it's worth getting professional advice. The following is general information, not tax advice.

### US federal taxes

A single-member LLC owned by a non-US person is, by default, a "disregarded entity" for US federal income tax purposes. In general terms, a foreign owner who isn't engaged in a US trade or business, and who has no US-source income that is effectively connected with such a business, typically has no US federal income tax liability on income earned from clients outside the US. Whether your activities count as a "US trade or business" depends on facts such as where the work is performed and where you have employees or agents. A Nigerian freelancer working from Nigeria for US clients commonly falls outside it, but this is a fact-specific question to confirm with a cross-border tax professional.

Even with no US tax due, there is still a reporting duty:

- **Form 5472 and a pro forma Form 1120** must be filed each year by foreign-owned single-member LLCs that have reportable transactions, such as owner contributions and draws. The penalty for failing to file is **$25,000 per form**, which makes this the most expensive mistake a new foreign owner can make. See our [Form 5472 filing guide](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/) and the IRS's [Form 5472 page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5472).
- **ITIN:** You generally don't need one just to own the LLC, but you may need one for certain filings or treaty claims. Our guide on [getting an ITIN as a foreign LLC owner](/blog/how-to-get-an-itin-as-a-foreign-llc-owner/) explains when.

Multi-member LLCs are taxed as partnerships by default and have different filing duties, including partnership returns and withholding rules for foreign partners.

### Nigerian taxes

Nigeria taxes residents on their worldwide income, and its tax framework has been reformed in recent years, with new legislation taking effect from 2026. A US LLC owned by a Nigerian resident does not make income invisible to Nigerian authorities. Depending on how the LLC is treated under Nigerian law, profits may be taxable to you personally in Nigeria. There is no US–Nigeria income tax treaty that would eliminate double taxation in a simple way, so planning matters. Work with a Nigerian tax adviser alongside a US cross-border specialist, and keep clean records of every transaction.

### Foreign exchange and banking rules

Money moving into Nigeria is subject to Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) rules and your bank's own compliance checks. Keep invoices, contracts, and LLC documents ready, as banks may ask for proof of the source of funds. This is a regulatory area that changes, so verify current requirements with your Nigerian bank.

## Beneficial Ownership (BOI) Reporting in 2026

Many guides online are out of date here. In March 2025, FinCEN issued an interim final rule that removed the beneficial ownership reporting requirement for US-formed companies and their US owners. Under that rule, only entities formed under foreign law and registered to do business in a US state are required to report. A Nigerian-owned LLC formed in Wyoming, New Mexico, or Delaware is a domestic company, so it is currently outside the requirement. Rules in this area have shifted repeatedly, so check [FinCEN's BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) before you rely on this, and read our [BOI report changes and updates](/blog/boi-report-changes-and-updates-2026/) article for the latest.

## Opening a US Bank Account and Accepting Payments

Opening a bank account is usually the step that takes longest. Some US banks require an in-person visit, while several online fintechs and neobanks accept non-resident owners remotely. Typical documents include:

- LLC articles of organization
- EIN confirmation letter (CP 575 or 147C)
- Operating agreement
- Passport and proof of address
- A short description of your business and expected activity

Approval is never guaranteed, and providers can change their policies or close accounts after review. Have a backup option, and don't make a single account your only way of getting paid. Our guide to a [US bank account for a foreign-owned LLC](/blog/us-bank-account-for-a-foreign-owned-llc/) compares current approaches.

Once the account is open, you can usually connect payment processors for US clients. Each processor has its own country and verification rules, so check eligibility for Nigerian owners before you assume it will work.

## Common Mistakes Nigerian LLC Owners Make

I've seen too many new founders spend money on formation and then ignore compliance for two years. The most common errors:

- **Skipping Form 5472.** The $25,000 penalty applies even when no tax is owed.
- **Mixing personal and business money.** This weakens liability protection and creates reporting headaches. Keep a separate account.
- **Using the LLC for activities that need licenses.** Lending, money transmission, and some financial services are regulated. Don't assume the LLC permits them.
- **Ignoring state annual reports and fees.** A missed filing can lead to administrative dissolution. See [what happens if you don't renew your LLC](/blog/what-happens-if-you-don-t-renew-your-llc/).
- **Trusting "tax-free" claims.** Be skeptical of any marketing that says a US LLC means you pay no tax anywhere.
- **Not planning for Nigerian reporting.** The US side is only half of the picture.

## FAQ: US LLC for Nigerian Residents

### Can a Nigerian citizen open an LLC in the US without living there?

Yes. No US state requires an LLC owner to be a US resident or citizen. You will need a US registered agent and a US EIN, but not a US address of your own.

### How much does it cost to form an LLC as a Nigerian resident?

Expect a state fee between $50 and $110 for formation, plus about $100 to $150 per year for a registered agent after the first year, and annual state fees (for example, $60 minimum in Wyoming or $300 in Delaware). Professional tax filing for Form 5472 is an additional cost. Formation services such as [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) start at $0 plus state fees.

### Do I need an SSN or ITIN to get an EIN?

No. You can apply with Form SS-4 by fax, phone, or mail without an SSN or ITIN. See our guide to [getting an EIN without an SSN](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-without-an-ssn/).

### Do I have to pay US taxes on my LLC income as a Nigerian resident?

It depends on the source and type of income. Foreign owners with no US trade or business and no US-source effectively connected income often owe no US federal income tax, but they still must file the required forms. Consult a cross-border tax professional.

### What happens if I don't file Form 5472?

The IRS can assess a $25,000 penalty per form, per year, and additional penalties can apply if the failure continues after notice. Read our [Form 5472 guide](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/).

### Which state is best for a Nigerian-owned LLC?

Wyoming and New Mexico are popular for low costs; Delaware is the choice for startups seeking US investment. See our [best state to form an LLC](/blog/best-state-to-form-an-llc/) comparison.

### Do I have to file a BOI report for my LLC in 2026?

Under FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule, US-formed companies are currently exempt, but the rules have changed several times. Verify current requirements on FinCEN's website.

### Can I open a US bank account from Nigeria?

Often yes, through online banking providers that accept non-resident LLC owners, though approval is not guaranteed. See our guide to [opening a US bank account for a foreign-owned LLC](/blog/us-bank-account-for-a-foreign-owned-llc/).
