Opening a chiropractic office means taking on a lease, X-ray and table financing, associate payroll, and a patient base that puts its spine in your hands every day. An LLC for chiropractors can separate much of that business risk from your house and savings. But chiropractic is a licensed profession, so the standard LLC advice written for freelancers and landscapers often doesn't apply.

**Many states require a chiropractor to use a professional LLC (PLLC) or professional corporation (PC), and California generally doesn't allow an LLC for chiropractic at all.** This guide covers what the rules look like, what the entity does and doesn't protect, how the 2026 tax numbers work, and the insurer and Medicare steps generic guides leave out. For the filing itself, a service such as [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) offers a $0 starter plan plus your state fee, though you should confirm it handles professional entities in your state before you order.

## PLLC, LLC, or PC: Which Entity Can a Chiropractor Use?

A PLLC is an LLC reserved for licensed professionals. It has members, an operating agreement, and pass-through taxation like a regular LLC. The differences come from your state's professional entity statute and your chiropractic board's rules, and they usually include:

- **Licensed owners only.** Members generally must hold an active chiropractic license in that state. A spouse who runs the front desk usually can't hold an interest.
- **Board involvement.** Some states want a license verification or approval before the Secretary of State will accept the filing.
- **Name restrictions.** The name usually needs "PLLC," "P.L.L.C.," or similar wording, and some boards regulate terms such as "clinic" or "institute."
- **Limited purpose.** The articles often must say the company exists to provide chiropractic services and related activities.

Here is how that plays out in a few larger states. Treat these as orientation, not as a substitute for checking the current rules.

| State | Typical entity for a chiropractic practice | Base filing fee | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas | Professional LLC or professional association | $300 | Certificate of formation must identify the professional service |
| Florida | Professional LLC under Chapter 621 | $125 | Ownership limited to licensed professionals |
| New York | PLLC | $200 | State Education Department approval, plus a publication requirement that can cost well over $1,000 in some counties |
| Pennsylvania | LLC structured as a restricted professional company | $125 | Chiropractic is treated as a restricted professional service |
| California | Professional chiropractic corporation, not an LLC | Varies | Board of Chiropractic Examiners rules; $800 minimum franchise tax generally applies |

If you practice in more than one state, each state's board rules apply separately. The [Federation of Chiropractic Licensing Boards](https://www.fclb.org) is a useful place to find your board. Before you file anything, ask that board two questions in writing: which entity types may render chiropractic services, and whether it needs to approve or register the entity. Our broader guide to [LLCs for medical practices](/blog/llc-for-medical-practice/) covers the parallel rules for physicians, and [what an LLC is](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) covers the basics if you're starting from zero.

The mistake I'd warn against above all others is filing a plain LLC because it was the first option on a website. If your state required a PLLC, you may have to re-form the entity, get a new EIN, re-open bank accounts, and redo insurer contracts. Those are weeks of delayed reimbursements caused by a ten-minute decision.

### Can a non-chiropractor invest in or own your clinic?

Often not directly. Licensed-only ownership rules, and in some states a corporate practice doctrine, limit outside investors. A common workaround is a **management services organization (MSO)**: a separate, non-professional company owned by the investor that provides staff, space, billing, and marketing to the clinic for a fair-market fee. Regulators scrutinize these arrangements, particularly where the fee looks like a share of patient revenue, so have a healthcare attorney design it.

## What an LLC Protects a Chiropractor From, and What It Doesn't

The liability shield of an LLC for chiropractors is real, but it's narrower for clinicians than the marketing suggests.

**What the entity typically helps with:**

- Clinic debts, vendor bills, and most contract disputes, unless you signed a personal guarantee
- A slip-and-fall in the waiting room or a patient injured by a faulty piece of equipment, beyond what insurance pays
- In many states, malpractice claims based on the work of another chiropractor or associate in the practice

**What it doesn't help with:**

- **Your own treatment.** If a patient alleges that your adjustment caused a disc injury or a cervical artery dissection, you remain personally answerable for your own professional negligence. Professional entity statutes consistently preserve this.
- **Personal guarantees.** Equipment lessors and commercial landlords usually require one from new practice owners, so your largest debts may follow you anyway.
- **Board discipline.** The entity has no effect on your license.
- **Regulatory penalties you personally caused**, such as improper billing.

So insurance does most of the work for your own clinical risk, and the entity handles most of the rest. Keep the shield intact by using a separate bank account, signing contracts in the company's name, and adopting a written operating agreement. Our [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) walks through the core terms. For a chiropractic PLLC, add provisions for license suspension, disability, death, and how patient records are handled if a partner leaves.

### The insurance gotcha most new owners miss

An individual malpractice policy doesn't automatically cover your entity. When you form a PLLC, the company itself can be named in a lawsuit, so ask your carrier to add it as an insured or to issue separate entity coverage, and ask whether limits are shared or separate. NCMIC is one of the best-known chiropractic malpractice insurers, and others write this coverage too. Premiums for a solo doctor often land in the low thousands of dollars or less per year, depending on state and limits, but the only reliable figure is a quote.

Also check whether your policy is occurrence or claims-made. If it's claims-made, price the tail coverage before changing carriers or retiring. A complete package usually adds general liability, property coverage for equipment, cyber coverage for patient records, and workers' compensation once you have employees.

## How a Chiropractor LLC Is Taxed in 2026

By default, a single-member PLLC is taxed like a sole proprietorship and a multi-member PLLC like a partnership. Profit passes through to your personal return, and it's subject to self-employment tax of 15.3%: 12.4% for Social Security up to the [2026 wage base of $184,500](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/cbb.html) and 2.9% for Medicare with no cap. Our overview of [how LLCs are taxed](/blog/llc-taxes-how-are-llcs-taxed/) covers the mechanics.

### S-corp election: a worked example

Many chiropractors elect S-corporation treatment using Form 2553 once profit is high enough. The entity stays a PLLC under state law. You pay yourself a reasonable W-2 salary and take the remaining profit as distributions, which aren't subject to payroll tax. The IRS summarizes the basics on its [S corporation page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporations).

Here's an illustrative comparison for a solo owner with $180,000 in net practice income:

| | Default PLLC | S-corp election, $95,000 salary |
|---|---:|---:|
| Income subject to Social Security and Medicare tax | $166,230 | $95,000 |
| Self-employment or payroll tax (both halves) | $25,433 | $14,535 |
| Gross tax difference | | $10,898 |
| Added payroll, bookkeeping, and Form 1120-S cost (estimate) | | $2,500 to $4,000 |
| **Estimated net annual benefit, before income tax effects** | | **About $6,900 to $8,400** |

The real result will be different, for three reasons:

- **Reasonable compensation is a real constraint.** A chiropractor who treats patients full time is generating most of the revenue personally, and a token salary invites IRS attention. Start with compensation data, including the [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/chiropractors.htm) figures and practice-specific surveys, then document your reasoning. Our guide to [reasonable salary for S-corp owners](/blog/reasonable-salary-for-s-corp-llc-owners/) goes further.
- **It's a poor fit at lower profit levels.** Below roughly $60,000 to $80,000 in net income, the added costs often absorb most of the savings.
- **State treatment varies.** Some states add entity-level taxes or fees to S corporations.

If you decide to proceed, [how to file Form 2553](/blog/how-to-file-form-2553-for-an-llc/) covers the deadline, which is generally two months and 15 days after the start of the tax year in which the election takes effect.

### The QBI deduction has a health-services catch

The Section 199A [qualified business income deduction](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/qualified-business-income-deduction) can be worth up to 20% of qualifying income, and 2025's tax legislation made it permanent. Chiropractic is generally treated as a "specified service trade or business" in the field of health. That means the deduction phases out entirely above a taxable income limit. For 2026, the phase-out begins at roughly $200,000 for single filers and $400,000 for joint filers and runs over a $75,000 or $150,000 range.

Two planning points follow. W-2 wages paid to you by an S corporation don't count as QBI, so a higher salary can reduce the deduction even as it adds payroll tax. And if your income lands near the threshold, the interaction matters enough to model both ways with a CPA.

### Equipment write-offs

Tables, digital X-ray systems, and decompression units are often eligible for Section 179 expensing, which has a base limit of $2.5 million indexed for inflation, or for 100% bonus depreciation on qualifying property acquired after January 19, 2025. Our [LLC tax deductions list](/blog/llc-tax-deductions-list/) covers other common write-offs. All of this is general education rather than tax advice, so have a CPA who knows healthcare practices run your numbers.

## Step-by-Step: Setting Up an LLC for Chiropractors

1. **Confirm the permitted entity** with your state chiropractic board.
2. **Pick a compliant name** with the required designator and check availability.
3. **Obtain board approval or license verification**, if your state requires it.
4. **File articles of organization** directly or through a service.
5. **Appoint a [registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/)** with a reliable in-state address.
6. **Sign an operating agreement** with license-loss and buy-sell terms.
7. **Get an EIN** free from the IRS.
8. **Open a dedicated [business bank account](/blog/llc-bank-account-how-to-open-one/)** and a separate account if you hold patient deposits or prepaid care plans.
9. **Get an organizational NPI** (Type 2) through [NPPES](https://nppes.cms.hhs.gov) in addition to your individual Type 1 NPI, if you'll bill insurance under the entity.
10. **Update insurer contracts and malpractice coverage** so they reflect the new entity.
11. **Update Medicare enrollment** through [PECOS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/enrollment-renewal/providers-suppliers/chain-ownership-system-pecos) if you see Medicare patients.
12. **Decide on S-corp treatment** with your CPA before the deadline.

Allow extra time for steps 9 through 11. Payer credentialing commonly takes 60 to 120 days, and claims billed under the wrong tax ID or NPI can be denied. If you're converting an established practice, keep the old billing setup running until each payer confirms the change. Our guide on [converting a sole proprietorship to an LLC](/blog/how-to-convert-sole-proprietorship-to-llc/) covers the rest of the transition.

On federal beneficial ownership reporting, FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule removed the requirement for companies formed in the United States. Check [FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) and our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for the current status in 2026.

## Chiropractic Compliance Traps After Formation

- **Medicare scope and opt-out.** Medicare covers chiropractors' manual manipulation of the spine to correct a subluxation, and generally not other services they provide. Chiropractors also can't formally opt out of Medicare the way some physicians can, which matters if you plan a cash-only or membership model. The [American Chiropractic Association](https://www.acatoday.org) publishes practice and billing resources.
- **Prepaid care plans.** Selling packages of visits can raise refund, unearned-revenue, and state consumer-protection questions. Some boards have specific rules, so check yours.
- **Associates and independent contractors.** Calling an associate a 1099 contractor doesn't make them one. If you set the schedule, supply the patients, and control the treatment setting, the worker may be an employee for tax purposes.
- **Referral arrangements.** Paying for patient referrals, including from attorneys in personal-injury cases, can violate state law and, for federal healthcare programs, the Anti-Kickback Statute.
- **Real estate.** Owners who buy their building often hold it in a separate LLC and lease it to the practice, which keeps a malpractice claim further from the property. See [whether an LLC can own property](/blog/can-an-llc-own-property/) and [holding company structures](/blog/holding-company-llc-structure-explained/).
- **License status.** If a member's license lapses, the PLLC's ability to operate may be at risk. Put every renewal date on the same calendar as your annual report.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a chiropractor have a regular LLC?

It depends on the state. Some states require a PLLC or professional corporation, and others permit a standard LLC under certain conditions. California generally requires a professional chiropractic corporation instead. Get your board's answer before filing.

### Does an LLC protect a chiropractor from malpractice lawsuits?

Not from claims based on your own treatment. A PLLC can protect your personal assets from business debts and, in many states, from another clinician's negligence. Malpractice insurance is what protects you against your own clinical risk.

### How much does it cost to form a chiropractic PLLC?

State fees are commonly $100 to $300, though some reach $500. Add board fees where required, a registered agent if you don't serve yourself, and any publication costs. Most solo owners spend roughly $300 to $900 in the first year, excluding insurance.

### Should a chiropractor elect S-corp status?

It's often worth modeling once net practice income passes about $80,000. The election can cut payroll taxes, but you must pay a reasonable salary, run payroll, and file a separate return. A higher salary can also reduce your QBI deduction.

### Can my spouse co-own my chiropractic PLLC?

Usually not, unless your spouse is also a licensed chiropractor in that state. Ownership is generally restricted to licensees. Your spouse can often be an employee, or own a separate company that leases real estate or provides management services at fair market value.

### Do I need a new NPI when I form a PLLC?

Your individual Type 1 NPI stays with you for life. If you bill insurers under the entity, you'll generally also need a Type 2 organizational NPI, along with updated payer contracts and Medicare enrollment.

### What happens to my insurance contracts when I form an LLC?

They don't transfer automatically. Most payers tie contracts to a tax ID, so a new EIN usually means notifying each payer and possibly re-credentialing. Start early and don't change your billing until each payer confirms the update.

### Can I use an online service to form a chiropractic PLLC?

Often yes, for the filing step. Confirm that the provider supports professional entities in your state and who handles any board approval. If the rules are complex, an attorney-backed option such as [LLC Attorney](/go/llc-attorney/) may be a better fit.
