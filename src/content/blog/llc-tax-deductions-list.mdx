Every dollar your LLC spends to earn money is potentially a dollar the IRS doesn't tax. That sounds simple, but most first-year owners leave real money on the table: they forget the formation costs they paid before they had a business bank account, skip the home office deduction because they've heard it "triggers audits," or never learn that health insurance premiums come off the top of their return.

This LLC tax deductions list covers the write-offs that matter most in 2026, grouped by category, with the dollar limits, the IRS form each one lands on, and the mistakes I see most often. It even covers the cost of forming the LLC itself. If you set yours up through [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/), whose Starter plan costs $0 plus your state filing fee, that state fee and any paid add-ons count as deductible start-up and organizational costs.

One quick ground rule before the list: an LLC is a legal entity, not a tax category. How your deductions are reported depends on how the LLC is taxed. A single-member LLC reports on Schedule C, a multi-member LLC files Form 1065, and an LLC that elected S-corp status files Form 1120-S. If you're not sure which applies to you, start with our guide on [how LLCs are taxed](/blog/llc-taxes-how-are-llcs-taxed/).

## The Test Every LLC Deduction Must Pass

Under Internal Revenue Code Section 162, a business expense is deductible if it is **ordinary** (common and accepted in your industry) and **necessary** (helpful and appropriate for your business). It doesn't have to be indispensable. A $40/month design software subscription is necessary for a graphic designer even though they could technically draw by hand.

Three practical consequences follow:

- **Mixed-use items get split.** If your phone is 60% business, you deduct 60% of the bill. Not 100%.
- **Documentation is the deduction.** The IRS can disallow an expense you can't substantiate. Keep receipts, invoices, and a short note on the business purpose. For meals, travel, and vehicles, the standards in [IRS Publication 463](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463) are stricter than for ordinary purchases.
- **Capital purchases are different.** Equipment, vehicles, and property that last more than a year are normally depreciated over time, though Section 179 and bonus depreciation often let you deduct them immediately (more on that below).

In my experience, the owners who get into trouble aren't the ones claiming aggressive deductions. They're the ones running personal and business spending through the same checking account and trying to reconstruct it in March. Opening a separate business account on day one does more for your deductions than any single tax trick.

## LLC Tax Deductions List: Startup and Formation Costs

These are the deductions most new owners never claim, because the money was spent before the business "officially" started.

**1. Start-up costs.** Market research, pre-opening advertising, training, and travel to line up suppliers or customers before you open. You can deduct up to **$5,000** in your first year. That allowance shrinks dollar-for-dollar once total start-up costs pass $50,000, and the remainder is amortized over 180 months.

**2. Organizational costs.** State filing fees, operating agreement drafting, and legal fees to form the entity get their own *separate* $5,000 first-year deduction under the same phase-out rules. For a typical LLC, the state fee runs from about $35 to $500 (see [how much an LLC costs](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/)), so most owners deduct the whole amount in year one.

**3. Formation service fees.** What you paid ZenBusiness, [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/), or another provider counts as an organizational cost. ZenBusiness's Starter plan is $0 plus state fees, with paid tiers adding things like EIN filing and an operating agreement. LegalZoom's paid tiers similarly bundle EIN and compliance tools. Either way, the fee is deductible. Our [ZenBusiness vs LegalZoom comparison](/compare/zenbusiness-vs-legalzoom/) breaks down what each tier includes at the time of writing.

**4. Registered agent fees.** Annual registered agent service, typically $99–$300 per year, is an ordinary operating expense. If privacy is your priority, [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) is a common choice at $125/year.

**5. Annual report fees and state franchise taxes.** State-level LLC fees are deductible business expenses. That includes California's $800 minimum franchise tax, Delaware's $300 annual tax, and the annual report fees most states charge. Federal income tax itself is not deductible.

## Operating Expenses: The Everyday LLC Tax Write-Offs

This is the bulk of most owners' small business tax deductions. On Schedule C, most of these have their own line.

**6. Advertising and marketing.** Google and Meta ads, website hosting, domain names, business cards, sponsorships, and promotional printing.

**7. Software and subscriptions.** Accounting software, CRM, project management, design tools, cloud storage, and industry databases.

**8. Office supplies.** Paper, ink, postage, and small items consumed within the year.

**9. Rent and leases.** Office or warehouse rent, coworking memberships, and equipment leases.

**10. Utilities, phone, and internet.** Fully deductible for a separate business location. Deduct the business-use percentage for a home or personal line.

**11. Business insurance.** General liability, professional liability (E&O), commercial auto, cyber, and workers' compensation premiums.

**12. Professional fees.** CPAs, bookkeepers, attorneys, and consultants. Tax prep fees for the business portion of your return are deductible here.

**13. Bank and merchant fees.** Monthly account fees, wire fees, Stripe or Square processing charges, and PayPal fees.

**14. Business loan interest.** Interest on business loans, lines of credit, and business credit cards. The principal is never deductible.

**15. Contract labor.** Payments to freelancers and independent contractors. Starting with payments made in 2026, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act raised the Form 1099-NEC reporting threshold from $600 to **$2,000**, but the payment is deductible either way.

**16. Wages and payroll taxes.** Employee salaries, the employer half of FICA, and federal and state unemployment taxes. For S-corp LLCs, this includes the owner's own W-2 salary. See [how to pay yourself from an LLC](/blog/how-to-pay-yourself-from-an-llc/).

**17. Education and training.** Courses, certifications, books, and conferences that maintain or improve skills in your *current* business. Education that qualifies you for a new trade doesn't count.

**18. Licenses and permits.** Professional licenses, local business licenses, and industry permits.

**19. Bad debts.** If you use accrual accounting and recorded income you never collected, you can write it off. Cash-basis businesses generally can't, since the income was never reported.

**20. Business gifts.** Limited to **$25 per recipient per year**, a figure unchanged since 1962.

## Home Office, Vehicle, and Travel Deductions

These three categories generate the biggest savings for solo owners, and the most IRS scrutiny. Get the documentation right.

### 21. Home office deduction

To qualify, part of your home must be used **regularly and exclusively** for business, and it must be your principal place of business. A guest room you also use for visitors doesn't qualify. A dedicated spare room with nothing but your desk does. You have two methods, both explained in [IRS Publication 587](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p587):

| Method | How it works | Typical result |
|---|---|---|
| Simplified | $5 per square foot, up to 300 sq ft | Max $1,500, no depreciation recapture |
| Regular (actual expense) | Business % of mortgage interest or rent, utilities, insurance, repairs, depreciation | Often $3,000–$8,000+ for higher-cost homes |

Example: a 250 sq ft office in a 2,000 sq ft home is 12.5% business use. With $30,000 in annual rent and utilities, the regular method yields $3,750, compared with $1,250 using the simplified method.

### 22. Vehicle expenses

Choose either the **standard mileage rate** or **actual expenses** (gas, insurance, repairs, depreciation, times your business-use percentage). The IRS set the 2026 standard mileage rate at 72.5 cents per mile, up from 70 cents in 2025. Check the [IRS standard mileage rates page](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/standard-mileage-rates) for the current figure. At 72.5 cents, 12,000 business miles is an $8,700 deduction.

Commuting from your home to a regular office is never deductible. Driving from a qualifying home office to a client site is.

### 23. Business travel

When you travel away from your tax home overnight for business, airfare, hotels, rental cars, rideshares, baggage fees, and Wi-Fi are 100% deductible. Meals while traveling are 50% deductible.

### 24. Business meals

Meals with clients, prospects, or business partners are **50% deductible** when there's a clear business purpose and you or an employee are present. Entertainment, including concert tickets, golf, and sporting events, has been 0% deductible since 2018. Under the 2025 tax law, meals provided for employees' convenience on business premises become fully nondeductible starting in 2026, so check with your CPA if you provide in-office meals.

## Big-Ticket Deductions: Equipment, QBI, Health, and Retirement

These items can each be worth thousands of dollars, and several were reshaped by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act signed in July 2025.

**25. Section 179 deduction.** Instead of depreciating equipment over 5–7 years, the Section 179 deduction lets you expense qualifying property (computers, machinery, furniture, certain vehicles, off-the-shelf software) in the year you place it in service. The 2025 law raised the limit to **$2.5 million**, with a phase-out starting at $4 million in purchases, both indexed for inflation in 2026. Section 179 can't create a loss: the deduction is limited to your business income. Heavy SUVs over 6,000 lbs are capped at roughly $31,000 under Section 179.

**26. Bonus depreciation.** The same law permanently restored **100% bonus depreciation** for qualifying property acquired after January 19, 2025. Unlike Section 179, bonus depreciation *can* create a net operating loss, which matters for capital-heavy first years. Rules for both are in [IRS Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946).

**27. QBI deduction (Section 199A).** Owners of pass-through LLCs may deduct up to **20% of qualified business income** on their personal return, even if they take the standard deduction. The 2025 law made the QBI deduction permanent and added a $400 minimum deduction, starting in 2026, for owners with at least $1,000 of QBI. Above roughly $200,000 of taxable income (single) or $400,000 (joint), limits based on W-2 wages and property kick in. For "specified service" businesses like law, medicine, consulting, and accounting, the deduction phases out entirely at higher incomes. Details are on the [IRS QBI page](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/qualified-business-income-deduction).

**28. Self-employed health insurance.** If your LLC is taxed as a sole proprietorship or partnership, you can deduct 100% of medical, dental, vision, and qualified long-term care premiums for yourself, your spouse, and your dependents. This is an above-the-line deduction on Schedule 1, and you can't take it for any month you were eligible for an employer-subsidized plan. S-corp owners with more than 2% ownership must run the premiums through payroll first.

**29. Retirement contributions.** This is the largest legal tax shelter available to most LLC owners. For 2026, a **Solo 401(k)** allows $24,500 in employee deferrals (more if you're 50+) plus an employer profit-sharing contribution, up to a combined $72,000. A **SEP IRA** allows up to 25% of compensation (about 20% of net self-employment earnings for sole-prop LLCs), up to the same $72,000 cap.

**30. Half of self-employment tax.** You pay 15.3% self-employment tax on net earnings, with the Social Security portion applying up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, and you deduct half of it above the line. Our [self-employment tax guide](/blog/self-employment-tax-for-llc-owners/) shows how this interacts with the QBI deduction.

**31. Research and experimental costs.** For tax years starting in 2025, domestic R&E spending can again be deducted immediately instead of amortized over five years. That's significant relief for software and product companies.

## What's NOT on the LLC Tax Deductions List

The IRS disallows these no matter how your LLC is taxed:

- Federal income tax payments, including your quarterly estimates (see our [quarterly tax payments guide](/blog/llc-quarterly-tax-payments-guide/))
- Fines and penalties paid to any government, including parking tickets
- Political contributions and most lobbying expenses
- Commuting between home and a regular workplace
- Everyday clothing, even if you only wear it to client meetings (uniforms and protective gear are fine)
- Country club, golf club, and social club dues
- Entertainment of any kind
- Owner draws or distributions, which are not wages
- Personal expenses paid with the business card. These are a fast way to weaken your liability protection, too.

## How S-Corp Election Changes Your Deductions

If your LLC elects S-corp status, most deductions on this list still apply. A few work differently:

- **Accountable plan.** You reimburse yourself for home office, phone, and mileage under a written accountable plan. The LLC deducts the reimbursement and you receive it tax-free. Without the plan, those costs are often lost.
- **Owner salary.** Your reasonable W-2 salary and the employer payroll taxes are deductible to the S-corp. That salary is also what Solo 401(k) contributions are calculated on.
- **Health insurance.** Premiums go through payroll in Box 1 of your W-2, then you take the self-employed health insurance deduction personally.

Here's a quick example. A consultant LLC with $150,000 in profit, paying itself an $80,000 salary as an S-corp, avoids roughly $10,700 in self-employment tax on the $70,000 distributed as profit. That's before stacking a Solo 401(k) and QBI on top. Whether the election pays off depends on payroll costs and state taxes. Compare the trade-offs in [LLC vs S-Corp](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/).

## Putting the LLC Tax Deductions List to Work in 2026

A deductions list is only useful if you capture expenses as they happen. Here's the simple system I recommend:

1. **Separate everything.** Use a business bank account and a business credit card, and run nothing personal through them.
2. **Use bookkeeping software from month one.** Categorize transactions weekly, not annually.
3. **Track mileage automatically.** A phone app beats a reconstructed log every time in an audit.
4. **Snap receipts for meals and travel** and write the attendee and business purpose on each one.
5. **Review mid-year with a CPA.** Equipment purchases, retirement contributions, and S-corp timing decisions all have deadlines before December 31.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What can an LLC write off on taxes?

An LLC can write off any ordinary and necessary business expense. Common examples include formation and start-up costs, advertising, software, rent, insurance, professional fees, contractor payments, wages, business mileage, a qualifying home office, travel, 50% of business meals, and equipment through Section 179 or bonus depreciation. Owners of pass-through LLCs may also qualify for the 20% QBI deduction.

### How much can an LLC deduct in its first year?

There's no overall cap on ordinary business expenses, but start-up costs and organizational costs each have a separate $5,000 first-year deduction, phased out above $50,000. Equipment purchases can be fully expensed in year one through Section 179 (up to $2.5 million, indexed) or 100% bonus depreciation.

### Can I deduct my LLC formation fees?

Yes. State filing fees, formation service fees (like ZenBusiness or LegalZoom), and legal fees for your operating agreement are organizational costs. You can deduct up to $5,000 of them in your first year and amortize anything above that over 15 years.

### Does the home office deduction increase my audit risk?

A properly documented home office deduction isn't a red flag on its own. The IRS mainly challenges claims that fail the "regular and exclusive use" test. Take photos of the space, measure it, and keep utility bills. The simplified method ($5 per square foot, up to $1,500) has minimal recordkeeping.

### Can a single-member LLC deduct health insurance premiums?

Yes, if the LLC shows a profit and you weren't eligible for an employer-subsidized health plan (including a spouse's) during those months. Premiums for you, your spouse, and your dependents are deductible above the line on Schedule 1, which reduces your income tax but not your self-employment tax.

### What happens if I can't prove a deduction?

The IRS can disallow it, assess the additional tax, add interest, and potentially impose a 20% accuracy-related penalty. For vehicles, travel, meals, and gifts, the law requires written records of amount, date, place, and business purpose. Bank statements alone usually aren't enough.

### Is the QBI deduction available in 2026?

Yes. The 2025 tax law made the 20% QBI deduction permanent instead of letting it expire after 2025. It also widened the income phase-in ranges and added a $400 minimum deduction for owners with at least $1,000 of qualified business income.

### Do LLC deductions differ by state?

Federal deductions are the same everywhere, but states don't always conform. Some states decouple from bonus depreciation or have their own Section 179 limits. California, for example, caps Section 179 at $25,000 and doesn't allow federal bonus depreciation. Check your state's rules or ask a CPA licensed there.
