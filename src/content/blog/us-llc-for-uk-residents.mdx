## Can a UK Resident Own a US LLC?

Yes. No US state requires an LLC member to be a citizen or resident. Our broader guide on [whether a foreigner can own an LLC in the US](/blog/can-a-foreigner-own-an-llc-in-the-us/) covers the general rules. For a UK resident, the legal side is straightforward. The complexity is tax and compliance.

An LLC is a US legal structure. The IRS treats it differently depending on its ownership:

- **Single-member LLC:** By default, the IRS treats it as a "disregarded entity". For a non-US owner with no US-connected income, there is usually no US federal income tax to pay.
- **Multi-member LLC:** By default, it is taxed as a partnership and files Form 1065. Foreign partners can face withholding obligations.
- **Elected corporation:** The LLC can elect to be taxed as a corporation, which changes the filing picture entirely.

## Who Actually Benefits From a US LLC for UK Residents?

In my experience, UK founders usually want a US LLC for one of these reasons:

- **Selling to US customers.** A US entity and US bank account can make payment processing, invoicing and credibility easier.
- **Using US platforms.** Stripe, Amazon and other platforms often have simpler onboarding for US entities.
- **Holding digital assets or IP.** Some founders keep a software product or online brand in a US entity.
- **Preparing for US investors.** Investors usually prefer a Delaware C-corp, so an LLC is more often a starting point than an endpoint.

Where it often doesn't help is cost savings. I've seen too many UK freelancers form a US LLC because they read it would "avoid tax". It generally doesn't. If you live and work in the UK, HMRC can still tax the profits. A UK limited company is often simpler and cheaper for UK-based work.

## How HMRC and the IRS Treat Your LLC

This is the part that matters most. The two countries can classify the same LLC differently.

### The US side

A single-member LLC owned by a UK resident is generally a disregarded entity for US purposes. If the LLC has no US-source "effectively connected" income, such as income from a US office or US-based staff, there is typically no US federal income tax. Services performed from the UK for customers anywhere are normally treated as foreign-source. State taxes and other situations can differ, so confirm your facts with an adviser.

### The UK side

HMRC doesn't automatically treat an LLC as transparent. It assesses each LLC individually, and many are treated as companies (opaque) for UK purposes. The Supreme Court case *Anson v HMRC* (2015) concluded that a particular Delaware LLC's members were taxable on its profits as they arose. Later HMRC guidance stresses that the answer depends on the specific LLC's rights and terms. For a UK resident, this means:

- If HMRC treats the LLC as **transparent**, its profits are taxed on you personally, as sole trader-style income or partnership income.
- If HMRC treats it as **opaque**, it may be a company in UK terms. If the LLC is managed and controlled from the UK, it can be UK-resident for corporation tax, and extracting money may trigger dividend tax.

For reference, the [GOV.UK corporation tax rates page](https://www.gov.uk/corporation-tax-rates) lists the current rates. These include a 19% small profits rate and a 25% main rate, with marginal relief in between.

> **Practical point:** UK resident LLC tax is not a DIY topic. Because UK treatment depends on the LLC's terms and where it is managed, a short consultation with a UK accountant who understands US entities is money well spent. It costs far less than an HMRC enquiry.

### The UK-US tax treaty

The UK-US tax treaty can reduce double taxation, but treaty relief for hybrid entities like LLCs is technical. Don't assume it applies automatically.

## Mandatory US Filings for UK-Owned LLCs

Even when you owe no US tax, you have filing duties.

### Form 5472 and the pro forma 1120

A foreign-owned single-member LLC must file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 each year. It reports "reportable transactions" between the LLC and you, such as capital contributions and owner draws. The penalty for failing to file is $25,000 per form, per year, under IRS rules. See the [IRS Form 5472 page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5472) for the current instructions. Our walkthrough on [Form 5472 for foreign-owned single-member LLCs](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/) covers the process in more detail.

This is the single most common compliance failure I see. Many UK owners assume "no US tax" means "no US filing". It doesn't. Formation services generally don't file Form 5472 for you, so budget for a tax preparer.

### State annual reports and fees

Most states require an annual report or fee. Delaware charges a $300 annual tax, and Wyoming has a modest annual report fee. New Mexico has no annual report. Our [guide to the best state to form an LLC](/blog/best-state-to-form-an-llc/) compares the options.

### Beneficial ownership (BOI) reporting

As of the March 2025 FinCEN interim final rule, US-formed companies and US persons are exempt from beneficial ownership reporting. This rule has been in flux, so check the [FinCEN BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) for the current position before you rely on it.

## Step-by-Step: How to Open a US LLC From the UK

1. **Choose a state.** Wyoming, Delaware and New Mexico are popular with non-residents. Pick based on cost and your plans, not hype.
2. **Pick a name and check availability** with the state's business registry.
3. **Appoint a registered agent.** Every LLC needs a US address for legal notices. Our guide to [what a registered agent is](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) explains the role.
4. **File the Articles of Organization** with the state, usually online.
5. **Draft an operating agreement.** It matters even for single-member LLCs and can influence how HMRC views the entity. See our [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/).
6. **Get an EIN.** Without a US SSN, you apply using Form SS-4 by fax, mail or phone. Our guide to [getting an EIN without an SSN](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-without-an-ssn/) walks through it.
7. **Open a bank account.** Some US banks and fintechs onboard non-residents remotely. See [opening a US bank account for a foreign-owned LLC](/blog/us-bank-account-for-a-foreign-owned-llc/).
8. **Set up bookkeeping** and keep personal and business money separate.

The EIN is often the slowest step. Plan for several weeks if the IRS is processing by fax or mail.

## Common Mistakes UK Owners Make

- **Assuming zero tax everywhere.** The LLC may owe nothing in the US but you may owe tax in the UK.
- **Skipping Form 5472.** The penalty is steep and easy to avoid.
- **Mixing personal and business funds.** It weakens liability protection and creates reporting problems.
- **Managing the LLC entirely from the UK without advice.** Central management and control can affect UK company residence.
- **Forgetting UK self-assessment.** If HMRC treats you as taxable on the profits, they belong on your return.
- **Choosing a state for the wrong reasons.** A cheap state isn't cheap if you add an expensive annual fee later.

## Is a US LLC Right for You?

A US LLC makes sense if you have genuine US customers, need a US entity for platforms or payments, and are willing to run proper compliance. If you only serve UK customers, a UK limited company is usually simpler. If you're planning to raise US venture funding, speak to a lawyer about a Delaware C-corp from the start.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a UK resident open a US LLC?

Yes. US states don't require LLC members to be US citizens or residents. You'll need a registered agent, an EIN and a US bank account, but not a US visa.

### Do I pay tax in the UK on a US LLC?

Often yes. HMRC assesses LLCs case by case, and your profits may be taxable in the UK either personally or through the LLC if it's treated as a company. A UK accountant can confirm your position.

### Does a US LLC avoid UK tax?

No. Being UK resident generally means you're taxed on worldwide income. A US LLC does not by itself remove UK tax obligations.

### Do I need a Social Security number for a US LLC?

No. You can apply for an EIN with Form SS-4 using your UK passport and address, though the process is slower than for US applicants.

### What happens if I don't file Form 5472?

The IRS can charge a $25,000 penalty per form, per year, and further penalties if the failure continues after notice.

### How much does a US LLC cost for a UK resident?

Expect roughly $150–$500 in first-year formation and registered agent costs, plus annual state fees and tax preparation. Costs vary by state and provider.

### Can I open a US bank account from the UK?

Sometimes. Some fintechs and banks support remote onboarding for non-residents, while traditional banks often require an in-person visit. Requirements change, so check each provider.

### Do I need to file a BOI report in 2026?

Under the March 2025 FinCEN rule, US-formed companies are currently exempt. Rules have shifted repeatedly, so verify on FinCEN's website before relying on this.
