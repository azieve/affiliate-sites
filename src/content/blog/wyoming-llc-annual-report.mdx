Wyoming earns its reputation as one of the most business-friendly states in the country. But one question consistently trips up new LLC owners: does Wyoming actually require an annual report?

## Does Wyoming Require an Annual Report for LLCs?

Yes — Wyoming LLCs are required to file an annual report with the Wyoming Secretary of State each year to maintain active status. This surprises some people, because Wyoming is often lumped in with states like New Mexico (which has no annual report requirement at all). Wyoming does require one, but it's structured to be minimally burdensome.

The Wyoming annual report is sometimes called the **Annual License Tax**, because it functions as both a state license fee and an informational update. What it does *not* require is financial disclosure. You're not submitting revenue figures, profit and loss statements, or tax returns. The filing simply confirms basic identifying information about the LLC — your current registered agent, principal office address, and the names and addresses of members or managers.

This minimal disclosure is intentional. Wyoming has long positioned itself as a privacy-first state for business formation, and the annual report reflects that philosophy. You'll see the same approach throughout the [formation process itself](/blog/wyoming-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/), where Wyoming charges just $100 to file Articles of Organization and maintains far less public disclosure than states like California or New York.

## The Wyoming Annual License Tax: What You'll Actually Pay

The fee structure for the Wyoming LLC annual report in 2026 is straightforward:

**For most LLCs:** The minimum fee is **$62**.

**For larger LLCs:** If your Wyoming-based assets exceed **$310,000**, the fee is calculated as **$0.0002 × total Wyoming assets**. An LLC with $500,000 in Wyoming assets, for example, would owe $100 ($500,000 × 0.0002). An LLC with $2 million in Wyoming assets would owe $400.

For the vast majority of small business owners — solopreneurs, consultants, freelancers, e-commerce operators, and early-stage companies — the flat $62 minimum applies. This is among the lowest annual compliance fees in the country.

For comparison: [Delaware charges a flat $300 annual LLC tax](/blog/delaware-llc-annual-franchise-tax/), nearly five times Wyoming's minimum. California imposes an $800 minimum franchise tax annually regardless of income. Nevada, another state frequently marketed alongside Wyoming as "business-friendly," requires an annual list filing plus a state business license — combined costs typically run $350 or more.

**Late fee:** If you miss your due date, Wyoming adds a **$25 penalty** to whatever filing fee applies. So the minimum out-of-pocket for a late filing is $87.

In my experience advising small business owners, the asset-based fee calculation is the one that occasionally bites people. Most clients with a simple consulting LLC or online business have minimal Wyoming-based assets, so $62 is accurate. But if you're holding Wyoming real estate, equipment, or inventory inside the LLC, run the math before your due date. Underpaying — even accidentally — can cause your filing to be rejected or flagged.

## When Is the Wyoming LLC Annual Report Due?

Wyoming uses an **anniversary-based due date** rather than a uniform statewide deadline. Your annual report is due on the **first day of the anniversary month** of your LLC's formation — meaning the due date is specific to your LLC, not a shared April 15 or December 31 deadline.

**How it works:**

| Formation Date | First Annual Report Due | Second Annual Report Due |
|---|---|---|
| March 15, 2024 | March 1, 2025 | March 1, 2026 |
| July 8, 2024 | July 1, 2025 | July 1, 2026 |
| November 2, 2023 | November 1, 2024 | November 1, 2025 |

The rolling deadline system means there's no statewide filing rush, but it also means you can't rely on a universal reminder. You need to know your specific date.

The Wyoming Secretary of State typically sends a notice to your registered agent approximately **60 days before your report is due**. If you're using a registered agent service — which is required in Wyoming — make sure their contact information on file with the state is current. If your registered agent has a different address than what the state has on record, that 60-day reminder could end up nowhere.

## How to File the Wyoming LLC Annual Report: Step-by-Step

Filing online through the Wyoming Secretary of State's portal is fast, straightforward, and the method the state actively encourages. Here's exactly how it works:

**Step 1: Access the Wyoming Business Center**
Go to [wyobiz.wyo.gov](https://wyobiz.wyo.gov), the Wyoming Secretary of State's official online business filing platform.

**Step 2: Locate Your LLC**
Use the business search function to find your LLC by name or filing ID number. Click through to your entity's profile page.

**Step 3: Select the Annual Report Filing Option**
From your LLC's dashboard, select "File Annual Report." This option becomes available within the filing window before your due date. If you're filing after your due date, the late fee will be automatically calculated and added.

**Step 4: Review and Update Your Information**
You'll be prompted to confirm or correct:
- Registered agent name and Wyoming street address
- Principal office address (can be out of state)
- Names and addresses of all members (for member-managed LLCs) or managers (for manager-managed LLCs)

Make any necessary updates — this is the point where many LLCs also update addresses following a move or registered agent change.

**Step 5: Calculate and Pay the Filing Fee**
The system defaults to the $62 minimum. If your LLC's Wyoming assets exceed $310,000, input your asset value and the system will calculate the correct fee. Payment is by credit or debit card.

**Step 6: Confirm and Save Your Filing**
Once payment processes, you receive a confirmation number and can download a filing receipt. You can also request a Certificate of Good Standing from the same portal immediately after filing, which is useful if you need it for a bank account application or business contract.

**Filing by mail** is technically an option, but the online system provides instant confirmation and is significantly faster. There's no meaningful reason to use mail filing in 2026.

## What Happens If You Miss the Wyoming Annual Report Deadline?

Missing the deadline isn't the end of the world, but the consequences escalate meaningfully the longer the lapse continues.

**Immediately after the deadline:**
- A **$25 late fee** is added to your standard $62 filing fee — minimum cost to get current becomes $87.
- Your LLC's status with the state shifts out of "good standing."

**In the weeks and months following:**
- Your LLC appears as "delinquent" in the Secretary of State's public records. This can surface in business background checks, credit reviews, and due diligence conducted by banks, investors, or potential partners.
- You can no longer obtain a **Certificate of Good Standing** until you file and pay all outstanding fees. These certificates are frequently required for business bank account opening, SBA loans, commercial leases, and contracts with larger clients.

**If non-compliance continues:**
- Wyoming can **administratively dissolve** your LLC. Once dissolved, the entity loses its legal existence — and with it, your limited liability protection. Debts, lawsuits, or obligations incurred after dissolution can expose you personally.

Reinstatement after dissolution is possible in Wyoming, but it involves additional fees and processing time. The practical lesson: a $62 annual filing is an extraordinarily cheap way to maintain legal protection. I've seen too many business owners let good standing lapse because the reminder got lost in a spam folder or went to an old registered agent address — and then discover the problem at the worst possible moment, usually when they're trying to close financing.

## Wyoming LLC Compliance Beyond the Annual Report

The Wyoming LLC annual report is the headline requirement, but it's not the only compliance task Wyoming LLCs need to manage. Here's a complete checklist for 2026:

**Maintain a Wyoming Registered Agent**
Every Wyoming LLC must continuously have a registered agent with a physical street address in Wyoming (no P.O. boxes). Your registered agent receives legal process, state correspondence, and annual report reminders. If you don't have a Wyoming address, you'll need a commercial registered agent service. Most cost $50–$150 per year.

**Keep Registered Agent Information Current**
If you change registered agents, file a Statement of Change of Registered Agent with the Secretary of State (fee: $5). An outdated registered agent address is one of the most common reasons LLCs miss their annual report deadlines.

**Maintain an Operating Agreement**
Wyoming doesn't require you to file an operating agreement, but having one on record internally is essential for legal protection and internal governance. Our [LLC Operating Agreement Guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) walks through what should be included.

**Separate Business and Personal Finances**
Keep a dedicated business checking account and credit card. Commingling funds is one of the most common ways courts pierce the corporate veil and hold LLC owners personally liable for business debts — negating the entire reason you formed an LLC.

**Federal Tax Compliance**
Wyoming has no state income tax, which is a meaningful advantage. But federal obligations still apply. Single-member LLCs are disregarded entities for federal tax purposes by default (reported on Schedule C); multi-member LLCs are taxed as partnerships. Both can elect S-Corp or C-Corp treatment. Per [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc), the default classification applies unless you affirmatively elect otherwise using Form 8832 or Form 2553. Consult a tax professional to determine the optimal federal tax classification for your situation.

**Local and Industry Licenses**
Wyoming-level compliance doesn't cover local business licenses or industry-specific permits. Depending on your business type and operating location, additional licenses may be required through city, county, or federal agencies.

## Frequently Asked Questions About the Wyoming LLC Annual Report

**Does Wyoming require an annual report for LLCs?**
Yes. Wyoming LLCs must file an annual report — also called the Annual License Tax — with the Wyoming Secretary of State each year to maintain active status. The minimum fee is $62, and the filing is due on the first day of your LLC's anniversary month.

**How much does the Wyoming LLC annual report cost in 2026?**
The minimum fee is $62. If your LLC holds more than $310,000 in Wyoming-based assets, the fee increases to $0.0002 times your total Wyoming assets. A $25 late fee applies if you miss the filing deadline, bringing the minimum late-filing cost to $87.

**When is the Wyoming LLC annual report due?**
Your annual report is due on the first day of the anniversary month of your LLC's formation. If your LLC was formed on September 10, your annual report is due by September 1 each year. This is an LLC-specific due date, not a shared statewide deadline.

**Can I file the Wyoming LLC annual report online?**
Yes. The Wyoming Secretary of State's online portal at wyobiz.wyo.gov accepts online filings. The process takes approximately 5–10 minutes and requires payment by credit or debit card. Online filing is the recommended method.

**What happens if I miss the Wyoming annual report deadline?**
Missing the deadline triggers a $25 late fee added to the standard filing amount. Continued non-compliance results in your LLC losing good standing, being unable to obtain a Certificate of Good Standing, and eventually facing administrative dissolution — which eliminates your liability protection.

**Do I need a registered agent to maintain my Wyoming LLC?**
Yes. Wyoming law requires all LLCs to maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in Wyoming at all times. Your registered agent receives your annual report reminder notice roughly 60 days before the due date.

**Is the Wyoming annual report the same as a franchise tax?**
They serve similar functions but aren't identical. Wyoming calls it an Annual Report and Annual License Tax. Unlike Delaware's franchise tax — which is purely a state revenue mechanism — Wyoming's annual report also requires confirmation of your entity information on file with the Secretary of State.

**Can a formation service file the Wyoming annual report for me?**
Yes. Services like [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) and [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) offer annual report filing as an add-on, typically for $100–$150 plus the state fee. For Wyoming's simple one-page filing, most business owners can handle it themselves — but using a service for compliance monitoring (so you don't forget the date) is where the real value lies.
