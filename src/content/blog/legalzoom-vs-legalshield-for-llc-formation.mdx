If you're searching for **LegalZoom vs LegalShield for LLC formation**, the first thing to know is that these two companies aren't direct competitors. LegalZoom is a document-filing and formation service. LegalShield is a membership-style legal plan that gives you access to a law firm network for advice and document review. Choosing between them is less about which is "better" and more about whether you need a filing service, a lawyer on call, or both.

I've spent years helping small business owners get their entities set up, and this is one of the more common mix-ups I see. People assume that a company with "Legal" in its name will file their Articles of Organization. This guide explains what each one does, what you'd pay, and where a dedicated formation service such as [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) (our top pick, with packages that start at $0 plus state fees) usually makes more sense in 2026.

## The Short Answer: Filing Service vs. Legal Membership

Here's the quick version of **LegalZoom vs LegalShield for LLC formation**:

- **[LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/)** will prepare and file your LLC paperwork with the state, and it offers add-ons such as a registered agent, an operating agreement, and an EIN. You pay a package fee plus your state's filing fee.
- **LegalShield** is a monthly or annual membership that gives you attorney consultations and document review. As far as I can tell, LegalShield does not sell a standard "we file your LLC" product the way a formation company does, so you would typically use the attorney access to get guidance and then file yourself or hire someone else.
- **[ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/)** is the option I'd point most first-time owners to. It handles state filing, offers a clear tiered package structure, and bundles compliance help. See our [ZenBusiness review](/reviews/zenbusiness/) for the full breakdown.

If your only goal is to get an LLC formed correctly and quickly, you want a formation service. If you expect ongoing legal questions (contracts, disputes, lease reviews), a legal plan can be useful on top of that.

## What LegalZoom Actually Does for LLC Formation

LegalZoom has been around since 2001 and went public on Nasdaq in 2021 (ticker LZ), which means its financials and business model are open to scrutiny in a way that smaller competitors' aren't. For LLC formation, it works like this: you answer an online questionnaire, LegalZoom prepares your Articles of Organization, and it submits them to your state's Secretary of State.

### LegalZoom LLC pricing tiers

LegalZoom has generally offered three formation tiers. Approximate pricing as of 2026 (verify before buying):

- **Basic:** about $0 in service fees, plus the state filing fee. You get the filing itself and little else.
- **Standard:** roughly $249 plus state fees. Usually adds an operating agreement, an EIN service, and the first year of registered agent service.
- **Premium:** roughly $299 or more plus state fees. Typically adds extras such as a business license report and additional compliance tools.

The gotchas matter more than the headline price:

1. **State fees are separate.** Filing fees range from about $35 in Kentucky to $500 in Massachusetts, per each state's Secretary of State. Our [guide to LLC costs](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/) covers the range.
2. **Registered agent renewals.** After the first year, registered agent service renews at a separate price, often well above what the budget alternatives charge.
3. **Upsells.** The checkout flow pushes extras like an EIN filing, even though you can get an EIN directly from the IRS for free.

For a deeper look, read our [LegalZoom review](/reviews/legalzoom/) or the [LegalZoom review for 2026](/blog/legalzoom-review-2026/).

## What LegalShield Offers (and What It Doesn't)

LegalShield sells prepaid legal plans, and it's been doing so for decades. Individuals and small businesses pay a recurring fee, and in return they get access to a network of provider law firms for things like:

- Phone or email consultations on business questions
- Contract and document review
- Letters written on your behalf in some cases
- Discounted rates (rather than free service) for matters outside the plan's scope

Small business plans have typically been priced in the range of tens of dollars per month, with higher tiers costing more. The exact scope varies by plan and state, and I'd read the plan's terms closely, because "covered" and "discounted" are very different things in legal services.

### Where LegalShield fits in an LLC setup

In my experience, the value of a plan like this shows up *after* formation. Say you've formed your LLC and a client sends you a 12-page services agreement. Having an attorney review it for a flat monthly membership can be worth it. But for the formation itself, you'd still need to file your Articles of Organization with the state, whether yourself or through a service. I'd confirm with LegalShield directly whether formation filing is included for your state before signing up, since offerings can change.

That's the practical core of **LegalZoom vs LegalShield for LLC formation**: one files paperwork, the other provides access to legal advice.

## What You Still Need After Formation (Whichever You Pick)

No matter how you form, a few items remain on your plate in 2026:

1. **Operating agreement.** Even single-member LLCs benefit from one. See our [operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/).
2. **EIN.** Free directly from the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). Don't pay a service for what takes about ten minutes.
3. **Business bank account.** Keep finances separate to protect your liability shield.
4. **State annual reports and franchise taxes.** These vary widely, so check your state's requirements.
5. **Beneficial ownership reporting.** FinCEN's rules for the Corporate Transparency Act have changed repeatedly, and domestic companies were largely exempted in 2025 under an interim final rule. Check [FinCEN's BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) for the current status, and see our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/).

The U.S. Small Business Administration also offers a helpful overview of [business structures and licensing](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure) if you're still deciding whether an LLC is the right entity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does LegalShield form LLCs?

LegalShield is primarily a prepaid legal membership, not a dedicated formation filer. Its attorney network can advise on forming an LLC, but you should confirm directly whether it will file the paperwork for you in your state.

### Is LegalZoom or LegalShield cheaper for starting an LLC?

For the formation itself, LegalZoom's Basic tier has a $0 service fee plus state costs, whereas LegalShield charges a recurring membership. Over a full year, the total depends on how much attorney access you use.

### Is LegalZoom worth it for an LLC in 2026?

It can be if you want a recognized brand and bundled documents, but it's often pricier than alternatives like [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) once add-ons and registered agent renewals are included.

### Do I need a lawyer to form an LLC?

No. Most states let you file Articles of Organization yourself. A lawyer becomes more valuable for complex ownership structures, multiple members, or regulated industries. Consult a qualified attorney for advice specific to your situation.

### Can I use LegalShield and LegalZoom together?

Yes. Some owners form with a filing service and then keep a legal plan for contract reviews and general questions.

### What's the difference between a formation service and a legal plan?

A formation service prepares and files state paperwork. A legal plan gives you access to attorneys for advice. They solve different problems.

### Do I need a registered agent for my LLC?

Every state requires one. You can serve as your own, or use a service. See [what a registered agent is](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) for details.
