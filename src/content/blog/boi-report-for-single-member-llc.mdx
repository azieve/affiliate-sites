If you own a one-person LLC, you have probably seen conflicting advice about the BOI report for a single member LLC. Some sites still warn of $591-per-day penalties. Others say the rule is dead. The short answer for 2026 is that **an LLC formed in any U.S. state or territory no longer has to file a BOI report with FinCEN**, and that includes single-member LLCs. A narrow group of foreign-formed companies registered in the U.S. still has to file.

This guide explains what changed, why it happened, and how to check where your own company stands. It also covers what you should still do to keep a single-member LLC compliant. If you are still forming your company, a service like [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) (which typically starts at $0 plus state filing fees) or [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) can handle the state paperwork. Neither requires you to file a BOI report today for a U.S.-formed LLC.

## What Changed: FinCEN's March 2025 Interim Final Rule

The Corporate Transparency Act (CTA) took effect in 2024. It required most small companies to report their "beneficial owners" (the people who own or control them) to the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN). Single-member LLCs were squarely in scope. The owner was usually the only beneficial owner and often the "company applicant" as well.

Then the rules changed. In March 2025, FinCEN issued an interim final rule, which you can read on the [FinCEN beneficial ownership information page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) and in the Federal Register. The rule did three main things:

- It redefined "reporting company" to mean only entities formed under the law of a **foreign country** that have registered to do business in a U.S. state or tribal jurisdiction.
- It exempted entities created in the United States (including every LLC formed by filing articles of organization with a U.S. state) from BOI reporting.
- It exempted foreign reporting companies from reporting any U.S. person as a beneficial owner, and it exempted U.S. persons from having to provide their information to a foreign reporting company.

The Corporate Transparency Act itself is still a federal statute. What changed is how FinCEN's regulation applies it. That distinction matters, and it is why I recommend a quick check of FinCEN's site before you rely on any article, including this one. ## Does a Single Member LLC Need a BOI Report in 2026?

Under the current rule, the answer for most people is no. Here is how the BOI reporting requirement for 2026 plays out for common single-member setups:

| Your situation | BOI report required? |
|---|---|
| Single-member LLC formed in Texas, Florida, Wyoming, or any U.S. state, owned by a U.S. citizen | No |
| Single-member LLC formed in a U.S. state, owned by a nonresident foreign national | No. The LLC is domestic, so it is not a reporting company |
| Single-member LLC formed in Delaware as a holding vehicle for another business | No |
| Company formed under foreign law (for example, a UK or Canadian entity) that registered to do business in a U.S. state | Yes, as a foreign reporting company, with limits described below |

One point trips people up. The trigger is **where the company was formed**, not who owns it. A Delaware LLC owned by someone living abroad is still a domestic company, so the "single member LLC BOI filing" obligation does not apply to it. If you are a non-U.S. founder, our guide on [whether a foreigner can own an LLC in the US](/blog/can-a-foreigner-own-an-llc-in-the-us/) covers the tax and banking side, which is where the real work sits.

### Why the old advice is still all over the internet

In 2024 and early 2025, the deadlines were extended, enjoined by courts, and reinstated more than once. Most of the articles ranking on Google were written during that period and never updated. If a page tells you a single-member LLC owes $591 per day for skipping a BOI report, check its publish date. Treasury also announced in early 2025 that it would not enforce penalties against U.S. citizens or domestic reporting companies, and the interim final rule then removed the obligation for domestic entities.

I have seen too many small business owners pay a filing service $100 to $250 to submit a report that, under today's rules, was never required. If you filed earlier, that report did no harm. You just do not need to update or refile it.

For a full list of exemption categories from the original rule, see [who is exempt from BOI reporting](/blog/who-is-exempt-from-boi-reporting/). That article covers the categories in depth, so I will not repeat them here.

## When a Foreign-Registered LLC Still Files

The remaining group is entities formed outside the U.S. that register to do business here. Examples include a company incorporated in Canada, the UK, or the Cayman Islands that files a foreign qualification in Delaware, New York, or Texas so it can operate locally.

If that describes you, there are three things to know:

1. **You are a foreign reporting company.** You must report the beneficial owners, but not any U.S. persons who own or control you.
2. **Deadlines depend on the registration date.** Under the interim rule, companies registered before March 26, 2025 had until April 25, 2025. Companies registering on or after that date have 30 calendar days from when their registration becomes effective. Confirm the exact timing on FinCEN's site, since the agency has said it plans to finalize the rule.
3. **Updates are required.** If reported information changes, an updated report is due within 30 days. Corrections apply to reports that contained errors.

Say a single-member entity formed in the UK registers in Florida in October 2026. Its sole owner is a UK resident. That company would likely need to file within 30 days of the Florida registration becoming effective, listing the owner. If the same owner instead formed a Florida LLC directly, no BOI filing would be needed.

It is worth asking why the structure is foreign at all. A U.S.-formed LLC is often simpler and cheaper to run. That is a question for a cross-border tax professional, but I would ask it before locking in a foreign entity for a business that will mostly operate in the U.S.

## What a Compliant Filing Contains (If You Are a Foreign Reporting Company)

The information collected is much like what the original rule required. For a single-owner company, expect to gather:

- The company's legal name, any trade names, and its U.S. address
- Its jurisdiction of formation and the state where it registered
- Its tax identification number, or a foreign tax ID and issuing country if it has no U.S. EIN
- For each beneficial owner: full legal name, date of birth, residential address, and an acceptable ID number with an image of the document

Filing goes through FinCEN's secure BOI portal, and there is no filing fee. ## What Single-Member LLC Owners Should Do Instead

Skipping the BOI report does not mean you can ignore compliance. A one-person LLC still has several obligations that carry real penalties. From a CFO's perspective, these matter more than the BOI question ever did:

- **State annual or biennial reports.** Many states require them, and missing them can lead to administrative dissolution. Our guide to [what happens if you don't renew your LLC](/blog/what-happens-if-you-don-t-renew-your-llc/) explains the consequences.
- **Franchise and annual taxes.** Delaware charges a flat $300 LLC tax due June 1, and California charges an $800 minimum franchise tax. Other states have their own rules.
- **Federal and state tax filings.** By default, a single-member LLC is a "disregarded entity," reported on Schedule C of your personal return. You can elect S-corp treatment later. See [how LLCs are taxed](/blog/llc-taxes-how-are-llcs-taxed/) and [self-employment tax for LLC owners](/blog/self-employment-tax-for-llc-owners/).
- **A registered agent.** Every state requires one. If you want to keep your home address off public records, [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) is a strong privacy-focused option.
- **Separate finances.** Keep a dedicated business account. Commingling personal and business funds is the most common way single-member owners weaken their liability protection.

To be clear, none of this involves FinCEN. If you are weighing structures, [single-member vs. multi-member LLCs](/blog/single-member-llc-vs-multi-member-llc/) explains how ownership affects taxes and liability.

## Should You Keep Your BOI Records Anyway?

I think so. Even though the federal requirement is off the table for domestic LLCs, here is what I tell people: keep a simple internal record of who owns and controls the company. A one-page ownership ledger in your records folder costs nothing.

Banks still ask for ownership details under separate customer due diligence rules. Lenders and payment processors ask as well. And if the law changes again, and it has changed repeatedly, you will have the information ready. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do single-member LLCs need to file a BOI report in 2026?

No, if the LLC was formed in a U.S. state or territory. Under FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule, domestic entities are no longer "reporting companies." The BOI report for a single member LLC only applies if the entity was formed under foreign law and registered to do business in the U.S.

### Is there still a penalty for not filing a BOI report?

For U.S.-formed companies, there is no filing obligation, so there is nothing to be penalized for. Foreign reporting companies are still subject to the CTA's penalty provisions if they fail to file. Confirm current figures with FinCEN, since older articles quote inflation-adjusted amounts like $591 per day.

### I already filed a BOI report for my LLC. Do I need to do anything?

You do not need to file updates if your company is domestic. There is no requirement to withdraw the report. If you are a foreign reporting company, keep your information current and file updates when it changes.

### Does an LLC owned by a foreign person have to file a BOI report?

Not if the LLC was formed in a U.S. state. Ownership by a nonresident does not change that. Only the place of formation matters. Foreign owners still have tax obligations, such as Form 5472 for certain foreign-owned single-member LLCs, so talk to a tax professional.

### What is a foreign reporting company?

It is an entity formed under the law of a foreign country that has registered to do business in a U.S. state or tribal jurisdiction. These entities must still report their beneficial owners, but they do not have to report U.S. persons.

### Is the BOI rule likely to change again?

It could. FinCEN issued the March 2025 rule as an interim final rule and signaled it intends to finalize it. Congress and the courts could also act. Check the FinCEN website before making decisions based on any single article.

### Do I need to hire a service to file a BOI report?

No. FinCEN's portal is free to use. Formation providers may sell filing add-ons, but for the few companies that must file, self-filing is straightforward.

## The Bottom Line

For most solo founders in 2026, the BOI report for a single member LLC is no longer a requirement. If your LLC was formed by filing with a U.S. state, you are out of scope. If your company was formed abroad and registered in the U.S., you still have a filing to make. Either way, put your energy into the compliance items that carry real consequences: state annual reports, tax filings, a registered agent, and clean bookkeeping.
