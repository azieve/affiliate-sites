Alabama has quietly become one of the more sensible states to form a small business in, and 2026 is a genuinely good year to do it. The state has no separate annual report for LLCs, it has been phasing out the minimum Business Privilege Tax that used to nickel-and-dime small owners every year, and its online filing portal is faster than it used to be. The one thing that trips people up is that Alabama does formation slightly differently from most states — you generally reserve your business name *before* you file your formation document, and the document itself is called a **Certificate of Formation**, not the "Articles of Organization" you'll see in guides written for other states. ## Why Form an LLC in Alabama in 2026?

An LLC (limited liability company) puts a legal wall between your personal assets — your house, car, and personal savings — and your business's debts and lawsuits. If the business gets sued or can't pay a supplier, creditors generally can't come after your personal accounts. That single feature is why most Alabama sole proprietors eventually formalize into an LLC once they start signing contracts or taking on clients. If you're still deciding whether you even need one, our explainer on [what is an LLC](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) and our [LLC vs. sole proprietorship](/learn/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship/) comparison are both worth reading before you spend a dollar.

Alabama-specific advantages worth knowing in 2026:

- **No separate annual report for LLCs.** Unlike states such as California, Alabama does not make standard LLCs file a recurring annual report with the Secretary of State. That removes one deadline (and one fee) from your yearly calendar.
- **The minimum Business Privilege Tax has been phased out for small businesses.** For tax years beginning after December 31, 2023, Alabama fully exempts taxpayers whose calculated Business Privilege Tax would be $100 or less — which covers the vast majority of small single-member LLCs. Where owners used to owe a $100 annual minimum, many now owe $0. (More on this below — and yes, you should still confirm your situation with a tax professional.)
- **Moderate, flat filing cost.** At a $200 state filing fee for the Certificate of Formation, Alabama sits in the middle of the pack nationally — more than budget states like Kentucky, but far below premium-fee states like Massachusetts.

The tradeoff is Alabama's individual income tax, which tops out around 5%. Because LLC profits typically "pass through" to your personal return, that rate matters for your bottom line — this is exactly the kind of thing where a short conversation with a tax pro pays for itself, because the tax structure that works in a no-income-tax state like Texas or Florida doesn't automatically translate to Alabama.

## How to Start an LLC in Alabama: 6 Steps

Here's the full step-by-step process to start an LLC in Alabama in 2026, from reserving your name to staying compliant after formation.

### Step 1: Reserve Your Alabama LLC Name

This is the step that makes Alabama different. Before you can file your Certificate of Formation, you generally need a **Name Reservation** on file with the Alabama Secretary of State. You can do this online through the [Alabama Secretary of State's business portal](https://www.sos.alabama.gov/), and as of 2026 the online name reservation fee is **$25** (mail filings cost a few dollars more and take much longer).

Your name must be distinguishable from every other entity already registered in Alabama, and it must include one of the required designators: "Limited Liability Company," "L.L.C.," or "LLC." Run the state's free name availability search first so you don't reserve a name that's already taken. In my experience testing formation services across five states, Alabama's reserve-the-name-first sequence trips up more first-time filers than any other single step — people fill out the Certificate of Formation, hit submit, and get bounced because they skipped the reservation. Do this part first and the rest is smooth.

### Step 2: Appoint an Alabama Registered Agent

Every Alabama LLC must name a **registered agent** — a person or company with a physical street address in Alabama (not a P.O. box) who agrees to accept legal documents and state mail on the business's behalf during normal business hours. You can be your own registered agent if you have an Alabama address and don't mind your name and address appearing in the public record. Not sure what this role actually involves? Our guide on [what is a registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) breaks it down.

Most owners who work from home, travel, or care about privacy hire a commercial registered agent instead — it keeps your home address off the public filing and means you'll never miss a lawsuit notice because you were out of the office. [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) is the service I most often point privacy-conscious owners toward, since registered agent service and address privacy are the core of what they do. Most formation services bundle the first year of registered agent service into their packages.

### Step 3: File the Alabama Certificate of Formation

The Certificate of Formation is the document that legally creates your LLC. You file it with the Alabama Secretary of State, and the **filing fee is $200** for online submissions in 2026. You'll need to include:

- Your reserved LLC name (and the name reservation certificate)
- The name and Alabama street address of your registered agent
- The LLC's principal office address
- Whether the LLC is member-managed or manager-managed
- The organizer's signature

Filing online through the Secretary of State's portal is by far the fastest route — online Certificates of Formation are typically processed within a couple of business days, versus weeks for mailed paper filings. Add up the pieces and the real-world cost to form an Alabama LLC yourself in 2026 is roughly **$225** — the $200 formation fee plus the $25 name reservation. ### Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Alabama does not legally require an operating agreement, but skipping one is a mistake I see constantly. This internal document spells out who owns what percentage, how profits are split, how decisions get made, and what happens if a member leaves or the business dissolves. For single-member LLCs it reinforces the liability protection that keeps your personal assets safe by showing the business is a genuinely separate entity; for multi-member LLCs, it's the single best tool for preventing partner disputes down the road. You don't need a lawyer to draft a basic one — most formation services include a customizable template, and our [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) walks through what to include.

### Step 5: Get an EIN from the IRS

An **EIN** (Employer Identification Number) is your business's federal tax ID. You'll need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file taxes. It's completely free directly from the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) — apply online and you'll usually have the number in minutes. Be wary of sites that charge $50–$100 for an EIN; you're paying for something the IRS gives away at no cost, though most formation services will obtain it for you as part of a package so you don't have to deal with the IRS portal yourself.

### Step 6: Handle Local Licenses and the Business Privilege License

This is the Alabama-specific compliance step that catches people off guard. Separate from your state LLC filing, most Alabama businesses need a **Business Privilege License** issued by the county probate office or license commissioner in each county where you operate. Costs and requirements vary by county and by the type of business you run, so check with your local probate office. Cities such as Birmingham and Montgomery may also require a municipal business license on top of the county one. None of this replaces your LLC — it's an additional local layer, and budgeting for it up front keeps you from getting a surprise notice a few months in.

## Alabama LLC Taxes and Ongoing Compliance in 2026

Once your LLC exists, staying compliant in Alabama is refreshingly light compared to many states — but there are a few things to track:

- **Business Privilege Tax (BPT).** Alabama historically imposed an annual Business Privilege Tax with a $100 minimum. Under legislation phasing that minimum out, for tax years beginning after December 31, 2023, taxpayers whose calculated BPT is $100 or less are fully exempt and owe $0. Larger LLCs with significant net worth may still owe BPT on a graduated scale, so confirm your specific liability with the [Alabama Department of Revenue](https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/) or a tax professional. This is genuinely good news for small owners in 2026, but "exempt from the minimum" is not the same as "exempt from filing" for every entity — get the specifics for your situation.
- **Federal and state income tax.** By default, a single-member LLC is taxed as a sole proprietorship and a multi-member LLC as a partnership, with profits passing through to your personal return. Alabama's individual income tax tops out around 5%. Some LLCs elect S-corporation taxation once profits are high enough to make it worthwhile — our [LLC vs. S-corp](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) guide explains when that math starts to make sense.
- **Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI).** The federal Corporate Transparency Act once required most new LLCs to file a BOI report with FinCEN. As of 2026, FinCEN's interim final rule issued in March 2025 removed the reporting obligation for domestic U.S. companies, leaving it in place primarily for certain foreign entities. Because these rules have shifted more than once, verify the current requirement on [FinCEN's website](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) before assuming you're off the hook — our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) tracks the latest state of play.
- **Local license renewals.** Your county Business Privilege License and any municipal licenses generally renew annually. Put the renewal date on your calendar the day you get the license.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to start an LLC in Alabama?

In 2026, expect roughly **$225** to form an Alabama LLC yourself: a $200 Certificate of Formation filing fee plus a $25 name reservation fee, both paid to the Alabama Secretary of State. Using a formation service adds their fee (starting at $0 plus the state fee with providers like ZenBusiness), and you may also owe a county Business Privilege License fee that varies by location.

### Do I need to reserve my business name before forming an LLC in Alabama?

Yes — this is Alabama's distinctive quirk. You generally must file a Name Reservation with the Secretary of State (around $25 online) before your Certificate of Formation will be accepted. Most formation services handle the reservation for you automatically as part of the filing process.

### How long does it take to form an LLC in Alabama?

Online filings through the Alabama Secretary of State's portal are typically processed within a couple of business days once your name reservation is approved. Mailed paper filings can take several weeks, so filing online is strongly recommended if you want to get started quickly in 2026.

### Does Alabama require an annual report for LLCs?

No. Alabama does not require a standard annual report for LLCs the way some states do. Your main recurring state obligation is the Business Privilege Tax return — and for tax years beginning after December 31, 2023, small LLCs whose calculated tax is $100 or less are exempt from the minimum and owe $0.

### Can I be my own registered agent in Alabama?

Yes, as long as you have a physical street address in Alabama (not a P.O. box) and are available during normal business hours to receive legal documents. Many owners hire a commercial registered agent like Northwest instead to keep their home address off the public record and avoid missing important notices.

### Do I need an operating agreement for an Alabama LLC?

Alabama doesn't legally require one, but you should have one anyway. It documents ownership, profit splits, and management rules, reinforces your liability protection, and prevents disputes — especially important for multi-member LLCs. A basic template from a formation service or your own drafting is usually enough to start.

### What's the difference between an LLC and a sole proprietorship in Alabama?

A sole proprietorship offers no separation between you and your business — your personal assets are exposed to business debts and lawsuits. An LLC creates that legal separation and protects your personal assets, while still allowing pass-through taxation. For most Alabama owners generating real revenue in 2026, the modest formation cost is worth the protection.
