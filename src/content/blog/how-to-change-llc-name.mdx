## Can You Change Your LLC Name? What Actually Happens Legally

Yes. An LLC is a legal entity registered with a state, and its name is listed in the document that created it, usually called the articles of organization (or certificate of formation in states like Texas and Delaware). Changing the name means formally amending that document with the state.

Here is the point that matters most: **a name change does not create a new company.** Your LLC keeps its EIN, its formation date, its liability protection, its contracts, and its assets. You are changing what the entity is called, not what it is. That is why this process is far simpler than dissolving and re-forming.

Two things people confuse with a legal name change:

- **A DBA (assumed name).** If you only want to operate under a different brand while keeping the legal name, you can file a "doing business as" registration instead. This is often cheaper and faster. Our guide on [LLC vs DBA differences](/blog/llc-vs-dba-doing-business-as-differences/) explains when a DBA is the smarter move.
- **A change of registered agent.** Swapping your agent is a separate filing that does not touch your company name. If that's what you need, see our guide on [how to change your registered agent](/blog/how-to-change-registered-agent/).

## Step 1: Choose a New Name and Confirm It's Available

Before you touch any paperwork, make sure the new name will clear three hurdles.

**State naming rules.** Nearly every state requires the name to contain "Limited Liability Company," "L.L.C.," or "LLC," and to be distinguishable from other entities already on file. Some states restrict words like "Bank," "Insurance," or "University," and professional LLCs (PLLCs) have their own rules.

**State database search.** Search your Secretary of State's business entity database. Most states also let you reserve a name for a fee, commonly $10 to $50 for 60 to 120 days, if you need time before filing.

**Trademark and domain check.** State approval only means no one else in that state has the same entity name. It does not mean you're clear to use it. Search the [USPTO trademark database](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search) and check domain and social handle availability. In my experience, this is the check owners skip most often, and it's the one that causes the most expensive second renames. I've seen business owners pay to print signage, update everything, and then receive a cease-and-desist letter six weeks later.

## Step 2: Review Your Operating Agreement

Your [LLC operating agreement](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) may spell out who has to approve a name change. In a multi-member LLC, the safe default is a written consent or resolution signed by the members, even if the agreement is silent. If your agreement requires a specific vote (majority, unanimous), follow it. Skipping this step is how a routine rename turns into a member dispute.

Also update the operating agreement itself. It should reflect the new name in the title, the preamble, and the signature blocks, ideally through a short amendment noting the effective date of the change.

## Step 3: File the Amendment With Your State

This is the core of how to change LLC name legally. Depending on your state, the document is called:

- Articles of Amendment (Florida)
- Certificate of Amendment (Delaware, Texas, New York)
- Form LLC-2, Amendment to Articles of Organization (California)

Most states let you file online through the Secretary of State or equivalent office. Paper filing by mail is usually available but slower. Expect to provide:

- Your current LLC name and file number
- The new name, exactly as it should appear
- The effective date (often upon filing)
- A signature from an authorized member or manager

That gives you a realistic LLC name change cost range for the state filing alone: roughly $25 to $200 in these states, with some states falling outside that range. Processing time ranges from same-day or a few business days for online filings to several weeks for mailed paper filings, depending on the state's backlog. Expedite options often cost extra.

Two gotchas are worth flagging:

1. **Publication requirements.** A few states (New York is the well-known example for formation) have newspaper publication rules that can add hundreds or even thousands of dollars. Confirm whether your state applies any publication requirement to amendments before you file.
2. **Foreign registrations.** If your LLC is registered to do business in other states, you generally must file amendments there too. Each state charges its own fee. For a simple single-state rename, DIY through the state portal is usually the cheapest route. If the rename involves multiple states or a tangled ownership structure, paying for help or consulting a business attorney is reasonable.

## Step 4: Update Your IRS and Tax Records

You do **not** need a new EIN when you change your LLC's name. Per the [IRS guidance on business name changes](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/business-name-change), how you notify the IRS depends on how your LLC is taxed. In general:

- **Single-member LLCs and partnerships** notify the IRS in writing (or by marking the name change box on the next filed return, where the form provides it), signed by an authorized person.
- **LLCs taxed as corporations or S corporations** typically mark the name change box on the return and follow the IRS's instructions for the relevant form.

Tax treatment affects the details, and this is where a quick call to your CPA saves time. If you haven't yet decided how your LLC should be taxed, see our comparisons of [LLC vs S-Corp](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) and our overview of [how LLCs are taxed](/blog/llc-taxes-how-are-llcs-taxed/).

Also update:

- **State tax accounts**: sales tax permits, payroll withholding accounts, and unemployment insurance registrations
- **Payroll provider**: so W-2s and 1099s carry the correct name at year-end
- **Estimated tax payments**: make sure any 2026 quarterly payments are credited to the right entity name and EIN

A mismatch between the name on your return and the name the IRS has on file is a common cause of rejected filings and confusing notices.

## Step 5: The Post-Filing Checklist Nobody Warns You About

The state filing takes an afternoon. The cleanup takes weeks. Work through this list in roughly this order.

**1. Get your amended documents.** Order a certified copy of the approved amendment. Banks, licensing agencies, and lenders will ask for it.

**2. Update your bank accounts.** Banks generally require the certified amendment plus an updated ID and, sometimes, a new signature card. Do this before you order new checks or debit cards. Our guide to [opening an LLC bank account](/blog/llc-bank-account-how-to-open-one/) lists the documents banks typically request, and most of the same paperwork applies here.

**3. Update licenses and permits.** Local business licenses, professional licenses, health permits, and industry-specific registrations often have to be amended separately, and some cannot be transferred and must be reissued.

**4. Contracts, leases, and vendor accounts.** Existing contracts remain valid, since it is the same entity. Still, send a formal notice to counterparties with the effective date and a copy of the amendment. For key agreements, a short written acknowledgment is worth having.

**5. Insurance.** Notify your carriers so policies and certificates of insurance show the new name. A claim filed under a name that doesn't match your policy invites delays.

**6. Registered agent and state records.** Confirm the state has your correct name on the registered agent record and annual report. If you're also considering swapping providers, that's a good moment to compare options in our [best registered agent services](/blog/best-registered-agent-services-2026/) roundup.

**7. Trademarks.** If you own a registered trademark, a name change may require updating the owner record with the USPTO. If you're adopting a new brand name, consider applying for a fresh mark.

**8. Website, marketing, and legal notices.** Update your website footer, terms of service, invoices, email signatures, Google Business Profile, and any state-required disclosures.

**9. Beneficial ownership reporting.** FinCEN's rules on beneficial ownership information (BOI) have changed considerably since 2024. As of FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule, U.S.-formed companies were exempted from BOI reporting, though foreign reporting companies remain covered and rules may continue to evolve. Read our summary of [BOI report changes and updates](/blog/boi-report-changes-and-updates-2026/) and confirm the current status before assuming there is nothing to update.

## Common Mistakes When Changing an LLC Name

- **Forming a new LLC instead of amending.** This creates a second entity, potentially triggers asset transfers and tax consequences, and leaves the original LLC on the books.
- **Forgetting the "LLC" designator.** The state will reject the filing.
- **Not notifying the bank before using the new name.** Checks and payments under the new name may bounce or be held.
- **Ignoring pending annual report deadlines.** In some states, you can't amend if you're not in good standing. If your LLC has lapsed, see how to [reinstate a dissolved LLC](/blog/how-to-reinstate-a-dissolved-llc/) first.
- **Skipping member approval.** Even in a friendly LLC, get it in writing.

## When You Might Not Want to Change the Legal Name

Sometimes the better answer is a DBA. If you're testing a new brand, running multiple product lines, or just want to keep the legal name stable for contracts and loan documents, a DBA lets you trade under a new name without amending anything. You keep your existing bank accounts and licenses, and you avoid the notification burden. The tradeoff is that the legal name still appears on contracts and public records. Consider a DBA if the new name is temporary or experimental. Do a full legal name change if the brand is permanent and you want consistency everywhere.

## FAQ: People Also Ask About Changing an LLC Name

### How much does it cost to change an LLC name?

The state filing fee is typically between $25 and $200, depending on where your LLC is registered. Florida is around $25, California $30, Texas $150, and Delaware $200. Add certified copies, any expedite fees, and a service fee if you hire a filing service. Costs can be higher if you must amend in multiple states.

### How long does an LLC name change take?

Online filings can be processed in a day or a few business days in many states, while mailed paper filings may take several weeks. Downstream updates such as bank, licenses, and insurance often take an additional two to six weeks.

### Do I need a new EIN if I change my LLC name?

No. Changing the name does not create a new entity, so your existing EIN stays the same. You do need to notify the IRS of the new name using the method that applies to your tax classification.

### Can I change my LLC name without filing anything with the state?

Not the legal name. The legal name is on file with your state, so you must file an amendment. If you only want to use a different brand name publicly, you can register a DBA instead.

### Do I have to notify my customers and vendors?

You aren't legally required to send formal notices in every case, but you should. Existing contracts stay valid because it's the same entity, yet sending a written notice with the effective date and a copy of the amendment prevents payment mix-ups and confirms continuity.

### Can I change my LLC name if I have a business loan or lease?

Usually yes, but read the loan and lease terms. Many agreements require you to notify the lender or landlord of a name change and may need an amendment or acknowledgment. Your lender may also need updated UCC filing information.

### Will changing my LLC name affect my liability protection?

No. Your liability protection continues because the entity is the same. Protection depends on maintaining the LLC properly, such as keeping business and personal funds separate and staying in good standing, not on the name itself. Make sure the new name is used consistently on contracts so counterparties know who they're dealing with.

### Should I change my registered agent when I change my LLC name?

It isn't required, but a rename is a convenient time to review your provider.
