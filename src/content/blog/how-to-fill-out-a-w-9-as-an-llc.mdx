## What Is a W-9 and Why Does Your LLC Need One?

Form W-9 is the "Request for Taxpayer Identification Number and Certification." A business or individual who pays you (the "requester") uses it to collect your name, tax classification and taxpayer identification number (TIN). They use that information to prepare Form 1099-NEC, 1099-MISC or 1099-K at year-end.

Three points are worth knowing up front:

- **You never send a W-9 to the IRS.** You give it to the person or company requesting it. They keep it in their files.
- **It isn't a tax return.** It reports no income. It only tells the payer who you are for tax purposes.
- **The IRS revised the form in March 2024.** The current version adds a line 3b for flow-through entities with foreign owners. Always download the latest version from the [IRS Form W-9 page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-9) rather than reusing an old PDF.

Clients usually ask for a W-9 for LLC vendors before the first payment. Paying you without one means they may have to withhold taxes. For more on what happens after you submit it, see our guide on [whether LLCs get a 1099](/blog/do-llcs-get-a-1099/).

**A 2026 timing note:** the 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC reporting threshold was $600 for years. Recent federal legislation raised it to $2,000 for payments made after December 31, 2025. Even so, many clients still collect a W-9 from every vendor up front, so expect the request regardless of how much you'll be paid. Confirm current thresholds with the IRS or your tax professional.

## Before You Start: Know Your LLC's Tax Classification

An LLC is a state-law entity, and the IRS doesn't have a tax category called "LLC." The W-9 asks how the IRS taxes your LLC, and the answer depends on how many owners you have and whether you made an election:

| LLC situation | Default IRS treatment | What you check on line 3a |
|---|---|---|
| One owner (individual) | Disregarded entity (taxed like a sole proprietor) | Individual/sole proprietor or single-member LLC |
| One owner (another entity) | Disregarded entity | LLC box, with the classification of the owner |
| Two or more owners | Partnership | LLC box, enter **P** |
| Filed Form 8832 to be taxed as a C corp | C corporation | LLC box, enter **C** |
| Filed Form 2553 (S corp election) | S corporation | LLC box, enter **S** |

If you aren't sure how your LLC is taxed, check your IRS acceptance letters (CP261 for an S corp election, or the confirmation of a Form 8832 election). Without an election, you're taxed under the default rule. Our overview of [how LLCs are taxed](/blog/llc-taxes-how-are-llcs-taxed/) covers the options in more depth.

## How to Fill Out a W-9 as an LLC: Line by Line

Here is each line of the current form, and what to enter for the most common LLC setups.

### Line 1: Name of Entity/Individual

This line is where most mistakes happen. The rule is to enter the name **shown on your income tax return**.

- **Single-member LLC (disregarded):** enter **your own name** (the owner's), not the LLC's name.
- **Multi-member LLC (partnership):** enter the **LLC's legal name** exactly as it appears on your Articles of Organization.
- **LLC taxed as a C or S corporation:** enter the **LLC's legal name**.

Using the LLC name on line 1 for a disregarded entity is a classic error. Your 1099 will then show the LLC name next to your personal SSN, and the IRS matching system may flag it as a mismatch.

### Line 2: Business Name/Disregarded Entity Name

Use this line for any name that differs from line 1:

- **Single-member LLC:** enter the LLC's legal name here, such as "Mitchell Creative LLC."
- **A DBA or trade name:** you can add it here too if you want payments made out to it.
- **Multi-member or corporate-taxed LLC:** leave it blank unless you operate under a separate trade name.

### Line 3a: Federal Tax Classification

Check **one** box only.

- **Single-member LLC disregarded as separate from an individual owner:** check "Individual/sole proprietor." The IRS instructions treat this as the correct box for these LLCs, even though it feels odd when you're holding an LLC.
- **Any other LLC:** check the "LLC" box and enter the tax classification letter in the space provided: **C** for C corporation, **S** for S corporation, or **P** for partnership.

This is where your W-9 tax classification as an LLC gets decided, so confirm your status before you tick the box. Checking "Partnership" because you "have a business partner" doesn't work if your LLC elected S corp status. The election controls.

### Line 3b: Foreign Partners, Owners or Beneficiaries

Check this box only if you're a partnership, trust or estate that has foreign partners, owners or beneficiaries **and** you're giving the form to an entity you have an ownership interest in. Most small LLCs leave line 3b blank.

### Line 4: Exemptions

The payee code exemption has two parts, and most LLCs have little to enter:

- **Exempt payee code:** this relates to exemption from backup withholding. LLCs taxed as C or S corporations often qualify under code 5 (corporations). **Disregarded single-member LLCs and partnership-taxed LLCs generally leave it blank.**
- **FATCA reporting code:** this is rarely relevant to a domestic LLC with ordinary business income. Leave it blank unless your accountant tells you otherwise.

Even if your LLC is exempt as a corporation, certain payments, such as legal fees, are still reportable. Don't be surprised to receive a 1099 anyway.

### Lines 5 and 6: Address, City, State, ZIP

Enter the address where the requester should mail tax forms such as the 1099. It should be an address you actually monitor. If you use a registered agent address or a virtual mailbox, make sure someone can still get your mail there. A missed 1099 doesn't excuse you from reporting the income. If privacy matters, our guide to [forming an anonymous LLC](/blog/anonymous-llc-how-to-form-one/) explains how to separate your public filing address from your tax mail.

### Line 7: Account Numbers (Optional)

List account numbers only if the requester asks. Most don't, so you can usually leave it blank.

### Part I: Taxpayer Identification Number

This is the LLC EIN or SSN question, and it's easy to get wrong:

- **Single-member LLC (disregarded):** enter **your SSN, or your EIN** if you have one. The IRS tells disregarded entities to use the **owner's** TIN on the W-9. Many freelancers use their LLC's EIN instead because they don't want clients holding their Social Security number. The IRS accepts this when you've gotten an EIN for the LLC, but make sure the name on line 1 matches the TIN type you're using.
- **Multi-member LLC, or an LLC taxed as a C or S corporation:** enter the **LLC's EIN**.

If you don't have an EIN yet, you can get one free from the IRS. Our step-by-step guide on [getting an EIN for your LLC free](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-for-your-llc-free-irs-method/) walks through the online application, which usually takes under 15 minutes. Paid formation packages often add an EIN service as an upsell, which is rarely worth it on its own.

### Part II: Certification and Signature

By signing, you certify under penalties of perjury that:

1. The TIN you provided is correct.
2. You aren't subject to backup withholding (or the IRS has notified you that you're no longer subject to it).
3. You're a U.S. citizen or other U.S. person.
4. Any FATCA code you entered is correct.

Sign and date the form. For many payments to contractors the IRS technically doesn't require a signature, but nearly every requester asks for one, so sign it. Many companies also accept a secure electronic signature, as long as the system meets IRS requirements.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1: Freelance designer with a single-member LLC.** Maya owns "Brightline Studio LLC" alone and hasn't made any tax elections.

- Line 1: Maya Torres
- Line 2: Brightline Studio LLC
- Line 3a: Individual/sole proprietor
- Part I: her SSN, or the LLC's EIN if she obtained one
- Result: her 1099-NEC will list her name and TIN, and the income flows to Schedule C on her personal return.

**Example 2: Two-member consulting LLC.** Dan and Priya co-own "Northgate Advisory LLC," taxed as a partnership.

- Line 1: Northgate Advisory LLC
- Line 2: blank
- Line 3a: LLC, enter **P**
- Part I: the LLC's EIN

**Example 3: S corp-taxed LLC.** A single-member LLC that filed Form 2553 and is taxed as an S corporation.

- Line 1: the LLC's legal name
- Line 3a: LLC, enter **S**
- Line 4: possibly exempt payee code 5, as a corporation
- Part I: the LLC's EIN

## Common W-9 Mistakes LLC Owners Make

In my experience, most W-9 problems come from a handful of repeated mistakes. I've seen too many new business owners fill the form out in two minutes, send it to a client, and then spend weeks sorting out IRS notices about mismatched names and numbers. Watch for these:

1. **Entering the LLC name on line 1 for a disregarded entity.** Your own name belongs there.
2. **Checking "LLC" and leaving the classification blank.** If you check the LLC box, the letter (C, S or P) is required.
3. **Using an EIN that doesn't match the name on line 1.** The name and TIN must correspond to each other.
4. **Not updating the W-9 after a change.** A new name, address or tax election means every client needs a new form.
5. **Checking multiple boxes on line 3a.** Only one is allowed.
6. **Sending the W-9 over unsecured email.** It contains your TIN. Use a secure portal or encrypted upload whenever possible.
7. **Skipping the form entirely.** The IRS can impose a $50 penalty per failure to furnish a correct TIN, and payers must generally begin backup withholding at **24%** if you don't give them one. Check the [current IRS instructions for Form W-9](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw9) for details.

## What Happens After You Submit a W-9?

The payer keeps your W-9 on file and doesn't forward it to the IRS. After year-end, they report what they paid you on a 1099 and send you a copy by the end of January. You report that income on your tax return, with the details depending on your LLC's classification. If your income is substantial, set aside money for estimated taxes and review the rules on [self-employment tax for LLC owners](/blog/self-employment-tax-for-llc-owners/).

Banks, payment platforms and marketplaces may also ask you to confirm your TIN. If the number on file with the IRS doesn't match, they may begin backup withholding after sending a "B notice." Responding quickly with a corrected W-9 resolves it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a W-9 if I have an LLC?

If a client or platform pays you for services, they'll almost always ask for one. The W-9 is requested from you as the payee, regardless of whether you operate as an LLC, a sole proprietor or a corporation.

### Do I use my SSN or EIN on a W-9 for an LLC?

It depends on your LLC. A disregarded single-member LLC can use the owner's SSN, or an EIN if the owner has one. A multi-member LLC, or one taxed as a corporation, should use the LLC's EIN. The name on line 1 must match the type of TIN you provide.

### What do I check for a single-member LLC W-9?

Check "Individual/sole proprietor" on line 3a, enter your own name on line 1, and put the LLC's name on line 2. This applies if your single-member LLC is owned by an individual and hasn't elected corporate tax treatment.

### Which LLC tax classification do I enter if I elected S corp status?

Check the LLC box and enter **S** in the space provided. Use the LLC's legal name on line 1 and the LLC's EIN in Part I.

### Does an LLC have to fill out a W-9 for every client?

Yes, each business that pays you will usually ask for its own copy. You can reuse the same information, but each requester keeps its own signed form. Update every copy if your name, address or tax classification changes.

### What happens if I don't give a client my W-9?

The payer may be required to withhold 24% of your payments as backup withholding and send it to the IRS. The IRS can also assess penalties on you for failing to furnish a correct TIN. Providing the form promptly avoids both problems.

### Can I send a W-9 by email?

You can, but it's not the safest option because the form includes your SSN or EIN. A secure upload portal or encrypted file is better. Don't post it publicly or send it to anyone who doesn't have a legitimate reason to ask for it.

### Is the W-9 different for a foreign-owned LLC?

U.S. LLCs with foreign owners often have different requirements. A disregarded entity owned by a foreign person generally uses the LLC's EIN, and the foreign owner may need a W-8 form instead. Talk to a tax advisor familiar with cross-border rules.
