Moving your office, switching to a virtual mailbox, or relocating your home-based business all raise the same question: how do you update your LLC's address without causing a compliance headache? The short answer is that you file a change with your state, notify the IRS, and then update every bank, license, and vendor that has your old address on file. The order matters, and so do the deadlines.

This guide walks through **how to change your LLC address** in 2026, including which state form you need, what it costs, and the mistakes that cause LLCs to miss legal notices and fall out of good standing. ## Which LLC Address Are You Changing?

Before you file anything, work out which address is changing. An LLC can have several, and each one is updated in a different place.

- **Principal office address.** The main place of business. States usually collect it on your formation documents and annual report.
- **Mailing address.** Where the state and the IRS send correspondence. It can differ from your principal office.
- **Registered agent address.** The physical address where legal papers (service of process) are delivered. This is a separate filing in almost every state. Our guide to [what a registered agent is](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) explains the role in more detail.
- **Member or manager addresses.** Some states list these publicly. Others do not.

I've seen too many business owners update their mailing address with the IRS and assume the job was finished. Then a lawsuit or a state notice goes to the old registered office, and they find out weeks later. Treat each address as its own task.

## Step 1: Check Your Operating Agreement and State Rules

Start with two documents. Your [LLC operating agreement](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) may say who has authority to approve a change of principal office. In a multi-member LLC, a short written consent from the members or managers is good practice even if the agreement doesn't require it. A one-paragraph resolution in your records costs nothing.

Then look up your state's rules. Most Secretary of State websites have a page for LLC amendments or changes of address. Some states let you update the principal address through a simple online form. Others require a formal filing.

## Step 2: Change Your LLC Address With the State

This is the core of **how to change your LLC address** legally. The method depends on the state, and there are three common routes.

### Route A: Update it on your annual or periodic report

Many states let you change the principal or mailing address when you file your annual report. This is often free beyond the normal report fee. The drawback is timing. If your report isn't due for months, your public record stays wrong until then. If you're in one of these states, check whether a standalone update form is also available.

### Route B: File a standalone statement or form

Some states offer a dedicated filing. A few examples as of 2026, which you should confirm on the state website before filing:

- **California:** File an updated Statement of Information (Form LLC-12) through the Secretary of State. California generally doesn't charge a fee for an update filed outside your regular filing period. See our [California LLC cost breakdown](/blog/california-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/) for the standard fees.
- **Texas:** A change of registered office or agent uses a Form 424 filing with the Secretary of State. The fee has historically been small, around $15. Texas has no annual report, so your public address updates only when you file. Our [Texas LLC franchise tax guide](/blog/texas-llc-franchise-tax-no-annual-report/) covers the related tax reporting.
- **Florida:** The principal and mailing address can usually be updated on the annual report, which is due between January 1 and May 1. See our [Florida LLC annual report guide](/blog/florida-llc-annual-report-requirements/) for deadlines and fees.

### Route C: Amend your Articles of Organization

If your Articles of Organization list an address and your state treats that as a formation detail, you may need a Certificate or Articles of Amendment. Fees vary widely, from roughly $25 to $150 or more depending on the state. If you're also changing the company name at the same time, see our [guide to changing your LLC name](/blog/how-to-change-llc-name/). You can often combine the two changes into one filing.

### Moving to a different state

This is a different situation. Changing your LLC address across state lines doesn't automatically change your home state. If you're moving the business to a new state, you may need to register as a foreign LLC there. Some states also allow a conversion or domestication. Talk to a business attorney before you move. The wrong approach can leave you owing fees in two states. Our [foreign LLC registration guide](/blog/foreign-llc-registration-doing-business-in-another-state/) explains how that works.

## Step 3: Handle the Registered Agent Address Change

A **registered agent address change** is the filing people forget most often. If your LLC acts as its own registered agent and you move, the address on file for service of process is now wrong. That can lead to missed lawsuits, default judgments, and administrative dissolution.

There are two scenarios:

1. **You are your own registered agent.** File the state's change-of-registered-office form with your new address.
2. **You use a commercial registered agent.** Usually you don't need to do anything, because your agent's address stays the same. Your agent forwards documents to your new address. Just update your contact information in their portal.

The second scenario is one reason I recommend a commercial agent to anyone who moves, or might move, in the next few years. Your agent's address on file never changes, so you avoid a state filing each time you relocate. ## Step 4: Update Your LLC's Address With the IRS

After the state, the federal side. To **update your LLC address with the IRS**, you have a few options.

- **Form 8822-B (Change of Address or Responsible Party – Business).** This is the standard **LLC change of address form** for federal purposes. You mail or fax it to the address in the form instructions. The form is available on [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8822-b).
- **Your next tax return.** For some filers, writing the new address on your next return can serve as notice. Using Form 8822-B is cleaner and leaves a record.
- **Written notice.** A signed letter that includes your name, EIN, old address, and new address also works in many cases.

One timing detail. If you also change your "responsible party," the person who controls the LLC, you generally have 60 days to report that to the IRS. An address change alone has no fixed filing deadline, but waiting is a bad idea. IRS notices go to the address on file, and a missed notice can mean missed deadlines and penalties.

If your LLC has a payroll or sales tax account, update those too. State tax agencies and the IRS Employer Tax Center keep separate records.

## Step 5: Update Everyone Else

The government filings are done. Now work through this list. I'd print it and tick off each item.

- **Business bank accounts and credit cards.** Banks often require a signed form or an in-branch visit for business accounts. See our [LLC bank account guide](/blog/llc-bank-account-how-to-open-one/) if you're also considering a change of bank.
- **State and local licenses and permits.** Many are tied to a physical location. A move may require a new license, not just an address update. Some local rules, like zoning and home occupation permits, apply to the new location too.
- **Business insurance.** Your general liability or professional policy should reflect the new address. Premiums can change by location.
- **Sales tax, payroll, and unemployment accounts** at the state level.
- **USPS.** File a business change of address at [USPS.com](https://www.usps.com/manage/forward.htm). It's a stopgap, not a replacement for the formal filings above.
- **Website, Google Business Profile, invoices, contracts, and email signatures.** Mismatched addresses across the web hurt local search visibility and make payments look suspicious to vendors.
- **Customers, vendors, and payment processors.**
- **Your EIN-related accounts** such as Stripe, PayPal, or Amazon seller accounts, where an address mismatch can trigger a verification hold.

## Does Changing Your LLC Address Affect BOI Reporting?

You may have heard that LLCs must file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN and update it when information changes. The rules have shifted considerably. Since FinCEN's 2025 interim final rule, domestic U.S. companies are generally exempt from BOI reporting, and the requirements now apply mainly to certain foreign reporting companies. Rules can change again, so check the current status in our [BOI report changes and updates for 2026](/blog/boi-report-changes-and-updates-2026/) article and on [FinCEN.gov](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) before you assume anything either way.

## Common Mistakes When Changing an LLC Address

1. **Only changing the mailing address.** The registered office stays wrong, so legal papers go missing.
2. **Forgetting the IRS.** The state and the IRS do not share address updates.
3. **Ignoring local licenses.** A new city or county may require a new permit.
4. **Letting the state record lag.** If your annual report isn't due for months, file a standalone update.
5. **Not documenting the decision.** Keep a signed consent and copies of all filings in your records.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I change my LLC address with the Secretary of State?

Log in to your state's business filing portal, or complete the paper form, and submit the correct filing type. This is usually an address update, a statement of information, an annual report amendment, or Articles of Amendment. Pay any fee and keep the filed copy. The exact steps depend on your state.

### How much does it cost to change your LLC address?

Many states charge nothing for a simple address update filed with an annual report. Standalone filings typically run from $0 to $50. Amendments can cost $25 to $150 or more. The IRS charges nothing for Form 8822-B.

### Do I need to tell the IRS if I change my LLC address?

Yes. File Form 8822-B so IRS notices go to the right place. There's no fixed deadline for a simple address change, but you should do it promptly. Changes to the responsible party have a 60-day reporting window.

### Can I change my LLC address online?

In most states, yes. Many Secretary of State portals accept address updates online. The IRS requires Form 8822-B by mail or fax, though you can confirm the current options on IRS.gov.

### What happens if I don't update my LLC address?

You can miss annual report reminders, tax notices, and service of process. Consequences include late fees, loss of good standing, administrative dissolution, and default judgments in lawsuits you never knew about. See our guide on [what happens if you don't renew your LLC](/blog/what-happens-if-you-don-t-renew-your-llc/).

### Do I need to change my registered agent when I move?

Not always. If you use a commercial registered agent, their address stays the same and you only update your contact details with them. If you act as your own agent, you must file a registered agent address change with the state.

### Can I use a P.O. box or virtual address for my LLC?

You can often use a P.O. box or virtual mailbox as a mailing address. Registered agent addresses generally must be a physical street address in the state. Rules for the principal office vary, so check your state's requirements.

### Can I change my LLC address if I'm moving to another state?

Moving the address doesn't move the LLC's home state. You may need to register as a foreign LLC in the new state or convert the entity. Speak with a business attorney before you relocate.

## Final Thoughts

Knowing **how to change your LLC address** comes down to a repeatable checklist: confirm which address you're changing, file with the state, handle the registered agent, notify the IRS, and then update everyone else. The paperwork is simple. What causes real trouble is missing one of the steps and finding out only when a notice goes astray. Set aside an afternoon, work through the list above, and keep copies of everything.
