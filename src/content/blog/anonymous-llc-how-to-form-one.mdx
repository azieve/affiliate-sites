## What Is an Anonymous LLC?

An anonymous LLC is not a separate legal entity type — it's a standard LLC formed in a state that does not require you to publicly disclose member or manager names in your articles of organization. Articles of organization are a public document filed with the state, so keeping your name off them is what creates the privacy layer.

Most states do require member names somewhere in the formation process. But a handful of states — most notably Wyoming, New Mexico, Delaware, and Nevada — allow articles that either omit member names entirely or simply don't require them.

**What an anonymous LLC does:**
- Keeps your name off state business registries
- Protects your home address from public business record searches
- Shields your identity from competitors, litigants, and curious members of the public

**What an anonymous LLC does NOT do:**
- Hide your identity from the IRS or federal government
- Protect you from court-ordered disclosure in active litigation
- Eliminate federal beneficial ownership reporting requirements under the Corporate Transparency Act

I've seen too many business owners assume "anonymous LLC" means total invisibility — to everyone, including the government. It doesn't. It means public invisibility. That's still enormously valuable, but the distinction matters, and any guide that skips over it is doing you a disservice.

## How to Form an Anonymous LLC: Step-by-Step

Here's anonymous LLC how to form one in practice, using Wyoming as the working example:

**Step 1: Choose Your Privacy State**

For most people in 2026, Wyoming or New Mexico. Wyoming if you want maximum legal protections and don't mind a $60/year annual report; New Mexico if you want zero ongoing state fees and can accept slightly less robust charging order protection. If you plan to actively operate in another state, you'll need to either register there as a foreign LLC or use the double LLC strategy described in the next section.

**Step 2: Hire a Registered Agent**

Every LLC must have a [registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) — a person or entity with a physical address in the state who accepts legal documents on your LLC's behalf. Here's where privacy is won or lost: your registered agent's name and address appear on public formation documents, not yours. Using a professional registered agent service means your personal address never shows up.

For anonymous LLCs, never use your home address as a registered agent. Always use a professional service.

**Step 3: File Articles of Organization Without Your Name**

When filing, do not list yourself as a member or manager. Leave those fields blank or use a nominee if the form requires something. List only:
- Your LLC name
- Your registered agent's name and address
- The effective date (if applicable)

If your formation service fills out the form for you (as ZenBusiness and most services do), confirm with them that no personal information is being listed on the public document.

**Step 4: Draft an Operating Agreement**

Your [LLC operating agreement](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) identifies members and ownership percentages. This document is private — it is never filed with the state and does not become part of the public record. Your identity is protected publicly while still being formally documented in the internal governing structure of the LLC.

**Step 5: Obtain an EIN From the IRS**

Apply for your Employer Identification Number at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). Use your registered agent's address (or your LLC's mailing address) rather than your home address. Important: the IRS will know who you are through this process. Federal anonymity is not achievable, nor should it be — this is about public record privacy.

**Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account**

Banks are required by federal law to verify beneficial ownership when opening business accounts. You will provide personal identification. Your name will be known to your bank — this is non-negotiable under FinCEN's Customer Due Diligence rules. However, that information stays with the bank and does not become part of any public record.

## The Double LLC Strategy: Maximum Privacy in Any State

If you live in a state that requires member disclosure — California, Texas, Florida, New York — you can still achieve meaningful anonymity through what's known as the double LLC (or LLC stacking) strategy.

**How it works:**

1. **Form a Wyoming LLC (the "parent"):** This is your anonymous holding entity. Your name does not appear anywhere in Wyoming's public records.

2. **Form an LLC in your home state (the "operating" LLC):** When filing articles in your home state, list the Wyoming LLC as the sole member — not your personal name.

3. **Result:** Your home state's public records show your Wyoming LLC as the owner. Anyone trying to trace ownership would need to search Wyoming's records — and find nothing there, because Wyoming doesn't require member disclosure.

This structure is entirely legal and widely used by real estate investors, high-net-worth individuals, and entrepreneurs in disclosure-heavy states. For a [Wyoming LLC cost breakdown](/blog/wyoming-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/), our state-specific guide covers every fee involved.

## What an Anonymous LLC Cannot Hide: Critical Limitations

This is the section that separates accurate guides from misleading ones.

**Federal Beneficial Ownership Reporting**

The Corporate Transparency Act (CTA), enforced by FinCEN, requires most LLCs formed or registered in the US to file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report. In 2026, this means reporting the names, dates of birth, addresses, and government ID numbers of all beneficial owners to FinCEN.

[According to FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/boi), BOI reports are confidential — accessible only to law enforcement, certain federal agencies, and qualifying financial institutions under specific circumstances. They are not public records. So your anonymous LLC still protects you from public business record searches and general public exposure.

But you cannot hide your identity from the federal government, and attempting to do so by filing incomplete or false BOI reports carries penalties of up to $591 per day plus potential criminal liability. See our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for complete 2026 requirements and deadlines.

**Courts and Litigation**

If a lawsuit proceeds and a court orders member disclosure, your anonymity can be pierced. An anonymous LLC reduces opportunistic litigation (your name is harder to find), but it doesn't make your LLC judgment-proof once litigation is underway.

**Banks and Financial Institutions**

Federal law requires banks to collect and verify beneficial ownership information when opening business accounts. Your bank will know who you are — full stop.

**Foreign Registration in Your Home State**

If you form a Wyoming LLC but register it as a foreign LLC in your home state to conduct business, your home state may impose additional disclosure requirements that could partially undercut the privacy benefit. The double LLC strategy exists specifically to address this problem.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to form an anonymous LLC?**

A Wyoming anonymous LLC typically costs $100 in state filing fees plus formation service fees. With ZenBusiness's Starter plan, you can get it done for approximately $100–$150 total in year one. New Mexico is cheaper at $50 with no annual report. Using the double LLC strategy adds the filing cost and registered agent fee for a second state, bringing total year-one costs to roughly $350–$750.

**Which states allow anonymous LLCs?**

The four states most commonly used are Wyoming, New Mexico, Delaware, and Nevada. Wyoming and New Mexico are the most popular because of low costs and strong privacy provisions. Wyoming is generally preferred for charging order protection; New Mexico for having no annual report requirement.

**Can I form an anonymous LLC if I live in a state that requires member disclosure?**

Yes — through the double LLC strategy. You form an anonymous Wyoming or New Mexico LLC as the parent entity, then form your home state LLC with the Wyoming LLC listed as the sole member. Your name doesn't appear in either state's public records. This is entirely legal and widely used.

**Does an anonymous LLC protect me from lawsuits?**

An anonymous LLC makes it harder for opportunistic plaintiffs to identify you, but does not make you immune from litigation. If a suit proceeds and a court orders disclosure, your anonymity can be pierced. Think of an anonymous LLC as a privacy shield that complements the liability protection an LLC structure already provides — not a replacement for it.

**Do I still have to file a BOI report for an anonymous LLC?**

Yes, in almost all cases. The Corporate Transparency Act requires most LLCs to report beneficial ownership information to FinCEN. Critically, BOI reports are confidential (not public), so your identity is disclosed to the federal government but not to the general public. Failing to file carries penalties up to $591 per day. See our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for full 2026 requirements.

**Can an anonymous LLC open a bank account?**

Yes, but you'll need to provide personal identification to the bank. Federal anti-money-laundering rules require banks to verify beneficial ownership — your name will be known to your bank regardless of your LLC's formation structure. That information stays with the bank and does not appear in public records.

**Is an anonymous LLC legal?**

Completely legal. Forming an LLC in a state that doesn't require member disclosure and using a professional registered agent are both standard, legal business practices. The double LLC strategy is also legal. You must still comply with all federal requirements, including BOI reporting and tax obligations — there is no legal way to be invisible to the federal government.

**What's the difference between an anonymous LLC and a shell company?**

"Shell company" is a general term for any entity with no active operations, often used as an asset-holding vehicle. An anonymous LLC is specifically about owner privacy in public records. All shell companies aren't anonymous, and anonymous LLCs don't have to be shells — you can run an active business through one. The two concepts overlap but are not the same thing.
