If you've spent more than ten minutes researching how to form an LLC, you've almost certainly landed on two names: [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) and [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/). They dominate search results, advertising, and word-of-mouth recommendations alike. But they are built for different customers, and choosing the wrong one can cost you real money — and real headaches.

This is the highest-stakes decision most first-time business owners don't realize they're making. The formation service you choose determines how smoothly your LLC gets filed, what compliance support you'll have going forward, and how much you'll quietly pay in renewal fees every year. The difference between these two platforms can easily run into hundreds of dollars annually once you account for add-ons.

In this ZenBusiness vs LegalZoom comparison, we'll break down pricing, features, turnaround times, customer support quality, and which types of businesses are genuinely better served by each. We'll skip the filler and give you a direct answer at the end.

If you're still earlier in your research, it's also worth reading our [full guide to the best LLC formation services](/best-llc-services/) to understand the full competitive landscape before committing.

## Pricing Breakdown: What You'll Actually Pay

### ZenBusiness Pricing

[ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) offers three tiers:

- **Starter: $0/year + state fees** — Basic LLC filing only. No registered agent included, no operating agreement.
- **Pro: $199/year + state fees** — Includes registered agent service for the first year, operating agreement, and faster filing.
- **Premium: $349/year + state fees** — Adds rush filing, business document template library, and enhanced support.

The critical detail: after the first year on the Pro plan, registered agent renewal runs $99–$149/year. That's competitive but not free. For an overview of what these costs mean in practice, see our breakdown of [how much it costs to form an LLC](/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-form-an-llc/).

### LegalZoom Pricing

[LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) structures its plans differently:

- **Basic: $0/year + state fees** — Filing only, minimal support.
- **Pro: $249/year + state fees** — Faster processing, operating agreement, and some compliance support.
- **Premium: $299/year + state fees** — Adds attorney consultation and expedited filing.

LegalZoom's registered agent service is a separate product priced at $299/year — significantly higher than ZenBusiness's ongoing rate. If you need registered agent service (and in most states, you do — it's legally required), this gap matters.

**Bottom line on pricing:** ZenBusiness is measurably cheaper across a 3–5 year ownership window for most small businesses. LegalZoom's higher registered agent fees and attorney-access pricing make it cost more unless you're actively using their legal services.

## Features That Actually Matter

### Registered Agent Service

Every LLC must have a registered agent — a person or company designated to receive legal documents on behalf of your business. If you're not familiar with the mechanics, our guide on [what is a registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) covers the full picture.

ZenBusiness includes registered agent service free for the first year on its Pro and Premium plans, then charges a renewal fee that's well below the industry average. LegalZoom's registered agent service is a standalone product at $299/year. If you're forming an LLC today and plan to maintain registered agent service (which you must, legally), ZenBusiness wins this category outright.

### Operating Agreement

An operating agreement is the internal governing document for your LLC. It defines ownership percentages, voting rights, profit distribution, and what happens if a member wants to exit. Without one, you're operating on your state's default rules — which rarely match what you actually intended.

ZenBusiness includes a customizable operating agreement template on its Pro tier and above. LegalZoom includes it on Pro tier as well, but their Basic plan leaves you without one. Neither service produces a bespoke operating agreement negotiated by counsel — if your situation is complex (unequal ownership, investor relationships, industry-specific provisions), you'll want a real attorney regardless of which platform you use.

### Annual Report Filing

Most states require LLCs to file annual or biennial reports to stay in good standing. Missed filings can result in late fees, administrative dissolution, or loss of liability protection.

ZenBusiness includes annual report filing in its Premium plan and offers it as a paid add-on at lower tiers. LegalZoom treats annual report filing as a separate service with its own fee structure. For businesses that want one-stop compliance management, ZenBusiness's bundled approach is more convenient and typically more economical.

### EIN Registration

Your Employer Identification Number (EIN) is required to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal taxes. The IRS issues EINs for free through its website ([irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online)), and most business owners can do this themselves in under 10 minutes. Both ZenBusiness and LegalZoom charge approximately $70 to handle this for you. Unless you have a specific reason to outsource it, this is one add-on worth skipping.

### Attorney Access

This is LegalZoom's clearest differentiator. Their legal plan bundles attorney consultations, document review, and ongoing legal support for a monthly fee (typically $49–$249/month depending on the tier). If you're forming an LLC and simultaneously need contract review, trademark registration, or other legal work, the bundled access has value.

ZenBusiness doesn't offer attorney services in any meaningful way. They're a filing and compliance platform, not a legal services provider.

I've seen too many small business owners overpay for LegalZoom's legal plan when they only needed basic formation help. If your legal needs extend beyond filing documents, consider whether a local business attorney might serve you better than a subscription service — particularly for anything involving contracts with third parties, IP protection, or employment agreements.

## Which Service Is Better for Different Business Types?

### Freelancers and Solopreneurs

If you're a freelancer, consultant, or one-person operation forming your first LLC, ZenBusiness is almost always the better call. The cost savings are real, the platform is intuitive, and you don't need attorney access for a straightforward single-member LLC. For more on why LLCs make sense for this group, see our guide on [LLC vs sole proprietorship](/blog/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship/).

### Small Multi-Member LLCs

For businesses with two or more members — partners, co-founders, or family members sharing ownership — the operating agreement becomes critical. Both platforms provide templates, but neither will negotiate the agreement for you. If your ownership or profit-sharing arrangement is anything other than 50/50 between two equal partners, budget for an attorney to customize the agreement. Between the two services, ZenBusiness is still the more cost-effective filing platform.

### Businesses Needing Ongoing Legal Support

If you expect to need regular attorney access — contract drafting, employment agreements, vendor negotiations — LegalZoom's legal subscription may offer value. But price it carefully: $49–$249/month adds up fast, and independent business attorneys are often more practical for complex, specialized work. The [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org) has documented extensively that small business owners frequently overpay for subscription legal services that don't match their actual usage patterns.

### Businesses in High-Filing States

If you're forming an LLC in California, New York, or other states with complex requirements (publication requirements in New York, for instance, or California's $800 minimum franchise tax), the compliance picture gets more nuanced. Both services handle most state filings, but it's worth checking state-specific guides — see our pages for [California](/start-llc/california/), [Texas](/start-llc/texas/), and [Florida](/start-llc/florida/) for state-level details.

## Turnaround Times and Processing Speed

Both services offer standard and expedited processing. Standard filing times vary by state — most states process within 5–15 business days, though states like Delaware and Wyoming move faster.

According to the [National Conference of State Legislatures](https://www.ncsl.org), LLC formation volumes have increased significantly since 2020, and state agencies in high-volume states have experienced processing backlogs. If timing is critical — say, you need the LLC formed before signing a lease or opening a bank account — opt for expedited state processing through either service. ZenBusiness's Premium plan includes rush filing; LegalZoom offers rush options as a paid add-on.

In practice, the platform you choose has less impact on processing speed than your state's current queue. Neither ZenBusiness nor LegalZoom can accelerate the state's review timeline — they can only submit your application faster.

## Customer Support Quality

Customer support is where ZenBusiness has historically outperformed LegalZoom in user reviews. On Trustpilot, ZenBusiness maintains a score above 4.5/5 from tens of thousands of reviews, with users consistently citing helpful, responsive support agents. LegalZoom's Trustpilot profile shows a more mixed picture, with common complaints about billing disputes and difficulty reaching knowledgeable support representatives.

This matters in practice. When your Articles of Organization get rejected by the state (it happens more than people expect, particularly with name conflicts or incomplete filings), you need a support team that can diagnose the issue quickly and resubmit without drama.

## ZenBusiness vs LegalZoom: Our Verdict

For the majority of small business owners forming a straightforward LLC, **ZenBusiness is the better choice**. It's less expensive across the full ownership lifecycle, the platform experience is purpose-built for formation and compliance, and customer support quality is measurably stronger.

LegalZoom earns its recommendation in a specific scenario: you're forming an LLC *and* you genuinely need ongoing attorney access for other legal work. Their legal subscription plan can bundle meaningful value if you're a high-usage legal services customer. But if you're primarily focused on getting your LLC formed correctly and maintaining it affordably, the premium LegalZoom charges for its brand and attorney network isn't worth it.

## Final Checklist Before You Choose

Before clicking "submit" on either platform, make sure you've thought through:

1. **Registered agent coverage** — Do you need ongoing service after year one? Price it out over 3 years, not just year one.
2. **Operating agreement complexity** — Is your ownership structure straightforward? If not, budget for an attorney to review the template.
3. **Annual report obligations** — Know your state's requirements and whether your plan includes compliance reminders.
4. **EIN** — Consider doing this yourself through [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov) and saving the add-on fee.
5. **State fees** — Both services pass state filing fees through to you directly. These aren't negotiable and aren't part of either platform's pricing.

Forming an LLC is one of the best financial decisions a self-employed person or small business owner can make. The liability protection, tax flexibility, and credibility it provides are real and meaningful. The difference between ZenBusiness and LegalZoom won't make or break your business — but choosing the right one means you're not overpaying for features you don't use, and that money stays in your business where it belongs.
