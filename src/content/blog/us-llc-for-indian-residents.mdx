A US LLC for Indian residents is legally possible. Forming one is the easy part, though. The harder part is staying compliant on two fronts at once: US filing rules and Indian foreign-exchange and tax rules. Many founders in India focus on the US side, because a formation service can finish it in a few days, and then discover the Indian side only when a bank queries a remittance or a tax notice arrives.

This guide covers both sides. It explains what an LLC is for an Indian founder, how to form one, which Indian rules apply, and what it costs in 2026. If you only want the formation step, a service such as [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) can file your paperwork and act as your registered agent, starting at $0 plus state fees on its entry plan. Whether you *should* form the LLC is a separate question, and you should settle it with a Chartered Accountant (CA) before you send any money abroad.

## Can an Indian Resident Own a US LLC?

Yes. The US places no citizenship or residency requirement on LLC members. You do not need a Social Security Number, a US address of your own, or a visa to own one. Our guide on [whether a foreigner can own an LLC in the US](/blog/can-a-foreigner-own-an-llc-in-the-us/) covers the US side in more detail.

The Indian side is where most of the complexity sits. As an Indian resident, you are subject to:

- **FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act):** controls how you move money abroad and whether you may hold equity in an overseas entity.
- **The Income-tax Act:** taxes residents on worldwide income and requires you to disclose foreign assets.
- **The Black Money Act:** imposes heavy penalties for undisclosed foreign assets.

Under Indian rules, your US LLC is a foreign entity in which you hold an interest. Whatever the US thinks of it, India will see it as an overseas investment or asset. That distinction drives most of what follows.

## Is Forming a US LLC Right for You? Start With the "Why"

In my experience, the most expensive mistake Indian founders make is forming the LLC first and working out the purpose afterward. I have seen freelancers pay for a Delaware LLC, a registered agent and a bank account, then learn that their real need was simply to get paid by US clients. That need could often be met without owning a US entity at all.

A US LLC tends to make sense when:

- You sell to US customers and want a US-based entity for credibility, contracts or payment processors.
- You are building a software or e-commerce business with a US-facing storefront.
- You plan to raise US capital later (though investors usually prefer a Delaware C-corp rather than an LLC).
- You need a US bank account and US payment rails for a real operating business.

It tends to make less sense when:

- You are a freelancer billing a few clients who are happy to pay you in INR or through standard export-of-services channels.
- You want to hold passive assets or invest in US securities through the LLC. FEMA rules on overseas investment can be restrictive here.
- You want the LLC mainly to reduce taxes. As a tax resident of India, you generally cannot do that, because India taxes your worldwide income.

If your situation is a freelance practice, our overview of an [LLC for freelancers](/llc-for-/freelancers/) explains the liability and tax mechanics in more detail.

## Indian Regulatory Rules You Must Understand First

Treat this section as a briefing for a conversation with your CA and your bank's authorised dealer (AD) branch, not as a substitute for one. These rules change, and RBI has updated its overseas investment framework in recent years.

### The Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)

Under the RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme, a resident individual can remit up to **US $250,000 per financial year** (April to March) for permitted purposes. Funding your US LLC is the most common use for founders. Other permitted uses include education, travel and gifts. The limit covers all your LRS remittances combined, not just the LLC. The RBI publishes the scheme details in its [LRS FAQs](https://www.rbi.org.in/) so you can check the current position yourself.

Two practical points:

- **TCS (tax collected at source):** Remittances under LRS can attract TCS, with the rate and threshold depending on the purpose. These rates have been adjusted in recent budgets, so confirm the 2026 position with your bank or CA. TCS is generally creditable against your Indian tax liability, but it affects cash flow.
- **Purpose codes and documentation:** Your bank will ask for the purpose of the remittance and may ask for supporting documents. Describe the transaction honestly and consistently.

### Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) Considerations

Holding equity in a foreign operating entity is treated as Overseas Direct Investment under FEMA. For resident individuals, the rules are narrower than many founders expect. For example, the framework restricts investment in certain activities such as financial services and real estate, and has conditions around round-tripping and structure. It also has reporting obligations, including an annual Foreign Liabilities and Assets (FLA) return.

I will be direct about this. The ODI rules are the part of the process where informal online advice is least reliable. Ask your CA or an FEMA specialist whether your intended LLC structure and business activity fit within the current rules before you remit funds. A mistake made here can mean compounding or regularisation procedures later.

### Disclosure in Your Indian Tax Return

If you own a foreign entity, you must report it. Resident individuals generally disclose foreign assets and foreign-source income in their income tax return, including the schedule for foreign assets, even if the LLC earns nothing that year. The Black Money Act carries a penalty (historically ₹10 lakh per default) for failing to disclose. Do not skip this to avoid complexity.

## How a US LLC Is Taxed When an Indian Resident Owns It

There are two layers of tax treatment: the US view and the Indian view.

### The US view

A single-member LLC owned by a non-US person is, by default, a "disregarded entity" for US income tax. If it has no US-connected business activity, it may owe no US federal income tax at all. This is the point many founders have in mind when they talk about the LLC being "tax free." The statement is incomplete. A US LLC with no US trade or business, owned by a foreign person, commonly owes no US federal income tax. But if the LLC does business through a US office, employees or other US presence, "effectively connected income" can arise, and US tax can apply.

Multi-member LLCs are taxed as partnerships by default and file Form 1065 and issue K-1s, which brings withholding obligations when foreign partners are involved. Our explainer on [how LLCs are taxed](/blog/llc-taxes-how-are-llcs-taxed/) covers the general rules.

### The Indian view

For an Indian tax resident, income earned through the LLC is generally part of your taxable income in India, depending on how the Indian authorities treat the entity and the way profits are distributed. Whether the LLC is looked at as a transparent entity or as a separate company can matter, as can your residential status. The US-India tax treaty and Form 67 for foreign tax credits may come into play if US tax has been paid. Because treatment is not always straightforward, get written advice from a CA who has dealt with US LLCs before.

## The US Compliance You Cannot Skip: Form 5472 and Annual Filings

Even if the LLC owes no US tax, a foreign-owned single-member LLC has a **filing obligation**. This is the most overlooked point in this entire topic.

- **Form 5472 plus a pro forma Form 1120:** A foreign-owned, US-disregarded LLC must file these annually with the IRS to report certain transactions between the LLC and its owner. These include capital contributions and loans. The IRS explains the form on its [Form 5472 page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5472).
- **The penalty:** The IRS can assess a penalty of **$25,000** per form for failure to file, and it can increase if the problem continues after notice.

For a worked walkthrough, read our guide to [Form 5472 for foreign-owned single-member LLCs](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/). If you hire a CPA, ask for a fixed fee for this filing before you start. Prices vary widely.

You also have state-level obligations. Delaware charges a flat annual franchise tax of $300 for LLCs, due June 1. Wyoming charges a minimum annual report fee, and California charges an $800 minimum franchise tax. These can differ from the formation fee by a wide margin, so check the state before you choose it.

A note on BOI reporting: Beneficial Ownership Information rules under the Corporate Transparency Act have changed repeatedly. Following FinCEN's 2025 interim final rule, US-formed companies were generally exempted from BOI reporting. Our [BOI changes and updates](/blog/boi-report-changes-and-updates-2026/) article tracks the current position, which you should verify before filing or skipping anything.

## Step-by-Step: Forming a US LLC as an Indian Resident

1. **Get advice first.** Speak to a CA about FEMA/ODI fit, LRS planning, and tax treatment. Do this before forming.
2. **Choose a state.** Delaware, Wyoming and New Mexico are popular with non-residents. New Mexico's filing fee is about $50. Wyoming's is $100, and Delaware's is about $110, with the $300 annual tax noted above. If you will have US customers or operations in a specific state, you may need to register there as a foreign LLC. See our [best state to form an LLC](/blog/best-state-to-form-an-llc/) comparison.
3. **Pick a name and a registered agent.** You need a US street address in the state to receive legal papers. A formation service usually provides one. Our [registered agent guide](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) explains the role.
4. **File the Articles of Organization** with the state, which a formation service can handle for you.
5. **Draft an operating agreement.** Even single-member LLCs should have one. It helps support liability protection and is often requested by banks. See the [operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/).
6. **Get an EIN.** You can apply without an SSN. Foreign applicants usually apply by fax, mail or phone using Form SS-4, since the online application requires a US-issued tax ID. Our guide to [getting an EIN without an SSN](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-without-an-ssn/) walks through the methods.
7. **Open a US bank account.** This is often the hardest step. Some banks need an in-person visit, while others will onboard foreign owners remotely. See [how to open a US bank account for a foreign-owned LLC](/blog/us-bank-account-for-a-foreign-owned-llc/).
8. **Remit funds through LRS.** Use your AD bank, state the purpose accurately, and keep records of every transfer.
9. **Maintain compliance.** File Form 5472, pay state fees, and disclose the entity in your Indian tax return every year.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Using LRS funds without a clear purpose.** Vague or inconsistent descriptions create problems at your bank.
- **Skipping Form 5472** because "the LLC earned nothing." The filing is required whether or not there was income.
- **Not disclosing the LLC in your Indian return.** The penalties are disproportionate to the effort of disclosure.
- **Mixing personal and business funds.** It undermines the liability shield and creates a messy record for both tax authorities.
- **Assuming an LLC is a tax shelter.** For an Indian tax resident, it generally is not.
- **Relying on a formation company for tax or FEMA advice.** They file paperwork. They do not advise you on your Indian obligations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an Indian citizen open a US LLC?

Yes. US law allows non-US citizens and non-residents to own LLCs. The Indian side is separate: you must follow FEMA and RBI rules for sending money abroad and holding an overseas entity, and you must disclose it in your tax return.

### How much money can I send from India to fund a US LLC?

Under the LRS, resident individuals can remit up to $250,000 per financial year for permitted purposes, in total across all uses. Whether your specific LLC investment fits within the overseas investment rules is a question for your CA and your authorised dealer bank.

### Do I need a US address or SSN to form an LLC?

You need a US registered agent address in the state of formation, which a formation service typically supplies. You do not need an SSN. For an EIN, foreign applicants can apply using Form SS-4 by fax, mail or phone.

### Do Indian residents pay US tax on a US LLC?

It depends on the LLC's activities. A single-member LLC with no US-connected business may owe no US federal income tax, but it must still file Form 5472. If the LLC has a US presence, US tax may apply. You will also generally owe tax in India on income you earn through it.

### What is the penalty for not filing Form 5472?

The IRS can assess a $25,000 penalty per form for failure to file, with additional penalties possible if the failure continues after notice. See the IRS page for the current rules.

### Which state is best for an Indian-owned LLC?

Delaware, Wyoming and New Mexico are common choices because of their cost structures and business-friendly laws. The right answer depends on where you will operate, your annual fee tolerance, and your banking needs. If you will do business in another state, you may need to register there too.

### Do I need an LLC to work with US clients from India?

Not always. Many freelancers invoice US clients directly, using standard export-of-services channels. An LLC may help with credibility, contracts and payment platforms, but it adds compliance obligations. Weigh the benefits against the cost and paperwork.

### Can I open a US bank account for my LLC from India?

Often yes, though banks vary. Some require an in-person visit, while others accept remote applications with an EIN, formation documents and passport. See our guide to opening a US bank account for a foreign-owned LLC.
