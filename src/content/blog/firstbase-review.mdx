Firstbase is a US company-formation platform built mostly for founders who live outside the United States. It also serves US-based startups. If you're choosing between Firstbase and a mainstream LLC service, the question is whether you need what it specializes in. Most domestic small business owners don't. This Firstbase review explains what it does well, where it falls short, and which service fits which situation in 2026.

Most US residents forming a standard LLC can get a Wyoming, Texas or Florida company through [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) for a $0 service fee plus state filing costs. Paid plans add extras such as a registered agent, an operating agreement and compliance reminders. Firstbase makes more sense when you're a non-US founder, or a startup that wants a Delaware structure and a bundled stack of banking and tax tools. Details below.

## Firstbase Review: What It Is and Who It's For

Firstbase positions itself as a startup-focused formation platform. Its pitch is a single dashboard for incorporating, getting a registered agent, obtaining an EIN, and connecting to banking and bookkeeping partners. It is best known for helping international founders open a US entity remotely, without a Social Security number or a US address.

The platform is oriented around Delaware, the state many venture-backed startups use. In my experience, Delaware is often the wrong default for small operating businesses. It adds a second layer of cost, because you pay Delaware fees and often a foreign-qualification fee in your home state. My [Delaware LLC cost breakdown](/blog/delaware-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/) shows how those layers stack up.

That focus defines the right audience for Firstbase:

- **Non-US founders** who want a US entity for payments, Stripe, or investor credibility.
- **Early-stage startups** that expect to raise money and want a Delaware C-corp or LLC.
- **Founders who value convenience** and are willing to pay for a bundled, guided setup.

It is a weaker fit for:

- **US-resident freelancers and local service businesses** who just need a low-cost LLC in their home state.
- **Cost-sensitive owners** who can file directly with their state or use a $0 service-fee provider.

## Firstbase Pricing: What You'll Actually Pay

Firstbase pricing is tiered, with packages that bundle formation with extras. Published starting prices have been in the mid-hundreds of dollars for formation. Higher tiers add services such as a registered agent, a business address, and tax or compliance support. Tier names, inclusions and prices change, so check the current page before you commit.

Whatever the package price, the real cost has several layers:

1. **The platform fee.** This is the package price Firstbase charges.
2. **State filing fees.** Delaware charges its own filing fee for an LLC, and the state's annual obligations are separate. See the [Delaware Division of Corporations](https://corp.delaware.gov/) for current fees.
3. **Registered agent renewal.** Many services include a registered agent for the first year only. Ask what year two costs.
4. **Annual compliance.** Delaware LLCs owe a flat annual tax, and corporations owe franchise tax. Our guide to the [Delaware LLC annual franchise tax](/blog/delaware-llc-annual-franchise-tax/) covers the $300 flat fee and the June 1 deadline.
5. **Foreign qualification.** If you operate in another state, you may also need to register there. See our [foreign LLC registration guide](/blog/foreign-llc-registration-doing-business-in-another-state/).

Compare this with a typical domestic service. [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) starts at $0 plus the state fee, and its Pro and Premium plans (roughly $199 and $349 per year at last check) add compliance and business tools. [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) also advertises a $0 service-fee tier plus state fees, with paid upgrades. On price alone, a non-US founder still pays more through Firstbase. In exchange, they get a process designed for their situation.

## Key Features and What They Mean in Practice

### Remote formation for non-residents

This is the headline value. A non-US founder generally cannot get a US Social Security number, so some steps are awkward on their own. The EIN, for example, is typically requested by fax or phone with the IRS instead of online. Our guide on [getting an EIN without an SSN](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-without-an-ssn/) explains the routes. A platform that handles this for you saves real time.

### Registered agent and mailing address

A registered agent receives legal notices and state mail on your company's behalf, which every state requires. If you want to understand the role first, read [What Is a Registered Agent?](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/). If privacy and agent quality are your main concerns, I'd point you to [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/), which is built around them.

### Banking and tax tool referrals

Firstbase connects users to banking, bookkeeping and tax partners. This is convenient, but treat the partner products as options to compare, not defaults. Many US banks will open accounts for foreign-owned LLCs under certain conditions. Our [foreign-owned LLC bank account guide](/blog/us-bank-account-for-a-foreign-owned-llc/) walks through the requirements.

### Ongoing compliance

A US entity with foreign owners often has extra tax filings. A single-member LLC owned by a non-US person generally must file IRS Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, even with no US income. The [IRS Form 5472 page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5472) explains the requirement, and the penalty for missing it can be steep. Our [Form 5472 filing guide](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/) covers the practical steps. Whichever provider you choose, confirm whether it files this for you or only reminds you. That is a common gap.

## Firstbase Pros and Cons

**Pros**

- Designed around the non-US founder journey rather than treating it as an edge case.
- Delaware focus suits startups that plan to raise venture capital.
- Bundled approach reduces the number of vendors you manage.
- A guided dashboard is friendlier than doing everything by hand.

**Cons**

- Higher upfront cost than $0 service-fee providers for simple domestic LLCs.
- Delaware is not automatically the best state, and it can add cost for businesses operating elsewhere.
- Bundled partner services may not be the cheapest option in each category.
- A formation platform is not a law firm or tax adviser. It cannot replace cross-border tax advice.

I've seen too many founders pick Delaware because "that's what startups do," then pay for a second state registration they never needed. Decide where you'll actually operate before you buy any package.

## Do You Need Firstbase LLC Formation, or Something Simpler?

Use this quick framework. It reflects how I'd advise a friend, not personalized advice.

**You probably want Firstbase or a similar international platform if:**

- You live outside the US and don't have an SSN.
- You expect to raise outside capital and want a Delaware entity from day one.
- You value a guided process and don't want to coordinate multiple vendors.

**You probably want a domestic service like ZenBusiness if:**

- You live and work in the US.
- You're forming a single-member or small multi-member LLC.
- You want the lowest upfront cost and can add services later.

A US freelancer who spends $500 on a Delaware package when a $0-fee filing in their home state would do is paying for features they won't use. Our guide on the [cheapest way to form an LLC](/blog/cheapest-way-to-form-an-llc/) shows how to avoid that.

## Compliance Points to Check Before You Buy

A few regulatory notes apply to any formation service in 2026:

- **Beneficial ownership reporting.** In March 2025, FinCEN issued an interim final rule that removed the BOI reporting requirement for US-formed companies and their US owners. Foreign companies registered to do business in the US remain in scope. Rules continue to evolve, so check [FinCEN's BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) and our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for current status.
- **Taxes.** An LLC's tax treatment depends on its members, elections and residency. Non-US owners face different rules than US owners. A cross-border tax professional is worth the fee.
- **Banking.** Opening a US business bank account can still require documents or a visit, depending on the bank.

## Final Verdict

Firstbase is a legitimate option built for a specific audience: international founders and startups that want a US entity with guided setup. It isn't the cheapest route, and it isn't the right choice for most domestic LLCs. If you're a US resident forming a standard LLC in 2026, [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) is where I'd start, with [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) as the runner-up. If you're abroad and building a venture-style company, compare Firstbase with doola and Stripe Atlas, and weigh the total cost, including annual renewals and tax filings, instead of the headline package price.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Firstbase legit?

Firstbase is an established company-formation platform that serves founders worldwide. As with any provider, read the current terms, check what's included and what renews, and compare at least one alternative before paying.

### How much does Firstbase cost?

Firstbase pricing is tiered, and published starting prices have been in the mid-hundreds of dollars, before state fees and renewals. Check the provider's current pricing page, because plans change.

### Can a non-US resident form an LLC through Firstbase?

Yes. Serving non-US founders is a core use case. A foreign owner can generally form a US LLC, but there are extra tax filings. See our guide on [whether a foreigner can own an LLC in the US](/blog/can-a-foreigner-own-an-llc-in-the-us/).

### Is Firstbase better than ZenBusiness?

It depends on your situation. ZenBusiness is usually better for US-based owners who want a low-cost LLC. Firstbase is more relevant for international founders and startups focused on Delaware.

### Do I need a registered agent if I use Firstbase?

Every LLC needs a registered agent in its state of formation. Confirm whether your Firstbase package includes one, for how long, and what it costs to renew.

### Is Delaware the best state for my LLC?

Not always. If you operate mainly in one other state, you may pay both Delaware and home-state fees. Our [best state to form an LLC](/blog/best-state-to-form-an-llc/) guide compares the options.

### Does a formation service file my taxes?

Generally no. Formation platforms handle the state filing and related setup. Ongoing tax returns, including forms like 5472 for foreign-owned LLCs, usually need a tax professional unless a plan says otherwise.

### Can I form an LLC myself instead of using a service?

Yes. You can file directly with the state. A service mainly saves time and handles paperwork and reminders. See [How Much Does an LLC Cost?](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/) for the DIY math.
