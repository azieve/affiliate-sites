Running a daycare means being responsible for other people's children eight to ten hours a day. That responsibility is rewarding, but it is also one of the highest-liability small business categories there is. A single playground injury, a allergic reaction, or a parent dispute can turn into a claim that reaches well past your business bank account. That is why many providers set up an LLC for a daycare and childcare business before they enroll their first child.

This guide walks through what an LLC does and does not do for a childcare provider, how it interacts with state licensing and insurance, how home daycares and centers are taxed in 2026, and how to form one without overpaying. Formation itself is straightforward. Online services such as [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) start at $0 plus your state's filing fee, so the real work is getting the structure and the compliance details right.

If you are new to the entity itself, start with our primer on [what an LLC is](/learn/what-is-an-llc/), then come back here for the childcare-specific details.

## Why an LLC Makes Sense for a Daycare or Childcare Business

A limited liability company separates your business from your personal finances. If the daycare is sued or runs up debt, creditors generally can reach business assets, not your house, savings, or car. For a home-based provider this matters even more, because the business and the home are physically the same place.

Here are the situations where the structure earns its keep:

- **Contract and lease debts.** If your center lease, equipment loan, or vendor invoices go unpaid, the LLC is typically the obligor, not you personally.
- **Employee-related claims.** If an assistant teacher is accused of wrongdoing or an employment claim is filed, the LLC is the named employer.
- **Premises claims.** A parent's visitor slips on your wet entryway. The claim is against the business.
- **Business-only creditors.** Supplier disputes or a landlord claim stay with the entity.

Now the part many guides skip. **An LLC does not protect you from your own negligence.** If you personally leave a toddler unsupervised and the child is hurt, courts can hold you personally liable regardless of your entity. The same applies to any personal guarantee you sign on a lease or loan, which landlords and lenders routinely require from small-business owners. In my experience, providers who treat the LLC as a substitute for insurance are the ones who get hurt. The LLC is one layer, insurance is another, and good supervision practices are the third. You need all three.

Liability protection also depends on you treating the LLC as a real, separate entity: its own bank account, no mixing personal and business expenses, and a written [operating agreement](/blog/llc-operating-agreement-template-and-guide/). Courts can "pierce the veil" when owners ignore these basics.

## Licensing Comes First: How It Interacts With Your LLC

Childcare is regulated at the state level, and sometimes the county or city. Requirements vary significantly, so treat anything below as a framework, not a rule for your state. [Child Care Aware of America](https://www.childcareaware.org/) and your state's child care licensing agency are the authoritative sources, and the federal [Child Care and Development Fund](https://www.acf.hhs.gov/occ) program sets baseline health, safety, and background-check expectations that states must meet for providers who accept subsidies.

Typical daycare licensing requirements include:

- Fingerprint-based criminal background checks for owners, staff, and often adult household members (for home daycares)
- Provider-to-child ratios and group-size limits by age
- Pre-service training in CPR, first aid, and safe sleep practices
- Health and fire inspections of the premises
- Immunization and record-keeping systems
- Zoning approval, especially for home-based programs

**Sequencing tip:** Form the LLC first, then apply for the license in the LLC's name. Licenses are often tied to the named operator and are frequently not transferable. If you get licensed as an individual and form the LLC later, you may have to file a change of ownership or reapply. Call your licensing specialist before you file anything and ask which legal name should appear on the application.

Also check whether your state allows or restricts specific entity types. Most do allow LLCs for childcare businesses, but some licensing forms ask for the owner's personal details and a "responsible person" regardless of structure.

## Insurance: The Layer an LLC Cannot Replace

Childcare business insurance is the part of your risk plan that actually pays claims. Common coverages include:

| Coverage | What It Handles | Typical Notes |
|---|---|---|
| General liability | Injuries to children or visitors, property damage | Foundation of any policy |
| Abuse and molestation liability | Allegations of abuse | Often excluded from standard policies. Ask specifically |
| Professional liability | Claims of negligent care or supervision | Frequently bundled in childcare-specific policies |
| Commercial property | Equipment, toys, furniture, buildings | Home-based owners: standard homeowners policies often exclude business activity |
| Commercial auto | Transporting children | Required if you run field trips or pickups |
| Workers' compensation | Employee injuries | Required in most states once you have employees |
| Accident medical | Minor injuries regardless of fault | Common add-on in daycare policies |

Costs vary widely with state, enrollment, and whether you operate from home. Many home daycares pay a few hundred dollars per year for a basic liability policy, while centers with larger enrollment and staff pay considerably more. Get quotes from carriers that specialize in childcare, and read the exclusions line by line. The question to ask every agent is: "Does this policy cover abuse and molestation allegations, and what is the limit?"

If you rent or own a home, tell your homeowners insurer you operate a daycare. Failing to disclose it can give them grounds to deny a claim.

## How Daycares and Childcare Businesses Are Taxed in 2026

By default, a single-member LLC is a "disregarded entity" for federal tax purposes. You report income and expenses on Schedule C of your personal return, and you pay self-employment tax of 15.3% on net earnings (up to the Social Security wage base), plus income tax. A multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership by default. The IRS explains these classifications in its [LLC guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc).

### Deductions Specific to Childcare

Childcare providers have some deductions that other small businesses do not:

- **Home use (time-space percentage).** Home daycare providers can deduct a portion of mortgage interest or rent, utilities, repairs, and depreciation. A distinctive feature of home daycare is the "time-space" calculation, which may let you claim a share of the home even if the space is also used by your family when the children are gone. IRS [Publication 587](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p587) covers the rules, and a tax pro should confirm your percentage.
- **Food costs.** Family childcare providers can often use IRS standard meal and snack rates instead of tracking every grocery receipt. Providers participating in the USDA [Child and Adult Care Food Program (CACFP)](https://www.fns.usda.gov/cacfp) can receive reimbursements for eligible meals, and those reimbursements are generally treated as business income with related food costs deductible, so ask your tax preparer how to report them.
- **Supplies, toys, and equipment.** Educational materials, cribs, safety gear, and playground equipment may be deductible or depreciable.
- **Training and licensing costs.** CPR certification, continuing education, and licensing fees are generally deductible business expenses.
- **Insurance, software, and marketing.** Premiums, enrollment software, and advertising typically qualify.

For a broader list of write-offs, see our [LLC tax deductions list](/blog/llc-tax-deductions-list/).

### Should Your Childcare LLC Elect S-Corp Status?

Once net profit climbs well above a reasonable salary for your role, an S-corp election can reduce self-employment tax, because you pay payroll tax on your salary rather than on all profit. Here is a simplified scenario for illustration only. Suppose your center nets $90,000 after expenses. As a default sole-owner LLC, roughly the full amount is subject to self-employment tax. With an S-corp election, you might pay yourself a reasonable salary of, say, $60,000 and take the remainder as distributions, which are not subject to self-employment tax. The savings can be real, but they come with payroll filings, additional tax returns, and the IRS requirement that your salary be reasonable.

Most home daycares with modest profit do not benefit until income is solidly above that range. Compare the math in our [LLC vs S-corp guide](/blog/llc-vs-s-corp-which-is-better-for-taxes/) and see how owner pay works in [how to pay yourself from an LLC](/blog/how-to-pay-yourself-from-an-llc/). A CPA should run your numbers before you file Form 2553.

### Parents' Tax Documentation

Parents claiming the Child and Dependent Care Credit or using a dependent care FSA will ask for your name, address, and taxpayer ID. Providers can use [Form W-10](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-10) to collect this information from the parent's side. Using an EIN for the LLC, rather than your personal Social Security number, protects your SSN from being handed out to every enrolled family. That alone is a practical reason to get one.

## Step-by-Step: Starting Your Childcare LLC

1. **Talk to your licensing agency.** Confirm requirements, entity-name questions, and zoning before spending money.
2. **Choose a compliant name.** It should be distinguishable on your state's registry and match the name you will use on your license and marketing.
3. **Pick your state.** For a local daycare, form in the state where you operate. Forming elsewhere adds cost and foreign-registration requirements with little benefit.
4. **File Articles of Organization.** State fees commonly range from about $35 to $500 depending on the state, so check yours. Compare costs in our [how much does an LLC cost](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/) guide.
5. **Appoint a registered agent.** Required in every state. You can serve as your own, but your address becomes public and you must be available during business hours.
6. **Write an operating agreement.** Even single-member LLCs benefit, and banks and some insurers ask for one.
7. **Get an EIN.** It is free from the IRS and is needed for payroll, a business bank account, and tax forms for parents.
8. **Open a business bank account.** Keep tuition deposits and expenses separate from personal funds.
9. **Apply for your childcare license and permits** in the LLC's name, as discussed earlier.
10. **Buy insurance before the first child arrives.**
11. **Check federal beneficial ownership reporting.** FinCEN's rules changed in 2025, and domestic U.S. companies are currently exempt from reporting. Rules can shift, so verify the current status at [FinCEN.gov](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) before you file.

## Common Mistakes Childcare Owners Make

- **Skipping the LLC for a "small" home daycare.** Smaller programs face the same injury risks, and the home is the asset most exposed.
- **Co-mingling funds.** Paying tuition into a personal Venmo and groceries from the business account undermines liability protection and makes taxes painful.
- **Assuming homeowners insurance covers daycare operations.** Many policies exclude business activity entirely.
- **Missing annual reports.** If your LLC falls out of good standing, your licensing agency may notice. Check your state's requirements, such as our [Florida annual report guide](/blog/florida-llc-annual-report-requirements/) for one example.
- **Misclassifying workers.** Assistants who work set hours under your direction are usually employees, not contractors. Misclassification brings back taxes and penalties.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need an LLC to start a daycare?

No law requires one in most states, but it is strongly recommended. Without an entity, your personal assets are exposed to business debts and certain claims. Pair it with insurance and ask your state's licensing agency about naming requirements.

### How much does it cost to set up an LLC for a daycare and childcare business?

You will pay your state's filing fee, which typically falls between roughly $35 and $500, plus optional service fees. Services like ZenBusiness start at $0 for base formation. Budget separately for licensing fees, background checks, and insurance, which are often larger than the filing cost itself.

### Does an LLC protect me if a child gets hurt in my care?

Partially. It can protect personal assets from business-level claims, but it does not shield you from liability for your own negligent acts. That is why liability and abuse-and-molestation insurance coverage matter so much.

### Can I run a home daycare through an LLC?

Yes, in most states. Check local zoning, landlord or HOA restrictions, and your licensing agency's rules. Also confirm with your homeowners or renters insurer that you have appropriate business coverage.

### How are daycare owners taxed in 2026?

A single-member LLC files Schedule C and pays income tax plus 15.3% self-employment tax on net earnings. You may be able to deduct home-use costs, food, supplies, and training. An S-corp election might lower self-employment tax at higher profit levels. Work with a CPA.

### Do I need an EIN for my childcare LLC?

Practically, yes. It is free, it is required if you hire employees, and it lets you give parents an ID number other than your Social Security number.

### Is a PLLC required for childcare providers?

Generally not. Professional LLCs are typically required for licensed professions like doctors and attorneys. Childcare providers usually use a standard LLC, but confirm with your state.

### What happens if my LLC isn't in good standing?

You may lose liability protections, face late fees, or be administratively dissolved. Licensing agencies can also question your status. Keep your annual reports and registered agent current.
