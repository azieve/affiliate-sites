If you searched for a Harbor Compliance review, you are probably past the "should I form an LLC?" stage and into the more expensive question of how to keep it compliant. That matters, because formation is a one-time task. Compliance is a recurring obligation, and it usually costs more over the life of the business.

Harbor Compliance is a Portland, Oregon-based company that sells registered agent service, business licensing, foreign qualification, annual report filings, and related compliance work. It is not a budget formation shop. If you only need to file Articles of Organization in one state, a service like [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) will usually get you there for far less. ZenBusiness's Starter plan has been advertised at $0 plus state fees, and its paid tiers add things like an operating agreement and EIN help. This Harbor Compliance review covers where the company earns its price, where it doesn't, and which alternatives fit better.

**Quick verdict:** Harbor Compliance suits businesses with multi-state, licensing-heavy, or nonprofit compliance needs. For a simple single-state LLC, a formation-first provider is usually the better value.

## What Is Harbor Compliance?

Harbor Compliance positions itself as a compliance partner rather than a formation factory. Its service lines generally fall into these buckets:

- **Registered agent service:** a physical address in your state that receives legal papers and state notices.
- **Business license research and filing:** identifying which federal, state, and local licenses your business needs and helping file them.
- **Entity formation and foreign qualification:** forming an LLC or registering an existing one to do business in other states.
- **Annual report and statutory filings:** tracking deadlines and submitting required reports.
- **Nonprofit compliance:** charitable solicitation registrations and related filings.
- **Dissolution and withdrawal:** closing an entity or exiting a state properly.

That mix is unusual. Most LLC formation companies are built around the sale on day one. Harbor Compliance is built around staying in good standing on day 400. In my experience, that difference matters most to the businesses that get surprised by compliance, such as an e-commerce company that discovers it triggered registration in three states, or a contractor that never realized a county license was required.

## Harbor Compliance Review: Services and Features in Detail

### Registered Agent Service

The Harbor Compliance registered agent offering is the most common entry point. A registered agent must have a physical address in the state (not a P.O. box) and be available during business hours to accept service of process. If you want a primer first, our guide on [what a registered agent is and whether you need one](/blog/what-is-a-registered-agent-and-do-i-need-one/) covers the basics.

Harbor Compliance has historically advertised its registered agent service at roughly $99 per state, per year. Because pricing changes, confirm the current rate on their site before you buy. Practical points to check:

- **Per-state pricing:** the fee typically applies to each state where you hold a registration, so costs scale with your footprint.
- **Document handling:** scanned notices are delivered to you electronically, which matters if you travel or run a remote team.
- **Coverage:** a nationwide commercial agent network is one of the draws for multi-state owners.

For comparison, ZenBusiness has offered its registered agent service at about $119 per year as an add-on. LegalZoom's registered agent service has run around $249 per year after any included first-year period. Verify current rates, because both vendors adjust pricing and promotions often.

### Business Licensing

This is the area where I think Harbor Compliance is most differentiated. Most formation companies will sell you a "business license report" that is little more than a list of links. Harbor Compliance does research-and-filing work, which is closer to what a paralegal would do. If you run a regulated business, such as food service, contracting, staffing, or financial services, that hands-on help can save real time. It will not replace a licensed attorney for questions about legality or regulatory strategy.

### Foreign Qualification and Multi-State Compliance

If your LLC operates in a state other than where it was formed, you generally need to register as a "foreign" LLC there. Each state sets its own rules, fees, and annual requirements. We break down the process in our guide to [foreign LLC registration when doing business in another state](/blog/foreign-llc-registration-doing-business-in-another-state/). A multi-state footprint is exactly the scenario Harbor Compliance is built around, and it is also where DIY filing gets error-prone.

## Harbor Compliance Pricing: What to Expect

Harbor Compliance pricing is service-by-service rather than the tiered "Basic / Pro / Premium" bundle model used by ZenBusiness, LegalZoom, and Bizee. That has two consequences:

1. **Costs are more transparent per task,** but harder to compare at a glance.
2. **Totals add up with each state and each service.** A business registered in four states with a registered agent in each could pay several hundred dollars per year before any state fees, license fees, or filing charges.

Keep in mind that state fees are separate from any provider's service fee. Formation fees range widely, from about $35 to $500 depending on the state, and some states charge annual fees that can be much higher. California's $800 minimum franchise tax is the best-known example, covered in our [California LLC $800 franchise tax guide](/blog/california-llc-800-franchise-tax/).

I'd treat the figures above as directional. Always get a written quote for the exact services and states you need.

## Compliance Realities for 2026

A few things worth knowing as you plan your 2026 compliance calendar:

- **Beneficial ownership reporting has changed.** FinCEN's March 2025 interim final rule removed BOI reporting requirements for U.S.-formed companies and U.S. persons, narrowing the rule to foreign reporting companies. Rules like this can shift again, so check [FinCEN's BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) for the latest. Our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) tracks developments.
- **State annual reports and fees remain mandatory.** Missing them can lead to administrative dissolution, which is the compliance failure that costs the most to fix.
- **Tax classification is a separate decision.** An LLC can be taxed as a sole proprietorship, partnership, or elect S-corp or C-corp treatment. See the [IRS guidance on LLCs](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc) and discuss your situation with a CPA.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Harbor Compliance legit?

Harbor Compliance is an established compliance services company, not a scam. As with any vendor, read the current terms, confirm pricing in writing, and check recent customer reviews before you commit.

### How much does Harbor Compliance registered agent service cost?

The Harbor Compliance registered agent service has historically been advertised at around $99 per state per year. Pricing can change, so confirm the current rate directly with the company before purchasing.

### Does Harbor Compliance form LLCs?

Yes. Harbor Compliance offers entity formation as part of its broader service lineup, but it is mainly known for ongoing compliance work rather than low-cost startup bundles.

### Do I need a registered agent for my LLC?

Every state requires an LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical address in that state. You can serve as your own agent, but many owners hire a commercial agent for privacy and reliability.

### What are the best Harbor Compliance alternatives?

For most new LLC owners, [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) and [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) are the strongest alternatives. For privacy, look at [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/). For attorney-backed formation, consider [LLC Attorney](/go/llc-attorney/).

### Do I need to register my LLC in every state where I sell products?

Not necessarily. Foreign qualification is triggered by "doing business" in a state, and the definition varies. Physical presence, employees, or property often trigger it, while remote sales alone often do not. Because tax "nexus" rules differ from qualification rules, talk to a CPA or attorney about your specific situation.

### What happens if I miss an annual report or fall out of compliance?

States can charge late fees, mark your LLC as "not in good standing," or administratively dissolve it. That can affect your liability protection, bank access, and ability to do business. See our guide on [what happens if you don't renew your LLC](/blog/what-happens-if-you-don-t-renew-your-llc/).

### Is Harbor Compliance worth it for a single-state LLC?

Usually not. A single-state LLC has simple needs, and a formation-first provider generally offers better value. Harbor Compliance makes more sense once your obligations multiply.

## Final Thoughts

This Harbor Compliance review comes down to fit. If your business has outgrown a single state or a single license, a compliance specialist can be well worth the cost. If it hasn't, don't pay for complexity you don't have. Start with a formation-focused provider, then upgrade as your compliance footprint grows.
