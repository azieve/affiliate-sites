## What Is the Florida LLC Annual Report?

Contrary to what the name might suggest, the Florida annual report is **not a financial document**. You don't submit balance sheets, tax returns, income statements, or any figures related to your business's finances. The state doesn't care how much money you made — not through this filing, anyway.

What the annual report actually does is confirm (or update) your LLC's basic contact and ownership information in Florida's public business registry. Specifically, you're verifying:

- Your **registered agent's name and Florida street address**
- Your **principal office address and mailing address**
- The **names and addresses** of all managing members or managers

That's the entire scope of the filing. Think of it as the state asking once a year: "Is this information still accurate?" You have to answer, even if nothing has changed.

The legal basis for this requirement is [Florida Statute §605.0212](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2021/605.0212), which mandates annual reporting for all active Florida LLCs. The filing is processed through the [Florida Division of Corporations at sunbiz.org](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/) — the same portal used for the original LLC formation.

One common misconception worth addressing: the annual report is a *state* requirement, entirely separate from any federal filings like your tax return, quarterly estimated payments, or BOI report. Each has its own deadlines and rules. If you're not sure which federal filings apply to your LLC, our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) covers the federal beneficial ownership requirements that went into effect alongside state-level obligations.

## Florida LLC Annual Report Deadline and Filing Window

Understanding the Florida annual report deadline is the most important part of staying compliant. The filing window runs as follows:

- **Opens:** January 1
- **Standard deadline (no late fee):** May 1
- **Late fee period begins:** May 2
- **Administrative dissolution date:** Third Friday of September

In 2026, the third Friday of September falls on **September 18, 2026**. If you're reading this article after the May 1 deadline but before mid-September, your LLC can still be saved — but you'll pay the $400 late fee, and you need to act immediately.

**If you haven't filed yet for 2026:** File at sunbiz.org today. Every day you wait shrinks the window before dissolution.

**For newly formed LLCs:** If your LLC was registered with the state **after September 30**, you are exempt from filing an annual report in your first calendar year. An LLC formed in October or November 2026, for example, would not need to file until sometime in 2027. That said, the exemption only applies to the first year — after that, you're in the same annual cycle as every other Florida LLC.

## How Much Does the Florida LLC Annual Report Cost?

The Florida annual report fee structure is straightforward:

## How to File Your Florida LLC Annual Report Step-by-Step

Filing the Florida annual report is an online-only process. Florida does not accept paper filings. Here's exactly how it works:

**Step 1: Go to sunbiz.org**

Navigate directly to the [Florida Division of Corporations website](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/). On the homepage, look for the "File Annual Report" link. Avoid third-party sites that charge inflated service fees for what is a straightforward state-portal transaction — you can and should file directly.

**Step 2: Locate your LLC**

You'll need one of the following to pull up your LLC's record:
- Your **document number** — a letter-number code on your original Articles of Organization
- Your **Federal Employer Identification Number (EIN)**

If you don't have your document number handy, use the "Search Records" feature at sunbiz.org to look up your LLC by name.

**Step 3: Review your current information**

The system will display everything currently on file for your LLC. Go through each section carefully:

- **Registered agent:** Confirm the name and Florida street address are correct. P.O. boxes are not permitted for registered agent addresses.
- **Principal office address:** This can be in any state — it doesn't have to be a Florida address.
- **Manager/member information:** Every person or entity with management authority should be listed with their current address.

Update anything that's changed. If nothing has changed, you're simply confirming what's already there.

**Step 4: Pay the filing fee**

After reviewing and confirming your information, you'll reach the payment screen. The system will show the applicable fee — $138.75 if you're before May 1, or $538.75 if you're in the late filing period. Enter your payment information and submit.

**Step 5: Save your confirmation**

Once payment processes, you'll receive both an on-screen confirmation number and a confirmation email. Save both. Your LLC's status on sunbiz.org should update to reflect the current year's filing within 24 to 48 hours.

The entire process, if your information is unchanged, typically takes under 10 minutes. Even if you need to update several fields, you're looking at 15–20 minutes at most.

## What Information Do You Need Before You File?

Before sitting down at sunbiz.org, gather the following:

1. **Your Florida LLC document number** — check your original Articles of Organization or search sunbiz.org by company name

## What Happens If You Miss the Florida Annual Report Deadline?

This section matters more than any other for owners who are already past the May 1, 2026 deadline. Florida's enforcement has two distinct stages:

### Stage 1: The $400 Late Fee (May 2 – Third Friday of September)

Beginning the day after the May 1 deadline, the state automatically adds a $400 late fee to your filing. There is no grace period, no appeals process, and no exceptions — the fee is assessed by the state's system automatically. Your total cost to file jumps from $138.75 to $538.75 overnight.

You can still file and save your LLC during this period. It's painful financially, but your business remains intact.

### Stage 2: Administrative Dissolution (After the Third Friday of September)

If the annual report still hasn't been filed by the third Friday of September — September 18 in 2026 — Florida will **administratively dissolve** your LLC. This isn't a warning notice or a suspension. It's a termination of your LLC's legal existence in the state.

Dissolution has real consequences:

- **Liability protection disappears.** The "limited" in limited liability company ceases to apply. Your personal assets can become exposed to business debts and judgments.
- **The LLC can't legally enter contracts.** Any agreements signed in the name of a dissolved LLC may be unenforceable.
- **You lose standing to sue or defend in Florida courts** as the LLC.
- **Business licenses and permits** tied to the LLC may become invalid.

In my experience reviewing business compliance issues, this is where people get into serious trouble. I've heard from business owners who didn't realize their LLC was dissolved for months — often because they assumed the state would send a physical warning letter. Florida does not. The dissolution happens on a date, and the burden is on you to file before that date.

**Reinstating a dissolved Florida LLC** is possible but more expensive and more involved. You'll need to file a reinstatement application through sunbiz.org, pay all past-due annual reports with accumulated late fees, plus a separate reinstatement fee. Our guide on [how to reinstate a dissolved LLC](/blog/how-to-reinstate-a-dissolved-llc/) covers the full process. But no reinstatement guide is a substitute for simply filing on time — the cost and hassle of reinstatement far exceeds the $538.75 late filing fee.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does the Florida LLC annual report cost in 2026?**

The standard Florida annual report fee is **$138.75**, payable online at sunbiz.org. If filed after May 1, a $400 late fee is added, bringing the total to **$538.75**. After the third Friday of September (September 18 in 2026), the LLC is administratively dissolved and the standard filing is no longer accepted — only reinstatement is possible.

**When is the Florida LLC annual report due?**

The annual report is due by **May 1** each year. The filing window opens January 1, so you can file as early as New Year's Day. For LLCs formed after September 30, the first annual report isn't required until the following calendar year.

**Can I file the Florida LLC annual report by mail?**

No. Florida only accepts annual reports filed **online at sunbiz.org**. Paper filings are not accepted. The online process is straightforward and typically takes under 10 minutes.

**What happens if I miss the Florida annual report deadline?**

If you miss the May 1 deadline, you'll owe a $400 late fee (total: $538.75) and can still file until the third Friday of September. After that date, Florida administratively dissolves your LLC, stripping it of liability protection and legal standing. Reinstatement is possible but costs more and requires additional paperwork.

**Do I need an accountant to file the Florida annual report?**

No. The Florida annual report contains no financial data and requires no professional certification. Any member or manager can file directly at sunbiz.org. If your LLC has ownership changes, registered agent issues, or compliance complications, a formation service or attorney can assist — but for a standard filing, it's a DIY task.

**Does every Florida LLC have to file an annual report?**

Yes — every active Florida LLC must file annually, regardless of revenue, activity level, or number of members. A single-member LLC that earned nothing in the past year still must file. The only exception is for LLCs formed after September 30, which skip the annual report in their first calendar year.

**What if my LLC's information hasn't changed since last year?**

You still must file. Even if every piece of information is identical to what's on file, the state requires you to confirm it annually. Think of it as an affirmation rather than an update — Florida needs you to say "yes, this is still correct," not just assume it.

**How long does it take for the Florida annual report to be processed?**

Online filings at sunbiz.org are typically processed immediately or within 24 hours. Your LLC's status page on sunbiz.org will reflect the updated filing date once processed. There's no option to expedite processing since online filings are already near-instant.
