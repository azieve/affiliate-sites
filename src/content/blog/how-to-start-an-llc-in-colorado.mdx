## Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Colorado LLC

Your LLC name must comply with the [Colorado Secretary of State's](https://www.sos.colorado.gov/) naming rules before you can file.

**Required elements:**
- Must include "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." as part of the business name
- Abbreviations like "Ltd. Liability Co." are also acceptable

**Prohibited elements:**
- Cannot suggest your business is a government entity (avoid "Federal," "National," "State," or "United States")
- Cannot be deceptively similar to an existing Colorado-registered business name
- Certain words — "Bank," "Insurance," "Engineer," "Attorney" — require additional regulatory approval or licensing to use

**How to check name availability:**

Run a free name search through the Colorado Secretary of State's business database at sos.colorado.gov before you file. A name that comes back clean (no close matches) is ready to use. If you find something similar, it's worth calling the SOS office to confirm your name won't be rejected on similarity grounds — a rejection wastes time and delays your filing.

**Name reservation:**

If you're not ready to file yet, you can reserve a name with the Colorado Secretary of State for $25, which locks it for 120 days. This is optional, but useful if you're still arranging your registered agent or finalizing your operating agreement.

**Federal trademark check:**

Also run your proposed name through the USPTO's trademark database (USPTO.gov) — especially if you plan to operate nationally, sell products online, or eventually expand beyond Colorado. A name can be available at the state level and still infringe on a federally registered trademark, which creates legal exposure you want to avoid from day one.

## Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent in Colorado

Every Colorado LLC must designate a registered agent — a person or company authorized to receive official government correspondence, legal notices, and service of process on the LLC's behalf. To understand the full scope of this role, read our guide on [what a registered agent is](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/).

**Colorado registered agent requirements:**
- Must maintain a physical Colorado street address (no PO boxes)
- Must be consistently available during standard business hours (Monday–Friday, 9am–5pm)
- Must be at least 18 years old if serving as an individual

**Your three main options:**

**1. Yourself** — Free, but your name and home or office address become part of the public record. You also need to be reliably present during business hours every weekday, which doesn't work if you travel or work remotely.

## Step 3: File Your Articles of Organization with the Colorado Secretary of State

Filing Articles of Organization is the legal act that creates your Colorado LLC. You're submitting a foundational document to the state that officially registers your business entity.

**Filing options:**
- **Online (recommended):** sos.colorado.gov — $50 fee, processed same-day in most cases
- **By mail:** $50 fee, processing takes 5–7 business days

**What the Articles of Organization require:**

1. Your LLC's proposed name (confirmed available)
2. The LLC's principal office address (your home address is fine)
3. Your registered agent's full name and Colorado street address
4. Whether the LLC will be member-managed or manager-managed
5. Your name and signature as the organizer (can be any member or a third party)

**Member-managed vs. manager-managed:**

The majority of small LLCs choose member-managed, where all owners share in day-to-day decisions and authority. Manager-managed is appropriate when some owners are silent or passive investors who don't want operational involvement, or when you bring in a non-owner to run the business. This distinction matters for your operating agreement and for how authority is represented to third parties like banks and vendors.

**After approval:**

You'll receive a stamped confirmation from the Colorado Secretary of State, typically within hours for online filings. Save this document — you'll need it to open a business bank account, apply for an EIN, and set up any required business licenses.

## Step 4: Create a Colorado LLC Operating Agreement

Colorado does not legally require an operating agreement, but this is one area where treating "optional" as "skippable" is a costly mistake.

An operating agreement is the internal governing document for your LLC. Without one, Colorado's default statutory rules fill every gap — and those defaults may not match what you actually want. For instance, Colorado law defaults to equal profit sharing among all members regardless of how much capital each person contributed. That surprises a lot of multi-member LLC owners who assumed profits would follow investment percentages.

Our [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) covers what a complete agreement should include. At minimum, your agreement should address:

- **Ownership percentages** — who owns what share of the company
- **Capital contributions** — what each member brought to the table at formation
- **Profit and loss allocation** — how earnings and losses are divided among members
- **Voting rights** — which decisions require a simple majority versus unanimous agreement
- **Management structure** — confirms member-managed or manager-managed authority
- **Member exit provisions** — what happens when a member wants to sell, retire, or leave
- **Dissolution procedure** — how the business is wound down if needed

Even for single-member LLCs, an operating agreement reinforces the legal separation between you and your business. Courts sometimes "pierce the corporate veil" and hold LLC owners personally liable for business debts when the LLC looks like it's indistinct from the individual — a solid operating agreement is evidence that you're running a real, separate business entity.

## Step 5: Obtain an EIN and Open a Business Bank Account

**Getting an Employer Identification Number (EIN):**

An EIN is your LLC's federal tax identification number — the business equivalent of a Social Security number. You need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, file federal business taxes, and apply for certain licenses.

Apply directly through the [IRS website](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). It's free, takes about 15 minutes online, and the IRS issues your EIN immediately upon completion. There is no reason to pay a third-party service to do this for you — the IRS application is straightforward and publicly available.

Multi-member LLCs always require an EIN. Single-member LLCs with no employees can technically use their Social Security number for tax purposes, but obtaining a separate EIN is cleaner and adds another layer of separation between personal and business identity.

**Opening a business bank account:**

Most banks require your Articles of Organization confirmation, your EIN, and government-issued ID to open a business checking account. Online business banks — Mercury, Relay, and Bluevine are popular in 2026 — offer fast account opening, no minimum balances, and low fees that suit early-stage LLCs well. Traditional banks like Chase, Bank of America, or a local credit union are solid if you prefer in-person service or need cash deposit capabilities.

Never mix personal and business finances. Commingling funds is one of the most common ways LLC owners inadvertently weaken their liability protection — and one of the first things a plaintiff's attorney will look for.

## Step 6: Understand Colorado's Ongoing Compliance Requirements

Forming your LLC is a one-time event. Keeping it in good standing is an ongoing responsibility that runs parallel to operating your business.

**Colorado Periodic Report:**

Colorado calls its annual filing a "Periodic Report" rather than an annual report — a distinction that trips up business owners who search for "Colorado annual report" and get confused by the terminology.

- **Cost:** $10 per year (online filing)
- **Due date:** During your LLC's anniversary month each year (the month in which you originally filed your Articles of Organization)
- **Filing method:** Online through the Colorado Secretary of State's website
- **Consequence of missing it:** Your LLC can be administratively dissolved — meaning it loses its legal standing and liability protection

At $10 per year, Colorado's Periodic Report fee is one of the lowest in the country. Most states charge $50–$500 annually. This is a genuine long-term financial advantage of forming in Colorado versus states with higher ongoing compliance costs.

For a complete annual fee comparison, see our [Colorado LLC cost and fees breakdown](/blog/colorado-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/).

**Business licenses and permits:**

Colorado has no state-level general business license, but local requirements vary significantly. Denver requires a Denver Business License for most businesses operating within city limits. Boulder, Colorado Springs, and other municipalities have their own requirements. Check your specific city or county before assuming you're covered.

Industry-specific licenses — contractors, healthcare providers, food service operators, financial services professionals — are regulated by Colorado's relevant state licensing boards and are separate from the LLC formation itself.

**State income taxes:**

By default, a Colorado LLC is a pass-through entity — the LLC pays no state income tax directly. Instead, profits and losses flow to members, who report them on their personal Colorado income tax returns at the flat 4.4% state rate.

If you elect S-Corp tax status for your Colorado LLC (a move that can reduce self-employment tax once the business is earning above roughly $40,000–$60,000 in net profit), you'll file a separate Colorado corporate income tax return. This is a meaningful tax planning opportunity for some businesses — consult a CPA before making that election, as it adds administrative overhead.

**BOI Report (Federal):**

Most LLCs are required to file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN under the federal Corporate Transparency Act. This requirement — and its associated deadlines — has been subject to significant legal challenges and regulatory updates through 2026. Verify current filing requirements directly at FinCEN.gov or through our detailed [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) before assuming you're exempt.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Colorado?**

The minimum cost to start an LLC in Colorado is $50 — the Articles of Organization filing fee paid to the Colorado Secretary of State. If you use a formation service, add $0–$299 depending on the provider and plan. Annual costs after formation include a $10 Periodic Report fee and registered agent service ($49–$299/year) if you use a professional. Total first-year cost typically falls between $50 and $650.

**How long does it take to form a Colorado LLC?**

Online filings through the Colorado Secretary of State are typically processed same-day, often within a few hours of submission. Mail filings take 5–7 business days. If you use a formation service, factor in 1–3 additional business days for document preparation before the state receives your filing.

**Do I need a registered agent to start an LLC in Colorado?**

Yes. Colorado law requires every LLC to designate a registered agent with a physical Colorado street address that is staffed during normal business hours. You can serve as your own registered agent, name a trusted colleague, or hire a professional service — the choice depends on your privacy needs and availability.

**Does Colorado require an LLC operating agreement?**

No. Colorado does not legally require an operating agreement for LLCs. However, operating without one leaves your LLC governed entirely by Colorado's default statutory rules — which may not reflect your actual intentions around profit sharing, voting rights, or member exits. An operating agreement is strongly recommended for all Colorado LLCs, especially multi-member structures.

**What is the Colorado Periodic Report and when is it due?**

Colorado's Periodic Report is the state's equivalent of an annual report. It costs $10 and is due during the anniversary month of your LLC's original formation date each year. File it online at the Colorado Secretary of State's website. Failure to file can result in administrative dissolution of your LLC, which strips your liability protection until reinstated.

**Can a non-resident form a Colorado LLC?**

Yes. Colorado has no residency or citizenship requirement for LLC members or organizers. Non-U.S. residents and foreign nationals can form a Colorado LLC, though international tax implications vary significantly — consult a tax professional familiar with cross-border business structures before filing.

**What taxes does a Colorado LLC pay?**

By default, a Colorado LLC is a pass-through entity. The LLC itself pays no Colorado state income tax. Instead, profits and losses pass through to members, who pay Colorado's 4.4% flat state income tax on their share. Federal self-employment tax (15.3% on net earnings up to the Social Security wage base) also applies to active members. LLCs can elect S-Corp or C-Corp tax treatment for different federal tax results — a decision worth discussing with a CPA.

**Do I need to file a BOI report for my Colorado LLC?**

Most Colorado LLCs are covered by the federal Beneficial Ownership Information reporting requirement under the Corporate Transparency Act. The enforcement landscape has been volatile due to legal challenges that continued through 2025 and into 2026. Verify current requirements and deadlines at FinCEN.gov directly, or check our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for updated guidance.
