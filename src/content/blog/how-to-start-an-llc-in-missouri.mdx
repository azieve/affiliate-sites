## Why Form an LLC in Missouri?

Before getting into mechanics, it's worth understanding what makes Missouri an attractive formation state. Not every entrepreneur needs to form where they live — though for most people, that's the right call — so it helps to know what you're actually getting.

**Low upfront cost.** The Missouri Secretary of State charges $50 for online Articles of Organization filings and $105 for paper submissions. Compare that to California, which charges $70 to file and then hits you with an $800 annual minimum franchise tax, or Massachusetts, where formation costs $500 just to file. Missouri's cost structure is hard to argue with.

**No annual report requirement for LLCs.** This is Missouri's most underrated advantage and the one most people miss when comparing states. Florida LLCs owe $138.75 per year in annual report fees. New York requires a biennial statement. California has its $800 minimum franchise tax. Missouri LLCs face none of these recurring filing requirements — which eliminates an administrative deadline and a meaningful recurring cost.

**Strong liability protection.** Missouri's LLC statute follows a well-established framework that provides robust personal asset protection. As long as you maintain proper separation between personal and business finances — separate accounts, proper record-keeping — your personal savings, home, and other assets are generally shielded from business debts and lawsuits.

**No state franchise tax on LLCs.** Missouri does impose a personal income tax that applies to LLC pass-through profits, but there is no separate franchise or privilege tax levied simply for having an LLC, unlike states such as Texas or Delaware.

One caveat: if you're based in another state and considering Missouri purely for cost advantages, you'll likely still need to register as a foreign LLC in your actual home state if that's where you conduct business. The savings can evaporate when you're paying registered agent fees and compliance costs in two states. For most people reading this, forming in Missouri where you actually operate is the right move.

## How to Start an LLC in Missouri: 6 Steps

### Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Missouri LLC

Your LLC name must satisfy a few Missouri requirements before you file anything:

- It must contain "Limited Liability Company," "L.L.C.," or "LLC."
- It must be **distinguishable** from all other business names already registered with the Missouri Secretary of State — not identical, and not confusingly similar.
- It cannot imply a different legal structure (no "Corporation" or "Inc." unless you are one).
- Certain regulated words — "Bank," "Trust," "Insurance," "Attorney" — require additional approvals or documentation from relevant Missouri regulatory agencies.

**Check name availability first.** Use the [Missouri Secretary of State's free business entity search](https://www.sos.mo.gov/business/corporations/soskb/Corp.asp) to verify your chosen name isn't already taken. This takes five minutes and can save you a rejected filing.

**Name reservation:** If you're not ready to file immediately, Missouri allows you to reserve a business name for 60 days by filing an Application for Reservation of Name and paying a $25 fee.

**DBA (fictitious name) registration:** If you want to operate your LLC under a name other than its legal name, you'll need to register that fictitious name separately with the Missouri Secretary of State.

I've seen too many business owners skip the name availability check and submit their Articles of Organization, only to receive a rejection notice two weeks later because their name was too close to an existing registration. It's an entirely avoidable delay that sets your formation back by weeks. Run the search first, then file.

### Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Every Missouri LLC must designate a [registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) — an individual or company responsible for receiving official legal and government documents (including service of process, tax notices, and state correspondence) on behalf of the LLC during regular business hours.

Missouri registered agent requirements:
- Must maintain a **physical street address** in Missouri (P.O. boxes are not permitted)
- Must be available during standard business hours
- Can be yourself, a member of the LLC, or a professional registered agent company

### Step 3: File Articles of Organization With the Missouri Secretary of State

The Articles of Organization is the document that officially creates your Missouri LLC in the eyes of the state. You file this with the Missouri Secretary of State's Business Services Division.

**Information required in your Articles of Organization:**
- LLC name (exactly as you want it registered)
- Registered office address (your registered agent's physical Missouri address)
- Registered agent name
- Whether the LLC is member-managed or manager-managed
- Name and address of the organizer (the person filing — doesn't have to be an owner)
- Effective date, if you want it different from the filing date

**How to file:**
- **Online** at sos.mo.gov: **$50 fee**, processing typically **3–5 business days**
- **By mail** to the Missouri Secretary of State: **$105 fee**, processing **10–15 business days**
- **Through a formation service:** [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/), [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/), and others handle the filing for you

The online route is the right call in almost every situation. You save $55 over the paper option and get your LLC confirmed faster. If you use a formation service, they'll handle the online submission as part of their fee structure.

### Step 4: Draft an Operating Agreement

Missouri does not legally require LLCs to have an operating agreement. That doesn't mean you should skip it — quite the opposite.

An [operating agreement](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) establishes the internal rules of your LLC:
- Ownership percentages among members
- How profits and losses are allocated and distributed
- Voting rights and how major decisions get made
- What happens when a member wants to exit, sells their interest, or dies
- Procedures for adding new members
- What triggers dissolution

Without an operating agreement, Missouri's default LLC statutes govern all of these questions. Those defaults may be fine for a single-member LLC with no complex needs, but for any multi-member structure, relying on statutory defaults is a significant risk. Disagreements about profit distribution or decision-making authority — when there's no document to fall back on — become far more expensive to resolve.

**Cost:** A basic operating agreement can be drafted using free templates. [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) includes an operating agreement template in its Pro plan ($99/year), making the full package genuinely affordable. LegalZoom includes one starting at its Pro tier ($249+), though at substantially higher cost for largely the same output. For complex multi-member structures with specific profit-sharing arrangements or investor provisions, it's worth having an attorney review the document before you sign.

### Step 5: Get an EIN From the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your LLC's federal tax identification number — functionally equivalent to a Social Security Number but for your business entity. You'll need one to:

- Open a business bank account (most banks require this)
- Hire employees
- File federal and Missouri state business taxes
- Apply for certain business licenses and permits

**Cost: Free.** Apply directly through the [IRS EIN online application](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) in about 15 minutes. Your EIN is issued immediately upon completing the application.

Technical note: a single-member LLC with no employees can use the owner's Social Security Number for federal tax purposes. But using an EIN is strongly advisable regardless — it protects your SSN from unnecessary exposure and maintains the clean personal-business separation that underlies your liability protection.

### Step 6: Obtain Business Licenses and Register for Missouri State Taxes

Missouri does not issue a single statewide general business license. What you need depends on your industry and location:

- **Missouri sales tax permit:** Required if you sell taxable goods or certain services in Missouri. Register through the Missouri Department of Revenue's business portal.
- **Professional licenses:** Healthcare providers, contractors, accountants, real estate agents, and many other professions require state-level licensing through Missouri's applicable regulatory boards.
- **City and county licenses:** Many Missouri municipalities — including Kansas City, St. Louis, Springfield, and Columbia — require local business licenses. Requirements and fees vary widely, so check directly with your city's business licensing office.
- **Federal permits:** Certain industries (food processing, firearms, financial services, transportation) require federal licensing regardless of state.

The Missouri Business Portal at business.mo.gov is a useful starting point for mapping which requirements apply to your specific situation.

## Missouri LLC Ongoing Compliance Requirements

This is where Missouri genuinely earns its business-friendly reputation. Ongoing compliance for a Missouri LLC in 2026 is among the lightest in the country:

**Annual report: Not required.** Missouri is one of a small group of states that does not require LLCs to file annual reports or pay annual renewal fees. This eliminates a recurring deadline and saves real money compared to states like California ($800+ per year), Florida ($138.75/year), or Illinois ($75/year).

**Registered agent maintenance:** Your LLC must always have an active registered agent with a valid Missouri physical address. If your agent resigns, moves, or you change providers, file a Statement of Change of Registered Agent with the Missouri Secretary of State. The fee is typically $10.

**Operating agreement updates:** Whenever ownership changes, a member is added or removed, or significant operational decisions alter the structure of the business, update your operating agreement to reflect current reality.

**Federal BOI Report:** Under the Corporate Transparency Act, most LLCs formed in 2026 must file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN — the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network — within 90 days of formation. This is a federal requirement, not a Missouri-specific one, but it surprises many new business owners who weren't tracking it. See our [BOI Report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for full filing instructions and deadline details.

**State income taxes:** Missouri LLC profits pass through to members and are reported on individual federal and Missouri state income tax returns. Missouri has a graduated individual income tax rate; consult a CPA for current rates and whether an S-corp election might reduce your overall tax burden once your LLC's net profit reaches a meaningful threshold.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Starting an LLC in Missouri

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Missouri?**

The minimum cost is $50 — the state filing fee for online Articles of Organization. If you add a professional registered agent, budget $125–$299 per year. Formation service fees range from $0 to $199 depending on the provider and plan. Most Missouri LLC owners spend $100–$400 in total during year one, making it one of the more affordable states to form in.

**How long does it take to form a Missouri LLC?**

Online filings with the Missouri Secretary of State typically process in 3–5 business days. Paper filings take 10–15 business days. Missouri doesn't offer a standard expedited processing option through the Secretary of State, but most formation services submit online and can get your documents back within the normal 3–5 day window.

**Does Missouri require an annual report for LLCs?**

No. Missouri does not require LLCs to file annual reports or pay annual renewal fees to maintain good standing. This is one of the state's most significant advantages for small business owners. You'll still need to maintain a registered agent and stay current on federal requirements like the BOI report, but there's no Missouri-specific annual filing deadline to track.

**Do I need a registered agent for a Missouri LLC?**

Yes. Missouri law requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in Missouri at all times. You can serve as your own registered agent if you have a Missouri address and can reliably receive documents during business hours, or you can hire a professional service for $49–$299/year. Professional services keep your personal address off public records and provide more reliable document handling.

**Does Missouri require an operating agreement?**

Missouri does not legally require an operating agreement, but having one is strongly recommended — especially for multi-member LLCs. Without an operating agreement, your LLC is governed by Missouri's default statutory rules, which may not reflect how you actually want to run the business. Issues around profit distribution, voting, and member exit procedures become significantly harder to resolve without a governing document.

**Can I be my own registered agent in Missouri?**

Yes, provided you have a physical street address in Missouri (not a P.O. Box) and are available during normal business hours to receive legal documents. Many solo business owners do this to save money. The main drawback: your address becomes part of the public record, and if you're ever away when a time-sensitive legal document arrives, you may miss important deadlines.

**What taxes does a Missouri LLC pay?**

By default, a single-member LLC is taxed as a disregarded entity (like a sole proprietorship) and a multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership. In both cases, profits pass through to members and are taxed at the individual level on both federal returns and Missouri state income tax returns. Missouri has a graduated income tax rate — consult a CPA for the current applicable rate given legislative changes in recent years. LLC members who actively participate in the business also owe federal self-employment taxes on their share of profits. Once your net profit grows meaningfully, an S-corp election can reduce self-employment tax exposure — your CPA can model whether the math works for your situation.

**What is the difference between member-managed and manager-managed?**

In a member-managed LLC (the default and most common structure), all members have authority to make decisions and act on behalf of the business. In a manager-managed LLC, designated managers — who may or may not be members — handle day-to-day operations and have authority to bind the company. You declare this structure in your Articles of Organization. Manager-managed is most useful when you have passive investors who want economic participation without operational involvement.
