Both will file your Articles of Organization and serve as your registered agent. The way they treat your money, your privacy, and your inbox afterward could hardly be more different.

Before we get into the head-to-head: if your goal is simply the best overall value on a standard LLC, neither of these is automatically the right answer in 2026. [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) starts at $0 + state fees, bundles a worry-free compliance guarantee, and consistently beats LegalZoom on both price and support quality. That said, the Northwest Registered Agent vs LegalZoom matchup is a genuinely useful one to settle — because each of these two wins decisively in specific situations, and knowing which situation you're in will save you a few hundred dollars and a lot of junk mail.

Here's the complete breakdown: pricing, registered agent renewal costs, privacy practices, support, and the hidden fees that don't show up until checkout.

## What Is Northwest Registered Agent?

[Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) is a Spokane, Washington–based formation and registered agent company that has spent two decades building its brand around a single promise: "Privacy by Default." Unlike most competitors, Northwest doesn't sell or share your data with third parties, uses its own physical office addresses to keep your home address off the public record where possible, and assigns real people — it calls them "Corporate Guides" — to answer your questions instead of routing you through an offshore call center.

**Northwest Registered Agent pricing in 2026:**
- LLC formation: $39 + state fees
- Registered agent service: $125/year (free for the first year when bundled with formation)
- Operating agreement: Included free
- Annual report reminders and filing: Available as add-on
- EIN service: Available as add-on (~$50)
- BOI report filing assistance: Available as add-on

The thing that consistently shows up in a Northwest Registered Agent review is mail handling. Northwest scans every document it receives as your registered agent and uploads it to your online account the same day — not just the legally required service-of-process documents, but general mail too. For business owners who use the registered agent's address to keep their home address private, that local-scanning-and-uploading workflow is the entire point. In my experience advising small founders, the single biggest regret I hear about cheap registered agents is the flood of data-broker mail that follows; Northwest is one of the few that genuinely avoids creating that problem.

## What Is LegalZoom?

[LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) is the original online legal services company, founded in 2001 and now publicly traded on the NASDAQ under the ticker LZ — a detail worth remembering, because a public company answers to shareholders, and that pressure shows up in its pricing and upsell strategy. More than four million businesses have been formed through LegalZoom, and its brand recognition is unmatched. Name recognition, however, is not the same as value.

**LegalZoom LLC formation pricing in 2026:**
- Basic: $0 + state fees (Articles of Organization filing only — no operating agreement, no EIN)
- Pro: $249 + state fees (adds operating agreement, EIN, one year of registered agent service)
- Premium: $299 + state fees (adds attorney guidance and business document templates)
- Registered agent renewal: $249/year starting in year two

The recurring theme in any honest assessment of LegalZoom pricing in 2026 is the renewal math. The "$0 Basic" headline is real, but it covers only the bare state filing. By the time most users add an operating agreement, EIN, and registered agent service, a "free" formation has quietly become a $400–$600 experience. And the registered agent renewal at $249/year is the highest in the industry — almost exactly double Northwest's $125/year. For a closer look at how those upsells stack up at checkout, see our [LegalZoom Review](/reviews/legalzoom/).

## Privacy: Where Northwest Pulls Ahead

If there's one dimension where the Northwest Registered Agent vs LegalZoom comparison isn't close, it's privacy. Northwest's entire business model is built on not monetizing your information. It doesn't sell your data to marketers, it uses its own addresses to shield yours from the public record where the state allows it, and it staffs the phones with people who can actually answer a compliance question.

LegalZoom, by contrast, operates like the large public company it is. Its privacy practices are standard for the industry — which is to say acceptable, but not a selling point. If keeping your home address off public filings and avoiding the data-broker mail cascade is a priority, Northwest is purpose-built for exactly that. This is also why Northwest is frequently the recommended registered agent in guides about [anonymous LLC formation](/blog/anonymous-llc-how-to-form-one/) and privacy-first states.

It's worth understanding what a registered agent actually does before you over- or under-pay for one. ## Support and User Experience

Northwest's Corporate Guides are the most praised element of nearly every Northwest Registered Agent review, and for good reason — you reach a knowledgeable person quickly, and they don't try to upsell you mid-conversation. The trade-off is a website and dashboard that feel more utilitarian than polished. Northwest optimizes for substance over shine.

LegalZoom's platform is slicker and more feature-rich, with a polished onboarding flow and a genuinely broad menu of legal products beyond formation. The downside is the upsell architecture: the checkout flow is engineered to add products, and support quality varies by the tier you've purchased. You'll get faster, more attentive help on a Premium plan than on the $0 Basic.

For a founder who values being left alone with their filing handled correctly, Northwest's approach tends to win. For a founder who wants a comprehensive legal dashboard and is comfortable navigating upsells, LegalZoom's breadth is a real advantage.

## Hidden Costs and Gotchas

Every LLC formation service has fine print. Here's what to watch on each:

**Northwest gotchas:**
- The headline $39 formation fee is service-only; you still pay your state's filing fee (anywhere from $35 in Montana to $500 in Massachusetts).
- EIN service and BOI report assistance are paid add-ons, not bundled.
- The dashboard is functional but dated compared to flashier competitors.

**LegalZoom gotchas:**
- The $0 Basic plan is genuinely bare — no operating agreement, no EIN, minimal support.
- Registered agent renewal jumps to $249/year after the first year, the steepest in the industry.
- Aggressive upsells at checkout can push a "free" formation toward $500+.
- Subscription products (LZ Legal Advantage at $249/year) renew automatically.

The single biggest long-term cost difference isn't the formation fee — it's the recurring registered agent charge. If you keep your LLC for a decade, choosing Northwest over LegalZoom for registered agent service alone saves you roughly $1,240. That's the kind of number that should anchor your decision more than a one-time $39-vs-$0 formation comparison.

## A Note on Compliance in 2026

Whichever service you choose, remember that forming the LLC is the beginning, not the end, of your compliance obligations. Most LLCs must file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with [FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/boi), and all must keep a registered agent on file and stay current on state annual reports. Both Northwest and LegalZoom offer add-ons to help with these — Northwest's tends to be more transparently priced — but the underlying legal responsibility is yours. The [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed) also recommends consulting a tax professional about how your LLC should be taxed, since the default pass-through treatment isn't always optimal once your business starts generating meaningful profit. If you ever need to swap providers, the process is straightforward and covered in our [guide to changing your registered agent](/blog/how-to-change-registered-agent/).

Northwest charges $39 + state fees for LLC formation, with the first year of registered agent service included free. After year one, registered agent service is $125/year. Northwest, decisively. Northwest's registered agent service is $125/year versus LegalZoom's $249/year — a $124 annual difference. Over five years that's $620, and over a decade roughly $1,240. **Does Northwest Registered Agent really protect my privacy?**
