## Why Maryland Is a Solid Choice for Your LLC

Not every business owner has the luxury of choosing which state to form in — if you live and work in Maryland, forming there is almost always the right call. Registering in Delaware or Wyoming and then foreign-qualifying in Maryland adds cost and paperwork that rarely pays off for small and mid-size businesses.

Maryland offers some genuine advantages worth knowing:

- **No franchise tax**: Maryland doesn't impose a franchise tax on LLCs, unlike Delaware's minimum $300 annual franchise fee or California's punishing $800 minimum tax.
- **Strong liability protection**: Maryland's LLC Act provides robust personal asset protection consistent with the best LLC statutes in the country.
- **Pass-through taxation by default**: Profits and losses flow directly to members' personal returns — no entity-level federal income tax.
- **Federal contracting proximity**: Maryland's D.C. suburbs are a hub for government contractors, and a registered LLC is often the baseline for vendor eligibility.

One caveat worth flagging early: Maryland's $300 annual report fee is on the higher end. Compare that to Ohio ($0 annual report), Wyoming ($60 minimum), or New Mexico ($0 annual report). If you're budgeting your first year, plan for $400–$700 total all-in.

## How to Start an LLC in Maryland: Step-by-Step

### Step 1: Choose Your LLC Name

Your Maryland LLC name must include "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." The name must be distinguishable from any existing business entity registered with SDAT.

Check availability using the [SDAT Business Entity Search tool](https://sdat.dat.maryland.gov/SDAToqq/default.aspx) before you file anything. You can also reserve a name for 30 days by submitting a Name Reservation application for $25 — useful if you're not quite ready to file.

Maryland naming rules to keep in mind:
- Cannot imply government affiliation (e.g., "FBI," "Treasury Department")
- Regulated words like "Bank," "Insurance," or "Attorney" require additional agency approval
- The name must be genuinely distinguishable — not just different by punctuation or an extra space

### Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Maryland law requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical Maryland street address. The registered agent receives legal documents, state notices, and service of process on behalf of your LLC during regular business hours.

You have three options: serve as your own registered agent, appoint a trusted individual, or hire a professional registered agent service. For most business owners, a professional service is worth the $49–$125/year cost. It ensures you don't miss critical legal notices and keeps your home address off public records.

### Step 3: File Articles of Organization with SDAT

The Articles of Organization is the document that officially creates your Maryland LLC. You file it with the Maryland State Department of Assessments and Taxation (SDAT).

**Filing options:**
- **Online**: Through the Maryland Business Express portal — typically 5–7 business days for standard processing
- **By mail**: Maryland SDAT, Charter Division, 301 W. Preston St., Baltimore, MD 21201
- **In person**: Drop-off at the SDAT office in Baltimore

**Base filing fee: $100**

Expedited processing is available:
- **7-business-day expedited**: $150 total (add $50 to the base fee)
- **Same-day processing**: $425 total (add $325 to the base fee)

The Articles of Organization require:
- LLC name and principal office address
- Registered agent name and Maryland street address
- Purpose of the LLC (can be stated broadly: "any lawful purpose")
- Organizer name and signature (the organizer does not need to be a member)

### Step 4: Draft an Operating Agreement

Maryland does not legally require an LLC to have an operating agreement — but I've seen too many business disputes, especially in multi-member LLCs, that could have been settled in minutes if the owners had documented the terms from day one. Skip it at your own risk.

An operating agreement establishes:
- Member ownership percentages and capital contributions
- How profits and losses are allocated and distributed
- Voting rights and decision-making procedures
- What happens when a member exits, dies, or wants to sell their interest
- Procedures for admitting new members

### Step 5: Get an EIN from the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) functions like a Social Security number for your business. You'll need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal and state taxes.

Multi-member LLCs are required to have an EIN. Single-member LLCs are not required to have one unless they have employees, but it's still strongly recommended — it lets you keep your Social Security Number off business documents and vendor agreements.

Apply for free directly through the [IRS EIN online application](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). Approval is typically instant for online applications, and there is no fee.

### Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

Keeping business and personal finances separate is non-negotiable for maintaining your liability protection. Courts can "pierce the corporate veil" if you commingle funds — exposing your personal assets to business debts and judgments.

To open a business account you'll typically need your EIN, a copy of your Articles of Organization, and your operating agreement. Most traditional banks, credit unions, and online business banks (Mercury, Relay, Bluevine) accept these documents and can have an account open in one to three business days.

### Step 7: File Your BOI Report

Under the Corporate Transparency Act, most LLCs formed on or after January 1, 2024 must file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN within 30 days of formation. This is a federal requirement — not Maryland-specific — but it catches many new LLC owners off guard.

The [FinCEN BOI filing portal](https://boiefiling.fincen.gov/) is free to use. Penalties for non-compliance can reach $591 per day per the most recent 2026 penalty schedule. Our [BOI Report Guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) covers exactly who must file, what information is required, and how to submit.

### Step 8: Handle Ongoing Annual Compliance

Forming the LLC is step one. Staying in good standing in Maryland requires attention every year:

- **Annual Report**: Due April 15 each year, $300 fee — required even if the LLC had zero activity
- **Personal Property Tax Return**: Filed alongside the Annual Report if your LLC owns personal property in Maryland
- **State and local tax filings**: Members pay Maryland income tax on their share of LLC profits at the individual level
- **Registered agent maintenance**: Ensure your registered agent information stays current with SDAT

Missing the April 15 Annual Report deadline doesn't trigger an immediate penalty, but SDAT will eventually place your LLC in "not in good standing" status and may forfeit it entirely — which complicates contracts, bank accounts, and future filings.

## Maryland LLC Tax Considerations

Maryland LLCs are treated as pass-through entities by default — the LLC itself owes no federal income tax. Profits and losses flow through to members' personal returns.

Key Maryland tax considerations for 2026:

- **Maryland state income tax**: Rates range from 2% to 5.75% on taxable income, applied to each member's distributive share of LLC profits
- **County income tax**: Maryland is one of the few states with a mandatory county-level income tax, typically ranging from 2.25% to 3.2% depending on where you live. This is collected on the same Maryland Form 502 alongside state income tax
- **Sales and use tax**: Maryland's sales tax rate is 6%. LLCs selling taxable goods or certain services must register with the Maryland Comptroller's Office before collecting tax
- **Self-employment tax**: Active LLC members typically owe 15.3% federal self-employment tax (Social Security and Medicare) on net earnings

If your LLC generates north of $60,000–$80,000 in annual profit, it's worth a conversation with a CPA about an S-Corp tax election, which can meaningfully reduce self-employment tax exposure. Our [LLC vs S-Corp guide](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) walks through the math with concrete examples.

Maryland also requires LLCs to register separately with the Comptroller's Office if they plan to hire employees, collect sales tax, or withhold state income tax — this is a separate step from the SDAT formation process and is easy to miss.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Starting an LLC in Maryland

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Maryland?**

The minimum out-of-pocket cost to start an LLC in Maryland is $100 — the state filing fee for the Articles of Organization. Add a registered agent service ($0–$125/year), an operating agreement ($0–$150), and the $300 annual report due by April 15, and your realistic first-year total lands between $400 and $700 depending on how you handle formation.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in Maryland?**

Online filings through Maryland Business Express typically take 5–7 business days for standard processing. Mail filings can take 4–6 weeks. If you need faster approval, same-day processing is available for an additional $325 fee on top of the $100 base.

**Do I need a registered agent for my Maryland LLC?**

Yes. Maryland law requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical Maryland street address. The agent must be available during regular business hours to receive legal documents and government notices. You can serve as your own registered agent, appoint a trusted individual, or hire a professional service for $49–$125/year.

**Does Maryland require an operating agreement?**

Maryland does not legally require an operating agreement for LLCs, but every LLC — especially multi-member ones — should have one. Without it, your LLC defaults to Maryland's standard statutory rules, which may not reflect how you actually intend to operate or distribute profits. Courts also look favorably on LLCs with documented internal governance when liability questions arise.

**What is the Maryland LLC annual report fee?**

Maryland LLCs must file an Annual Report with SDAT each year by April 15, with a $300 filing fee. This requirement applies even if the LLC had no revenue or activity during the year. Failure to file consistently will result in SDAT placing your LLC out of good standing and eventually forfeiting it.

**Can I be my own registered agent in Maryland?**

Yes, if you have a physical Maryland street address (P.O. boxes are not accepted) and are reliably available at that address during regular business hours. However, using a professional registered agent is worth the modest annual cost — it keeps your personal home address off public state records and ensures you don't miss time-sensitive legal notices if you travel or work variable hours.

**What taxes does a Maryland LLC pay?**

Maryland LLCs don't pay entity-level income tax. Each member pays Maryland state income tax (2%–5.75%) and county income tax (typically 2.25%–3.2%) on their share of LLC profits. Active members also owe federal self-employment tax (15.3%) on net earnings. LLCs selling taxable goods or services must collect and remit Maryland's 6% sales tax. Consult a CPA to understand your specific tax obligations based on your LLC's activity and structure.

**Do I need a business license to operate in Maryland?**

Forming an LLC doesn't automatically grant you a business license. Depending on your industry and jurisdiction, you may need additional licenses or permits from the Maryland Department of Labor, the state agency governing your profession, or your county or municipality. Service-based businesses often have lighter licensing requirements than food service, healthcare, or construction.
