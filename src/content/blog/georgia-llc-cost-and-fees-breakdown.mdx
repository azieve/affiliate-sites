## Georgia LLC Filing Fee: The $100 You Pay on Day One

Every Georgia LLC cost and fees breakdown starts in the same place: the Articles of Organization. This is the formation document you file with the Georgia Secretary of State to officially bring your LLC into existence.

**Filing fee: $100 (online) or $110 (paper/mail)**

Online filing through the [Georgia Secretary of State's Corporations Division](https://sos.ga.gov/index.php/corporations) is faster and cheaper by $10. That difference feels small, but paper filings can take four to six weeks to process — compared to the seven to ten business day standard for online submissions.

If you need your LLC active quickly, Georgia offers expedited processing for an additional $100, reducing turnaround to roughly two to three business days. Here's how the timing and cost breaks down:

One thing Georgia gets right: the online filing portal is genuinely functional. For a basic single-member LLC, the form is manageable without professional help. ## Georgia LLC Annual Registration Fee: The $50 You Owe Every Year

Here's where many Georgia LLCs stumble: the **annual registration requirement**.

**Annual registration fee: $50 per year**
**Due date: April 1 each year**
**Late penalty: $25 (applied after April 1)**

Every Georgia LLC must file an annual registration with the Secretary of State and pay the $50 fee. This is not optional — miss it consistently and your LLC can be administratively dissolved. Reinstating a dissolved Georgia LLC costs $250 in addition to the $50 registration fee, making the $25 late penalty look like a bargain by comparison.

The good news: the annual registration itself takes about five minutes online. You'll confirm your LLC's principal office address and registered agent information. No financial disclosures, no detailed reporting — just a basic maintenance filing.

In my experience, the annual registration is the single most common compliance failure among Georgia LLCs — particularly for businesses that formed in October or November and forget the following April 1 deadline entirely. If you're forming in 2026, put a recurring April 1 reminder in your calendar today.

Georgia's $50 annual fee compares very favorably to other states. California charges a minimum franchise tax of $800 per year regardless of revenue. New York imposes publishing requirements that can cost $1,000–$2,000 depending on the county. Georgia's $50 is among the most affordable maintenance costs of any U.S. state. ## Georgia Registered Agent Requirement and Costs

Georgia law requires every LLC to maintain a **registered agent** — a person or entity with a physical Georgia street address (not a P.O. box) who can receive official legal and government documents during business hours. This is an ongoing requirement, not a one-time formation detail.

Your three options:

**Serve as your own registered agent — $0**
If you have a physical Georgia address and can be reliably present during business hours, you can list yourself. The tradeoff: your address becomes part of the public record searchable by anyone, and you must be available to receive documents whenever they arrive.

**Designate an employee or colleague — $0**
Same rules apply: must have a Georgia physical address and be consistently available during business hours.

**Hire a professional registered agent service — $49–$300/year**
Professional services receive legal documents on your behalf, forward them promptly, and keep your personal or home address off the public record. For solo operators working from a home office, the privacy benefit alone often justifies the annual cost.

## Optional Georgia LLC Costs to Budget For

Beyond the mandatory filing fee and annual registration, several optional costs come up frequently. Not all of these will apply to your situation, but knowing about them prevents sticker shock later.

**Name Reservation: $25**
Georgia allows you to reserve your LLC name for 30 days while you prepare your filing. Filed through the Secretary of State's online portal. Optional — most business owners simply file the Articles of Organization directly rather than paying $25 to hold the name.

**Operating Agreement: $0–$1,500+**
Georgia does not legally require an LLC to have an operating agreement. But operating without one is a genuine mistake. An operating agreement defines management authority, profit and loss allocation, what happens when a member exits, and how decisions get made. It also reinforces your liability shield in the event of litigation. Your options:
- **DIY free template**: Adequate for very simple single-member LLCs
- **Formation service template** (included in most paid tiers): Good for most standard situations
- **Attorney-drafted** ($500–$1,500+): Worth the cost for multi-member LLCs, real estate holdings, or any entity that might attract outside investment

**EIN (Employer Identification Number): Free**
Apply directly through the [IRS website](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). The process takes roughly 10 minutes and the number is issued immediately. Some formation services charge $50–$70 to handle this for you — that's a convenience fee for a task you can easily do yourself. Unless your time is extremely constrained, skip the EIN add-on and apply directly.

**Business License: Varies by city/county ($0–$500+)**
Georgia does not issue a statewide general business license. However, many Georgia cities and counties require local business licenses or occupation tax certificates. Atlanta, for instance, requires an occupation tax certificate — the fee varies based on business type and gross revenue. Budget $50–$300 for most general business activities, but check your specific local jurisdiction before assuming you're in the clear.

**Trade Name / DBA Registration: ~$25–$150 by county**
If you want your LLC to operate under a name that differs from the official LLC name, you register a trade name (sometimes called a DBA) at the county level in Georgia. Fees vary by county — most fall in the $25–$150 range.

**Certificate of Existence: $10**
Banks, lenders, and some business partners require proof that your LLC is active and in good standing with the state. A Certificate of Existence from the Georgia Secretary of State costs $10 and is commonly required when opening a business bank account or applying for a loan.

**Certified Copies of Formation Documents: $10 per page**
If you need certified copies of your Articles of Organization, the state charges $10 per page. Most banks and lenders will accept uncertified copies directly from your records — confirm before ordering.

## Georgia LLC Tax Obligations: What You'll Owe Beyond Formation

The Georgia LLC cost and fees breakdown doesn't end with formation and annual registration. Understanding Georgia's tax landscape is essential to projecting your true operating costs.

**Georgia State Income Tax: ~5.49% flat rate (subject to phased reductions)**
Georgia has adopted a flat income tax structure, with the rate currently in the mid-5% range and continuing a phased reduction trajectory set by state legislation. For LLC members, business income passes through to your personal return and is taxed at this rate. Georgia's flat tax structure is generally more favorable than states with steep marginal rates — especially for higher-income LLC owners.

**No Separate Georgia LLC Income Tax on Pass-Through LLCs**
Single-member LLCs taxed as disregarded entities and multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships do not pay a separate entity-level income tax to Georgia. Income flows through to members' personal returns. This is meaningfully different from Georgia's treatment of corporations, which are subject to both a corporate income tax and a net worth tax.

**Federal Self-Employment Tax**
LLC members who are actively involved in running the business pay federal self-employment tax — currently 15.3% on net earnings (covering Social Security and Medicare). This isn't Georgia-specific, but it's a significant line item. Once net income consistently exceeds roughly $60,000–$80,000, electing S-Corp status can reduce this burden materially. See our guide on [LLC vs. S-Corp tax implications](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) for a full comparison.

**Georgia Sales Tax: 4% state rate + local surcharges**
If your LLC sells taxable goods or certain services in Georgia, you must register with the Georgia Department of Revenue and collect sales tax. The statewide rate is 4%, but local jurisdictions add their own rates — combined rates in most Georgia counties run 7%–8%.

**Federal BOI Reporting Requirement**
As of 2026, most newly formed LLCs are subject to federal Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reporting requirements administered by FinCEN. This isn't a Georgia-specific cost, but it's a compliance obligation that carries significant penalties for non-compliance. Review our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) to confirm your LLC's filing obligations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to form an LLC in Georgia in 2026?**

The mandatory state filing fee is $100 for online submission or $110 by mail. That's the floor. Add a formation service (optional, $0–$349 for the first year depending on tier), a registered agent service ($0–$300/year), and the $50 first-year annual registration for a realistic total. Most Georgia LLCs spend $100–$350 in year one.

**What is the Georgia LLC annual registration fee?**

Georgia LLCs owe a $50 annual registration fee to the Secretary of State, due by April 1 each year. A $25 late penalty applies after April 1. Failing to file at all can result in administrative dissolution, which costs $250 to reverse.

**Does Georgia have a franchise tax on LLCs?**

No. Georgia does not impose a minimum franchise tax on LLCs structured as pass-through entities. You owe the $50 annual registration fee — and nothing more at the state level for the privilege of maintaining your LLC. This compares favorably to California ($800/year minimum), Nevada ($350/year minimum list fee), and several other states with flat franchise or privilege taxes.

**Do I need a registered agent for my Georgia LLC?**

Yes, it's a legal requirement. Every Georgia LLC must maintain a registered agent with a physical Georgia street address. You can serve as your own registered agent if you have a qualifying address and are reliably available during business hours. Otherwise, professional registered agent services range from $49–$300/year.

**Can I form a Georgia LLC for free?**

The $100 state filing fee is unavoidable. However, several formation services — including ZenBusiness (Starter tier), Inc Authority, and Bizee — offer their own service layer at no charge, meaning you pay only the state fee for basic formation. The first-year EIN is also free when you apply through the IRS directly. So the minimum true cash outlay is $100.

**How long does it take to get a Georgia LLC approved?**

Standard online processing runs approximately seven to ten business days from submission. Expedited processing (additional $100) reduces that to two to three business days. Paper filings take four to six weeks and are generally not worth the wait or the $10 premium.

**What happens if I miss the Georgia annual registration deadline?**

You'll owe a $25 late fee for filing after April 1. If you skip the annual registration entirely for a year or more, Georgia can administratively dissolve your LLC. Reinstatement requires a $250 reinstatement fee plus the $50 registration fee — a $300 consequence that far outweighs the cost of staying current.

**Does Georgia require LLCs to publish a notice of formation?**

No. Georgia has no newspaper publication requirement for LLCs. This is a meaningful cost advantage over states like New York, where county-level publication requirements can add $1,000–$2,000 to formation costs.
