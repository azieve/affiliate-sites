## How to Start an LLC in Indiana: 6 Steps

### Step 1: Choose Your Indiana LLC Name

Your Indiana LLC name must include one of the following designators: "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." The name cannot be identical or deceptively similar to an existing business name on file with the state, and it can't use restricted words like "Bank," "Insurance," or "University" without specific regulatory approval.

Before filing, run a free name availability search through the [Indiana Secretary of State's INBiz portal](https://inbiz.in.gov). The search takes about two minutes and shows you registered entities with similar names — you're not just checking for exact duplicates, but for names that could confuse the public.

If you've found the right name but aren't ready to file yet, you can reserve it for 120 days by filing a Name Reservation application with a $10 fee through INBiz.

A few things worth noting on naming strategy: generic names are harder to trademark and can look unprofessional to lenders or clients. Check domain name availability alongside the state search — ideally you want the .com. And if you plan to operate under a different trade name, you'll need to register a DBA (assumed business name) with the Indiana Secretary of State.

### Step 2: Appoint a Registered Agent

Indiana law requires every LLC to designate a [registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) — an individual or entity with a physical Indiana street address that accepts legal documents, lawsuits, and official state correspondence on behalf of the LLC during normal business hours.

You can serve as your own registered agent, but there are practical downsides worth considering: your address becomes part of the public record permanently, you need to be physically present at that address during business hours every weekday, and you'll receive service of process — meaning you could be handed a lawsuit in front of clients or employees.

### Step 3: File Your Articles of Organization

This is the official document that creates your LLC. You file it with the Indiana Secretary of State through the INBiz online portal. Most business owners can complete this step in under 30 minutes.

**Indiana Articles of Organization filing fees in 2026:**

| Filing Method | Fee | Processing Time |
|---|---|---|
| Online (INBiz portal) | $95 | 1–3 business days |
| Mail | $100 | 2–4 weeks |

Online filing is faster, cheaper, and trackable — there's almost no reason to file by mail unless you have a specific reason for it. Indiana does not offer expedited processing for mail submissions, so the delay is real.

Your Articles of Organization require:
1. LLC name
2. Principal office address
3. Registered agent name and Indiana street address
4. Management structure (member-managed or manager-managed)
5. Organizer's name and signature

One privacy note: Indiana doesn't require you to list member names in the Articles of Organization. Only the registered agent and organizer appear in the public record, which offers more privacy than many states require.

### Step 4: Draft an Operating Agreement

Indiana does not legally require an operating agreement, but having one is essential — and I say that based on having seen what happens when LLC disputes land in court without one. When there's no operating agreement, Indiana's default statutory rules under the Indiana Business Flexibility Act apply, and those defaults frequently produce outcomes that none of the members intended or anticipated.

An [operating agreement](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) establishes how your LLC is run: how profits and losses are divided, how decisions are made, what happens when a member wants to exit, and how the business would be dissolved if needed. For a single-member LLC, it's simpler but still serves a critical function — it reinforces the legal separation between you and your business, which is the whole point of the LLC structure.

You can find usable templates online, or use a formation service that includes one. [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) offers operating agreement preparation as well, but treats it as an add-on starting around $99 — a meaningful difference if you're already paying for formation services.

### Step 5: Obtain an EIN from the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is issued by the IRS and functions as a tax ID number for your LLC. You'll need one to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal business taxes.

Multi-member LLCs must have an EIN. Single-member LLCs with no employees can technically use the owner's SSN for tax purposes, but getting an EIN is strongly advisable regardless — it keeps your Social Security number off business documents and makes banking significantly easier.

The IRS issues EINs for free through their [online application portal](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). The process takes about 10 minutes and you receive your EIN immediately. Formation services that charge extra for EIN assistance are charging for convenience — the underlying filing is always free.

### Step 6: Complete Post-Formation Requirements

After your LLC is officially formed, a few more steps apply:

**Licenses and Permits**
Indiana has no general statewide business license, but you likely need one or more of the following:
- City or county business permits (varies by municipality)
- Industry-specific licenses: contractor licenses, food service permits, childcare licenses, etc.
- Sales tax registration if you sell taxable goods or certain services (via Indiana Department of Revenue's INtax portal)
- Professional licenses for regulated occupations

**Business Bank Account**
Open a dedicated business bank account as soon as you have your EIN. Commingling personal and business finances is one of the most reliable ways to expose yourself to personal liability despite your LLC — courts call it "piercing the corporate veil," and it happens more often than it should.

**Indiana Biennial Business Entity Report**
Indiana LLCs must file a Business Entity Report every two years. This report is due during the anniversary month of your LLC's original formation.

**Biennial Report Fees:**
- Online: $32
- By mail: $50

This is notably cheaper and less frequent than many states. Compare it to Florida's annual report fee of $138.75, or California's $800 minimum franchise tax. Indiana's biennial structure keeps ongoing compliance costs low.

## Indiana LLC Taxes: What to Expect

Understanding the tax picture before you form is important — the wrong structure can cost you more than the formation service fees combined.

**Default federal tax treatment:**
- Single-member LLC: taxed as a sole proprietorship (Schedule C)
- Multi-member LLC: taxed as a partnership (Form 1065 + K-1s)

In both cases, income passes through to members, who report it on their personal returns. The LLC itself pays no federal income tax.

**Indiana state income tax:**
Indiana imposes a flat individual income tax rate on all income, including LLC pass-through income. The state has been reducing this rate in recent legislative sessions — consult the [Indiana Department of Revenue](https://www.in.gov/dor/) for the rate applicable to your current tax year. Indiana's relatively low flat tax structure makes it friendlier than many states for pass-through business income.

**Self-employment tax:**
This is a federal obligation, not state-specific. Active LLC members owe 15.3% on the first ~$168,600 of net earnings (2024 Social Security wage base, adjusted annually) and 2.9% on any amount above that. This applies regardless of which state your LLC is formed in.

**S-Corp election for tax savings:**
If your Indiana LLC is generating $50,000 or more in net profit after paying yourself a reasonable salary, electing S-Corp status can significantly reduce your self-employment tax exposure. This is a meaningful optimization for profitable LLCs, but it comes with additional compliance costs (payroll, separate S-Corp tax return). See our [LLC vs. S-Corp](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) guide for a detailed breakdown of when the switch makes financial sense.

**Indiana sales tax:**
The state sales tax rate is 7%. If your LLC sells taxable goods or taxable services in Indiana, register for a Retail Merchant Certificate through the Indiana Department of Revenue before you make your first sale.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to start an LLC in Indiana?

The required state filing fee is $95 online through the INBiz portal ($100 by mail). Add a commercial registered agent ($49–$299/year) and you're looking at roughly $144–$395 for the first year. Optional costs include operating agreement preparation, name reservation ($10), and DBA registration if applicable. The EIN is always free through the IRS.

### How long does it take to form an LLC in Indiana?

Online filings through INBiz are typically processed within 1–3 business days. Mail filings can take 2–4 weeks. Indiana does not offer expedited processing for mail submissions, so online filing is almost always the better choice for speed.

### Does Indiana require a registered agent for an LLC?

Yes. Indiana law requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical Indiana street address. You can serve as your own registered agent, but most business owners use a commercial service to keep their personal address out of public records and ensure consistent availability during business hours.

### Does Indiana require an operating agreement?

Indiana does not legally require LLCs to have an operating agreement, but having one is strongly advisable. Without it, Indiana's default statutory rules govern your LLC — and those defaults often don't reflect what members actually intended in terms of profit sharing, voting rights, or exit procedures.

### What are Indiana's ongoing LLC requirements?

Indiana LLCs must file a Business Entity Report every two years (biennial), due in the anniversary month of formation. The fee is $32 online or $50 by mail. There is no annual report — only this biennial filing. You must also keep your registered agent designation and principal office address current with the state.

### Can I be my own registered agent in Indiana?

Yes. Indiana permits individual members or managers to serve as registered agents. Requirements: you must have a physical Indiana street address (no PO boxes), and you must be available at that address during normal business hours. The main tradeoff is that your address becomes part of the permanent public record.

### Do I need an EIN for my Indiana LLC?

Multi-member LLCs always need an EIN. Single-member LLCs with employees also require one. Single-member LLCs with no employees can use the owner's SSN, but obtaining an EIN is still best practice — it separates your personal tax ID from business documents and simplifies opening a business bank account. EINs are free and issued instantly through the IRS.

### What happens if I miss Indiana's biennial report deadline?

If you fail to file the Business Entity Report by the due date, Indiana will administratively dissolve your LLC. This means you lose the liability protection and legal standing of your LLC until you file for reinstatement and pay any applicable fees. Set a recurring reminder around your LLC's anniversary month every two years to avoid this.
