## The DIY Route: Lowest Upfront Cost, Highest Time Investment

Filing directly with your state's secretary of state website is technically the cheapest way to form an LLC — you pay only the state filing fee, nothing more. The process generally looks like this:

1. Confirm name availability using your state's online business name search
2. Complete the Articles of Organization (typically a one-to-two-page form)
3. Submit online or by mail with the filing fee
4. Draft your own operating agreement (free templates are available from most state bar associations)
5. Apply for an EIN at no cost directly at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online)
6. Designate yourself as registered agent if your state permits it and you have a physical in-state address

**The honest tradeoff:** In states like New Mexico or Wyoming, DIY filing is a genuine 30-minute task. In states with publication requirements, complex forms, or strict name reservation rules, errors are common — and a rejected filing means paying re-filing fees and losing days or weeks of processing time. I've seen small business owners spend entire weekends correcting a DIY filing error that a formation service would have caught upfront. When you value your time honestly, the incremental cost of a formation service often pays for itself immediately.

DIY works best when you're forming in a simple state, you've filed business documents before, and you can serve as your own registered agent.

## Hidden Costs That Blow "Cheap" LLC Budgets

In my experience reviewing LLC formation services, the sticker price almost never reflects the true year-one cost. These are the fees that catch business owners off-guard most often:

**1. Registered agent renewal shock.** Services that bundle year-one registered agent service "free" often renew at $100–$250/year. If you're comparing a $0 plan with included RA vs. a $39 plan with transparent RA pricing, model out year three before deciding. Our guide on the [best LLC formation service with free registered agent](/blog/best-llc-formation-service-with-free-registered-agent/) breaks this down in detail.

**2. Formation service annual report markups.** Your state charges an annual report fee; formation services often charge an *additional* $50–$100 to file it on your behalf. Once you're familiar with your state's filing portal, this is a 10-minute task you can do yourself for no extra cost.

**3. EIN as a paid add-on.** Applying for an Employer Identification Number through the IRS takes about 10 minutes online and is completely free. Many formation services charge $50–$75 for this. Always decline this upsell and apply directly at IRS.gov.

**4. Boilerplate operating agreement charges.** A single-member LLC operating agreement is a straightforward document. Paying $50–$150 for a generic template through a formation service is rarely necessary — your state bar association or the [SBA's small business resource library](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure) can point you to free alternatives.

**5. State-specific ongoing obligations.** California's $800 minimum franchise tax is the most notorious ongoing cost, but Delaware's $300 flat franchise tax, Nevada's combined ~$350 annual list fee, and Tennessee's $300 minimum franchise excise tax all add up. These aren't formation-service fees — they're mandatory state charges that no service eliminates.

**6. Publication requirements.** New York requires new LLCs to publish formation notices in two local newspapers for six consecutive weeks — a requirement that typically costs $1,000–$2,000 depending on county. Nebraska and Arizona have historically had similar requirements (though Arizona sunset its publication mandate in 2023). Always research your state's specific obligations before choosing where to form.

## Cheapest States to Form an LLC in 2026

If your business is location-flexible — online-only, consulting, content creation, digital products — choosing a low-cost state is one of the most effective strategies for minimizing both formation and ongoing costs.

**New Mexico — $50 filing fee, no annual report.** New Mexico charges $50 to form an LLC and imposes no annual report requirement. It also permits anonymous ownership structures. For a solo online business with no physical New Mexico presence, this is often the lowest total multi-year cost option available. See our guide on [how to start an LLC in New Mexico](/blog/how-to-start-an-llc-in-new-mexico/) for the full filing process.

**Wyoming — $100 filing fee, $60 minimum annual report.** Wyoming is a consistently popular choice in 2026 for its strong charging order protections, no state income tax, and modest annual report fees. The annual report is based on in-state assets, so for most small businesses the minimum applies.

**Kentucky — $40 filing fee, $15 annual report.** The absolute lowest state filing fee in the country. The annual report at $15/year is equally minimal. Kentucky doesn't carry the brand recognition of Wyoming or Delaware, but the numbers are hard to argue with on pure cost grounds.

**Colorado — $50 filing fee, $10 annual report.** An underrated option: very low ongoing costs and a straightforward filing process.

**Critical caveat:** Forming in a state where you don't operate is not always cheaper once you account for **foreign qualification** — the process of registering your out-of-state LLC to legally do business in your home state. Foreign qualification typically costs $100–$300 in filing fees plus a second registered agent fee. For most businesses with a physical location or employees in one state, forming in that state is cheaper and simpler.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the absolute cheapest way to form an LLC?**

The absolute cheapest way is to file directly with your state's secretary of state website and pay only the mandatory state filing fee — no formation service charges, no add-ons. In low-fee states like Kentucky ($40) or New Mexico ($50), this DIY approach means your entire formation cost is under $60. The tradeoff is that you handle all paperwork, registered agent setup, and compliance tracking yourself.

**Can I form an LLC for free?**

Not completely — the state filing fee is always mandatory. But several formation services charge $0 for their own labor, meaning you pay only what the state requires. [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/), [Bizee](/go/bizee/), and [Inc Authority](/go/inc-authority/) all offer free formation tiers where the service prepares and submits your documents at no additional charge.

**How much does it cost to form an LLC in 2026?**

Total first-year cost depends on your state and the services you use. A bare-bones Kentucky LLC filed DIY costs $40. A more typical setup — moderate-fee state, free formation service, registered agent add-on — runs $200–$450. California adds an $800 minimum franchise tax, pushing year-one costs above $1,000 before service fees. See our full [how much does an LLC cost](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/) breakdown for state-by-state figures.

**Do I need a registered agent for my LLC?**

Yes — every LLC is legally required to maintain a registered agent in its state of formation. You can serve as your own registered agent in most states if you have a physical in-state street address (not a P.O. box) and are consistently available during business hours. If you travel frequently or want your personal address kept off public record, a commercial registered agent service ($99–$250/year) is worth the cost. For more on this, see our guide on [what is a registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/).

**Is it worth paying for a formation service, or should I DIY?**

When the formation service charges $0 (as ZenBusiness, Bizee, and Inc Authority all do at their free tiers), there's essentially no cost argument for DIY over using a service. The service handles preparation, catches common errors, and provides compliance reminders. DIY makes more sense if you have specific experience with business filings and are forming in a simple state with no publication requirements.

**What is the cheapest state to form an LLC in?**

Kentucky has the lowest state filing fee at $40, with a $15/year annual report. New Mexico ($50 filing fee, no annual report) often wins on multi-year total cost for online businesses. Wyoming ($100 filing fee, $60 minimum annual report) is the most popular choice for combining low cost with strong liability protections and no state income tax.

**Are there ongoing costs after I form my LLC?**

Yes — this is where many new business owners are surprised. Registered agent service costs $99–$250/year if you use a commercial provider. Annual report fees vary by state ($0–$500/year). Some states impose minimum franchise taxes regardless of income (California: $800/year; Delaware: $300/year; Nevada: ~$350/year). Budget for these before choosing your state — the ongoing cost often exceeds the one-time formation cost over a business's lifetime.

**Should I use Delaware or Wyoming to save money on my LLC?**

Only if you have a specific strategic reason. Delaware is ideal for VC-backed companies that need access to its sophisticated corporate court system; it's not a cost-saving move for most small businesses. Wyoming is a legitimate low-cost option for online businesses with no state preference. But if your business has a physical presence or employees in one state, foreign qualification costs ($100–$300 to register in your operating state, plus a second registered agent) typically eliminate any savings from forming elsewhere.
