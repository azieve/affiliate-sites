## Why Form an LLC in North Dakota?

Before walking through the mechanics, it's worth understanding why the LLC structure makes sense for most small business owners — and why North Dakota, specifically, is a solid jurisdiction for it.

An LLC (Limited Liability Company) creates a legal firewall between your personal assets and your business liabilities. If a client sues your LLC or a vendor comes after you for an unpaid invoice, your personal savings, home, and other property are generally shielded. That protection alone justifies the modest $135 filing fee for most operators. If you're still weighing whether the LLC structure is right for you versus operating as a sole proprietor, our [LLC vs Sole Proprietorship](/learn/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship/) guide covers the key differences in liability, taxes, and costs.

North Dakota also has an attractive tax profile. Unlike California, where every LLC must pay an $800 minimum franchise tax annually regardless of revenue, North Dakota imposes no state franchise tax on LLCs. The state does levy a personal income tax, but rates are among the lowest in the country — and LLC income typically passes through to members' personal returns, so you avoid the double-taxation risk that comes with C-corporation status.

In my experience reviewing LLC formation across all 50 states, North Dakota consistently underperforms in name recognition but overperforms in actual business-friendliness. The absence of a franchise tax and the low annual compliance costs make it genuinely economical to maintain an LLC here year over year — a fact that tends to surprise entrepreneurs who assume smaller states are less business-friendly.

## How to Start an LLC in North Dakota: 6 Steps

### Step 1: Name Your North Dakota LLC

Your LLC name must be distinguishable from all existing businesses registered with the North Dakota Secretary of State and must include one of the following designators: "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." The shorthand "Ltd." or "Co." alone is not sufficient.

You can check name availability for free through the [North Dakota Secretary of State's business search portal](https://sos.nd.gov/). If you've landed on a name but aren't ready to file your Articles of Organization immediately, you can reserve it for 12 months by submitting a Name Reservation form and paying a $10 fee.

A few additional naming rules to keep in mind:
- Words that imply governmental affiliation (e.g., "Federal," "Treasury," "State Agency") are prohibited
- Regulated terms like "Bank," "Insurance," or "Attorney" require additional licensing or agency approval before use
- Your name cannot be confusingly similar to an existing registered entity in North Dakota

### Step 2: Choose a Registered Agent

Every North Dakota LLC must designate a registered agent — an individual or entity with a physical street address in North Dakota who is available during standard business hours to receive legal documents, service of process, and official correspondence on behalf of the LLC. P.O. boxes are not accepted.

You have three practical options: serve as your own registered agent (using your North Dakota address), designate a trusted individual with a North Dakota address, or hire a professional registered agent service. Most experienced advisors recommend the third route. A professional [registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) keeps your personal address out of public records, ensures you never miss time-sensitive legal notices, and typically provides compliance reminders for annual reports and other deadlines.

### Step 3: File Your Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization is the foundational formation document that legally creates your North Dakota LLC. You file it with the Secretary of State and pay the $135 state filing fee.

The form requires:
- Your LLC's name (with required designator)
- Principal office address
- Registered agent name and North Dakota street address
- Management structure (member-managed or manager-managed)
- Names and addresses of the organizers
- Optional effective date (you can backdate or forward-date within limits)

You can file online at [sos.nd.gov](https://sos.nd.gov/) or submit a paper filing by mail. Online submissions typically process within one to three business days. Mail filings take five to seven business days. Unlike some states, North Dakota does not charge an expedited processing fee — the standard online turnaround is already fast.

### Step 4: Draft an Operating Agreement

North Dakota does not legally require LLCs to have an operating agreement, but it would be a mistake to skip it. The operating agreement governs how your LLC runs: how profits and losses are allocated, how major decisions are made, what happens when a member wants to exit, and what process governs dissolution.

Without a written operating agreement, your LLC defaults to North Dakota's state LLC statutes — rules written for generic situations that may not align with what you and your co-founders actually intended. For multi-member LLCs in particular, operating without one is a common source of expensive disputes down the road.

### Step 5: Obtain an EIN from the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is the federal tax ID for your business — the equivalent of a Social Security number for your LLC. You need one to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file certain federal tax returns. The IRS issues EINs for free through its [online application portal](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online), and the number is issued immediately upon completion.

Technically, single-member LLCs with no employees can use the owner's SSN for federal tax purposes. In practice, getting an EIN is always recommended — it reinforces the separation between personal and business finances and is required by virtually every bank when opening a business checking account.

### Step 6: Register for State Taxes and Licenses

North Dakota does not have a general statewide business license, but your industry may require specific professional licenses or local permits. Check with the North Dakota Secretary of State's office and your county or city government for applicable requirements.

If your LLC sells taxable goods or services, register for a sales tax permit with the North Dakota Office of State Tax Commissioner. North Dakota's state sales tax rate is 5%, with some municipalities adding local surcharges on top.

## North Dakota Registered Agent Requirements

The registered agent requirement is not optional — it's a statutory requirement that persists for the life of your LLC. If your registered agent's address changes, they resign, or they're otherwise unavailable, you must update the information with the Secretary of State promptly. Failing to maintain a valid registered agent is one of the most common reasons LLCs fall into administrative dissolution in North Dakota, often without the owner realizing it until they need a Certificate of Good Standing and discover the LLC isn't in good standing at all.

## Ongoing Compliance: Annual Reports and BOI Filings

Once your North Dakota LLC is active, two ongoing compliance requirements deserve your attention in 2026.

**Annual Report**

North Dakota LLCs must file an annual report with the Secretary of State by **November 15** each year. The filing fee is $50. The report simply updates the state on your registered agent, principal office address, and management structure — it's not a financial disclosure. You can file online at the Secretary of State's website in a few minutes.

Missing the deadline results in late fees and, eventually, administrative dissolution. The practical risk isn't just the fee — it's that a dissolved LLC loses its liability protections, meaning you could be personally exposed during the gap period.

**BOI Report**

Since January 2024, most new LLCs are also required to file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN, the federal financial crimes enforcement network. LLCs formed in 2026 have 30 days from their formation date to complete this filing. The filing is free through FinCEN's portal. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in North Dakota?**

The minimum cost is $135 — that's the state filing fee for the Articles of Organization. Add the $50 annual report due each November, and your first-year baseline is $185. If you use a formation service, expect an additional $0–$200 in service fees. Professional registered agent service runs $50–$300/year depending on the provider.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in North Dakota?**

Online filings with the North Dakota Secretary of State typically process within one to three business days. Paper mail filings take five to seven business days. North Dakota does not currently offer paid expedited processing, but the standard online turnaround is already faster than most states.

**Do I need an operating agreement for my North Dakota LLC?**

North Dakota law does not require a written operating agreement, but you should have one regardless. Without it, your LLC is governed entirely by the state's default statutory rules, which may not reflect your actual intentions — particularly around profit distribution, voting rights, and exit procedures for departing members.

**Does North Dakota require a registered agent?**

Yes. Every North Dakota LLC must maintain a registered agent with a physical North Dakota street address. You can serve as your own registered agent, but most business owners use a professional service to keep their personal address off public records and ensure they never miss a legal notice.

**What are the annual maintenance requirements for a North Dakota LLC?**

North Dakota LLCs must file an annual report by November 15 each year ($50 fee), maintain a valid registered agent, and keep their principal office address current with the Secretary of State. LLCs formed in 2026 must also file a BOI report with FinCEN within 30 days of formation.

**Can a non-resident start an LLC in North Dakota?**

Yes. North Dakota has no residency requirement for LLC organizers or members. You do, however, need to maintain a registered agent with a physical North Dakota address. For non-residents, hiring a professional registered agent service is the practical way to meet that requirement.

**Is North Dakota a good state to form an LLC if I live there?**

For North Dakota residents operating a business in the state, it's an excellent jurisdiction: no franchise tax, a modest filing fee, and straightforward annual compliance. Non-residents considering North Dakota as a formation state often find that Wyoming or Delaware offer similar benefits — but if you're actually doing business in North Dakota, you'll need to register there anyway, so there's no tax or compliance advantage to forming elsewhere.

**Do I need an EIN for my North Dakota LLC?**

You'll need an EIN if your LLC has multiple members, plans to hire employees, or needs to open a dedicated business bank account (banks almost universally require one). Single-member LLCs with no employees can technically use the owner's SSN for federal tax purposes, but obtaining a free EIN is strongly recommended for maintaining clear separation between personal and business finances.
