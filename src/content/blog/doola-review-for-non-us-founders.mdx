If you live outside the United States and want a US company, you have two problems. The first is forming the LLC. The second is everything after it: the EIN, the bank account, the annual IRS filings, and the registered agent. Most LLC services handle the first problem well. Far fewer handle the second. That gap is why doola has built a following among overseas founders, and it's why this doola review for non-US founders looks at the whole lifecycle, not only the filing day.

Mainstream providers are often cheaper for the filing itself. [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/), for example, advertises a $0 base formation package plus state fees, with paid tiers that add compliance tools. But a US LLC for foreigners comes with obligations that a domestic founder never faces. Whether doola's premium is worth paying depends on how much of that burden you want to hand off.

Here is what doola does, what it costs, where it falls short, and who should pick something else.

## What doola Is and Who It Is Built For

doola is a US-based formation and compliance company that focuses almost entirely on non-resident founders. Most LLC services serve US residents first and treat international customers as an edge case. doola reverses that. Its onboarding, support, and add-on products assume you have no Social Security number, no US address, and no US bank account.

Its core offering usually includes:

- **LLC formation** in a state you choose, commonly Delaware, Wyoming, New Mexico, or Florida
- **Registered agent service** in that state
- **EIN assistance**, which is the hard step for people without an SSN
- **A US business address** and mail handling
- **Bookkeeping, tax filing, and compliance add-ons**, including help with IRS Form 5472
- **Banking introductions** to US-accessible providers

I want to be direct about one point. doola is not a law firm and not a CPA firm. It is a service company with partner accountants. That distinction matters when you hit a complex tax question, which I'll come back to below.

## What doola Does Well

### EIN help for people without an SSN

This is the real value. A US-formed LLC needs an EIN to open accounts, accept payments, and file taxes. Applicants without an SSN or ITIN can't use the IRS online application. They have to file Form SS-4 by fax or mail, and the process can be slow and error-prone. The [IRS explains the options for foreign applicants](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/employer-id-numbers). doola handles this on your behalf, which saves real time. If you'd rather do it yourself, our walkthrough on [getting an EIN without an SSN](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-without-an-ssn/) covers the three IRS-approved methods.

### Awareness of foreign-owner tax filings

A foreign-owned single-member LLC is generally treated as a disregarded entity for income tax. It usually has no US income tax return of its own. But it still has to file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, and the [IRS states the penalty for failing to file is $25,000 per form](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5472). I've seen too many overseas founders assume "no US income means no US filing," and then receive a five-figure penalty notice. doola's reminders and filing support address this risk head-on. Our [Form 5472 filing guide for foreign-owned single-member LLCs](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/) explains the mechanics.

### Banking pathways

Opening a US bank account without being physically present is the other classic obstacle. doola has partnerships and guidance for fintech and neobank options that accept non-resident-owned LLCs. Approval is never guaranteed, because banks make their own compliance decisions, but a referral from a provider that already works with these banks can smooth things. For the wider picture, read our piece on [opening a US bank account for a foreign-owned LLC](/blog/us-bank-account-for-a-foreign-owned-llc/).

### Support that understands the question

Generic support teams struggle when a customer asks, "I live in Lagos and need a US LLC for Stripe." doola's team answers those questions daily. That is worth something.

## Where doola Falls Short

### It costs more than the filing itself justifies

The base filing is a commodity. State fees are the same everywhere, and competitors offer formation for $0 plus state fees. You are paying doola for the services around the filing. If you only need the filing, you're overpaying.

### It isn't a law or tax firm

When a founder in India, the UK, or Canada asks how a US LLC interacts with their home-country tax treatment, the answer depends on treaty rules, controlled foreign company (CFC) regimes, and local law. doola can point you in a direction. It can't replace a cross-border tax adviser. Our country guides, such as [US LLC for Indian residents](/blog/us-llc-for-indian-residents/) and [US LLC for UK residents](/blog/us-llc-for-uk-residents/), show how different these outcomes are.

### Recurring fees add up

Registered agent, address, and compliance bundles renew annually. A cheap first year can become a much larger recurring bill, so check renewal terms before you pay.

### Add-on upselling

As with most formation services, the basic checkout nudges you toward upgrades. Decide in advance what you need so you don't buy a bundle that duplicates something you could do yourself.

## A Practical Decision Framework

After reading a lot of founder horror stories, my rule of thumb is simple: pay for the part that is hard, not the part that is easy.

**Choose doola if:**
- You have no SSN, no US address, and no US contacts
- You want one provider for formation, EIN, address, and tax filings
- You value time over saving a few hundred dollars

**Choose ZenBusiness if:**
- You have an ITIN or a US-based helper, and you're comfortable handling the EIN and annual filings with a separate accountant
- You mainly need an inexpensive, reliable filing with optional compliance tools

**Hire a cross-border CPA or attorney if:**
- You're forming a multi-member LLC, taking investment, or earning significant US-source income
- Your home country has strict rules on foreign entities

In my experience, the founders who run into trouble are not the ones who picked the "wrong" formation service. They're the ones who skipped the annual filings. Whichever provider you choose, put the Form 5472 deadline on your calendar.

Your state choice affects ongoing cost more than your provider choice does.

- **New Mexico:** Cheapest to form and no annual report. It also doesn't publicly list members. See our [New Mexico LLC guide](/blog/how-to-start-an-llc-in-new-mexico/).
- **Wyoming:** Low fees and a strong privacy reputation. See [Wyoming LLC costs](/blog/wyoming-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/).
- **Delaware:** Common for founders planning venture funding. The $300 annual tax adds recurring cost. See the [Delaware franchise tax breakdown](/blog/delaware-llc-annual-franchise-tax/).

On beneficial ownership reporting, the rules have shifted. In March 2025, FinCEN issued an interim final rule that removed BOI reporting for US-formed companies and their owners. The requirement now applies mainly to entities formed under foreign law that register to do business in the US. Rules in this area have changed several times, so check [FinCEN's BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) for the current position before you rely on any summary, including ours.

For 2026, the practical to-do list for a foreign-owned LLC is short: keep the registered agent active, file state annual reports where required, and file Form 5472 with the pro forma 1120 on time. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is doola legit?
doola is an established company that forms LLCs and provides compliance services for non-US founders. As with any service, read the current terms, confirm what each plan includes, and check recent customer reviews before paying.

### How much does doola cost?
doola pricing is layered. There's usually a low or $0 base formation fee plus state fees, then optional annual bundles and add-ons. Your total depends on your state and the services you choose, so check doola's current pricing page.

### Can a non-US resident form an LLC without an SSN?
Yes. US law doesn't require US residency to own an LLC. You can obtain an EIN without an SSN by filing Form SS-4 by fax or mail, as outlined by the IRS. See our guide on [getting an EIN without an SSN](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-without-an-ssn/).

### Do I need to file taxes for a US LLC if I live abroad?
Often yes, at least informational filings. A foreign-owned single-member LLC generally must file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, even if it owes no US income tax. Penalties for missing it can be $25,000. Talk to a qualified tax professional about your situation.

### What is the best state for a non-US founder?
It depends on your goals. New Mexico and Wyoming are popular for low cost, and Delaware is common if you plan to raise venture capital. Read our [best state to form an LLC](/blog/best-state-to-form-an-llc/) comparison.

### Can I open a US bank account for my LLC from abroad?
Sometimes. Several fintech and neobank providers accept non-resident-owned LLCs, but approval is at the bank's discretion and may require documents such as your passport and proof of address. See our [foreign-owned LLC banking guide](/blog/us-bank-account-for-a-foreign-owned-llc/).

### Is doola better than ZenBusiness for foreigners?
For founders with no US presence, doola's specialization in EINs and foreign-owner filings can be worth the premium. For founders who can handle those steps with another accountant, ZenBusiness is often cheaper. See our [ZenBusiness review](/reviews/zenbusiness/) to compare.

### Do I need an ITIN?
Not to form an LLC or get an EIN. You may need one for certain tax filings or treaty claims. See our guide on [getting an ITIN as a foreign LLC owner](/blog/how-to-get-an-itin-as-a-foreign-llc-owner/).
