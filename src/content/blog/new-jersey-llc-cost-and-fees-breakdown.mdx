## The New Jersey LLC Filing Fee: $125 to Form Your Certificate of Formation

New Jersey doesn't call it "Articles of Organization" like most states — it's a **Certificate of Formation**, and it's filed with the Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services rather than a Secretary of State (New Jersey doesn't have one in the traditional sense). The New Jersey LLC filing fee is $125, and as of 2026 it must be submitted through the state's online business formation portal — paper filings are no longer the norm.

The good news: New Jersey's online system is fast. Most Certificates of Formation are approved within a few business days, sometimes within 24 hours, without needing to pay for expedited processing (a cost that states like California and Texas charge extra for). That's one area where New Jersey is genuinely business-friendly.

## The New Jersey Annual Report: $75 Every Year, No Exceptions

Unlike states such as Ohio or Arizona that don't require an ongoing report, New Jersey requires every LLC to file an **Annual Report** and pay a $75 fee, due each year during the anniversary month of your formation. Miss it, and the state doesn't send a gentle reminder — it moves toward administrative dissolution, which means your LLC loses its good standing and, eventually, its legal existence.

This is a cost people underestimate when they first calculate their New Jersey LLC cost and fees breakdown. It's easy to budget for the one-time $125 filing and forget that $75 recurs every single year for the life of the business. Over a 10-year horizon, that's $750 just in annual reports — before you've paid a dime in registered agent fees or taxes.

Reinstating a dissolved LLC costs more than staying current: New Jersey charges a reinstatement fee on top of any back annual reports owed, and you'll need to bring the entity fully current before you can resume operating legally. ## The Hidden Cost Most Guides Skip: New Jersey's Partnership Filing Fee

Here's the part of the New Jersey LLC cost and fees breakdown that catches people off guard. If your LLC has more than one member and is taxed as a partnership (the default for multi-member LLCs that haven't elected corporate or S-corp status), New Jersey imposes a **Filing Fee under the Gross Income Tax Act**: $150 per member, per year, capped at $250,000 total. It's reported and paid alongside your NJ-1065 partnership return.

In my experience, this is the single most commonly missed cost in New Jersey LLC planning. A two-member LLC owes $300 a year just for this filing fee — completely separate from federal self-employment tax, New Jersey income tax, or the $75 annual report. A five-member consulting firm is looking at $750 annually before any actual tax on profit. If you're structuring a multi-member LLC in New Jersey, get a CPA or tax attorney to confirm this obligation before your first filing deadline — it's not optional, and penalties accrue if it's missed. This is exactly the kind of detail a [senior corporate lawyer](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) should review alongside your operating agreement, since member count and profit allocation directly affect what you'll owe.

Single-member LLCs are generally exempt from this fee since they're treated as disregarded entities for tax purposes, but if you ever add a second member, this obligation kicks in retroactively for that tax year. It's worth reviewing IRS guidance on [entity classification](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc) to understand how your federal tax treatment interacts with New Jersey's state-level rules.

## Registered Agent, EIN, and Other Recurring Costs

Every New Jersey LLC must maintain a **registered agent** with a physical New Jersey street address to receive legal and state correspondence. You have three options:

1. **Serve as your own registered agent** — free, but your name and address become part of the public record, and you must be available during business hours.
2. **Use a friend, family member, or employee with a New Jersey address** — also free, with the same public-record tradeoff.
3. **Hire a commercial registered agent service** — typically $100–$300 per year.

Formation services often bundle this in. [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) includes a free year of registered agent service on its Pro and Premium plans, while [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) charges it as an add-on running close to $249 annually after the first term. If privacy is your main concern rather than price, it's worth reading our [registered agent explainer](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) — and for a deeper look at agent-only pricing, Northwest Registered Agent has built its reputation specifically around this service, though ZenBusiness remains our top pick for most New Jersey filers who want formation and agent service bundled together.

Other recurring or situational costs to plan for:

- **EIN (Employer Identification Number):** Free directly from the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). Formation services often charge $50–$75 to obtain this for you — money you can save by applying yourself in about 10 minutes.
- **Business Registration (NJ-REG):** Free, but mandatory before you can legally hire employees, collect sales tax, or open certain business accounts.
- **Trade name / DBA registration:** If you operate under a name different from your LLC's legal name, county-level registration typically runs $50–$100.
- **Certificate of Good Standing:** $50, needed if you're opening a business bank account, applying for financing, or registering to do business in another state.
- **Local business licenses and permits:** Vary widely by municipality and industry — a Newark restaurant and a Cherry Hill consulting LLC face entirely different requirements.
- **BOI reporting:** Following FinCEN's 2025 interim final rule, most domestic New Jersey LLCs are now exempt from Beneficial Ownership Information reporting, with the requirement narrowed to apply primarily to foreign-formed entities registered to do business in the U.S. Rules here have shifted more than once, so check our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for the current 2026 status before assuming you're exempt.

## Is New Jersey a Cheap or Expensive State to Form an LLC?

Compared to Wyoming ($100 filing, no annual report) or New Mexico ($50 filing, no annual report), New Jersey sits in the middle of the pack nationally. It's meaningfully cheaper than New York, which layers a costly publication requirement on top of formation, and cheaper long-term than California's $800 annual franchise tax. But the combination of the $75 annual report and the partnership filing fee for multi-member entities means New Jersey isn't the bargain some out-of-state filers assume it to be.

According to U.S. Census Bureau Business Formation Statistics, new business applications have stayed elevated nationwide through the mid-2020s, and New Jersey — sitting in the New York metro economic orbit — has seen steady LLC formation activity from freelancers, consultants, and small e-commerce operators drawn to the state's relatively fast online filing system. If you're weighing New Jersey against forming in a lower-cost state and registering as a foreign LLC back home, run the math carefully: foreign qualification adds its own $125 fee plus dual compliance obligations, which usually erases any savings unless you have a specific legal reason (like Delaware's court system) to form elsewhere.

## Bottom Line on New Jersey LLC Costs in 2026

The realistic New Jersey LLC cost and fees breakdown for year one is: $125 to form, $75 for your first annual report cycle (due the following year), $0–$300 for a registered agent depending on whether you self-serve, and — if you have business partners — $150 per member for the partnership filing fee. Add local licensing costs specific to your industry and city, and most single-member service businesses land around $250–$400 in true first-year cost, while multi-member LLCs should budget closer to $500–$700.

My honest take after reviewing dozens of these state cost structures: New Jersey is a fine state to form in if you actually live and operate there, but it offers no particular advantage to out-of-state entrepreneurs the way Delaware, Wyoming, or Nevada sometimes do. Form where your business actually operates, budget for the annual report every year without fail, and get ahead of that partnership filing fee if you have co-owners — it's the detail that surprises people most.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in New Jersey in 2026?**
The state filing fee for the Certificate of Formation is $125. With a registered agent, EIN setup, and first annual report cycle factored in, most filers spend $250–$600 total in year one depending on whether they file themselves or use a formation service.

**Do I need a registered agent for a New Jersey LLC?**
Yes. New Jersey law requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical New Jersey address. You can serve as your own agent for free or pay a commercial service $100–$300 per year for privacy and reliability.

**What happens if I don't file my New Jersey annual report?**
Your LLC falls out of good standing and, if left unresolved, the state will administratively dissolve it. Reinstatement requires paying back fees plus a reinstatement charge, and your business loses legal protections in the interim.

**Is the New Jersey partnership filing fee mandatory for all multi-member LLCs?**
It applies to multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships under the default federal classification. Single-member LLCs (disregarded entities) are generally exempt. Confirm your specific obligation with a tax professional, since misclassification penalties can add up.

**Can I form a New Jersey LLC for free?**
No formation service can waive the $125 state filing fee — that goes directly to New Jersey. However, providers like ZenBusiness and Bizee charge $0 for their own formation service on entry-level plans, so you pay only the state's fee.

**How long does New Jersey LLC approval take?**
Online filings through the Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services are typically processed within a few business days, and often within 24 hours — one of the faster turnaround times among U.S. states.

**Does New Jersey require a BOI report for LLCs?**
Following FinCEN's 2025 rule change, most domestic New Jersey LLCs are currently exempt from Beneficial Ownership Information reporting. Requirements have shifted, so check current guidance before assuming your entity is unaffected.

**Is New Jersey a good state to form an LLC if I live elsewhere?**
Generally, no — unless you have a specific legal or tax reason. Forming in New Jersey while operating elsewhere typically means paying New Jersey's fees plus foreign qualification costs in your home state, with little practical benefit for most small businesses.
