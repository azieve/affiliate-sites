## Why Form an LLC in Oregon in 2026?

An LLC (limited liability company) separates your personal assets — your house, car, savings — from your business's debts and legal liabilities. If your business is sued or can't pay a vendor, creditors generally can't come after your personal bank account. That protection alone is why most sole proprietors in Oregon eventually convert to an LLC once revenue picks up. If you're still weighing entity types, our guide on [what is an LLC](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) breaks down the basics, and our [LLC vs. sole proprietorship](/learn/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship/) comparison is worth a read before you file anything.

Oregon-specific advantages worth knowing:

- **No state sales tax.** Oregon is one of only five states with no general sales tax, which simplifies bookkeeping for retail and e-commerce LLCs.
- **No LLC "franchise tax" like California's $800 minimum.** Oregon's ongoing costs are limited to the $100 annual report and any applicable Corporate Activity Tax (more on that below).
- **Fast, fully online filing.** The Oregon Secretary of State's Business Registry processes most online Articles of Organization within one to two business days.

The tradeoff is Oregon's personal income tax, which tops out at 9.9% — one of the higher marginal rates in the country — since LLC profits typically pass through to your personal return. This is where working with a tax professional matters more than people expect; the entity structure that saves you money in Texas or Florida (no state income tax) doesn't automatically translate to Oregon.

## How to Start an LLC in Oregon: 7 Steps

Here's the full step-by-step process to start an LLC in Oregon in 2026, from naming your business to staying compliant after formation.

### Step 1: Choose an Oregon LLC Name

Your name must include "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C.," and it needs to be distinguishable from every other business name on file with the state. Use the free business name search on the [Oregon Secretary of State's website](https://sos.oregon.gov/business/Pages/default.aspx) before you fall in love with a name.

If you're not ready to file but want to lock in a name, Oregon lets you reserve one for 120 days for a $100 fee. It's a nice-to-have, not a necessity — most people filing within a few weeks skip this step entirely and go straight to the Articles of Organization.

### Step 2: Appoint an Oregon Registered Agent

Every Oregon LLC needs a registered agent — a person or company with a physical Oregon street address (no P.O. boxes) who's available during business hours to accept legal documents and state correspondence on your behalf. You can serve as your own registered agent if you have an Oregon address and don't mind your name and address being part of the public record, or you can hire a commercial service. For a deeper explanation of what this role actually does, see our guide on [what is a registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/).

This is one area where I'd push back on the "just use your home address" instinct. If you work from home, your registered agent address becomes public record — meaning process servers, marketers, and anyone who looks you up can find your address tied to your LLC. If privacy matters to you, [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) built its entire reputation around this — it won't sell your data and uses its own address on your public filings, making it a solid pick specifically when anonymity is the priority. For most filers, though, bundling registered agent service into your formation package (ZenBusiness includes a full year free on its Pro and Premium plans) is the more cost-effective route.

### Step 3: File Your Oregon Articles of Organization

This is the actual document that legally creates your LLC. You'll file it online through the Secretary of State's Business Registry, and it requires:

- LLC name and principal business address
- Registered agent name and Oregon street address
- Organizer's name and signature
- Whether the LLC is member-managed or manager-managed
- Duration (perpetual, by default)

The **filing fee is $100**, and online submissions are typically processed within one to two business days — mailed filings can take two to three weeks. If you'd rather not deal with the state portal yourself, this is the step most formation services handle for you. [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) files your Articles of Organization as part of its $0 starter plan (you still pay the $100 state fee directly), and [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) offers a comparable service with more add-on options if you want legal document review bundled in — though LegalZoom's base plan typically runs a bit higher once you factor in their registered agent add-on pricing.

### Step 4: Draft an Oregon LLC Operating Agreement

Oregon doesn't legally require you to file an operating agreement with the state, but skip it at your own risk. This internal document spells out ownership percentages, profit distribution, voting rights, and what happens if a member wants out or passes away. Without one, Oregon's default LLC statutes govern your business — and those defaults rarely match what business partners actually intended.

I've seen multi-member LLCs fall apart over disputes that a one-page operating agreement would have prevented in five minutes. Even single-member LLCs benefit from having one on file — banks often ask for it when you open a business account, and it reinforces the liability separation between you and the business in the event of a lawsuit. Our full [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) walks through what to include. Most formation services, including ZenBusiness and LegalZoom, provide a free customizable template as part of their formation packages.

### Step 5: Get an EIN From the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your business's federal tax ID — you'll need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal taxes. You can apply directly through the [IRS website](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) for free in about 15 minutes; there's no legitimate reason to pay a third party for this specific step, though most formation packages bundle it in at no extra charge anyway.

### Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

Once you have your EIN and approved Articles of Organization, open a dedicated business checking account. Commingling personal and business funds is one of the fastest ways to lose your liability protection — if a court finds you've been treating the LLC's money as your own, it can "pierce the corporate veil" and expose your personal assets despite having an LLC on paper. This is a real risk, not a scare tactic — it's one of the most common ways small business owners lose the very protection they formed the LLC to get.

### Step 7: File Your Oregon LLC Annual Report

Oregon requires every LLC to file an **annual report** each year by the anniversary date of formation, with a **$100 fee** for domestic LLCs ($275 for foreign LLCs registered to do business in Oregon). This is separate from federal or state tax filings — it's purely a status update confirming your business is still active. Miss it, and Oregon will administratively dissolve your LLC, which strips away your liability protection until you pay to reinstate it.

## Oregon LLC Taxes: What You'll Actually Owe

This is where Expert B's legal hat comes on, because Oregon's tax structure catches a lot of new owners off guard.

**Pass-through taxation (default).** By default, a single-member LLC is taxed as a sole proprietorship and a multi-member LLC as a partnership — profits pass through to your personal Oregon income tax return, taxed at rates up to 9.9%. You can elect S-corp taxation once profits justify it, which can reduce self-employment tax exposure; our [LLC vs. S-corp](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) guide covers when that election actually makes sense.

**Oregon's Corporate Activity Tax (CAT).** This is the piece most out-of-state formation guides miss entirely. Oregon imposes a CAT on businesses with more than $1 million in Oregon-sourced commercial activity: $500 plus 0.57% of taxable commercial activity above that $1 million threshold. Businesses must register once they exceed $750,000 in Oregon commercial activity, even before the tax is actually owed. Most new single-member LLCs won't hit this threshold in year one, but if you're launching with existing revenue or acquiring an established business, it's worth checking with a CPA immediately — CAT registration deadlines are unforgiving. Details are available directly from the [Oregon Department of Revenue](https://www.oregon.gov/dor).

**No state sales tax.** Unlike most states, Oregon doesn't require you to collect sales tax on goods sold within the state, which is a genuine advantage for retail and e-commerce LLCs.

Tax law changes frequently, and Oregon has adjusted CAT thresholds and income brackets before — always confirm current rates with a licensed CPA or the Oregon Department of Revenue before filing. Nothing in this article should be treated as personalized tax advice.

## Mistakes to Avoid When Forming an Oregon LLC

- **Missing the annual report deadline.** Oregon doesn't send a hard-copy reminder to every filer — set a calendar alert for your formation anniversary date, or use a service with automatic compliance tracking.
- **Skipping the operating agreement.** Even for single-member LLCs, banks and courts both look for this document.
- **Using your home address as registered agent without thinking it through.** It becomes permanent public record.
- **Ignoring the Corporate Activity Tax registration threshold.** If you're acquiring a business or launching with existing revenue, check the $750,000 registration trigger immediately.
- **Forgetting federal Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reporting.** Requirements under the Corporate Transparency Act have shifted more than once — as of the most recent FinCEN guidance, reporting is narrowed largely to foreign companies registered to do business in the U.S., but rules in this area have changed multiple times since 2024 and could shift again in 2026. Check current requirements directly at [FinCEN.gov](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) or see our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) before assuming you're exempt.

It's also worth noting that Oregon isn't forming LLCs in a vacuum — U.S. business formation activity has stayed near record levels in recent years, according to the [U.S. Census Bureau's Business Formation Statistics](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/index.html), and Oregon has tracked that national trend closely, particularly in the Portland metro area's tech and creative-services sectors. That's part of why the state has continued investing in faster online processing for its Business Registry system.

## FAQ: How to Start an LLC in Oregon

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Oregon?**
The state filing fee for Articles of Organization is $100. Add optional costs like a $100 name reservation, registered agent service ($0 if you self-serve, or roughly $100–$250/year for a commercial service), and any local business license fees your city requires.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in Oregon?**
Online filings through the Oregon Secretary of State are typically processed within one to two business days. Mailed paper filings can take two to three weeks, so online filing is strongly preferred if timing matters.

**Do I need a registered agent in Oregon?**
Yes. Every Oregon LLC must designate a registered agent with a physical Oregon street address who is available during normal business hours to receive legal and state documents.

**Can I be my own registered agent in Oregon?**
Yes, as long as you have a physical Oregon address (not a P.O. box) and are reliably available during business hours. Just be aware your name and address become part of the public record.

**Do I need a business license to start an LLC in Oregon?**
Oregon doesn't issue a single statewide general business license, but many cities and counties do — Portland and Multnomah County, for instance, require separate registration. Check with your local city or county clerk after your LLC is formed.

**What is Oregon's Corporate Activity Tax, and does my new LLC owe it?**
The CAT applies to businesses with more than $1 million in Oregon-sourced commercial activity ($500 plus 0.57% of activity above that threshold), with registration required once you cross $750,000. Most brand-new LLCs won't hit this in year one, but confirm with a CPA if you're launching with existing revenue.

**Do I need an operating agreement for my Oregon LLC?**
Oregon doesn't legally require you to file one, but it's strongly recommended — even for single-member LLCs. It defines ownership, profit distribution, and what happens in a dispute, and banks frequently request it before opening a business account.

**What happens if I miss my Oregon LLC's annual report?**
Oregon will administratively dissolve your LLC if the annual report goes unfiled, which suspends your liability protection until you pay to reinstate. It's one of the most common — and most avoidable — compliance mistakes Oregon LLC owners make.

**Can a non-resident start an LLC in Oregon?**
Yes. You don't need to live in Oregon or be a U.S. citizen to form an Oregon LLC, though you'll still need a registered agent with a physical Oregon address, which is where a commercial registered agent service becomes especially useful for out-of-state or international owners.
