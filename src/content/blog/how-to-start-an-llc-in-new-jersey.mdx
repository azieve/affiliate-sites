## Why Form an LLC in New Jersey in 2026?

New Jersey is home to over 900,000 small businesses as of 2026, according to the [U.S. Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/). The state consistently ranks among the top states for access to capital and proximity to major markets — benefits that translate directly into business opportunity.

But the case for forming an LLC in New Jersey goes beyond geography:

- **Liability protection.** An LLC creates a legal firewall between your personal assets — your home, savings, and car — and your business debts or lawsuits. If your business gets sued or can't pay its bills, your personal finances are generally protected.
- **Pass-through taxation.** By default, LLC income passes directly to its members and is reported on personal tax returns. You avoid the double taxation that C-corporations face.
- **No publication requirement.** Unlike New York, which requires new LLCs to publish a formation notice in two local newspapers (a process costing $1,000–$2,000 in many counties), New Jersey has no publication requirement. You file your Certificate of Formation, pay $125, and you're done.
- **No state minimum tax.** California charges LLCs a flat $800 minimum annual tax regardless of income. New Jersey does not. Your only recurring state obligation is a $75 annual report fee.
- **Flexible management.** New Jersey LLCs can be member-managed or manager-managed, accommodating both solo operators and complex multi-partner arrangements.

## What You Need Before You File

Before submitting your Certificate of Formation to the New Jersey Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services, get these pieces in place:

**1. Your LLC Name**
Your business name must include "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." It cannot be identical or confusingly similar to an existing New Jersey business name. Check availability free at the [NJ Division of Revenue name search portal](https://www.njportal.com/DOR/BusinessNameSearch/). Name reservation is available for 120 days at a $50 fee — useful if you're not ready to file immediately.

**2. A Registered Agent**
New Jersey requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in the state (no P.O. boxes). The registered agent accepts legal documents, service of process, and official state correspondence on behalf of your business during business hours. You can serve as your own registered agent if you have an NJ address, but many business owners use a professional service. [Learn what a registered agent actually does — and whether you need a professional.](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/)

**3. Member or Manager Information**
You'll need the full legal names and addresses of all LLC members or managers, depending on your management structure.

**4. Your Principal Business Address**
This is the LLC's primary place of business. A home address is acceptable but becomes public record.

## Step-by-Step: How to Start an LLC in New Jersey

Here is the exact sequence to complete your NJ LLC formation in 2026.

### Step 1: Choose Your LLC Name

Search the NJ business name database to confirm your name is available. A few rules to keep in mind:

- Must include "LLC," "L.L.C.," or "Limited Liability Company"
- Cannot include restricted words like "bank," "insurance," or "university" without state approval
- Cannot be deceptively similar to any name already registered in New Jersey

Once confirmed, you can either file immediately or pay $50 to hold the name for 120 days.

### Step 2: Appoint a New Jersey Registered Agent

Your registered agent must maintain a physical NJ street address and be consistently available during business hours.

- **Self-serve:** List yourself or a trusted person at a physical NJ address — free, but your address enters public record.

In my experience, the biggest mistake new LLC owners make is listing their home address as the registered agent. That address becomes a permanent public record — and when a lawsuit arrives, it comes to your front door, potentially in front of clients, family members, or employees. A professional registered agent service running $99–$125 per year is one of the most cost-effective protections you can buy.

### Step 3: File Your Certificate of Formation

New Jersey uses the term "Certificate of Formation" rather than "Articles of Organization" (which many other states use). You file it with the [NJ Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services](https://www.njportal.com/DOR/BusinessFormation/).

**What you'll need to include:**
- LLC name
- Registered agent name and NJ street address
- Management type (member-managed or manager-managed)
- Names and addresses of all members or managers
- Effective date (immediate or a future date up to 90 days out)

**Filing options:**

| Method | Fee | Processing Time |
|---|---|---|
| Online (NJ portal) | $125 | 2–3 business days |
| By mail (Trenton, NJ 08646) | $125 | 2–3 weeks |

Online is always the better choice. New Jersey does not offer expedited processing, so mail filing simply delays everything by several weeks with no upside.

### Step 4: Draft an Operating Agreement

New Jersey does not legally require an operating agreement, but you should have one regardless — especially if your LLC has more than one member. This internal document governs ownership percentages, profit distributions, voting rights, what happens when a member exits, and how disputes get resolved.

Without a written operating agreement, your LLC falls back on New Jersey's default rules under the Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act (RULLCA), which assigns equal ownership to all members regardless of actual capital contributions. That default often doesn't reflect what founders actually intend.

[Our LLC Operating Agreement Guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) explains exactly what to include. For a ready-made template, [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) includes an operating agreement in its Pro and Premium plans — unlike [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/), which charges an additional $99 or more to add operating agreement drafting to any plan.

### Step 5: Get an EIN from the IRS

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your LLC's federal tax ID. You need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal taxes for multi-member LLCs.

Apply directly through the [IRS EIN application portal](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) — it's free, fully online, and issues the number immediately. There is no reason to pay a formation service to do this for you; it takes about five minutes.

Single-member LLCs with no employees can technically use a Social Security number for tax purposes, but an EIN is better practice for separating business and personal finances.

### Step 6: Register for New Jersey State Taxes

After formation, register with the [New Jersey Division of Taxation](https://www.state.nj.us/treasury/taxation/) if your LLC will collect sales tax (NJ rate: 6.625%), pay employer payroll taxes, or make quarterly estimated income tax payments. Registration is free.

You'll also want to understand your NJ gross income tax obligations. New Jersey taxes pass-through income at graduated rates ranging from 1.4% to 10.75% depending on income level.

### Step 7: Obtain Required Licenses and Permits

New Jersey has no universal statewide general business license, but your specific industry or municipality may require:

- Professional licenses (contractors, healthcare providers, accountants)
- Local zoning or home occupation permits
- Health department permits (food service, childcare)
- Federal licenses (transportation, firearms, alcohol)

Check requirements with your municipality's clerk office and the [NJ Business Action Center](https://www.nj.gov/state/dos-business-action-center.shtml), which provides free guidance for new businesses.

### Step 8: Open a Business Bank Account

A dedicated business bank account is essential for maintaining your liability protection. Commingling personal and business funds — even accidentally — is one of the most common ways courts "pierce the corporate veil" and hold LLC owners personally liable for business debts.

To open an account, you'll need your EIN and a certified copy of your Certificate of Formation.

## New Jersey LLC Tax Overview

**Pass-through taxation by default.** LLC profits and losses flow to members and are reported on personal NJ and federal returns. Members pay NJ gross income tax at rates from 1.4% (on income under $20,000) up to 10.75% (on income over $1 million).

**No NJ minimum LLC tax.** New Jersey does not impose a separate minimum annual tax on LLCs beyond the $75 annual report fee. This contrasts favorably with California's $800 minimum franchise tax and Delaware's $300 flat fee for LLCs.

**Sales tax obligations.** If you sell taxable products or services in New Jersey, you must register as a sales tax collector and remit the 6.625% state rate to the NJ Division of Taxation.

**S-Corp election consideration.** Once your LLC's net self-employment income consistently exceeds $40,000–$60,000 per year, it may be worth electing S-Corp tax treatment with the IRS. This strategy allows you to split income between a salary (subject to payroll taxes) and distributions (not subject to self-employment tax), potentially saving thousands annually. Our [LLC vs. S-Corp](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) guide walks through the math with concrete examples.

**Federal BOI reporting.** Under the Corporate Transparency Act, most new LLCs formed in 2024 or later must file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN within 90 days of formation. Penalties for non-compliance run as high as $591 per day. See our [BOI Report Guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for the current rules, exemptions, and how to file.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in New Jersey?**

The minimum cost to start an LLC in New Jersey is $125 — the state filing fee for the Certificate of Formation. In your first year, add registered agent service ($99–$125) and you're looking at roughly $225–$250 before any formation service fees. The $75 annual report fee kicks in every year after that.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in New Jersey?**

Online filings through the NJ Division of Revenue are typically processed within 2–3 business days. Mail-in filings take 2–3 weeks. New Jersey does not offer expedited processing, making online filing the clear default.

**Do I need a registered agent in New Jersey?**

Yes — it's a legal requirement. Every New Jersey LLC must maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in the state, available during regular business hours to accept service of process and official state correspondence. You can serve as your own agent if you have an NJ address, but most business owners use a professional service to keep their home address off public record.

**Does New Jersey require an operating agreement?**

No — New Jersey law does not require LLCs to have a written operating agreement. However, operating without one means your LLC defaults to RULLCA's standard rules, which may assign equal ownership regardless of actual contributions. For any LLC with multiple members, a written operating agreement is essential.

**What is the New Jersey LLC annual report requirement?**

New Jersey LLCs must file an Annual Report each year and pay a $75 filing fee. The deadline is the last day of your LLC's anniversary month — the same calendar month in which your Certificate of Formation was approved. Missing the deadline can result in a "revoked" status that affects your ability to do business and open bank accounts.

**Does New Jersey have a publication requirement for LLCs?**

No. New Jersey has no newspaper publication requirement for new LLCs. This stands in sharp contrast to New York, where publication in two local newspapers is mandatory and typically costs $1,000–$2,000. New Jersey's lack of this requirement is one of its genuine advantages for founders near the NY metro area.

**Can I form a New Jersey LLC if I live in another state?**

Yes. You do not need to be a New Jersey resident to form an NJ LLC. You will, however, need to designate a registered agent with a physical NJ address. A professional registered agent service like [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) or [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) satisfies this requirement.

**What taxes does a New Jersey LLC pay?**

A standard single-member LLC pays federal self-employment tax (15.3% on net earnings), federal income tax at individual rates, and NJ gross income tax at 1.4%–10.75% depending on income level. Multi-member LLCs follow the same structure, with each member paying tax on their proportionate share. LLCs that collect sales on taxable goods or services must also collect and remit NJ's 6.625% sales tax.
