## Wyoming LLC State Filing Fee: $100

The Wyoming Secretary of State charges a **$100 filing fee** to submit your Articles of Organization online. If you file by mail, the fee is $102 (the extra $2 covers a convenience processing charge for paper filings). There's no separate expedite fee for online submissions — online filings are typically processed within 1–2 business days as a standard.

This is the only mandatory upfront fee to the state. Compare that to states like California, which charges $70 just to file but then adds a mandatory $800/year franchise tax regardless of income — Wyoming has no equivalent. As the [Wyoming Secretary of State](https://sos.wyo.gov/Business/Docs/LLCFAQs.pdf) notes, the $100 fee covers both LLC formation and initial processing.

The Articles of Organization require:
- LLC name (must include "LLC," "L.L.C.," or "Limited Liability Company")
- Principal office address (can be out of state)
- Registered agent name and Wyoming address
- Organizer name and signature

That's it. No publication requirement (unlike New York, which can cost $1,000–$2,000), no list of members required in public filings.

## Wyoming Annual Report Fee: $60 Minimum

Wyoming requires all LLCs to file an **annual report** each year by the first day of the month in which the LLC was formed. The annual report fee is calculated as:

**$60 or 0.0002 × total assets located in Wyoming — whichever is greater.**

For the vast majority of small business LLCs with under $300,000 in Wyoming-located assets, the fee is simply **$60/year**. This is one of the lowest annual LLC fees in the country. By comparison:
- Delaware charges $300/year in annual franchise tax
- California charges $800/year minimum franchise tax
- Ohio charges $99/year

The 0.0002 multiplier only becomes relevant at higher asset levels. An LLC with $1 million in Wyoming assets would pay $200; an LLC with $5 million in Wyoming assets would pay $1,000. Most LLCs formed in Wyoming by non-resident owners for asset protection purposes report minimal Wyoming-located assets, keeping their annual fee at the $60 floor.

**Important:** Missing the annual report deadline results in administrative dissolution. Wyoming gives you a 60-day cure period, but after that, your LLC is dissolved and you'd need to reinstate it (additional fee of $100 for reinstatement). ## Optional Costs Worth Knowing About

### EIN (Employer Identification Number): $0
An EIN is your LLC's federal tax identification number. You get it directly from the [IRS website](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) for free. The application takes about 10 minutes online, and you receive the EIN immediately. **Never pay a service to get your EIN** — it's always free from the IRS, and any service charging $50–$100 for this is marking up a free government service.

### Operating Agreement: $0–$500
Wyoming doesn't legally require an operating agreement, but you should have one. Without it, your LLC defaults to Wyoming's generic LLC statutes, which may not reflect how you actually want to run your business. A basic operating agreement can be drafted for free using a template, or you can pay an attorney $200–$500 for a customized version.

Many formation services include a basic operating agreement template with their packages at no extra cost — [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) and [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) both do.

### Business Licenses and Permits: Varies
Wyoming has no general business license requirement at the state level, which is a notable advantage. However, depending on your industry and city/county, you may need:
- Local business licenses ($25–$100 typically)
- Professional licenses (contractors, healthcare, financial services)
- Sales tax permit (free from the Wyoming Department of Revenue if you sell taxable goods)

Check the [Wyoming Business Council's licensing guide](https://www.wyomingbusiness.org/) for industry-specific requirements.

### Business Bank Account: $0–$25/Month
Opening a separate business bank account is not legally required but is strongly recommended to maintain liability protection. Many banks offer free business checking accounts. You'll need your EIN and Articles of Organization to open one.

### Certified Copy of Articles of Organization: $2/Page
If you need a certified copy of your formation documents (sometimes required by banks or for foreign qualification in another state), the Wyoming Secretary of State charges $2 per page.

### Certificate of Good Standing: $2
Also called a Certificate of Existence in Wyoming, this document confirms your LLC is in active, compliant status. It costs $2 and can be ordered online from the Secretary of State's website.

## Why So Many Non-Residents Form Wyoming LLCs

In my experience advising small business owners, the Wyoming LLC is the most misunderstood formation decision. Clients frequently come to me having formed a Wyoming LLC when their actual business operates entirely in California or New York — and they end up paying fees in both states.

That said, there are legitimate reasons to choose Wyoming even if you don't live there:

**1. No state income tax.** Wyoming has no personal or corporate income tax. If your LLC makes a profit, none of it goes to Wyoming state income tax. (You still owe federal taxes and the income tax of your home state if it taxes pass-through income — Wyoming doesn't exempt you from that.)

**2. Strong charging order protections.** Wyoming's charging order protection is among the strongest in the country. A creditor who wins a judgment against an LLC member cannot seize the member's interest in the LLC — they can only receive distributions if and when the LLC chooses to make them. This makes Wyoming LLCs particularly attractive for asset protection.

**3. No public disclosure of members.** Wyoming does not require member or manager names to be listed in the Articles of Organization or the annual report. Combined with a registered agent, this provides meaningful privacy for LLC owners. Compare this to states like [Ohio](/blog/ohio-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/) or [North Carolina](/blog/north-carolina-llc-cost-and-fees-breakdown/), which require member disclosure in public filings.

**4. No LLC tax or franchise tax.** Beyond the $60 annual report fee, Wyoming imposes no franchise tax or minimum tax on LLCs. Delaware charges $300/year; California charges $800/year minimum — both regardless of whether your business earned a dollar.

**The caveat:** If you operate your business in another state, you may need to **foreign qualify** that LLC in your home state — paying that state's foreign registration fee plus its ongoing annual fees. The Wyoming savings can evaporate quickly. Before forming a Wyoming LLC as a non-resident, run the math on your home state's requirements. ## Filing Your Wyoming LLC: Step-by-Step

For those filing directly with the state:

1. **Choose your LLC name.** Check availability using the [Wyoming Secretary of State's Business Entity Search](https://wyobiz.wyo.gov/Business/FilingSearch.aspx). The name must be distinguishable from existing entity names in Wyoming.

2. **Designate a registered agent.** Either a Wyoming resident or a professional service. Get this arranged before you file.

3. **File Articles of Organization.** Do this online at the Wyoming Secretary of State's website. Filing fee: $100.

4. **Get your EIN.** Apply free at IRS.gov after formation. Takes 10 minutes.

5. **Draft an operating agreement.** Not required but strongly recommended.

6. **Open a business bank account.** Use your EIN and Articles of Organization.

7. **Register for taxes as needed.** If selling taxable goods or services in Wyoming, register with the Wyoming Department of Revenue for a sales tax license (free).

8. **Docket your annual report deadline.** Your first annual report is due by the first day of the month of your formation anniversary, one year after you form the LLC.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to form an LLC in Wyoming?**
The Wyoming LLC filing fee is $100. If you use a professional registered agent, add $49–$125/year. Using a formation service adds $0–$149 in service fees. Total first-year cost is typically $100–$249.

**What is the annual fee for a Wyoming LLC?**
Wyoming LLCs pay a minimum annual report fee of $60 per year, due by the first of the month in which the LLC was formed. If your LLC has over $300,000 in Wyoming-located assets, the fee is 0.0002 × total assets (whichever is higher than $60).

**Does Wyoming have an LLC franchise tax?**
No. Wyoming does not impose a franchise tax or minimum annual tax on LLCs beyond the $60 annual report fee. This is one of Wyoming's key advantages over states like California ($800/year minimum) and Delaware ($300/year).

**Does Wyoming have a state income tax on LLCs?**
No. Wyoming has no state income tax — neither personal nor corporate. LLC profits pass through to members without any Wyoming state tax on that income. Note that you still owe federal income tax and any applicable tax in your home state.

**Can I form a Wyoming LLC if I don't live in Wyoming?**
Yes. Non-residents can form Wyoming LLCs, and this is extremely common. You'll need a registered agent with a Wyoming street address. However, if you conduct business in another state, you may need to foreign-qualify your LLC there and pay that state's fees as well.

**How long does it take to form a Wyoming LLC?**
Online filings with the Wyoming Secretary of State are typically processed within 1–2 business days. There's no expedited filing option because standard processing is already fast.

**Do I need a business license in Wyoming?**
Wyoming does not have a general state business license. You may need local licenses depending on your city or county, and industry-specific licenses depending on your profession. Check with your local municipality and the Wyoming Department of Revenue for any applicable requirements.

**What happens if I miss the Wyoming annual report deadline?**
Wyoming gives you a 60-day grace period after the deadline before administratively dissolving your LLC. Reinstatement costs $100 plus any back annual report fees. Dissolution can jeopardize your liability protection — read our [guide on what happens if you don't renew your LLC](/blog/what-happens-if-you-don-t-renew-your-llc/) for the full consequences.
