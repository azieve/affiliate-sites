## Pricing Breakdown: What You'll Actually Pay in 2026

This is where the two companies genuinely diverge, and where most reviews gloss over the details that actually matter.

**LegalZoom's LLC formation pricing** is structured in three tiers, plus your state's filing fee (which ranges from $35 in Montana to $500 in Massachusetts):

- **Basic** — $0 + state fee. You get the Articles of Organization prepared and filed, and that's essentially it.
- **Pro** — roughly $249 + state fee. Adds an operating agreement template, an EIN, and expedited processing.
- **Premium** — roughly $299 + state fee. Adds a domain name, business email, and some compliance tooling.

The catch: registered agent service is not included in any tier. LegalZoom charges separately for that — typically around $249/year after a limited trial period. Add a few compliance add-ons (worry-free compliance monitoring, business licenses, banking resolution templates) and it's easy to walk away having spent $400–$600 in your first year for what looked like a "$0" LLC.

**Rocket Lawyer's pricing works differently.** Instead of one-time package tiers, Rocket Lawyer sells a subscription — around $39.99/month (billed monthly) for its "Premium" membership, which includes one free LLC filing (you still pay the state fee), access to attorney consultations, and unlimited legal document templates. If you don't want the subscription, you can pay a one-time fee of roughly $99.99 + state fee for formation without membership, but then you lose access to the registered agent and document library that make Rocket Lawyer worth considering in the first place.

Here's the part that trips people up: Rocket Lawyer's monthly plan auto-renews, and if you forget to cancel after your LLC is formed, you'll keep paying $39.99/month indefinitely — that's roughly $480/year for a service you may only need once. LegalZoom's model, while not cheap, at least ends when you stop paying for add-ons.

## Formation Speed and Process

Both companies process filings quickly, but neither is the fastest on the market.

LegalZoom typically submits your filing to the state within 2–3 business days on standard processing, or same-day/next-day with expedited service (an added fee). Rocket Lawyer's turnaround is similar — a few business days for standard filing, with rush options available depending on your state.

In my experience advising clients through this exact decision, the speed difference between LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer rarely matters in practice — state processing times (which can run 1–4 weeks depending on the state) are usually the real bottleneck, not how fast the formation company submits your paperwork. Where you actually save time is picking a service with a genuinely simple intake questionnaire. LegalZoom's has gotten more streamlined in recent years; Rocket Lawyer's leans more heavily on you navigating its broader legal-document platform to find the LLC formation flow, which can feel cluttered if you only want one thing done.

## Registered Agent, Operating Agreements, and Compliance Features

This is where the LegalZoom vs Rocket Lawyer for LLC comparison gets more nuanced, because "features" aren't apples to apples.

**Registered agent service:** Every LLC needs a [registered agent](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) — a person or company designated to receive legal and state correspondence on the business's behalf. LegalZoom charges roughly $249/year for this after any promotional period ends. Rocket Lawyer bundles it into the monthly membership, so it's technically "included" but you're paying for it every month regardless of whether you need anything else from the platform that month. Compare that to [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/), which is built around registered agent service first and includes a full year free with formation — a genuinely better structural fit if privacy and agent reliability are your top priority.

**Operating agreements:** Both LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer provide operating agreement templates, but neither is a substitute for a customized document reviewed with your specific ownership structure in mind — especially for multi-member LLCs splitting profits unevenly, or those with buy-sell provisions. Our [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) walks through what a template will and won't cover.

**Compliance monitoring:** LegalZoom's "worry-free compliance" add-on tracks your state's annual report deadlines for a recurring fee. Rocket Lawyer's compliance tracking is folded into the membership and includes reminders, but it's a less specialized tool than what dedicated formation services like ZenBusiness or Bizee build their entire compliance dashboard around.

**Beneficial Ownership Information reporting:** With FinCEN's BOI reporting requirements continuing to evolve for U.S. companies under the Corporate Transparency Act, it's worth checking whether your formation service proactively flags this for you — neither LegalZoom nor Rocket Lawyer makes BOI compliance a headline feature, so don't assume it's automatically handled. You can review current requirements directly on [FinCEN's official BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi), and our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) breaks down deadlines and penalties in plain English.

## Legal Services Beyond Formation: Where Rocket Lawyer Tries to Differentiate

Rocket Lawyer's pitch isn't really "we're the best LLC formation service" — it's "we're an ongoing legal services subscription that happens to include LLC formation." Its membership gives you access to on-call attorney consultations (typically 30-minute sessions at no extra charge, then discounted rates beyond that), plus a large library of legal document templates for things like NDAs, lease agreements, and employment contracts.

If you're a business owner who anticipates needing recurring legal document support — not just a one-time formation — that ongoing access has real value. A commercial lease review or a vendor contract consult from a licensed attorney can easily run $300–$500 an hour outside a subscription like this, so a few consultations a year can justify the membership cost on its own.

LegalZoom offers something similar through its own "LegalZoom Legal Advantage" attorney network, but it's positioned as more of an upsell than a core part of the platform's identity. For business owners who only need the LLC formed and don't anticipate needing regular attorney access, both companies' legal-services layers are essentially unnecessary overhead — you're paying for infrastructure you won't use.

## Tax and Liability Considerations Neither Service Will Tell You

This is the part I always stress with clients regardless of which formation company they choose: neither LegalZoom nor Rocket Lawyer is going to advise you on entity-level tax strategy, and that's not a criticism — it's simply outside the scope of a DIY formation product. Whether your LLC should elect S-corp taxation, how you should be paying yourself, and how your state's franchise or annual report fees affect your bottom line are all decisions that benefit from a conversation with a CPA or tax attorney, not a checkbox during checkout.

The IRS's own guidance confirms that an LLC has no separate federal tax classification by default — it's taxed as a sole proprietorship, partnership, or corporation depending on elections you make (see the [IRS's overview of LLC tax classification](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc)). Getting that election wrong, or missing it entirely, can cost far more over a few years than anything you'd save picking one formation service over another. The U.S. Small Business Administration's [guide to choosing a business structure](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure) is a solid starting point if you're still deciding whether an LLC is the right entity at all — our own [LLC vs S-Corp](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) and [LLC vs sole proprietorship](/learn/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship/) breakdowns go further into the tradeoffs.

I've seen too many business owners treat the formation company as a one-stop shop for every legal and tax decision tied to launching a business. Neither LegalZoom nor Rocket Lawyer is a substitute for that — and to their credit, neither actually claims to be, even if their marketing sometimes implies more hand-holding than you'll actually get.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is LegalZoom or Rocket Lawyer better for LLC formation?**
For a one-time LLC formation with no ongoing legal document needs, LegalZoom is generally the better fit because you're not locked into a recurring monthly charge. Rocket Lawyer makes more sense if you expect to need attorney consultations or legal templates regularly throughout the year, since the membership cost gets spread across more value.

**How much does it cost to form an LLC with LegalZoom vs Rocket Lawyer?**
LegalZoom's Basic tier starts at $0 plus your state's filing fee, with Pro and Premium tiers running roughly $249–$299 plus state fees. Rocket Lawyer charges about $39.99/month for membership (which includes one LLC filing plus your state fee) or roughly $99.99 plus state fee as a one-time formation without membership. Our [LLC cost guide](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/) breaks down state filing fees individually.

**Do I need a registered agent if I use LegalZoom or Rocket Lawyer?**
Yes — every U.S. state requires an LLC to maintain a registered agent. LegalZoom charges separately for this (about $249/year after any trial period), while Rocket Lawyer includes it as part of an active membership. If your service doesn't include it and you don't want to pay the add-on, you can also serve as your own registered agent in most states, provided you have a physical address in the state of formation.

**What happens if I cancel my Rocket Lawyer membership after forming my LLC?**
Your LLC remains legally formed and valid regardless of your membership status — cancelling the subscription doesn't affect your entity's existence. However, you'll lose access to the registered agent service bundled into the membership, so you'll need to appoint a new registered agent before your coverage lapses to stay in good standing with your state.

**Can I switch from LegalZoom or Rocket Lawyer to another formation service later?**
Yes. You can change your registered agent or compliance provider at any time — it doesn't require re-forming the LLC. Our guide on [how to change your registered agent](/blog/how-to-change-registered-agent/) covers the process step by step.

**Does either service help with BOI reporting requirements?**
Neither LegalZoom nor Rocket Lawyer makes Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reporting a central feature of its standard packages as of 2026. You're responsible for confirming your own filing obligations under the Corporate Transparency Act — check [FinCEN's BOI resource page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) directly, or see our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for deadlines and penalty details.

**Is ZenBusiness cheaper than both LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer?**
In most cases, yes — ZenBusiness's $0-plus-state-fee starting tier with a free first-year registered agent typically undercuts LegalZoom's paid add-on structure and avoids Rocket Lawyer's recurring monthly membership entirely. That said, exact savings depend on which add-ons and tiers you compare, so it's worth checking current pricing on each provider's site before deciding.

**Do I need an LLC at all, or would a sole proprietorship work?**
It depends on your liability exposure and growth plans. If you're taking on contracts, hiring employees, or carrying any meaningful liability risk, an LLC's personal asset protection is usually worth the formation and annual costs. Our [LLC vs sole proprietorship](/learn/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship/) guide and [what is an LLC](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) primer cover the tradeoffs in more depth.

## Final Thoughts

The LegalZoom vs Rocket Lawyer for LLC decision ultimately comes down to how you plan to use the service after formation day. If you want a single, clean transaction with no recurring commitment, LegalZoom's one-time-fee model is the more predictable choice. If you genuinely expect to lean on attorney consultations and legal templates throughout the year, Rocket Lawyer's membership can pay for itself. But for the majority of small business owners who just need a properly filed, compliant LLC without paying for infrastructure they won't use, [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) remains the option I point clients toward first in 2026 — and [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) is a reasonable second choice if you want the brand recognition and don't mind paying a bit more for it.

Whichever provider you choose, don't let the formation company be your only source of tax and compliance guidance — a quick conversation with a CPA or business attorney before you file can save you far more than any discount code.
