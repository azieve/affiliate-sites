## Why Virginia Is a Smart State for LLC Formation in 2026

Virginia doesn't generate the same headlines as Delaware or Wyoming in entity formation circles, but for businesses that actually plan to operate in the commonwealth, it holds up well against both.

- **No franchise tax.** Virginia doesn't impose a franchise tax on LLCs. Compare that to California's brutal $800 minimum franchise tax (paid regardless of revenue) or Texas's franchise tax on gross receipts above certain thresholds. Virginia simply charges a flat $50 Annual Registration Fee.
- **Streamlined online filing.** The Virginia State Corporation Commission's eFile system processes standard Articles of Organization filings in 1-3 business days. Same-day and next-day expedited options are available for an additional fee.
- **Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET) election.** Since 2022, Virginia has allowed LLCs taxed as partnerships or S-corps to pay state income tax at the entity level — a legitimate workaround for the federal SALT deduction cap. If your LLC generates significant net income, this is worth a conversation with your CPA.
- **Member privacy.** Virginia does not require member or manager names in the Articles of Organization, which means your ownership information stays off the public record at the state level.
- **Competitive cost structure.** At $100 to file and $50 per year to maintain, Virginia LLC formation is among the more affordable in the mid-Atlantic region.

## Step 1: Choose a Name for Your Virginia LLC

Your name must comply with Virginia's LLC naming requirements before you can file anything:

**Required identifier.** The name must include one of the following:
- "Limited Liability Company"
- "LLC" or "L.L.C."
- "Limited Company," "Ltd. Co.," or "LC"

**Distinguishable from existing entities.** Your name must be distinguishable from any other business entity already on record with the Virginia State Corporation Commission. Use the [SCC's Business Entity Search tool](https://cis.scc.virginia.gov/) to check availability before you commit to a name.

**Restricted words.** Certain words — "bank," "trust," "attorney," "university," "federal," "national" — require regulatory approval or imply government affiliation. Using them without authorization will get your application rejected.

**Optional: Name Reservation.** If you're not ready to file immediately, you can reserve your preferred name for 120 days by filing an Application for Reservation of Name with the SCC and paying a **$10 fee**. This is worth doing if you're still working on your operating agreement, business plan, or financing.

One practical step that often gets overlooked: check domain name availability before locking in your business name. Plenty of founders secure a Virginia LLC name only to discover the matching .com is parked or already claimed. Run both the SCC search and a domain search simultaneously.

## Step 2: Appoint a Virginia Registered Agent

Every Virginia LLC is required by law to maintain a registered agent — an individual or entity with a physical street address in Virginia who is available during standard business hours to receive service of process, official government correspondence, and legal notices on behalf of the LLC.

You have three routes:

**Option 1: Serve as your own registered agent.** You can designate yourself if you have a Virginia street address (P.O. boxes are not permitted) and are reliably available at that address during business hours. The drawback: your name and address appear in the public record, and you risk missing a critical legal notice if you're ever traveling or unavailable.

**Option 2: Designate a trusted individual.** An employee, partner, or attorney with a Virginia address can serve as your registered agent. Same availability requirements apply.

## Step 3: File Articles of Organization with the Virginia SCC

The Articles of Organization is the official document that legally creates your Virginia LLC. You file it directly with the Virginia State Corporation Commission.

**Filing fee: $100** (same whether filed online or by mail)

**Filing methods:**
- **Online via SCC eFile:** Fastest option; most filings processed within 1-3 business days.
- **By mail:** Send to the Clerk's Office of the SCC in Richmond. Processing is slower.
- **In person:** Walk-in submissions are accepted at the SCC office in Richmond.

**Expedited processing fees:**
- Same-day processing: +$200
- Next-day processing: +$100

**What the Articles of Organization must include:**
- LLC name (with the required designator)
- Principal office address (can be located outside Virginia, but must be a street address)
- Registered agent's name and Virginia street address
- Registered agent's signed consent to serve
- Name and signature of the organizer (doesn't have to be a member or manager)

Once the SCC approves your filing, you'll receive a Certificate of Organization. This is your LLC's official formation document — keep it on file, because banks and licensing agencies will ask to see it.

The whole process typically takes the same 1-3 business days plus any shipping time.

## Step 4: Create an Operating Agreement

Virginia does not legally require an LLC to have an operating agreement. That fact misleads a lot of founders into skipping it. Don't.

An operating agreement is your LLC's internal rulebook. It governs how the business is owned, how profits and losses are allocated, how decisions get made, what happens when a member wants to exit, and how disputes are resolved. Without one, Virginia's default LLC statutes (Title 13.1 of the Virginia Code) fill the gaps — and the statutory defaults often don't align with what co-founders actually intended when they started the business.

For a **single-member LLC**, an operating agreement:
- Strengthens your liability protection by documenting that the LLC is a separate legal entity from you personally
- Is typically required by banks before they'll open a business checking account
- Addresses what happens to the business if you become incapacitated or die

For a **multi-member LLC**, an operating agreement is non-negotiable. The document should define ownership percentages, voting rights, profit distribution, buy-out procedures, and what happens when a member wants out. I've seen co-founder disputes that ran into six-figure legal fees — entirely because there was no written agreement about exit terms.

## Step 5: Obtain an EIN and Open a Business Bank Account

**Employer Identification Number (EIN)**

An EIN is your LLC's federal tax identification number, issued by the IRS at no cost. You need one to:
- Open a business bank account
- Hire employees
- File certain federal and Virginia state tax forms
- Apply for most business licenses and permits

Apply directly through [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). The online application takes about 10 minutes and delivers your EIN immediately upon completion. There is no reason to pay a formation service for EIN filing — it's free and takes less time than filling out a service's order form.

Single-member LLCs with no employees can use their Social Security Number instead of an EIN in some contexts, but getting an EIN is still best practice. It keeps your SSN off business paperwork and signals that you're running a legitimate separate entity.

**Business Bank Account**

Separating business finances from personal finances is the single most important thing you can do to preserve your LLC's liability shield. Courts look at commingling of funds as evidence that the LLC and owner are not truly separate — which can lead to a "piercing of the corporate veil," exposing your personal assets to business creditors.

Most banks require: your EIN, your Certificate of Organization, your operating agreement, and sometimes a government-issued ID. Some Virginia banks may also ask for a local business license.

## Step 6: Handle Ongoing Virginia LLC Compliance

Forming the LLC is step one. Keeping it in good standing is what protects you year after year.

**Annual Registration Fee**

Virginia requires every LLC to pay an **Annual Registration Fee of $50** each year. The fee is due by the last day of the month in which your LLC was originally formed. The SCC mails a reminder notice, but responsibility for timely filing rests with you. Miss it long enough and the SCC will administratively dissolve your LLC — which creates significant headaches to reverse.

**Business Professional and Occupational License (BPOL)**

Most Virginia localities levy a BPOL tax on businesses operating within their jurisdiction. Rates and gross receipts thresholds vary significantly by city and county. Richmond, Virginia Beach, and Alexandria each publish their own BPOL schedules. Many localities exempt businesses with gross receipts below $100,000 in year one, but that threshold varies. Contact your local Commissioner of the Revenue's office to confirm what applies to your business.

**State and Local Taxes**

Virginia LLC members report their share of LLC income on personal Virginia income tax returns (Form 760). Virginia's individual income tax tops out at **5.75%** on taxable income above $17,000. If your LLC has elected S-corp status for federal purposes, Virginia follows the federal classification.

Virginia also conforms to the federal Pass-Through Entity Tax (PTET) election — an election worth evaluating with a CPA if your LLC clears significant net income and you itemize federal deductions.

**Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) Report**

Under the Corporate Transparency Act, most newly formed LLCs must file a Beneficial Ownership Information report with the U.S. Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN). For LLCs formed in 2026, the initial BOI report is due within 30 days of the formation date. This is a federal requirement — separate from anything the Virginia State Corporation Commission requires — and the penalties for non-compliance can be significant. See the [BOI Report Guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for current deadlines, exemptions, and step-by-step filing instructions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Virginia?**

The minimum mandatory cost is **$100** — the Articles of Organization filing fee payable to the Virginia State Corporation Commission. Add registered agent service ($99–$249/year) and you're looking at $199–$349 for a typical first year. The state's Annual Registration Fee of $50 kicks in each subsequent year.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in Virginia?**

Standard online filing through the SCC eFile system takes **1-3 business days** for approval. Same-day processing is available for an additional $200 fee; next-day processing costs an additional $100.

**Does Virginia require an operating agreement for an LLC?**

No, Virginia does not legally require one. However, most attorneys and financial advisors recommend having an operating agreement regardless — especially for multi-member LLCs. Without one, Virginia's default LLC statutes govern your business, and those defaults rarely match what co-founders actually intended. Banks also commonly require an operating agreement before opening a business account.

**What is a registered agent, and do I need one in Virginia?**

Yes, a registered agent is legally required for every Virginia LLC. This is an individual or company with a physical Virginia street address who receives official legal and government documents on your behalf during business hours. You can serve as your own registered agent, but most business owners hire a service ($99–$249/year) to keep their personal address off the public record and ensure documents are never missed.

**Can I be my own registered agent in Virginia?**

Yes, provided you have a physical Virginia street address (not a P.O. box) and are available at that address during regular business hours. The tradeoff: your address becomes part of the public record, and you're personally responsible for receiving time-sensitive legal documents. If you work remotely or travel frequently, a registered agent service is the safer choice.

**Does Virginia have a franchise tax on LLCs?**

No. Virginia does not impose a franchise tax on LLCs. You'll owe the $50 Annual Registration Fee to the SCC, and LLC members pay Virginia individual income tax (up to 5.75%) on their share of LLC earnings, but there is no separate entity-level franchise or privilege tax.

**Do I need a business license for my Virginia LLC?**

It depends on your location and industry. Most Virginia cities and counties require a Business Professional and Occupational License (BPOL), with rates and gross receipts thresholds that vary by locality. Certain industries also require state-level licenses (contractors, food service operators, healthcare professionals, etc.). Check with your local Commissioner of the Revenue and the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation for your specific requirements.

**Does a Virginia LLC need to file a BOI report?**

Most Virginia LLCs do, yes. Under the Corporate Transparency Act, LLCs formed in 2026 are generally required to file an initial Beneficial Ownership Information report with FinCEN within 30 days of formation. Significant penalties can apply for non-compliance. See the [BOI Report Guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for current exemptions, deadlines, and filing steps, and confirm current enforcement status with a qualified attorney given ongoing litigation history around this requirement.
