If you pay an LLC for services, or you run an LLC that gets paid, you've probably wondered: **do LLCs get a 1099?** The short answer is "sometimes." It depends on how the LLC is taxed, not on the fact that it's an LLC. An LLC isn't a tax category. It's a legal structure, and the IRS looks through that structure to decide which rules apply.

This guide explains which LLCs receive a 1099, which don't, the exceptions that catch people off guard, and what changes in 2026. ## The Short Answer: It Depends on How the LLC Is Taxed

The IRS doesn't have a separate tax classification for LLCs. By default, an LLC is taxed in one of these ways:

| LLC type | Default tax treatment | Usually receives a 1099-NEC? |
|---|---|---|
| Single-member LLC | Disregarded entity (taxed like a sole proprietor) | **Yes** |
| Multi-member LLC | Partnership | **Yes** |
| LLC that elected S corp status | Corporation | **Generally no** |
| LLC that elected C corp status | Corporation | **Generally no** |

So when people ask whether LLCs get a 1099, the practical answer is that **single-member and multi-member LLCs (the default setups) generally do get a 1099-NEC** when a business pays them $600 or more for services in the year. LLCs taxed as corporations generally don't, with two important exceptions covered below.

The IRS explains the reporting rules in its [Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec). They're worth bookmarking if you pay contractors.

## Why Single-Member and Multi-Member LLCs Get a 1099

A single-member LLC is a "disregarded entity." The IRS ignores the LLC and treats the owner as a sole proprietor. A payment to the LLC is treated as a payment to the individual. That's why the payer reports it on a 1099-NEC, just as they would for a freelancer.

A multi-member LLC is taxed as a partnership by default. The IRS rule that exempts payments to corporations from 1099 reporting doesn't cover partnerships. A payment to a partnership-taxed LLC is generally reportable.

Here's a concrete example. Maya runs a single-member LLC doing freelance web design. A client pays her $14,500 during the year. In January, the client should send Maya a 1099-NEC for $14,500. Maya reports that income on Schedule C, and she owes self-employment tax on the net profit. Our guide to [self-employment tax for LLC owners](/blog/self-employment-tax-for-llc-owners/) shows how that's calculated.

## When an LLC Does Not Get a 1099 (and the Exceptions)

If an LLC has elected to be taxed as an S corp or C corp, payments to it for services are generally exempt from 1099-NEC reporting. This is the "payments to corporations" exemption.

There are two big exceptions, and I see them missed constantly:

1. **Attorney fees.** Payments for legal services are generally reportable even when the law firm is a corporation. If you pay a corporate-taxed law firm $5,000, you should still issue a 1099-NEC (or a 1099-MISC for gross proceeds paid to attorneys).
2. **Medical and healthcare payments.** Payments to medical and healthcare providers are generally reportable even when the provider is taxed as a corporation. These go on a 1099-MISC.

There are other situations where a 1099 isn't required regardless of entity type:

- **Payments for merchandise or goods.** The 1099-NEC covers services, not inventory or products.
- **Payments made by credit card or payment app.** The processor reports these on a Form 1099-K, so you don't also report them. Payers who use credit cards usually skip the 1099-NEC for those payments.
- **Payments below the threshold.** More on that next.

In my experience, the most common mistake isn't failing to send a 1099 to a sole proprietor. It's assuming that every business with "LLC" in its name is a corporation for tax purposes. It isn't, and the only reliable way to know is to ask for a Form W-9.

## The 1099 Threshold in 2026

For payments made through 2025, the threshold for 1099-NEC and most 1099-MISC reporting was $600. Under legislation passed in 2025 (often called the One Big Beautiful Bill Act), the threshold increases to **$2,000 for payments made after December 31, 2025**, and it's indexed for inflation after that. The change is meant to reduce paperwork for small payments.

What that means in 2026:

- **For 2025 payments (reported in January 2026):** the $600 threshold still applies.
- **For 2026 payments (reported in January 2027):** the $2,000 threshold applies.

Thresholds and rules change, so confirm the current figure on IRS.gov or with a tax professional before filing. Even when you're under the threshold, the income is still taxable. The threshold only determines whether the payer must file a form, not whether the recipient reports the income.

## How to Know Whether to Send a 1099 to an LLC

If you're the one paying, follow this process:

1. **Collect a Form W-9 before the first payment.** The W-9 asks the LLC for its legal name, tax classification, and taxpayer identification number. For a single-member LLC, the form shows the owner's name and SSN or EIN. For a multi-member LLC, it shows the LLC's EIN.
2. **Check Box 3 (federal tax classification).** The box will say "Individual/sole proprietor or single-member LLC," "Partnership," "C Corporation," "S Corporation," or "Limited liability company" with a tax code letter (C, S, or P).
3. **Decide whether a 1099 is required.** If the box indicates a corporation (and the payment isn't legal or medical), you generally don't need a 1099. Otherwise, you probably do once you cross the threshold.
4. **File by January 31.** Furnish the 1099-NEC to the recipient and file with the IRS by January 31 of the following year.

A W-9 is free and takes the guesswork out. If a vendor refuses to give you one, you may have to apply **backup withholding at 24%**, which is a good incentive for them to complete the form.

## What Happens If You Skip a Required 1099

Failing to file correct 1099s on time brings per-form penalties. For forms due in 2026, the penalties range from roughly $60 per form if you correct quickly, to $340 per form if you file late or not at all. Intentional disregard carries a higher penalty with no annual cap. These amounts adjust periodically, so check the IRS penalty schedule.

For a business with ten contractors, that adds up fast. I've seen business owners spend more on late-filing penalties than a bookkeeper would have charged for the entire year. Many owners fix the problem by setting up a W-9 request as part of their vendor onboarding checklist.

## Receiving a 1099 as an LLC Owner: What to Do

If you own an LLC and receive a 1099-NEC, here's how to handle it:

- **Single-member LLC:** Report the income on Schedule C of your personal return. Expect to pay self-employment tax (15.3% on net earnings, subject to limits) plus income tax. Making [quarterly estimated payments](/blog/llc-quarterly-tax-payments-guide/) helps you avoid underpayment penalties.
- **Multi-member LLC:** The partnership reports income on Form 1065 and issues each member a Schedule K-1. The LLC itself may receive 1099s from customers. Those get reported on the partnership return.
- **LLC taxed as an S corp:** The business reports income on Form 1120-S. You pay yourself a reasonable salary through payroll. Our guide on [reasonable salary for S corp LLC owners](/blog/reasonable-salary-for-s-corp-llc-owners/) covers this in detail.
- **Mismatched numbers:** If a 1099 shows the wrong name, amount, or TIN, ask the payer for a corrected form. Don't ignore it. The IRS matches 1099 data against your return.

Also keep an eye on **Form 1099-K**. If you take payments through PayPal, Stripe, Venmo, or similar platforms, you may receive a 1099-K showing gross payments. Under the 2025 legislation, the 1099-K threshold reverted to the older standard of more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions. Even if you don't get a 1099-K, you still need to report all your income.

The LLC is about liability protection and credibility. ## A Quick 2026 Checklist

- Get a W-9 from every contractor or vendor before paying them.
- Note each vendor's tax classification (not just whether the name ends in "LLC").
- Track payments by vendor all year, not in a January scramble.
- Remember the legal and medical exceptions for corporate-taxed payees.
- Use the correct threshold: $600 for 2025 payments, $2,000 for payments made in 2026 and later.
- File and furnish 1099-NECs by January 31.
- If you own an LLC, set aside money for taxes on 1099 income.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do LLCs get a 1099?

Often, yes. Single-member LLCs and multi-member LLCs (taxed as partnerships) generally receive a 1099-NEC if they're paid at or above the threshold for services. LLCs that have elected to be taxed as S or C corporations generally don't, aside from legal and medical payments.

### Do I need to send a 1099 to an LLC I hired?

You need to if the LLC is taxed as a sole proprietorship (single-member) or partnership, you paid it at least the threshold amount for services, and you paid by check, cash, or ACH rather than by credit card. Request a W-9 to confirm its tax classification.

### Does a single-member LLC get a 1099-NEC?

Yes. A single-member LLC is a disregarded entity by default, so the IRS treats payments to it as payments to the owner. The payer typically issues a 1099-NEC under the owner's name and SSN or the LLC's EIN, as shown on the W-9.

### Do LLCs taxed as S corps get a 1099?

Generally no, because payments to corporations are exempt from 1099-NEC reporting. The exceptions are payments for legal services and medical or healthcare services, which are still reportable.

### What is the 1099 threshold for 2026?

For payments made in 2025 (reported in early 2026), the threshold is $600. For payments made in 2026 and later, the threshold rises to $2,000 under recent legislation. Confirm with the IRS or a tax professional before filing.

### What if an LLC doesn't give me a W-9?

You can still decide on reporting by researching the vendor, but you may be required to start backup withholding at 24% on payments. Document your requests for the W-9 so you can show a good-faith effort.

### Do I pay taxes if I don't receive a 1099?

Yes. All income is taxable whether or not you get a form. The 1099 is just an information return the IRS uses to cross-check what you report.

### Does paying an LLC through PayPal or a credit card require a 1099-NEC?

Generally no. Payments made by credit card or third-party payment network are reported by the processor on Form 1099-K, so the payer doesn't also report them on a 1099-NEC.

## Final Thoughts

So, do LLCs get a 1099? Most do. The default single-member and multi-member LLC setups receive 1099-NECs for service payments, while LLCs taxed as corporations are generally exempt except for legal and medical payments. The W-9 is your friend. It tells you what you need to know with almost no effort, and it protects you if the IRS ever asks questions.
