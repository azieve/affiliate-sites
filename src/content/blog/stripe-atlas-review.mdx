## What Is Stripe Atlas and How Does It Work?

Stripe Atlas is an incorporation service run by Stripe, the payments company. You complete an online application, and Atlas files your formation documents with the state, mainly Delaware. It also handles several follow-on tasks that founders often postpone.

In general, the Atlas package includes:

- **Delaware entity formation**, with the state filing fee bundled into the one-time price
- **A registered agent** for the first year (renewal is billed separately afterward)
- **An EIN** from the IRS
- **Founder stock issuance paperwork** and bylaws, if you choose a C-corp
- **An 83(b) election** filing guide, which matters for founders with vesting stock
- **Access to business banking partners** and a perks bundle from Stripe's partner network

The upfront price has historically been about $500. Stripe has updated its offering over time, so verify the current scope on [Stripe's Atlas page](https://stripe.com/atlas) before purchasing.

In my experience, the real value of Atlas isn't the filing itself. Filing a Delaware entity is not hard. The value is the packaged startup paperwork, which founders routinely get wrong when they DIY it.

## Stripe Atlas LLC vs. Delaware C-Corp: Which Should You Pick?

This is the first decision, and it matters more than the provider you choose. Atlas has centered on the Delaware C-corp, the structure most venture investors expect. A Stripe Atlas LLC option has also been available, but check the current entity menu, since Stripe has changed what it offers over the years.

Here is how I'd think about it:

- **Planning to raise venture capital or issue stock options?** A Delaware C-corp is the standard. This is where Atlas is strongest.
- **Running a service business, e-commerce shop, or consultancy with no outside investors?** An LLC is usually simpler, with pass-through taxation and fewer formalities. See our [LLC vs S-Corp guide](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) and the full [LLC vs corporation comparison](/blog/llc-vs-corporation-which-should-i-choose/) for the tax trade-offs.
- **Not sure?** Read [What Is an LLC?](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) before paying for anything.

One caution from a legal standpoint: forming in Delaware doesn't exempt you from your home state. If you live and operate in Texas, for example, you'll likely need to register as a foreign entity there too. Our guide to [foreign LLC registration](/blog/foreign-llc-registration-doing-business-in-another-state/) explains how that works, and it effectively means paying two states.

## Stripe Atlas Cost: What You'll Actually Pay in 2026

The headline number is about $500, but the true cost over the first few years looks different. Here's a realistic view for a Delaware entity in 2026.

| Cost item | Typical amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Atlas one-time fee | ~$500 | Includes Delaware filing; verify current price |
| Registered agent renewal | ~$100/year after year one | Confirm current rate with Stripe |
| Delaware LLC annual tax | $300/year | Due June 1; see our [Delaware franchise tax guide](/blog/delaware-llc-annual-franchise-tax/) |
| Delaware C-corp franchise tax | Varies (minimum often $175 under assumed par value method, up to much more under authorized shares method) | Easy to overpay; file carefully |
| Home-state foreign registration | Varies by state | Required if you operate outside Delaware |

The Delaware Division of Corporations publishes current fee schedules at [corp.delaware.gov](https://corp.delaware.gov/), which I recommend checking directly.

A gotcha I've seen too many first-time founders hit: the Delaware C-corp franchise tax notice. If you calculate it using the default "authorized shares" method, the bill can run into the thousands, even for a company with no revenue. The "assumed par value capital" method usually produces a far smaller number. Atlas gives guidance here, but you or your accountant still have to file correctly.

## Pros and Cons of Stripe Atlas

**What Atlas does well**

- **Investor-friendly structure.** A Delaware C-corp is familiar to venture investors and their lawyers.
- **Startup-specific paperwork.** Stock issuance and 83(b) guidance are things generic LLC services don't emphasize.
- **Speed and simplicity.** Everything happens in one online flow, and you don't need to assemble documents yourself.
- **Stripe ecosystem.** Perks and integrations with Stripe's products can help early-stage companies.

**Where Atlas falls short**

- **Overkill for simple businesses.** If you're a solo operator, you're paying for features you won't use.
- **Limited ongoing compliance support.** Annual reports, franchise tax, and state registrations remain largely your job, and you may want a service that sends reminders.
- **No substitute for legal advice.** Cap table design, founder agreements, and IP assignment often warrant a real attorney, even if you use Atlas for the filing.
- **Delaware isn't always cheaper.** You may pay Delaware taxes plus your home state's fees. For state selection, see our [best state to form an LLC](/blog/best-state-to-form-an-llc/) guide.

Both the CFO and the legal lens point to the same takeaway: Atlas shortens the first week of a startup's life but does not reduce the first year of administrative work.

## Who Should Use Stripe Atlas (and Who Shouldn't)

**Atlas is a reasonable choice if you:**

- Are a first-time founder building a venture-style startup
- Expect to grant equity to cofounders, employees, or advisors
- Want a Delaware C-corp without hiring a law firm for the basics
- Plan to use Stripe for payments anyway

**Choose a different service if you:**

- Run a freelance or consulting business (see our [LLC for freelancers](/llc-for-freelancers-and-independent-contractors/) resource, or the [freelancer guide](/llc-for/freelancers/))
- Operate in a single state with no outside investors
- Want ongoing compliance reminders and registered agent support bundled in

## Compliance Steps to Plan for After Forming

Whichever service you use, formation is just the start. Plan for these in 2026:

1. **Beneficial ownership reporting.** Rules for FinCEN's BOI reporting have shifted repeatedly, so check current requirements in our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) and [BOI updates for 2026](/blog/boi-report-changes-and-updates-2026/).
2. **An operating agreement or bylaws.** Even single-owner LLCs benefit from one. See our [operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/).
3. **A business bank account.** Keep finances separate to protect your liability shield; see how to [open an LLC bank account](/blog/llc-bank-account-how-to-open-one/).
4. **Tax elections.** Guidance on entity classification and S-corp elections is available at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc).
5. **Annual filings and franchise taxes.** Missing these can lead to penalties or loss of good standing.

If you're issuing founder stock subject to vesting, the 83(b) election has a strict 30-day deadline from the date of the stock transfer. Missing it can create significant tax consequences, so talk to a tax professional promptly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does Stripe Atlas cost?

Stripe Atlas has historically charged a one-time fee of about $500, which includes the Delaware state filing fee. Registered agent renewal after the first year and Delaware annual taxes are extra. Confirm current pricing on Stripe's website.

### Is Stripe Atlas worth it for an LLC?

It depends on your goals. If you want a Delaware entity and the startup paperwork bundle, it can be. For a standard single-state LLC, services like ZenBusiness are typically cheaper because the base package starts at $0 plus state fees.

### Can I form an LLC with Stripe Atlas?

Atlas has offered Delaware LLC formation alongside its C-corp option. Because Stripe updates its offerings, check the current entity choices before you apply.

### Do I need Stripe Atlas to use Stripe payments?

No. You can use Stripe with any properly formed business entity, including one formed through another provider or directly with your state.

### What happens after the first year with Stripe Atlas?

You'll owe Delaware annual taxes, typically $300 for an LLC due June 1, plus franchise tax and an annual report for a C-corp. You'll also pay for registered agent renewal if you keep Atlas's service. You may owe your home state fees, too.

### Is Stripe Atlas better than ZenBusiness?

They serve different customers. Atlas fits venture-backed startups seeking a Delaware C-corp. ZenBusiness fits small businesses that want an inexpensive LLC with ongoing compliance support.

### Do I still need a lawyer if I use Stripe Atlas?

Often, yes. Atlas automates filings, but it doesn't replace advice on founder agreements, equity splits, or intellectual property. A short consultation with a startup attorney can be worth the cost.

### How long does Stripe Atlas take?

Stripe advertises fast turnaround, often within days, once your information is complete. Timelines vary with state processing and IRS EIN issuance.
