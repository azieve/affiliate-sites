## ITIN vs EIN: What's the Difference for a Foreign LLC Owner?

Founders mix these two numbers up more than anything else, and mixing them up causes most of the delays I see.

- **EIN (Employer Identification Number):** a nine-digit number the IRS assigns to the *LLC*. It is used to open a bank account, file business returns, and report to the IRS. You do **not** need an SSN or ITIN to get one. Our walkthrough on [getting an EIN for your LLC](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-for-your-llc-free-irs-method/) covers the standard process.
- **ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number):** a nine-digit number the IRS issues to an *individual* who must file or appear on a U.S. tax return but isn't eligible for a Social Security Number. ITINs begin with the number 9.

An ITIN for an LLC owner is a personal tax ID. It is never a substitute for the LLC's EIN, and the EIN is never a substitute for your ITIN. The LLC gets the EIN. You, as the individual, get the ITIN if you need one.

According to the [IRS ITIN page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/individual-taxpayer-identification-number), ITINs are issued for federal tax reporting only. They do not authorize you to work in the U.S. and do not make you eligible for Social Security benefits.

## Do You Actually Need an ITIN as a Foreign LLC Owner?

Not always. Many non-resident owners spend weeks chasing an ITIN they never needed.

### When you probably do NOT need an ITIN

Consider a single-member LLC owned by a non-resident alien, with no U.S. trade or business, no U.S. office or employees, and income earned entirely from clients abroad. In that case the LLC is a disregarded entity. It generally files **Form 5472** with a pro forma Form 1120, and the owner often has no U.S. income tax return requirement. The LLC's EIN is enough for this filing. See our guide to [Form 5472 for a foreign-owned single-member LLC](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/) for the details.

### When you probably DO need an ITIN

An ITIN typically becomes necessary when you, as the individual, must file a U.S. tax return or be named on one. Common triggers include:

1. **Effectively connected income (ECI):** the LLC is engaged in a U.S. trade or business (for example, U.S. clients, U.S. employees, or a U.S. office), and you must file Form 1040-NR.
2. **Claiming a tax treaty benefit** to reduce or eliminate U.S. withholding on certain income.
3. **Receiving U.S.-source passive income** where a withholding agent needs your tax ID.
4. **Selling or owning U.S. real property**, which can trigger separate filing and withholding rules.
5. **Partners in a multi-member LLC,** because partnership reporting (Schedule K-1) requires each partner's tax ID, and non-resident partners may face withholding on their share of ECI.

The rules here are technical and fact-specific. In my experience, the most expensive mistake is guessing. I've seen owners skip an ITIN because "the LLC has no U.S. office," then discover their U.S. contractors and warehouse created a trade or business. A one-hour consultation with a cross-border tax professional is cheap insurance in 2026.

## How to Get an ITIN as a Foreign LLC Owner: Step by Step

### Step 1: Confirm your filing requirement

Work out *why* you need the ITIN. Is it a Form 1040-NR to report income from the LLC? Is it a treaty claim? The reason determines your W-7 box and whether you must attach a return.

### Step 2: Complete Form W-7

[Form W-7](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-7) asks for your name, foreign address, country of citizenship, foreign tax ID (if any), and the document details. Use the current revision from the IRS website, since outdated forms are a common reason for rejection.

### Step 3: Prepare your tax return (if required)

For most LLC owners, this means a Form 1040-NR reporting income from the LLC. The return and the W-7 go together as one package. Because you don't yet have an ITIN, you leave the ID field blank on the return and write "ITIN applied for" as the IRS instructs.

Note the nuance: you cannot just file the W-7 alone because you "might need" an ITIN later. Unless an exception applies, the IRS wants a real return supporting the request.

### Step 4: Gather identity documents

The passport is the strongest option. If you don't use a passport, you generally need at least two other documents from the IRS's accepted list, such as a national ID card, a driver's license, or a foreign voter registration card. At least one of them must include a photo.

Copies that are not certified by the issuing agency are rejected. Don't send an ordinary photocopy of anything.

### Step 5: Choose how to submit

You have three main routes:

| Submission route | Best for | Key advantage | Key drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mail to the IRS ITIN Operation (Austin, TX)** | Owners who can obtain certified copies | No agent fees | Your original documents leave your hands for weeks |
| **Certified Acceptance Agent (CAA)** | Most foreign LLC owners | CAA verifies your documents and sends only copies, so you keep your passport | CAA fees, often roughly $100–$400 or more |
| **IRS Taxpayer Assistance Center (by appointment)** | Owners physically in the U.S. | In-person verification | Limited availability, appointment required |

The IRS does not charge a fee for the ITIN. The only costs are agent fees, courier costs, document certification, and any tax preparer fees. Many overseas owners choose a CAA because they can't mail their only passport internationally and wait.

### Step 6: Submit and track

Mail the package (W-7, return if required, and documents) or submit through your CAA. Keep copies of everything.

### Step 7: Wait for your CAA letter or notice

The IRS typically responds with a CP565 notice containing your ITIN. The IRS says processing generally takes about seven weeks, and longer during peak periods or for applications submitted from outside the U.S. Plan for two to three months in 2026 to be safe.

## Documents and Details That Trip People Up

These are the issues I see most often:

- **Name mismatches.** Your name on the W-7 must match your passport exactly, including transliterations.
- **Expired documents.** Expired passports are not accepted.
- **Missing foreign tax ID.** If your country issues one, include it.
- **Wrong reason box.** Selecting the wrong category often leads to rejection, so match it to your real situation.
- **Failing to attach the return.** Unless you qualify for an exception, an application without a return will be returned.
- **Currency or income errors on the 1040-NR.** An incorrect return can delay the whole package.

## ITIN Expiration and Renewal

An ITIN doesn't last forever. Per the IRS, an ITIN that hasn't been used on a federal tax return at least once in the last three consecutive tax years will expire. If it expires and you need it again, you renew using Form W-7 and follow the renewal instructions.

If you only needed the ITIN once and don't anticipate more filings, expiration is not a problem. If you keep a U.S. LLC with ongoing ECI, make sure your ITIN stays active by filing each year.

## Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Applying for an ITIN you don't need.** It costs time and agent fees for nothing.
2. **Not applying when you do need one.** Late filings can lead to penalties and interest.
3. **Mailing your only passport.** If you can use a CAA instead, do so.
4. **Treating the EIN as a personal tax ID.** It identifies the LLC, not you.
5. **Ignoring treaty rules.** A treaty may reduce withholding, but you generally need a valid tax ID to claim it.
6. **DIY filing on complex facts.** If you have U.S. clients, employees, or real estate, get professional advice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need an ITIN to form an LLC as a non-U.S. resident?

No. You can form a U.S. LLC without an ITIN or SSN. The state filing doesn't ask for either. An ITIN may become relevant later for tax filing.

### Can a foreign LLC owner get an EIN without an ITIN?

Yes. A non-resident can apply for an EIN using Form SS-4 by mail, fax, or phone, entering "foreign" or "N/A" in the SSN/ITIN field as the IRS directs. The online EIN application generally requires a responsible party with an SSN or ITIN, so foreign owners often use the paper route.

### How long does it take to get an ITIN as a foreign LLC owner?

The IRS generally says about seven weeks, but applications submitted from abroad or during peak season often take longer. Budget two to three months.

### How much does an ITIN cost?

The IRS charges no fee. You may pay a Certified Acceptance Agent, a tax preparer, and certified-copy or courier costs, which can add up to a few hundred dollars or more depending on provider.

### Can I apply for an ITIN without filing a tax return?

Only if you meet one of the specific exceptions listed in the Form W-7 instructions, such as certain treaty benefit claims or third-party withholding situations. Otherwise, a return is generally required.

### Can I use my passport alone for an ITIN application?

Yes. A valid passport is the only single document that proves both identity and foreign status, provided it's the original or a copy certified by the issuing agency.

### What happens if my ITIN expires?

If an ITIN goes unused on a federal return for three consecutive years, it expires. You can renew it with Form W-7 if you need it again.

### Does having an ITIN make me a U.S. tax resident?

No. An ITIN is just an identification number. Your tax residency depends on separate tests, such as the green card test and the substantial presence test.
