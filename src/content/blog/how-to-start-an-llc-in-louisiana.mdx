Louisiana is one of the more distinctive states to form a business in — it's the only state in the country that runs on a civil law system rather than English common law, which means some of the LLC rules here don't look quite like what you'd find in Texas or Georgia. If you're figuring out how to start an LLC in Louisiana in 2026, the good news is that the process itself is still straightforward: you're looking at a $100 state filing fee, a same-day-to-one-week turnaround, and a handful of forms filed through the state's geauxBIZ portal.

You can absolutely file everything yourself directly with the Louisiana Secretary of State. This guide walks through every step of starting an LLC in Louisiana, what it actually costs once you strip away the marketing, and where a paid formation service earns its fee versus where you're better off doing it yourself.

## Step-by-Step: How to Start an LLC in Louisiana

### Step 1: Choose and Reserve Your LLC Name

Your name has to include "Limited Liability Company," "L.L.C.," or "LLC," and it needs to be distinguishable from every other business entity already registered with the Louisiana Secretary of State. Search the geauxBIZ database before you fall in love with a name — Louisiana's business registry is more crowded than people expect, especially around common industry terms like "Bayou," "Crescent," or "Cajun."

If you're not ready to file immediately, you can reserve a name for 120 days for $25. It's optional, but if you're waiting on a partner's signature or financing to close before you form, it's cheap insurance against someone else grabbing your name in the meantime.

### Step 2: Appoint a Louisiana Registered Agent

Every Louisiana LLC needs a registered agent — a person or company with a physical Louisiana street address (no P.O. boxes) who's available during normal business hours to accept legal documents and state correspondence on your LLC's behalf. If you're new to the concept, our guide on [what a registered agent is](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) covers the role in more depth.

You can serve as your own registered agent if you have a Louisiana address and don't mind your name and address becoming part of the public record. Most owners I've worked with skip this once they realize what it actually means in practice: if your LLC gets sued, the process server shows up at that address — potentially in front of clients, employees, or family. A commercial registered agent service keeps that off your personal address and typically runs $0–$125 a year, often bundled free for the first year with a formation package.

### Step 3: File the Articles of Organization — and Don't Skip the Initial Report

This is where Louisiana diverges from most states. When you file your Articles of Organization, Louisiana also requires an **Initial Report** — a document listing your registered agent, registered office address, and (if manager-managed) your initial managers — filed at the same time as the Articles. Miss this and your filing gets rejected, which is the single most common reason I see Louisiana LLC formations bounce back from the Secretary of State's office.

In my experience, this is the one step where DIY filers trip up most often, because every other state guide they've read only talks about "Articles of Organization" and never mentions a second document. File both online through geauxBIZ, and the $100 state fee covers the complete package. Expedited processing is available if you need your LLC formed faster — 24-hour and same-day options exist for an additional fee, though you should confirm current pricing on geauxBIZ since expedite fees are adjusted periodically.

### Step 4: Get Your EIN From the IRS

Once your LLC is approved, get a free Employer Identification Number directly from the IRS — you need this to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file taxes. Skip any service that charges you for this step; it's a five-minute process on IRS.gov. We've written a full walkthrough on [how to get an EIN for free using the IRS's own method](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-for-your-llc-free-irs-method/) if you want to do it yourself.

### Step 5: Draft an Operating Agreement

Louisiana doesn't legally require an operating agreement, but given the state's civil law foundations under Louisiana Revised Statutes Title 12, having clear, written internal rules matters more here than in common-law states where courts are used to filling gaps with judge-made precedent. Without one, Louisiana's default statutory rules govern things like profit splits, voting rights, and what happens if a member leaves — and those defaults rarely match what a small business actually wants. Our guide on [LLC operating agreements](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) breaks down what to include, whether you're single-member or have partners.

Most formation services include a basic operating agreement template in their paid packages; a lawyer-drafted version for a multi-member LLC with unequal ownership or specific buyout terms runs $300–$800 if you have one custom-drafted.

### Step 6: Register for State Taxes and Local Licenses

Depending on what you sell, you may need to register with the Louisiana Department of Revenue through the Louisiana Taxpayer Access Point (LaTAP) for sales tax collection. Louisiana also has no single statewide general business license — instead, most parishes and municipalities (New Orleans in particular) require a local occupational license. Check with your parish clerk of court or city office before you open your doors; this is easy to overlook because it isn't part of the Secretary of State's process at all.

### Step 7: File Your BOI Report (If It Applies to You)

Under the federal Corporate Transparency Act, most new LLCs were originally required to file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN. That changed in 2025: FinCEN issued an interim rule narrowing the requirement so that most U.S.-formed companies with U.S. beneficial owners are no longer required to file, with the obligation now focused primarily on foreign-formed entities registered to do business in the U.S. Rules in this area have moved quickly, so check FinCEN's official BOI page directly and read our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) before assuming you're exempt — your specific ownership structure matters.

### Step 8: Open a Business Bank Account

Once you have your approved Articles, Initial Report, and EIN in hand, open a dedicated business checking account. This is non-negotiable if you want the liability protection an LLC offers to actually hold up — commingling personal and business funds is one of the fastest ways to give a plaintiff's attorney an argument for "piercing the corporate veil" and coming after your personal assets anyway.

## Louisiana LLC Taxes and Ongoing Compliance

Forming the LLC is the easy part. Keeping it in good standing is where people fall down.

**Annual Report.** Louisiana requires every LLC to file an Annual Report each year, due on the anniversary of your formation date — not a fixed calendar date like some states use for corporations. The fee is $30. Miss it, and Louisiana will eventually administratively dissolve your LLC, at which point you lose your liability protection and have to pay reinstatement fees to get back in good standing.

**State income tax.** Louisiana taxes LLC profits as pass-through income by default, meaning profits flow through to your personal return. Louisiana's tax reform package, enacted under Governor Jeff Landry's administration, moved the state to a flat individual income tax rate of 3% starting with the 2025 tax year — a meaningful simplification from the state's old graduated bracket system, and one that continues to apply through 2026. The Tax Foundation has tracked these changes in detail if you want the underlying analysis.

**Franchise tax.** Louisiana's corporate franchise tax historically applied to corporations and LLCs that elected corporate tax treatment, not to standard pass-through LLCs. Recent state tax reform has continued to phase down and restructure this tax — the specifics depend on your entity's tax election and are exactly the kind of thing that changes from one legislative session to the next, so this is a genuine "talk to a CPA before you file" situation rather than something to take from a blog post.

**Sales tax.** If you sell taxable goods or certain services, you'll need to register through LaTAP and collect Louisiana state and parish-level sales tax, which varies by location and can be among the highest combined local rates in the country.

None of the above is tax advice — Louisiana's tax code has moved fast over the past two years, and your specific liability depends on your entity structure, revenue, and elections. Talk to a CPA licensed in Louisiana before you file your first return.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Forming a Louisiana LLC

**Forgetting the Initial Report.** As covered in Step 3, this trips up more DIY filers in Louisiana than in almost any other state, simply because it's not part of the standard "Articles of Organization" template most people expect.

**Treating the operating agreement as optional busywork.** I've seen too many multi-member Louisiana LLCs skip this, hit a disagreement two years in, and discover that Louisiana's default statutory rules split things in a way nobody in the room actually wanted.

**Using a home address as the registered agent address.** It's free, but it also means your home address is searchable by anyone who looks up your LLC — including in a lawsuit.

**Missing the Annual Report deadline.** Because it's tied to your formation anniversary rather than a fixed date like January 1 or April 15, it's easy to lose track of — set a calendar reminder the day your LLC is approved.

**Assuming BOI reporting rules from last year still apply.** Given how much FinCEN's requirements shifted in 2025, don't rely on outdated advice (including anything written before 2025) about whether you need to file.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Louisiana?**
The mandatory state filing fee is $100 for the Articles of Organization and Initial Report combined. Add optional costs like a $25 name reservation, a registered agent service ($0–$125/year), and any formation service fees, and most people spend between $100 and $300 total to get a Louisiana LLC up and running in 2026.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in Louisiana?**
Online filings through geauxBIZ are typically processed in 3–5 business days. Mailed filings can take 2–3 weeks. Expedited 24-hour and same-day options are available for an additional fee if you need it faster.

**Do I need a registered agent to start an LLC in Louisiana?**
Yes. Louisiana law requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical Louisiana street address. You can serve as your own agent for free, or pay a commercial service to keep your personal address off public record.

**Is an operating agreement required for a Louisiana LLC?**
No, Louisiana doesn't legally require one. It's still strongly recommended, particularly for multi-member LLCs, since without one you're governed entirely by Louisiana's default statutory rules rather than terms you actually chose.

**What is the Louisiana Annual Report, and how much does it cost?**
It's a mandatory yearly filing that keeps your LLC's information current with the Secretary of State. It's due on your LLC's formation anniversary each year and costs $30. Missing it can eventually lead to administrative dissolution.

**Does Louisiana have a franchise tax for LLCs?**
Historically, Louisiana's franchise tax applied mainly to corporations and LLCs electing corporate tax treatment, not standard pass-through LLCs. The state's tax code has been actively restructured in recent reform packages, so confirm your specific entity's treatment with a Louisiana CPA rather than relying on last year's rules.

**Do I still need to file a BOI report for my Louisiana LLC?**
It depends. FinCEN significantly narrowed BOI reporting requirements in 2025, largely exempting U.S.-formed companies with U.S. beneficial owners. Check FinCEN's official guidance directly, since your specific ownership structure determines whether an exemption applies to you.

**Can I start a Louisiana LLC on my own without a formation service?**
Yes — everything can be filed directly through geauxBIZ for the $100 state fee with no service markup. A formation service like ZenBusiness or LegalZoom mainly saves you time and reduces the risk of a rejected filing (especially around the Initial Report requirement), rather than unlocking anything you couldn't technically do yourself.
