## The Ohio Articles of Organization Filing Fee

The single most important number in any Ohio LLC cost and fees breakdown is the state filing fee. To legally form an LLC in Ohio, you must file **Articles of Organization** with the Ohio Secretary of State and pay a **$99 filing fee**.

This fee applies whether you file:
- **Online** through the Ohio Business Central portal (fastest — typically 1–3 business days)
- **By mail** to the Ohio Secretary of State's office (allow 5–10 business days)
- **In person** at the Secretary of State's office in Columbus

Ohio also offers **expedited processing** for an additional charge:
- **2-business-day processing:** $100 extra
- **Same-day processing:** $200 extra (must be submitted by 1:00 PM ET)

For most business owners, standard online filing is perfectly adequate and keeps your total state cost at exactly $99. Unless you have a contract deadline or a partnership agreement that needs an entity to be in place immediately, skip the expedite fee.

It's worth noting that Ohio's $99 fee sits in the middle of the national range. Delaware charges $90 but layers on a $300 annual franchise tax. California charges $70 to file but adds an $800 annual minimum franchise tax. Wyoming charges only $100 with minimal ongoing fees. ## Does Ohio Require Annual Reports for LLCs?

Here's the piece of the Ohio LLC cost and fees breakdown that surprises most new business owners: **Ohio does not require LLCs to file annual reports.** This is a significant financial and administrative advantage over states like California, Delaware, and Florida, which charge anywhere from $50 to $800+ per year just to keep your LLC in good standing.

In Ohio, once your LLC is formed and your Articles of Organization are accepted, there is **no recurring state filing fee** tied to LLC status alone.

That said, you're not entirely off the hook for ongoing state-level obligations:

**Ohio Commercial Activity Tax (CAT):** Businesses with Ohio-sourced gross receipts above certain thresholds may owe the Commercial Activity Tax. Starting in 2024, Ohio began phasing out the CAT for smaller businesses — receipts under $3 million were exempt beginning tax year 2024, and the threshold is expanding. However, if your business grows, you'll want to monitor this. See the [Ohio Department of Taxation](https://tax.ohio.gov/) for current thresholds, as they're subject to legislative updates.

**Industry-Specific Licenses:** Depending on your business type (contractors, food service, healthcare, childcare), Ohio may require state-level licensing renewals that carry their own annual fees.

**Local Business Licenses:** Some Ohio municipalities — Columbus, Cleveland, Cincinnati, Akron — require city business licenses or regional filings. Fees range from $25 to $250+ per year.

The absence of an LLC-specific annual report is one of the genuinely underrated features of operating in Ohio, and it's a recurring cost that savvy business owners factor into their long-term state selection decisions. ## Ohio Registered Agent Requirements and Costs

Every Ohio LLC is legally required to maintain a **registered agent** — a designated person or business entity with a physical Ohio street address (no P.O. boxes) available during normal business hours to receive official legal documents on your LLC's behalf.

Your options for meeting this requirement:

**Option 1: Serve as Your Own Registered Agent ($0)**
You can designate yourself or any LLC member as the registered agent, provided you have an Ohio street address. This costs nothing but comes with real drawbacks: your name and address become part of the public record, you must be physically available during business hours, and any service of process (including lawsuits) arrives at that address.

## Ohio LLC Operating Agreement and EIN Costs

Two items often missing from basic Ohio LLC cost and fees breakdowns:

**Operating Agreement**
Ohio does not legally require an LLC to have an operating agreement, but every serious business attorney (and every experienced CFO) will tell you to have one. It governs member rights, profit distributions, management structure, and what happens when a member wants to leave. Without one, Ohio's default LLC statutes fill in the gaps — and those defaults may not reflect your intentions.

Cost:
- DIY template (Bizee, ZenBusiness, Northwest): $0–$99 (often included in paid tiers)
- Attorney-drafted: $300–$1,500+ depending on complexity

**Federal EIN (Employer Identification Number)**
Your LLC will need an EIN from the IRS for tax filings, opening a business bank account, and hiring employees. The IRS issues EINs for free at [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). Formation services often charge $50–$70 for EIN procurement — that's a fee you can always skip by doing it yourself in about 10 minutes online.

## Hidden Costs in the Ohio LLC Formation Process

In my experience reviewing LLC formation services and talking with first-time business owners, there are a handful of costs that tend to catch people off guard:

**Name Reservation Fee ($39)**
Ohio allows you to reserve a business name for 180 days before filing, at a cost of $39. This is optional — if you're ready to file immediately, skip it.

**Certified Copies of Formation Documents ($5 per page)**
Banks, lenders, and some government agencies may request certified copies of your Articles of Organization. Ohio charges $5 per page for certified copies. Budget $15–$25 for a complete certified packet.

**Business Bank Account Fees**
Ohio doesn't charge for this, but your bank might. Many national banks charge $15–$30/month for business checking accounts. Mercury, Relay, and several online-first banks offer free business checking — worth considering when you're keeping startup costs low.

**Foreign Qualification Fees**
If your Ohio LLC wants to operate in another state (say, you're based in Columbus but signing contracts in Kentucky), you'll need to foreign qualify in that state. Fees vary from $50 (Kentucky) to $200+ (some Northeast states).

**BOI Report (FinCEN)**
As of 2026, most newly formed Ohio LLCs are required to file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN within 30 days of formation. There is currently no filing fee, but penalties for non-compliance are steep. See our [BOI Report Guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for full details.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Ohio LLC Costs

**How much does it cost to form an LLC in Ohio in 2026?**
The minimum cost to form an Ohio LLC in 2026 is **$99** — the Ohio Secretary of State's Articles of Organization filing fee. If you use a formation service and hire a registered agent, expect total year-one costs between $138 and $350 depending on the provider and package you choose.

**Does Ohio have an annual LLC fee or franchise tax?**
Ohio does not require LLCs to file annual reports or pay an annual LLC franchise tax. LLCs in Ohio may be subject to the Commercial Activity Tax (CAT) if gross receipts exceed applicable thresholds, but there is no blanket annual fee for simply maintaining an LLC in the state.

**Can I be my own registered agent in Ohio?**
Yes. Any Ohio LLC member or manager with an Ohio street address can serve as the registered agent at no cost. However, your address becomes part of the public record, and you must be available to receive legal documents during normal business hours.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in Ohio?**
Standard online filing through Ohio Business Central typically processes in **1–3 business days**. Mail filings take 5–10 business days. Same-day processing is available for an additional $200 fee (submit by 1:00 PM ET).

**Do I need an operating agreement for my Ohio LLC?**
Ohio does not legally require an operating agreement, but it is strongly recommended — especially for multi-member LLCs. Without one, Ohio's default LLC statutes govern your business relationships, which may not reflect your intentions. See our [LLC Operating Agreement Guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) for more.

**What is the Ohio LLC filing fee for a name reservation?**
Ohio charges a **$39 fee** to reserve an LLC name for 180 days before filing. Name reservation is optional; if you're ready to file immediately, you can skip this step.

**Does Ohio require a BOI report for new LLCs in 2026?**
Yes. Under FinCEN regulations effective in 2024 and continuing into 2026, most newly formed Ohio LLCs must file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report within 30 days of formation. There is no fee to file, but late filing carries daily penalties. See our detailed [BOI Report Guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for step-by-step instructions.

**What happens if I don't maintain a registered agent in Ohio?**
If your Ohio LLC fails to maintain a registered agent, the Secretary of State can cancel your LLC's active status, making it unable to legally conduct business. Reinstating a cancelled LLC in Ohio involves additional filing fees and paperwork.
