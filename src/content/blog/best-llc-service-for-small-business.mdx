## What Makes an LLC Service "Best" for a Small Business?

Before comparing brands, it helps to know what you're actually grading. After forming entities across multiple states, I've found five factors separate the good services from the mediocre ones:

1. **True starting cost** — not just the advertised "$0" plan, but what you're charged by month three once registered agent renewal and add-ons kick in.
2. **Speed of filing** — some states process in 24-48 hours with the right provider; others take 2-3 weeks with a slower one, even for the same state.
3. **Registered agent service** — required in all 50 states, and the difference between a free first year and a $299/year add-on adds up fast. See our guide on [what a registered agent actually does](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) if you're unfamiliar with this requirement.
4. **Compliance support** — annual report reminders, BOI reporting help, and operating agreement templates.
5. **Upsell aggressiveness** — this is the one review sites gloss over. Some checkout flows push $50-100 in add-ons before you can complete an order.

## Hidden Costs and Gotchas to Watch For

This is the section I wish more comparison articles included, because the advertised price is rarely the price you pay. A few things worth knowing before you file:

- **Registered agent renewal.** Nearly every "$0" plan bundles a free first year of registered agent service, then bills $100-300 annually starting year two. Read the renewal terms carefully — this is the single biggest source of surprise charges in this industry.
- **Expedited filing fees.** Standard processing with most providers runs 1-3 weeks depending on the state. Rush filing (24-48 hours) is typically a $50-100 add-on across ZenBusiness, LegalZoom, and Tailor Brands alike.
- **EIN filing charges.** Getting an Employer Identification Number directly from the IRS is completely free and takes about 10 minutes on [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). Several formation services charge $50-70 to do this exact same free step for you — convenient if you'd rather not deal with it, but know you're paying for convenience, not a service that isn't otherwise available.
- **BOI reporting confusion.** Since the Corporate Transparency Act's beneficial ownership reporting requirements took effect, several formation companies now sell "BOI filing assistance" as a paid add-on. Requirements and enforcement have shifted multiple times since 2024, so check our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) for the current state of things before paying extra for something you may be able to file yourself in minutes.
- **Operating agreement templates.** A generic template is often included free; a customized one (particularly important for multi-member LLCs) can run an extra $50-100.

None of this makes these services bad deals — it just means the true annual cost of the best LLC service for small business use is almost always higher than the homepage number, and you should budget accordingly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best LLC service for small business owners in 2026?**
Based on pricing, compliance support, and filing speed, [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) is our top recommendation for most small business owners in 2026, with [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) as a strong alternative if you want bundled attorney access.

**How much does it cost to form an LLC?**
Service fees range from $0 to $500+ depending on the provider and plan, plus a mandatory state filing fee that ranges from $35 to $500 depending on the state. See our full [LLC cost breakdown](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/) for state-by-state numbers.

**Do I need a registered agent for my LLC?**
Yes — all 50 states require an LLC to maintain a registered agent with a physical address in the state of formation. You can serve as your own agent in most states, but many owners use a paid service (see our [registered agent explainer](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/)) for privacy and reliability reasons.

**Is it worth paying for an LLC formation service, or should I file myself?**
Filing directly with your state's Secretary of State is usually the cheapest route since you only pay the state fee, but formation services save time, reduce paperwork errors, and typically bundle registered agent service and compliance reminders — valuable if this is your first time forming an entity.

**What happens if I don't file my LLC's annual report?**
Depending on the state, missing an annual report deadline can lead to late fees, loss of good standing, and eventually administrative dissolution of your LLC — meaning you'd lose liability protection and have to reinstate or re-form the entity.

**Can I get an EIN for free?**
Yes. An EIN is always free directly through the [IRS website](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) and takes about 10 minutes for U.S. applicants. Formation services that charge for this are charging for convenience, not a service unavailable elsewhere.

**Should I form my LLC in Delaware or Wyoming instead of my home state?**
For most small businesses operating primarily in one state, forming in your home state is simpler and cheaper — forming out-of-state usually means registering as a "foreign LLC" back in your home state anyway, doubling your fees. Delaware or Wyoming formation typically makes sense only for businesses planning outside investment or specific tax strategies, and that decision should be made with a tax professional.

**What's the difference between an LLC and a sole proprietorship?**
The core difference is liability protection — an LLC legally separates your personal assets from business debts and lawsuits, while a sole proprietorship offers no such separation. See our full [LLC vs. sole proprietorship guide](/learn/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship/) for the complete comparison.
