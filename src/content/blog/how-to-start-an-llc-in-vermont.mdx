## Step-by-Step: How to Start an LLC in Vermont

### Step 1: Choose Your LLC Name

Your Vermont LLC name must include one of the following designators: "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." It cannot be the same as, or deceptively similar to, any existing registered entity in Vermont.

Search the Vermont Secretary of State's business name database at sos.vermont.gov to check availability before you file. The search is free.

A few additional naming rules:
- The name cannot imply government affiliation (e.g., "Vermont State Services LLC")
- Certain terms — Bank, Insurance, Attorney, University — require approval from the relevant regulatory body
- You can operate under a different brand name using a "doing business as" (DBA) trade name, which requires a separate filing

If you find a name you want but aren't ready to file yet, Vermont allows you to reserve it for 120 days for a $20 fee — useful if you're waiting on financing or a co-founder agreement.

### Step 2: Appoint a Vermont Registered Agent

Vermont law requires every LLC to maintain a registered agent with a **physical street address in Vermont** — no P.O. boxes accepted. The agent must be available during normal business hours to receive official legal documents, tax notices, and state correspondence on behalf of your LLC.

Your three options:

1. **Act as your own registered agent** — only viable if you have a stable Vermont street address and are reliably available 9–5 on business days. Home-based owners and frequent travelers should think twice.
2. **Designate an employee or business associate** — same availability constraints apply; the person must provide written consent.
3. **Use a professional registered agent service** — costs $49–$299/year; the service maintains a Vermont address and forwards documents promptly.

### Step 3: File Vermont Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization is the document that legally creates your LLC. You file it with the Vermont Secretary of State, Corporations Division.

**What you'll include:**
- LLC name and principal office address
- Registered agent name and Vermont street address
- Management structure (member-managed or manager-managed)
- Name and address of each organizer
- Optional effective date (can be set to a future date)

**Filing options:**
- **Online at bizfilings.vermont.gov:** $125 filing fee; standard processing is 3–5 business days; expedited is $175 total ($125 + $50) with 1–2 business day turnaround
- **By mail to Vermont Secretary of State, 128 State Street, Montpelier, VT 05633-1104:** $125; processing typically takes 7–10 business days

Online filing is the clear choice for most applicants — faster, trackable, and less prone to address-legibility issues that can delay mailed submissions.

### Step 4: Draft an Operating Agreement

Vermont doesn't legally require an LLC operating agreement — but every LLC should have one. This internal document governs how your business actually runs:

- Ownership percentages and capital contributions
- Voting rights and decision-making thresholds
- How profits and losses are allocated and distributed
- What happens when a member wants to leave, transfer their interest, or dies
- Whether the LLC is member-managed or manager-managed

Without an operating agreement, Vermont's default LLC statutes fill the gaps. Those defaults may not reflect your intentions. For example, under default rules, profits are typically split equally among members regardless of how much each contributed — a serious problem in unequal-contribution partnerships.

Banks and investors will also routinely ask for your operating agreement before opening accounts or closing deals. Our [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) walks through every section you need to cover.

### Step 5: Obtain a Federal EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your LLC's federal tax ID, issued free by the IRS. You need one to:

- Open a business bank account (virtually all banks require it)
- Hire employees and process payroll
- Pay federal taxes and file business tax returns
- Apply for certain business licenses

Apply directly through [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) — the process takes under 15 minutes and your EIN is issued immediately upon completion. There's no legitimate reason to pay a third party to obtain one for you.

Single-member LLCs with no employees can technically use the owner's Social Security Number for some purposes, but using a separate EIN is better practice. It reinforces the separation between personal and business finances that's central to maintaining liability protection.

### Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account

A separate business bank account isn't legally required in Vermont, but it's functionally mandatory for maintaining your LLC's liability protection. Courts look at commingling of personal and business funds — paying personal bills from the LLC account, depositing business receipts into your personal account — as evidence that the LLC isn't a genuine separate entity. That's how owners end up personally liable for business debts despite having an LLC.

Vermont has a robust credit union ecosystem that often offers more favorable fee structures for small business accounts than national banks: Lake Champlain Credit Union, Members Advantage Community Credit Union, and New England Federal Credit Union are worth comparing. Most require your Articles of Organization certificate and EIN to open the account.

### Step 7: File Your BOI Report

This is the step most Vermont LLC founders overlooked when it was first introduced — and it carries the steepest penalties for non-compliance. Under the Corporate Transparency Act, most LLCs formed in 2026 must file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) within **30 days of formation**. The filing is free and done directly through FinCEN's online portal.

The report identifies who owns or controls the LLC: any person with 25% or more ownership, or anyone who exercises substantial control over the entity. Late or non-filing penalties run up to $591 per day.

## Vermont LLC Taxes and Ongoing Compliance

### State Tax Treatment

Vermont LLCs are pass-through entities by default — the LLC pays no Vermont state income tax at the entity level. Profits and losses flow through to members' personal returns, where they're taxed at Vermont's individual income tax rates. In 2026, those rates range from **3.35% to 8.75%** depending on taxable income, per the Vermont Department of Taxes.

Vermont also levies a 6% state sales tax on the sale of tangible personal property and certain services. If your LLC sells taxable goods or covered services, you'll need to register with the Vermont Department of Taxes and file periodic sales tax returns.

Multi-member LLCs taxed as partnerships must file a Vermont partnership return (Form BI-476). Single-member LLCs report business income on Schedule C of their individual return.

If your LLC grows to a point where S-Corp election makes sense for tax optimization — typically around $50,000–$60,000 in net profit — that's a separate conversation with a CPA. Our guide on [LLC vs S-Corp tax treatment](/learn/llc-vs-s-corp/) explains when the election is worth considering.

### Annual Report

Vermont LLCs must file an annual report each year to remain in good standing. As of 2026:

- **Filing fee:** $35
- **Due date:** Verify the current deadline at sos.vermont.gov; Vermont has adjusted its annual reporting schedule in recent years
- **Filing method:** Online through the Secretary of State portal
- **Consequence of non-filing:** Administrative dissolution — your LLC loses legal standing and you'll need to pay reinstatement fees to recover it

For reference, Delaware's annual franchise tax is $300 — more than eight times Vermont's $35 fee. Vermont's ongoing maintenance cost is genuinely reasonable.

I've seen too many business owners let annual report deadlines slip because they assumed someone else was handling it, or simply forgot the anniversary date. If you use a formation service, they'll typically send reminders. If you go DIY, put a recurring calendar event in now.

## People Also Ask: Vermont LLC FAQs

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Vermont?**

The state filing fee for Vermont Articles of Organization is $125 (online). Adding registered agent service ($49–$299/year) and the $35 annual report, your realistic first-year cost is $160–$460, depending on whether you use a professional service. There is no state franchise tax on Vermont LLCs and no publication requirement.

**How long does it take to form an LLC in Vermont?**

Standard online processing takes 3–5 business days. Vermont offers expedited processing for an additional $50, reducing turnaround to 1–2 business days. Mail-in filings typically take 7–10 business days.

**Do I need an operating agreement for a Vermont LLC?**

Vermont law does not require an operating agreement, but you should have one. Without it, your LLC operates under Vermont's default statutory rules — which may not reflect your intended profit distribution, voting structure, or exit terms. Banks, investors, and future buyers will often request it.

**Does Vermont require LLC owners to publish a formation notice?**

No. Vermont has no newspaper publication requirement for LLCs. This is a meaningful advantage over states like New York, where publication requirements can add $1,000–$2,000+ to first-year formation costs.

**What taxes does a Vermont LLC pay?**

Vermont LLCs are pass-through entities by default. Members pay Vermont individual income tax on their share of profits at rates ranging from 3.35% to 8.75% in 2026. The LLC itself does not pay state income tax at the entity level. Vermont's 6% sales tax applies to taxable goods and certain services.

**Can a non-Vermont resident form a Vermont LLC?**

Yes. Vermont has no residency requirement for LLC formation. Non-residents must still appoint a registered agent with a physical Vermont street address, which professional registered agent services handle for $49–$299/year.

**What is the Vermont LLC annual report requirement?**

Vermont LLCs must file an annual report with the Secretary of State each year to maintain good standing. The current fee is $35. Failure to file can result in administrative dissolution. Check sos.vermont.gov for the current due date.

**Do I need to file a BOI report for my Vermont LLC?**

Almost certainly yes, if your LLC was formed in 2026. Under the federal Corporate Transparency Act, most new LLCs must file a Beneficial Ownership Information report with FinCEN within 30 days of formation. The filing is free and completed through FinCEN's online portal. Penalties for non-compliance can reach $591 per day. See the [FinCEN BOI reporting portal](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) for official guidance.
