Mexican freelancers, consultants, e-commerce sellers and online entrepreneurs increasingly use a US limited liability company to invoice American clients, accept payments through Stripe and PayPal, and open a US bank account. A US LLC for Mexican residents is legal and common, and you don't need to live in the United States to own one. It does come with a US filing calendar and a separate Mexican tax picture that many people only discover after they've formed the company.

Formation is the easy part. Services like [ZenBusiness](/go/zenbusiness/) start at $0 plus state fees for a basic LLC filing, and [LegalZoom](/go/legalzoom/) offers a similar entry tier. The real work is what comes after: getting an EIN without a Social Security number, filing the annual IRS paperwork the US requires of foreign-owned LLCs, and reporting the income correctly to Mexico's SAT. This guide covers all three, using 2026 rules and typical costs.

## Can a Mexican Resident Own a US LLC?

Yes. No US state requires an LLC owner to be a citizen, a resident or even to visit the country. As we explain in our guide on [whether a foreigner can own an LLC in the US](/blog/can-a-foreigner-own-an-llc-in-the-us/), the main requirements are:

- A unique company name that is available in the chosen state
- A registered agent with a physical street address in that state
- Articles of Organization filed with the state and the filing fee paid
- An EIN from the IRS for tax reporting and banking

A Mexican citizen can open an LLC in the USA as the sole member (a single-member LLC) or alongside partners, Mexican or otherwise. If you're new to the structure itself, our [beginner's guide to what an LLC is](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) covers the basics of liability protection and pass-through taxation.

**An important limit:** forming an LLC does not give you a visa, work authorization, residency or any immigration status. It is a business entity only. If you will personally perform work while physically in the US, speak with an immigration attorney first.

## Why Mexican Entrepreneurs Form a US LLC

In my experience, the people who benefit most share a few traits. They sell to US customers, they need US payment processors, or they want a credible US business presence for contracts. Common reasons include:

- **Payment processing:** Stripe and many US marketplaces require a US business entity and EIN for US-based accounts.
- **US banking:** Fintech banks such as Mercury and Relay have historically accepted foreign-owned LLCs, though their policies change. See our guide to a [US bank account for a foreign-owned LLC](/blog/us-bank-account-for-a-foreign-owned-llc/).
- **Client trust:** Some US companies prefer to contract with a US entity and avoid cross-border invoicing.
- **Liability protection:** An LLC separates business liabilities from your personal assets, subject to proper formation and operation.

I've seen too many first-time founders form an LLC because a YouTube video promised "zero taxes." That's the wrong reason. An LLC can change *where* and *how* your income is reported, but it doesn't make Mexican tax residency disappear.

## Step-by-Step: How to Form Your LLC From Mexico

### 1. Pick a formation service and registered agent

You can file directly with the state, but a formation service handles the registered agent, document delivery and compliance reminders. That's useful when you're in a different country and time zone. We rank [ZenBusiness](/reviews/zenbusiness/) first for most non-US founders because its entry tier is inexpensive and its mid-tier plan bundles a registered agent, an operating agreement template and compliance alerts. Pricing changes, so check current plans before you buy: in recent checks, ZenBusiness' paid tiers ran roughly $199 (Pro) and $349 (Premium) per year, plus state fees.

[LegalZoom](/reviews/legalzoom/) is a solid second option with a well-known brand, though its add-ons tend to cost more. Our [ZenBusiness vs LegalZoom comparison](/compare/zenbusiness-vs-legalzoom/) breaks down the differences. If privacy is your top priority, [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) is the strongest choice, and our guide to an [anonymous LLC](/blog/anonymous-llc-how-to-form-one/) explains why.

Note that most formation services do not provide EIN services for applicants without an SSN. Some will not file the EIN application for you, or will tell you to apply directly with the IRS. Ask before you pay.

### 2. File the Articles of Organization

You'll submit the name, registered agent address, and member or manager information (depending on the state). Processing can take from a few days to a few weeks. Use a US address for your registered agent, not your Mexican home address.

### 3. Draft an operating agreement

Even for a single-member LLC, a written [operating agreement](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/) helps establish that the LLC is a separate entity and supports your liability protection. Banks also often request it.

### 4. Get your EIN without an SSN

The EIN is your LLC's federal tax ID. As a foreign owner, you can still get one. Enter "Foreign" or "N/A" where Form SS-4 asks for an SSN or ITIN. You apply by fax or mail, or by phone through the IRS international line, because the online EIN tool generally requires a US taxpayer ID for the responsible party. Processing by fax is usually a matter of days, while mail takes weeks. Our step-by-step guides on [getting an EIN without an SSN](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-without-an-ssn/) cover the exact process. The EIN itself is free from the IRS, so avoid anyone charging heavy fees for it beyond reasonable service fees.

### 5. Open a US bank account and keep finances separate

Many Mexican owners can open a business account remotely with a fintech bank, using the LLC documents, EIN and passport. Keep personal and business funds strictly separate to protect your liability shield.

### 6. Consider an ITIN only if you need one

A foreign owner doesn't automatically need an ITIN. You generally need one if you must file a US individual return (Form 1040-NR) or claim a treaty benefit. See our guide on [getting an ITIN as a foreign LLC owner](/blog/how-to-get-an-itin-as-a-foreign-llc-owner/).

## US Tax and Filing Obligations in 2026

This is where many foreign owners get caught. The rules depend on your LLC's structure.

**Single-member LLC (disregarded entity).** Even though the LLC is "disregarded" for income tax, the IRS treats a foreign-owned US single-member LLC as a corporation for reporting purposes. That means you must file **Form 5472** with a pro forma Form 1120 every year to report reportable transactions between you and the LLC, such as capital contributions, loans and owner draws. The penalty for failing to file is $25,000 per form, per year, with additional penalties for continued non-compliance. See the IRS's [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5472) and our walkthrough of [Form 5472 for a foreign-owned single-member LLC](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/).

**Multi-member LLC.** An LLC with two or more members is taxed as a partnership by default and files Form 1065, with Schedules K-1 issued to each member. Foreign partners may face US withholding if the LLC has US-effectively-connected income.

**US-source income.** If your LLC performs services inside the US, or has a US office or employees, it may have effectively connected income taxable in the US. Services performed entirely from Mexico for US clients are generally treated as foreign-source, but the facts matter. This is a point to confirm with a tax professional.

**BOI reporting.** FinCEN revised its Beneficial Ownership Information rules in 2025 so that US-formed companies are generally exempt from reporting. These rules have changed repeatedly, so confirm the current status in our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) before assuming you have no obligation.

**State filings.** Wyoming, Delaware and Florida each require annual reports or taxes, as shown in the table above. Missing them can lead to administrative dissolution. Our article on [what happens if you don't renew your LLC](/blog/what-happens-if-you-don-t-renew-your-llc/) explains the consequences.

## Mexican Tax Considerations for Your US LLC

Mexican tax residents are generally taxed on worldwide income. That means your US LLC's profits typically need to be reported to Mexico, even if the LLC pays no US federal income tax. A few points to discuss with a Mexican *contador* (accountant):

- **Registration with SAT:** You will typically need an active RFC (tax ID) and may need to declare foreign income, depending on your regime (for example, RESICO or personas físicas con actividad empresarial).
- **Entity classification:** Mexico may treat a US LLC differently than the US does. Depending on how it's classified, the LLC could be seen as a transparent entity, or as a foreign entity whose income falls under rules for controlled foreign companies or preferential tax regimes.
- **Foreign income and accounts:** Mexico requires disclosure of certain foreign income and may require reporting of foreign accounts and investments.
- **Tax treaty:** The US-Mexico income tax treaty may reduce or eliminate double taxation for certain income types, but its application to LLCs is nuanced.

The [SAT website](https://www.sat.gob.mx) publishes current guidance, but cross-border structuring is exactly where a local professional earns their fee. I'd budget for a one-time consultation with a bilingual cross-border accountant before you form the LLC, not after the first tax season. It's cheaper than fixing a misclassified entity later.

## Common Mistakes Mexican LLC Owners Make

1. **Skipping Form 5472.** The $25,000 penalty is real and steep for a small business.
2. **Assuming "no US tax" means "no tax."** Mexico can still tax the income.
3. **Mixing personal and business funds.** This weakens liability protection.
4. **Using a virtual address that isn't a true registered agent.** Official notices get missed.
5. **Ignoring the state annual report.** The LLC can fall out of good standing.
6. **Forming in Delaware for prestige alone.** A $300 annual tax is hard to justify for a small online business.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Mexican citizen open an LLC in the USA?

Yes. A Mexican citizen can form and own a US LLC without being a US resident. The LLC does not provide immigration status or work authorization.

### Does a Mexican resident need an ITIN to form an LLC?

No. You don't need an ITIN or SSN to form an LLC or to get an EIN. You may need an ITIN later if you must file a US individual tax return.

### How much does it cost to form a US LLC as a Mexican resident?

State fees range from $50 (New Mexico) to $125 (Florida). Formation services can add $0 to $349 per year, and ongoing filings and tax preparation add to that. Our [LLC cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-form-an-llc/) has current numbers.

### Do I have to pay US taxes if I live in Mexico?

It depends on your LLC's income. A foreign-owned LLC with no US-source business income often owes no US federal income tax, but it must still file Form 5472 and a pro forma 1120 (single-member) or Form 1065 (multi-member). Consult a tax professional.

### Will I still pay taxes in Mexico on my LLC's income?

Generally yes. Mexican tax residents are taxed on worldwide income, so LLC profits typically need to be reported to SAT. The treaty and Mexico's classification rules can affect how.

### What is the best state for a Mexican resident to form an LLC?

For most, New Mexico or Wyoming offers the lowest cost and simplest upkeep. Delaware suits founders planning to raise outside investment.

### Can I open a US bank account for my LLC from Mexico?

Often yes, with a fintech or online bank that accepts foreign-owned LLCs. Requirements vary, and banks may ask for your passport, EIN letter and operating agreement.

### What happens if I don't file Form 5472?

The IRS can assess a $25,000 penalty per missed form, plus additional penalties if you don't correct the failure after notice. File on time every year.
