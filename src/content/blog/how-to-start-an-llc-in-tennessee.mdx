## Why Form an LLC in Tennessee in 2026?

Before diving into the mechanics, it's worth understanding what makes Tennessee a genuinely solid state for LLC formation — not just a convenient one.

**No personal income tax.** Tennessee's Hall Income Tax was fully repealed as of 2022. In 2026, the state levies zero personal income tax, meaning LLC members pay no Tennessee state tax on pass-through distributions. The [Tennessee Department of Revenue](https://www.tn.gov/revenue) confirms this — a meaningful advantage for solo operators and small partnerships.

**No publication requirement.** Unlike New York — which can cost business owners hundreds of dollars to publish LLC formation notices in local newspapers — Tennessee imposes no publication requirement whatsoever.

**Fully online filing.** The Tennessee Secretary of State's Business Services portal processes most Articles of Organization in 1–3 business days. You don't need to mail anything or visit an office.

**Low overhead.** Commercial real estate costs in secondary Tennessee markets remain well below comparable cities in Georgia, North Carolina, or Virginia, making the state attractive for businesses with physical operations.

## Step 1 — Choose a Name for Your Tennessee LLC

Your LLC name must satisfy three requirements under Tennessee Code Annotated § 48-249-106:

1. **Include a proper designator.** The name must contain "Limited Liability Company," "LLC," or "L.L.C." Abbreviations like "Ltd. Liability Co." are also acceptable.
2. **Be distinguishable.** It must differ from any existing Tennessee registered business name. Search available names using the [Tennessee Secretary of State's business search tool](https://tnbear.tn.gov/ECommerce/FilingSearch.aspx) before filing.
3. **Avoid restricted words.** Terms like "Bank," "Trust," "Insurance," or "University" require regulatory approval or specific licensure.

**Name reservation:** If you've found the right name but aren't ready to file, Tennessee allows a four-month reservation for a $20 fee filed with the Secretary of State.

**Trade name (DBA):** If you want to operate under a different name than your legal LLC name, register a trade name with the Tennessee Secretary of State for $20.

## Step 2 — Appoint a Registered Agent in Tennessee

Every Tennessee LLC must designate a registered agent — a person or entity with a physical street address in Tennessee who is available during business hours to receive legal notices, lawsuits, and official state correspondence on the LLC's behalf.

Your options:

- **Serve as your own registered agent.** Any Tennessee resident with a physical address in the state can fill this role. The tradeoff: your home or office address becomes part of the public record, and you must be reachable during business hours every day your LLC is active.
- **Use a trusted contact.** A colleague or family member can serve, but this creates complications if they move, change jobs, or are unavailable when a legal notice arrives.
- **Hire a professional registered agent service.** This is the cleanest option for most business owners. A professional service ensures you never miss a critical legal notice, keeps your personal address off public filings, and handles document forwarding.

## Step 3 — File Your Articles of Organization

The Articles of Organization is the document that officially creates your Tennessee LLC. You file it with the Tennessee Secretary of State's Division of Business Services.

**How to file:**
- **Online:** Through the Tennessee Business Registration portal. This is the fastest method.
- **By mail:** Send Form SS-4270 to Tennessee Secretary of State, 312 Rosa L. Parks Ave., 6th Floor, Nashville, TN 37243.

**What the Articles require:**
- LLC name and principal office address
- Registered agent name and Tennessee street address
- Name and address of each organizer
- Whether the LLC is member-managed or manager-managed
- Number of members (this determines your filing fee)
- Effective date (immediate or a specified future date)

**Tennessee LLC filing fee:** Tennessee uses an unusual per-member fee structure. The state charges **$50 per LLC member**, with a **minimum fee of $300** and a **maximum cap of $3,000**. In practice:

| Members | Filing Fee |
|---|---|
| 1–6 members | $300 (minimum) |
| 7 members | $350 |
| 10 members | $500 |
| 60+ members | $3,000 (maximum) |

For the vast majority of small business LLCs — single-member or two-member — you'll pay the $300 floor. This is the core Tennessee LLC cost to budget for upfront.

In my experience helping clients structure new businesses, I've seen too many founders treat the Articles of Organization as a formality. It isn't. It establishes your LLC's legal existence, locks in your governance structure, and sets the public record for your registered agent. Getting it right the first time saves you the $20 amendment fee and avoids confusion with banks or counterparties down the line.

**Processing time:** Online filings through the Tennessee Secretary of State's portal typically process in 1–3 business days — among the faster turnarounds in the country.

## Step 4 — Create an Operating Agreement

Tennessee law does not require LLCs to have a written operating agreement. That said, skipping one is a mistake.

An operating agreement governs how your LLC actually runs: how profits are distributed, how decisions are made, what happens when a member wants to exit, and how disputes get resolved. Without one, Tennessee's default LLC statutes apply — and those defaults may not reflect what you actually want for your specific business.

For single-member LLCs, the operating agreement does something especially important: it reinforces the separation between you and the business. Courts look for evidence that an LLC is being operated as a genuinely separate entity. A signed operating agreement is one of the clearest pieces of that evidence.

## Step 5 — Get an EIN and Open a Business Bank Account

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is your LLC's federal tax identification number. You need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, file federal taxes, and apply for business credit.

You can apply for an EIN for free directly at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). The online application takes about 10 minutes and your EIN is issued immediately. There's no reason to pay a formation service to do this for you — it's a straightforward process.

Once you have your EIN, open a dedicated business checking account before you spend or receive a single dollar in the business's name. Commingling personal and business funds is one of the fastest ways to lose your liability protection. In the event of a lawsuit, a plaintiff's attorney will look for exactly this kind of evidence to "pierce the corporate veil" and reach your personal assets.

## Step 6 — Understand Tennessee LLC Taxes and Ongoing Compliance

This is where Tennessee has some genuine quirks, and where a conversation with a CPA before your first filing can save you real money.

**Franchise & Excise Tax (F&E Tax):** Despite having no personal income tax, Tennessee does impose a business-level tax on most LLCs:

- **Franchise tax:** $0.25 per $100 of net worth or the value of real and tangible property in Tennessee, with a minimum payment of $100.
- **Excise tax:** 6.5% of Tennessee net earnings (essentially a corporate-style income tax applied at the entity level).

**Small business exemption:** LLCs with total gross receipts below **$50,000** are exempt from the Tennessee franchise and excise tax. Many new solo businesses fall under this threshold in their first one or two years — worth tracking carefully.

**Tennessee LLC annual report:** All active Tennessee LLCs must file an annual report with the Secretary of State. The Tennessee LLC annual report is due by **April 1st** each year and carries the same per-member fee structure as the formation filing — a **minimum of $300** for most LLCs. Missing this deadline doesn't immediately dissolve your company, but consistent non-filing will eventually lead to administrative dissolution.

**Local licenses and permits:** Tennessee has no universal statewide business license, but cities and counties impose their own requirements. Nashville businesses typically need a Metro business license from Davidson County; Memphis businesses fall under Shelby County requirements. Contact your local city or county clerk before you start operating.

**Federal BOI Report:** Under the Corporate Transparency Act, most new LLCs formed in 2024 or later are required to file a Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) report with FinCEN. Our [BOI Report Guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/) explains deadlines, exemptions, and how to file.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost to start an LLC in Tennessee?**

The minimum Tennessee LLC filing fee is $300 (for single-member or small LLCs). The state charges $50 per member, with a $3,000 cap. You'll also owe $300 minimum for the annual report each year. Add a registered agent service ($99–$125/year) and any formation service fees on top of that. Our full guide to [how much an LLC costs](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/) breaks down every line item.

**How long does it take to start an LLC in Tennessee?**

Online filings through the Tennessee Secretary of State typically process in 1–3 business days. Mail-in filings take 2–4 weeks, depending on current backlog. Using a formation service doesn't accelerate the state's review, but it reduces the chance of a rejection due to paperwork errors.

**Do I need a registered agent in Tennessee?**

Yes. Every Tennessee LLC must maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in the state. The agent must be available during normal business hours to receive legal notices and official state correspondence. P.O. boxes are not acceptable as a registered agent address.

**Does Tennessee require an LLC operating agreement?**

Tennessee law does not require a written operating agreement, but it is strongly recommended for all LLCs — especially those with multiple members. Without one, Tennessee's default statutory rules govern your LLC's internal operations, which may not match your intentions for profit splits, management authority, or what happens if a member exits.

**Does Tennessee have a personal income tax in 2026?**

No. Tennessee eliminated its personal income tax (the Hall Income Tax) effective 2022. In 2026, the state levies no personal income tax, meaning pass-through income from your LLC is not subject to Tennessee state income tax at the individual level.

**What taxes does a Tennessee LLC actually pay?**

Most Tennessee LLCs owe the state's Franchise & Excise Tax: a franchise tax of $0.25 per $100 of net worth (minimum $100) and an excise tax of 6.5% on Tennessee taxable earnings. LLCs with gross receipts below $50,000 are exempt from F&E tax. At the federal level, a single-member LLC is taxed as a sole proprietorship and a multi-member LLC as a partnership by default — or you can elect S-Corp treatment, which may reduce self-employment tax once profits are substantial.

**Can I be my own registered agent in Tennessee?**

Yes, if you have a physical Tennessee address and are consistently available during business hours. Many owners prefer to hire a registered agent service to keep their home address off public filings and ensure they never miss a legal notice while traveling or working remotely.

**When is the Tennessee LLC annual report due?**

Tennessee LLC annual reports are due by **April 1st** each year. The fee is a minimum of $300 (using the same $50-per-member structure as formation). Late filings can result in late fees and, eventually, administrative dissolution if ignored long enough.
