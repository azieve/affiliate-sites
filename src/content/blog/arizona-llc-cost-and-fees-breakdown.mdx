## Arizona LLC Filing Fee: The $50 State Fee

The primary cost to form an LLC in Arizona is the **$50 Articles of Organization fee** paid to the [Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC)](https://azcc.gov/). This is the official state agency that processes LLC filings.

You can file:
- **Online through the ACC eCorp portal:** $50 flat
- **By mail or in person:** $50 standard, processed in 10-15 business days
- **Expedited (same-day) processing:** $85 ($50 filing + $35 expedite fee)

In my experience, the online portal is reliable and genuinely faster than mail — most online filings are approved within 2-5 business days at no extra charge. If you're in a rush to get your LLC active before signing a contract or opening a business bank account, the $35 expedite fee is worth it. Otherwise, standard processing is fine.

**Comparison note:** Arizona's $50 is considerably cheaper than California ($70), New York ($200), or Massachusetts ($500). Even compared to neighboring Nevada ($75 + a mandatory list of officers), Arizona looks attractive.

## No Arizona LLC Annual Report (This Is a Big Deal)

This is the feature that catches most people off guard: **Arizona LLCs are not required to file annual reports or pay annual renewal fees.** Once you're formed, there is no annual maintenance fee payable to the state.

For context, here's what other states charge annually:
- California: $800 minimum franchise tax + $20 annual report
- Florida: $138.75 annual report fee
- Texas: No annual report, but a franchise tax based on revenue
- Delaware: $300 minimum franchise tax (see our [Delaware LLC franchise tax breakdown](/blog/delaware-llc-annual-franchise-tax/))

In Arizona, your ongoing state-level obligation is essentially zero — assuming you're a domestic LLC with no special circumstances. That adds up to hundreds or thousands in savings over a 5-10 year business horizon.

The [IRS confirms](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc) that LLC tax obligations are handled at the federal and state income tax level, not through separate annual fees — and Arizona's structure aligns well with this model.

## The Publication Requirement (Now Eliminated)

Until recently, Arizona had an unusual requirement: LLCs formed outside Maricopa and Pima counties had to publish a notice of formation in a newspaper for three consecutive weeks. This cost anywhere from $30 to $300 depending on the publication.

**Good news: As of September 1, 2023, Arizona eliminated the LLC publication requirement entirely.** No matter which county you're in, you no longer need to publish anything. This saves money and simplifies the process considerably.

If you formed your LLC before September 2023 and didn't complete publication, the current law doesn't retroactively penalize you — but it's worth confirming your LLC's standing with the ACC if you're uncertain.

## Registered Agent Costs in Arizona

Every Arizona LLC must have a **registered agent** with a physical Arizona address to receive legal documents and official state correspondence. You have three options:

**1. Act as your own registered agent: $0**
You can list yourself (or any Arizona resident) as the registered agent. The catch is your name and address become public record in the ACC database, and you must be available at that address during business hours. For home-based businesses, this creates privacy concerns.

**2. Use a professional registered agent service: $49–$300/year**
A professional service provides a business address, accepts documents on your behalf, and notifies you immediately. This is strongly recommended if you work from home or travel frequently.

## Operating Agreement: $0–$500

Arizona doesn't legally require an operating agreement, but every LLC should have one. It governs how the business is run, how profits are split, and what happens if a member leaves.

Options:
- **Free template from ZenBusiness or your formation service:** Functional for most simple LLCs
- **Attorney-drafted:** $200–$500 for a basic agreement; more complex multi-member LLCs can run $1,000+

For a single-member Arizona LLC, a template operating agreement is usually sufficient. For multi-member LLCs — especially those with unequal profit splits or buyout provisions — pay for an attorney's time. It's cheaper than litigation later.

## EIN (Employer Identification Number): Free

An EIN is your LLC's federal tax ID. You need it to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file taxes.

The IRS issues EINs for free at [irs.gov/ein](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). The process takes about 10 minutes online. Avoid any service that charges you for this — it's always free.

Formation services sometimes bundle EIN filing as a paid add-on ($70–$100). Skip it unless you'd genuinely prefer to have someone else do it.

## Arizona Business Taxes: What to Expect Ongoing

The Arizona LLC cost and fees breakdown isn't complete without addressing state taxes:

**Arizona income tax:** Arizona passed a flat income tax of **2.5%** effective 2023, one of the lowest flat rates in the country. For a pass-through LLC, your share of profits is reported on your personal return at this rate.

**Transaction Privilege Tax (TPT):** If your LLC sells taxable goods or services in Arizona, you'll need a TPT license ($12 one-time fee) and must collect and remit sales tax. Rates vary by city and product type.

**Self-employment tax:** Like all LLC owners who take income, you'll owe self-employment tax on net earnings (15.3% up to the Social Security wage base). This is a federal obligation, not Arizona-specific.

Arizona's 2.5% flat income tax compares favorably to California (up to 13.3%), Oregon (up to 9.9%), and even neighboring Nevada — though Nevada has no personal income tax at all. ## Hidden Costs to Budget For

Beyond the standard Arizona LLC cost and fees breakdown, here are the expenses that catch people by surprise:

**Business bank account:** Most business checking accounts are free to open, but some charge $10–$25/month in maintenance fees. Chase, Mercury, and Relay offer solid free options for LLCs.

**Business licenses:** Arizona doesn't have a general statewide business license, but many cities (Phoenix, Tucson, Scottsdale) require local business licenses. Phoenix, for example, charges a one-time $168 privilege license fee for most businesses.

**Fictitious name (DBA) filing:** If you operate under a name different from your LLC's legal name, you'll need a DBA filing with the county recorder. Maricopa County charges $10 for the first DBA and $2 for each additional.

**Foreign qualification:** If your LLC is formed in another state but operates in Arizona, you'll pay $150 to register as a foreign LLC — three times the domestic filing fee.

**Professional licenses:** Certain industries (contractors, healthcare, real estate, financial advisors) require state-level professional licenses, which vary widely in cost.

## FAQ: Arizona LLC Costs and Fees

**How much does it cost to form an LLC in Arizona?**
The minimum cost is **$50** — that's the Arizona Corporation Commission's Articles of Organization filing fee. With a formation service and registered agent, typical Year 1 costs range from $50 to $150.

**Does Arizona require an annual report for LLCs?**
No. Arizona is one of the few states that does not require LLCs to file annual reports or pay annual renewal fees. This significantly reduces your ongoing costs compared to most other states.

**How much is the Arizona LLC expedited filing fee?**
Expedited (same-day) processing costs an additional $35, bringing the total state fee to $85.

**Do I need a registered agent for my Arizona LLC?**
Yes. Every Arizona LLC must designate a registered agent with a physical Arizona address. You can serve as your own agent at no cost, or use a professional service for $49–$300/year.

**Is Arizona a good state to form an LLC?**
Arizona is a solid choice for most small businesses — especially those operating in Arizona. The $50 filing fee, no annual report requirement, and 2.5% flat income tax rate make it one of the more affordable and straightforward states for LLC formation and maintenance.

**Do Arizona LLCs pay franchise tax?**
No. Arizona does not impose a franchise tax on LLCs. You may owe Transaction Privilege Tax (TPT) if you sell taxable goods or services, but there is no blanket annual franchise tax like California or Delaware imposes.

**Can I form an Arizona LLC online?**
Yes. The Arizona Corporation Commission's eCorp portal allows you to file Articles of Organization completely online. Most filings are approved within 2-5 business days.

**What is the cheapest way to form an LLC in Arizona?**
The absolute cheapest way is to file directly with the ACC for $50.
