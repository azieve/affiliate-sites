The harder part is the cross-border tax treatment, because the United States and Canada classify the same LLC differently. ## Why Canadians Form a US LLC in 2026

The reasons we see most often are practical rather than exotic:

- **Selling to US customers.** A US entity can look more credible to American clients and marketplaces.
- **US payment processing and banking.** Some processors and platforms work more smoothly with a US-registered company that has an EIN.
- **Holding US real estate or a US side business.** Some owners want to keep liability separate from their Canadian affairs.
- **Limiting personal liability.** The usual LLC shield applies. See [what an LLC is](/learn/what-is-an-llc/) for the basics.

That claim is incomplete for any Canadian tax resident.

## The Core Problem: Two Countries, Two Classifications

An LLC is a flexible entity, and each country classifies it on its own terms.

**The US view.** A single-member LLC is a "disregarded entity" by default. A multi-member LLC is a partnership by default. Either can elect to be taxed as a corporation by filing [Form 8832](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8832). The IRS summarizes these rules on its [LLC page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc).

**The Canadian view.** The CRA has generally treated a US LLC as a corporation, whatever the US check-the-box status. Canada does not have a "disregarded entity" concept for foreign companies.

This is called a hybrid mismatch. The US sees a pass-through. Canada sees a corporation owned by a shareholder. Several consequences follow.

- Profits may be taxed in the LLC's hands for Canadian purposes, and again when paid out to you.
- The CRA may not treat US tax you paid personally on LLC income as a creditable foreign tax in the way you would expect.
- Canadian foreign affiliate reporting can be triggered.

The exact outcome depends on your facts: whether the LLC earns active or passive income, how it is owned, and where its management happens. A cross-border CPA or tax lawyer should confirm it before you file anything.

## How the Canada-US Tax Treaty Applies to an LLC

The Canada-US tax treaty is meant to prevent double taxation and cut withholding rates. For LLCs it contains a set of special rules, mainly in Article IV, added by the Fifth Protocol (effective 2008). They address "fiscally transparent" entities, which are entities taxed differently in the two countries.

In plain terms, the treaty may deny reduced rates on certain amounts paid to or through a hybrid entity. For example, if a Canadian resident receives income through a US LLC that Canada treats as a corporation but the US treats as transparent, the treaty benefit on that income can be limited. The relevant text is in the [IRS treaty documentation](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/tax-treaties) and Canada's own treaty materials on [canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html).

Treat the Canada US tax treaty LLC rules as a specialist topic, not a DIY one. Three practical points help.

1. **Do not assume treaty relief applies automatically.** Reduced withholding on US-source dividends (commonly 15%, or 5% for qualifying corporate shareholders) depends on the payee being treated as the beneficial owner under the treaty's hybrid rules.
2. **Treaty claims need paperwork.** You claim treaty benefits on Form W-8BEN (individuals) or W-8BEN-E (entities), and you need a valid taxpayer ID.
3. **Residency tie-breakers matter.** If you are a Canadian resident for tax purposes, the treaty generally lets Canada tax your worldwide income. The US then taxes only US-source income, such as income effectively connected with a US trade or business.

## Do You Owe US Income Tax?

For many Canadian owners the answer is no, or very little, but it depends on the type of income.

- **Services performed in Canada for US clients.** This is usually not US-source income, so a disregarded LLC typically has no US income tax liability. You report it in Canada.
- **Services performed physically in the US.** This can create US-source, effectively connected income (ECI). The treaty's business profits rules (permanent establishment threshold) may help, but you must analyze your situation.
- **US rental income or sale of US real property.** This is a different regime. It often has withholding and filing requirements, and the Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act (FIRPTA) may apply.
- **Product sales shipped to US buyers.** Whether this creates US tax depends on where the activity takes place, plus US state sales tax rules after *Wayfair*.

Remember that the filing duties are separate from the tax owed. An LLC with zero US tax can still owe you forms.

## The Filings Canadian LLC Owners Must Not Miss

### US side

**Form 5472 and pro forma Form 1120.** Since 2017 a foreign-owned single-member LLC has been treated as a corporation for reporting purposes. It must file [Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5472) with a pro forma Form 1120 every year, reporting "reportable transactions" with its owner, such as capital contributions and owner draws. The penalty for failing to file is $25,000 per form, and it grows if you ignore IRS notices. Our [Form 5472 filing guide](/blog/form-5472-for-foreign-owned-single-member-llc/) walks through it.

**EIN.** The LLC needs an Employer Identification Number. Without a Social Security Number, you can apply by fax, mail or phone using Form SS-4. Read our guide to [getting an EIN without an SSN](/blog/how-to-get-an-ein-without-an-ssn/).

**ITIN (sometimes).** An EIN is not a personal tax ID. If you must file a US personal return or claim a treaty benefit that requires a personal taxpayer number, you may need an ITIN. See [how to get an ITIN as a foreign LLC owner](/blog/how-to-get-an-itin-as-a-foreign-llc-owner/).

**Beneficial ownership reporting (BOI).** In March 2025, FinCEN issued an interim final rule narrowing Corporate Transparency Act reporting to entities formed outside the US and registered to do business here. A US-formed LLC with a Canadian owner is now generally exempt, but the rule has moved several times. Check [FinCEN's BOI page](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) for the current status, and see our [BOI report guide](/learn/boi-report-guide/).

**State filings.** These are annual or biennial reports and franchise taxes, which vary by state.

### Canadian side

- **T1134 (foreign affiliate information return).** If the CRA treats your LLC as a foreign affiliate, you may need to report it. Penalties for late filing can be significant.
- **T1135 (foreign income verification).** Required when your specified foreign property costs more than C$100,000 at any point in the year. Shares of a foreign corporation, which is how the CRA treats the LLC, may count.
- **Foreign accrual property income (FAPI) rules.** These can tax passive income earned by a controlled foreign affiliate in your hands, even before it is distributed.
- **Reporting the income itself.** Business income, dividends or management fees all go on your T1 (or your corporation's T2).

This is a lot of reporting for a one-person business. It does not make an LLC a bad idea, but it is a fair reason to ask whether you need one. I've seen too many owners pay $400 to form the company and then $2,500 a year in cross-border accounting to maintain it, for a business that earned $30,000.

## Banking, Payments and Mail

Opening a US bank account is often the real bottleneck for LLC for non-US residents. Some banks require an in-person visit. Others work remotely with an EIN, the formation documents, your passport and proof of address. Fintech business accounts and multi-currency providers are often easier for Canadians. Our guide to a [US bank account for a foreign-owned LLC](/blog/us-bank-account-for-a-foreign-owned-llc/) covers the options.

Keep the LLC's money separate from your own. That is a liability-protection rule, and for foreign-owned LLCs it is also a Form 5472 rule. Every transfer between you and the LLC is a reportable transaction.

Write an operating agreement too. Even a single-member LLC benefits, and a Canadian owner has extra reason to document how the company is run. See our [operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/).

## Is a US LLC Right for You?

Sometimes it isn't. Canadians who carry on business only in Canada often do better with a Canadian corporation. Those with US-source activity may need a US corporation (a C-corp) instead, because a corporation matches how the CRA already sees the entity and may avoid the hybrid issues. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Canadian resident own a US LLC?

Yes. No state requires US citizenship or residency to own an LLC. The practical constraints are a US registered agent, an EIN and the tax filings in both countries.

### How much does a US LLC for Canadian residents cost?

State filing fees range from $50 (New Mexico) to about $500 (Massachusetts). Add a registered agent at roughly $100 to $300 a year if it is not bundled, plus cross-border accounting. Many Canadians pay more in annual compliance than in the formation fee. See [how much an LLC costs](/learn/how-much-does-an-llc-cost/).

### Does the Canada-US tax treaty protect my LLC from double taxation?

Not automatically. The treaty includes special rules for hybrid entities, and those rules can limit benefits where Canada and the US classify the LLC differently. A cross-border professional should model your numbers.

### Do I need an ITIN or SSN to form an LLC?

No. You need an EIN for the LLC, which you can obtain without an SSN. An ITIN is only needed if you must file a personal US return or claim certain treaty benefits.

### What happens if I don't file Form 5472?

The IRS can assess a $25,000 penalty per form per year, with additional penalties for continued non-compliance after notice. A foreign-owned single-member LLC must file even when it has no income.

### Do I need to file a BOI report for my US LLC?

Under FinCEN's March 2025 interim rule, domestic US-formed companies are generally exempt, but rules can change. Verify the current requirement on FinCEN's site.

### Will the CRA tax my US LLC income?

Generally yes if you are a Canadian tax resident. The CRA taxes residents on worldwide income, and may treat the LLC as a foreign corporation with reporting duties such as T1134 and T1135.

### Is an LLC or a Delaware corporation better for a Canadian?

It depends on your income type and goals. Because Canada treats an LLC like a corporation anyway, some advisors prefer an actual US corporation to avoid hybrid-entity mismatches. This is a question for a cross-border tax professional.
