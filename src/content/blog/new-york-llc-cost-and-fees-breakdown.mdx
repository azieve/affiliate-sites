## The $200 Articles of Organization Filing Fee

Every New York LLC starts with the **Articles of Organization** (Form DOS-1336), filed with the New York Department of State, Division of Corporations. The filing fee is **$200** — paid once, when you form the company. You can file online through the state's [Business Express portal](https://dos.ny.gov/limited-liability-company-llc) or by mail.

This $200 fee is non-negotiable and identical for every LLC, whether you're a solo freelancer in Buffalo or a multi-member real estate venture in Manhattan. It's higher than many states — Texas charges $300, but states like Arizona ($50) and New Mexico ($50) are far cheaper — yet it's the publication requirement, not the filing fee, that makes New York expensive.

Standard processing takes about seven business days. If you're in a hurry, New York offers expedited service: **$25** for 24-hour processing, **$75** for same-day, and **$150** for two-hour turnaround. For most founders, standard processing is fine — the publication requirement (more on that below) will be your real timeline bottleneck anyway.

If you'd rather not wrestle with the state portal, a formation service handles this step for you. The difference is in the service fee: ZenBusiness starts at $0 plus state fees, while LegalZoom's paid tiers add $249 or more on top of the $200 you owe New York.

## The New York Publication Requirement — The Hidden Cost

Here's the part nobody warns you about. Within **120 days** of forming your LLC, New York law (Section 206 of the Limited Liability Company Law) requires you to **publish a notice of formation in two newspapers** — one daily and one weekly — for **six consecutive weeks**. The newspapers must be designated by the county clerk in the county where your LLC's principal office is located.

After the six weeks, the newspapers give you affidavits of publication, which you submit along with a **Certificate of Publication** and a **$50 filing fee** to the Department of State.

Sounds simple. The problem is the cost of the newspaper ads, which the state doesn't set — the newspapers do, and they vary wildly by county:

- **New York County (Manhattan):** $1,000–$2,000+. The designated newspapers here are among the most expensive in the country.
- **Kings (Brooklyn), Queens, Bronx:** $300–$1,000, depending on the designated papers.
- **Upstate counties (Albany, Erie, etc.):** $80–$300.

In my experience advising founders, this single line item causes more sticker shock than anything else in the New York LLC cost and fees breakdown. I've watched business owners budget $250 for "starting an LLC in New York" only to get a $1,500 publication bill from a Manhattan newspaper. It's the kind of cost that feels almost unbelievable until the invoice lands.

**The legal workaround that saves real money:** Your "principal office" county determines which newspapers you must use. Many founders legitimately use a registered agent located in a low-cost county — most commonly **Albany County** — which lets them satisfy the publication requirement for as little as $80–$300 instead of $1,500+. This is a well-established, fully legal strategy, and it's exactly why a [registered agent service](/learn/what-is-a-registered-agent/) is more than a convenience in New York — it can save you well over a thousand dollars. [Northwest Registered Agent](/go/northwest/) is a popular choice for this because its Albany-area address keeps publication costs at the low end, and privacy-minded owners appreciate keeping their home address off the public record.

Skipping publication isn't an option you want to rely on: if you don't complete it within 120 days, the state can **suspend your LLC's authority to do business** in New York. The company technically still exists, but it can't legally conduct business or bring lawsuits until it cures the lapse.

## The $9 Biennial Statement

Unlike most states that demand an annual report, New York uses a **Biennial Statement** — filed once every **two years**, in the calendar month your LLC was originally formed. The fee is a refreshingly low **$9**.

That's it. There's no income-based formula, no graduated scale — just a flat $9 every two years to keep your contact and registered agent information current with the Department of State. You file it online through the [Department of State's e-filing system](https://dos.ny.gov/limited-liability-company-llc). We cover the deadlines and exact steps in our dedicated [New York LLC Biennial Statement guide](/blog/new-york-llc-biennial-statement/).

Miss it, and your LLC's status changes to "past due." New York won't dissolve you for a missed biennial statement the way some states do, but a past-due status can complicate opening bank accounts, securing financing, or proving good standing to partners — so don't let it slide.

## The Cost New Yorkers Forget: The LLC Annual Filing Fee (IT-204-LL)

This is the second New York-specific cost that trips people up, and it's separate from the biennial statement. If your LLC has **more than one member** (or is treated as a partnership for tax purposes), or is a single-member LLC with New York-source income, you owe an annual **LLC filing fee** to the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance, reported on **Form IT-204-LL**.

The fee is based on your LLC's New York-source gross income for the year:

| NY-Source Gross Income | Annual Filing Fee |
|------------------------|-------------------|
| Single-member LLC (disregarded entity) | $25 |
| Up to $100,000 | $25 |
| $100,000 – $250,000 | $50 |
| $250,000 – $500,000 | $175 |
| $500,000 – $1,000,000 | $500 |
| $1,000,000 – $5,000,000 | $1,500 |
| $5,000,000 – $25,000,000 | $3,000 |
| Over $25,000,000 | $4,500 |

For most new and small businesses, this is just **$25 a year** — but it's a recurring cost you must budget for, and the form is due within 60 days after the end of your tax year. You can confirm the current brackets directly on the [New York Department of Taxation and Finance website](https://www.tax.ny.gov/). As always, tax thresholds can change, so verify your bracket each year or have your accountant confirm it.

## Registered Agent: Optional by Law, Valuable in Practice

Technically, New York does **not** require you to name a separate registered agent. The Secretary of State is automatically designated as your LLC's agent for service of process, and the state forwards any legal documents to the address you provide.

So why pay for one? Two reasons specific to New York:

1. **Publication savings.** As covered above, a registered agent in a low-cost county can slash your publication bill by over $1,000.
2. **Privacy.** If you don't name an agent, the address you list with the state — often your home — becomes part of the public record and the destination for any lawsuit served on your business.

A commercial registered agent typically runs **$0–$299 per year**. ## Operating Agreement: Required, but Often Free

New York is one of the few states that **legally requires** every LLC to adopt a written **operating agreement**. The law says it must be entered into before, at the time of, or within 90 days after filing the Articles of Organization.

Here's the saving grace: the state doesn't require you to file it or pay any fee for it. You simply need to have one. Your cost options:

- **$0** — Use a free template (most formation services, including ZenBusiness, include one).
- **$50–$200** — A customized template from an online legal service.
- **$500+** — A custom agreement drafted by a business attorney (worth it for multi-member LLCs with complex ownership).

For a deeper walkthrough of what belongs in the document, see our [LLC operating agreement guide](/learn/llc-operating-agreement/). For a single-member LLC, a solid template is almost always sufficient.

## EIN (Federal Tax ID): Always Free

Your **Employer Identification Number** comes directly from the IRS at **no cost**. You can apply online at [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number) and receive your number in minutes.

Be wary of any service that charges a standalone fee for "obtaining your EIN" — it's a free, five-minute task. Some formation packages bundle it as a convenience, which is fine, but you should never pay a separate $50–$100 line item for something the federal government gives away.

## Local Licenses and Industry Permits

New York State doesn't issue a general statewide business license, but depending on your industry and location you may need:

- **New York City business licenses** (for many regulated trades) through NYC's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection
- **Sales tax Certificate of Authority** if you sell taxable goods or services (free, but mandatory)
- **Professional licenses** for regulated professions (law, medicine, accounting, etc.)

These costs are entirely business-specific, ranging from **$50 to $500+**. Check both your city/county clerk and the relevant state agency before you open your doors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to form an LLC in New York in 2026?

The mandatory state cost starts at **$200** for the Articles of Organization, plus a **$50** Certificate of Publication fee and the newspaper publication cost (**$80–$2,000+** depending on county). Realistically, expect **$425–$600** total in a low-cost county, or up to **$1,775+** if you publish in Manhattan.

### Why is forming an LLC in New York so expensive?

It's not the filing fee — it's the **newspaper publication requirement**. New York requires you to publish a formation notice in two newspapers for six consecutive weeks, and in expensive counties like New York County (Manhattan), the ad costs alone can exceed $1,500.

### Can I avoid the New York publication requirement?

You can't skip it legally, but you can **dramatically reduce its cost** by using a registered agent address in a low-cost county (commonly Albany), which lets you publish in inexpensive newspapers for $80–$300 instead of $1,500+. Skipping publication entirely risks suspension of your LLC's authority to do business.

### What is the annual cost to maintain a New York LLC?

The recurring costs are the **$9 biennial statement** (every two years) and the **LLC annual filing fee (IT-204-LL)**, which is **$25** for most small businesses but scales up to $4,500 based on New York-source income. Add registered agent fees if you use one.

### Does New York require a registered agent for an LLC?

No. The New York Secretary of State is automatically your agent for service of process. However, hiring a commercial registered agent is highly recommended — it protects your privacy and, critically, can slash your publication costs by over $1,000.

### How long does it take to form an LLC in New York?

Standard processing of the Articles of Organization takes about **seven business days**. Expedited options range from $25 (24-hour) to $150 (two-hour). The real timeline driver is the **120-day publication window**, which itself takes six weeks of consecutive newspaper runs to complete.

### Do I have to pay the $9 biennial statement and the IT-204-LL fee?

Yes, if they apply to you. The **$9 biennial statement** is required of all New York LLCs every two years. The **IT-204-LL filing fee** applies to multi-member LLCs and single-member LLCs with New York-source income — most pay the $25 minimum annually.
